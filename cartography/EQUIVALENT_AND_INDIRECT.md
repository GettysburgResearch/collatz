# Equivalent and indirect counterexample lanes

## 12. Full-map equivalent-witness lanes

### 12.1 Solution cone

Issue #24 defines the pullback operator \((Fa)_m=a_{T(m)}\). Proposed results identify fixed sequences with component-constant sequences and extreme rays of the nonnegative fixed cone with component indicators. Thus Collatz is equivalent to exactly two components/extreme rays.

This is an exact reformulation. The nontrivial deliverable is one of:

- a new binary fixed ray with \(a_0=a_1=0\) and some \(a_n=1\);
- a non-root-of-unity unit-circle **point** eigenvalue with a proved support theorem;
- an analytic invariant that separates a cycle component from a cycle-free component and constructs the latter.

Ambient operator spectra, truncated kernel dimensions, or smooth invariant densities do not by themselves supply a component. The packet itself records Hilbert-space blindness and boundary-artifact risks.

### 12.2 Coverage deficit

For

\[
C(x)=\#\{n\le x:T^k(n)=1\text{ for some }k\},
\]

the exact disproof criterion is \(C(x)<x\) for one integer \(x\).

Conditional inverse-tree lower bounds reshape a hypothetical failure: one counterexample brings an entire inverse forest and likely polynomially many companions. That multiplicity is a consequence of failure, not evidence that failure occurs. The missing theorem is a genuine upper bound on backward coverage.

### 12.3 Spectral witness accounting

Issue #27 proposes a nuclear transfer-operator route. Its central risk is point-spectrum extraction: approximate spectrum or a locally nilpotent quotient may mimic spectral excess without any cycle or divergent component.

The route becomes valid only after proving a support theorem converting a certified nontrivial eigenvector/eigenvalue into a new functional-graph component, and then supplying a rigorous spectral certificate beyond the trivial cycle contribution.

## 14. Cross-direction connections not to miss

### 14.1 The product-formula constant is appearing in different notation

The ordinary-code complexity floor, the centered recurrence cone, and the completion-height zero-carry bound all contain the same ratio

\[
\frac{\log M}{\log(N/M)}.
\]

This is not coincidence: copied symbolic information, zero carries, and rational approximation all measure how long completion agreement can persist relative to archimedean height. These should be treated as one reusable “completion criticality” theorem family.

### 14.2 Finite-word universality is a negative strategic result

Several branches prove that every finite word or finite phase block is physically realizable. Therefore no finite forbidden-block grammar can settle the ordinary infinite problem. A successful theorem must be about:

- all-scale coherence of one root;
- stabilization of appended digits;
- a global height/product formula;
- or a finite invariant set that already lives in ordinary integers.

### 14.3 The proposed Evertse closure dominates the frozen room search

The collision lane reduces eventual room prefixes to at most 64, but PR #33's proposed exclusion does not need to decide those rooms one by one. It uses the cap/co-cap height and the fixed 258-coordinate physical equation to rule out every signed ordinary completion at once. Room-prefix or seam enumeration is now relevant mainly to auditing/narrowing `T-9705` or to designing an architecture outside its hypotheses.

### 14.4 Fresh primes and high complexity are two faces of memory

Fixed-prime multiplicative schemas fail in the collision and H lanes. Fixed-state symbolic schemas fail in the centered and foundry lanes. Both say that any survivor needs unbounded memory—arithmetically in prime support and symbolically in factor complexity. PR #33 shows that fresh endpoint primes alone do not save a fixed-width architecture when their outside-\(S\) height stays below the admissible exponent. An escape must create quantitatively enough new arithmetic mass or unbounded internal rank.

### 14.5 The direct-cycle and sanctuary routes are structurally special

They are the only active native routes whose final certificate is finite and ordinary from the start. They do not need a \(2\)-adic-to-integer passage. This is why they should remain visible even if finite searches have so far been negative.

### 14.6 Analytic reformulations need an extraction theorem

The solution cone and spectral programs exactly encode unknown components, but encoding is not extraction. Their next theorem must turn analytic data into a point-supported component, not merely establish a large function space or approximate spectral feature.
