# Atomic problems added by cartography pass 2

## ACL-P019 — Cross-cycle ordinary-spine closure

**Statement.** Starting from an exact physical state obtained by a legal phase-`-34` room opening, construct a finite rule generating an infinite sequence of negative-cycle phase handoffs. Prove that:

1. every handoff is the actual shortcut-Collatz trajectory of one explicit positive integer;
2. each normalization, valuation, odd core, and residue transition is exact;
3. the ordinary marker remains positive;
4. either the state returns to an earlier ordinary state in a nontrivial positive cycle, or an explicit resource potential is unbounded above along the orbit.

**Required first instance.** Reproduce issue #39's scale-22 head `2130`, the four tower types `2→1→3→0`, the odd core `49 mod 64`, and the forced phase cascade before generalizing.

**Full-conjecture implication.** The positive cycle or infinite nonterminating ordinary spine is a Collatz counterexample.

**Non-certificate.** The 187-million-bit phase-1 quotient and a 10,000-renewal prefix are finite evidence only.

## ACL-N019 — Cross-cycle net-resource dichotomy

**Statement.** Define an exact additive or multiplicative potential on states

```text
(phase family, signed quotient, odd core residue,
 available binary height, primitive prime support)
```

such that every legal cross-cycle handoff satisfies one of:

1. the potential decreases by a uniform positive amount and the orbit reaches a finite trap; or
2. a specified regenerative transition increases the potential enough to compensate all intervening losses.

Prove that an infinite ordinary handoff sequence either contains infinitely many regenerative transitions or is impossible.

**Implication.** A universal decrease closes the issue-#39 architecture. A physically realizable regenerative cycle supplies the central invariant needed by `ACL-P019`.

**Statement.** For every fixed positive period `r`, construct the `r`-component block moment functional associated with the decimated q-Gaussian moments. Prove a block Christoffel/multiple-orthogonality factorization whose normalized Cramer minors have enough common content—equivalently, a sufficiently small outside-prime gcd/height—to combine with the exact finite-place error and product formula.

**Firewall.** The proof must use one rational Padé pair with real and finite-place bounds on the same coefficients; it may not identify distinct real and `Q_2` limits.

## ACL-N034 — H primitive endpoint-product gate

**Statement.** For each branch of the PR #19 iteration-7 dichotomy, normalize the exact renewal bridge equation to a primitive nondegenerate projective zero sum and compute its endpoint product `Xi_H`. Prove one of:

1. `limsup Xi_H < 1`, with finitely many equation types, so a source-qualified `T-9831`-style theorem excludes every ordinary infinite H orbit in that branch; or
2. an explicit physical mechanism forcing `Xi_H >= 1` infinitely often.

**Implication.** The first alternative closes the branch. The second identifies the precise arithmetic resource a positive H survivor must exploit.

**Existing input.** PR #34 `L-9903` already proves the necessary growth laws `XY/H^d -> infinity` and `UV/J^d -> infinity` for every `d<1`; the missing step is the uniform endpoint-height estimate.

## ACL-N073 — Verify and maximize the finite-type Evertse boundary

**Statement.** Independently reconstruct PR #34 `T-9831` from the original Evertse source and prove a maximal classification theorem. Given a regenerative affine architecture, decide effectively whether it reduces to finitely many primitive nondegenerate equation types with fixed term count and endpoint product `<1`.

The theorem must state exactly which operations preserve the excluded class:

- bounded word concatenation;
- finite phase changes;
- bounded prime alphabets;
- bounded-rank regrouping;
- cap/co-cap sign normalization.

It must also state exact escape conditions for growing width, changing phase families, persistent degeneracy, or endpoint-product criticality.

**Implication.** Prevents relabeling excluded finite-type systems as new constructions and gives a formal target for issue #39 and future growing-type programs.
