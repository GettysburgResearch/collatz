## Statement

Put

\[
L=22,
\qquad a=14,
\qquad M=2^{22}=4194304,
\qquad N=3^{14}=4782969.
\]

Let

\[
r=621248,
\qquad s=708587,
\]

and let

\[
\begin{aligned}
D=\{&0,16,20,21,32,34,35,40,42,49,68,69,70,\\
    &78,79,92,93,94\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Then, for every \(q\ge0\) and every \(d\in D\),

\[
\boxed{
T^{22}(4194304q+621248+d)
=4782969q+708587.
}
\tag{2}
\]

Every branch has exactly fourteen odd steps. Since

\[
4782969>4194304,
\]

this is a supercritical collision fiber of cardinality eighteen.

By `T-0002`, it induces

\[
\boxed{
H(4194304B+d)=4782969B+d,
\qquad d\in D.
}
\tag{3}
\]

The invariant lifting class is

\[
\boxed{
A\equiv87339\pmod{588665},
}
\tag{4}
\]

because

\[
N-M=588665,
\qquad s-r=87339.
\]

The lift to an ordinary integer is

\[
\nu(A)=
\frac{4782969A-619363136}{588665}.
\tag{5}
\]

An infinite admissible orbit of (3), beginning in (4) with \(A\ge M\) and
\(\nu(A)>0\), would therefore be a positive-integer Collatz counterexample.
No such orbit is claimed here.

## Motivation

The earlier atlas recorded only consecutive runs, whose best width through
length 17 was six. The present fiber is sparse: its span is only 95 residues,
but its eighteen valid branches are not one interval. `T-0002` shows that all
eighteen branches are legitimate digits of one induced chart.

The chart also has a small expansion margin:

\[
\frac NM\approx1.1403486729,
\]

and its base-\(M\) boundary-growth slope from `T-0003` is

\[
\log_M(N/M)\approx0.00861250046,
\]

or roughly one new base-\(M\) digit per 116 induced steps. The combination of
a richer alphabet and slowly moving high-order boundary may be more useful for
a macro-tile construction than raw expansion alone.
