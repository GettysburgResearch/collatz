# T-0032 — Infinite corrected-stage paths require unbounded fresh-prime support

Claim ID: `T-0032`  
Title: No infinite ordinary corrected-stage trajectory can remain in a fixed finite-rank multiplicative group

## Statement

Consider an infinite ordinary trajectory through corrected stages at scales

\[
m=m_0,m_0+1,m_0+2,\ldots,
\qquad m_0\ge12,
\]

with one source-type word

\[
w_m=(i_{m,0},\ldots,i_{m,255})
\in\{0,1,2,3\}^{256}
\]

at every scale. Let

\[
W_m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}
\]

be the scaled ordinary boundary tail of `L-0031`.

Then the prime support

\[
\boxed{
\bigcup_{m\ge m_0}
\{p\text{ prime}:p\mid W_m\}
}
\tag{1}
\]

is infinite.

Equivalently, no finite set of primes `S` can contain the prime divisors of all
stage-boundary words `W_m`.

In particular, the following candidate classes cannot contain an infinite
ordinary corrected-stage trajectory:

1. `W_m` lying in one fixed finitely generated subgroup of `Q^*`;
2. monomial schemas
   \[
   W_m=c_m2^{a_m}3^{b_m}
   \]
   with `c_m` chosen from a fixed finite library;
3. any finite collection of fixed-prime-support affine or multiplicative
   templates.

Thus every hypothetical cap-stitch trajectory must create genuinely fresh
ordinary prime support at infinitely many scales.

## Proof

Assume for contradiction that all prime divisors of all `W_m` belong to one
finite set `S`. Let

\[
\Gamma_0
=
\langle -1,2,3,S\rangle
\subset\mathbb Q^*
\tag{2}
\]

be the finite-rank multiplicative group generated by those primes together
with `2` and `3`.

There are only finitely many stage source words. Hence one fixed word

\[
w=(i_0,\ldots,i_{255})
\tag{3}
\]

occurs at infinitely many scales. Restrict to that infinite set of scales.

For such a scale, `L-0031` gives

\[
2^{\mathcal E_m}W_{m+1}
=
3^{\mathcal A_m}W_m
+
\sum_{k=0}^{255}
2^{U_{m,k}+\alpha_{i_k}}
3^{V_{m,k}+\beta_{i_k}}.
\tag{4}
\]

Divide by the positive left side and define

\[
x_{m,*}
=
\frac{3^{\mathcal A_m}W_m}
{2^{\mathcal E_m}W_{m+1}},
\tag{5}
\]

\[
x_{m,k}
=
\frac{
2^{U_{m,k}+\alpha_{i_k}}
3^{V_{m,k}+\beta_{i_k}}
}
{2^{\mathcal E_m}W_{m+1}}
\qquad(0\le k<256).
\tag{6}
\]

Every coordinate belongs to `Gamma_0`, and

\[
\boxed{
x_{m,*}+\sum_{k=0}^{255}x_{m,k}=1.}
\tag{7}
\]

Regard the coordinate tuple as lying in the finite-rank subgroup

\[
\Gamma_0^{257}\subset(\mathbb Q^*)^{257}.
\]

### Nondegeneracy

Every coordinate in (7) is a positive rational number. Therefore no nonempty
proper subsum of the left side can vanish. Every solution is nondegenerate in
the exact sense of PR #13 `LIT-KTHM-0043`.

### Distinct scales give distinct solutions

Use the two toll coordinates `k=0` and `k=1`. Their ratio is

\[
\frac{x_{m,0}}{x_{m,1}}
=
2^{
\alpha_{i_0}-\alpha_{i_1}-11(t_{m,1}+1)
}
3^{
\beta_{i_0}-\beta_{i_1}+7(t_{m,1}+1)
}.
\tag{8}
\]

Here

\[
t_{m,1}=2^m+2^{m-8}.
\tag{9}
\]

If two distinct scales gave the same ratio, multiplicative independence of
`2` and `3` would force equality of both exponents in (8), hence equality of
`t_(m,1)`, which is impossible. Thus the infinitely many occurrences of `w`
produce infinitely many distinct solutions of the same equation (7) inside
one fixed finite-rank group.

PR #13 `LIT-KTHM-0043`, the Evertse--Schlickewei--Schmidt finiteness theorem,
allows only finitely many nondegenerate solutions. This contradiction proves
(1). ∎

## Applicability checklist for `LIT-KTHM-0043`

The literature review requires six items; all are explicit here.

1. **One fixed equation.** After the repeated word `w` is selected, equation
   (7) always has 257 terms and coefficient one.
2. **Finite-rank group.** Under the contradiction hypothesis, every coordinate
   lies in `Gamma_0`, generated by one finite prime set.
3. **Injective parameter map.** Equation (8) distinguishes all scales.
4. **Proper-subsum audit.** Positivity rules out every vanishing nonempty proper
   subsum.
5. **Finite alphabet.** One of the `4^256` source words occurs infinitely often.
6. **Ordinary boundary.** The contradiction concerns the actual positive
   ordinary integers `W_m`, not a 2-adic completion.

## Strategic interpretation

The fixed-word S-unit route does apply, but not in the strongest hoped-for
form. The connector terms themselves collapse perfectly to a fixed positive
S-unit equation. The sole escape is the endpoint prime support.

Therefore the cap-stitch frontier splits into two regimes:

```text
persistent finite prime support
    -> impossible by T-0032;

unbounded fresh prime support
    -> the only remaining ordinary regime.
```

This is analogous to the persistent/fresh-prime split identified independently
in the H and completion-height packets. It explains why a direct S-unit slogan
cannot finish the unrestricted problem, while still giving a rigorous and
large exclusion class.

The positive quadratic generator `V_m` from `T-0030` is not automatically
excluded: numbers of the form `3^(7*2^m)-1` naturally have changing arithmetic
support. Any use of that generator must now prove that its fresh primes are
routed compatibly into the canonical cap-to-correction stitches.

## Gap audit

- Infinite prime support is necessary, not contradictory by itself.
- The theorem gives no effective bound on when a new prime must appear; the
  imported S-unit theorem is used qualitatively.
- It does not prove that the canonical correction sequence has finite prime
  support.
- It does not construct a cap-stitch tail, a finite initialization, or a
  Collatz counterexample.
