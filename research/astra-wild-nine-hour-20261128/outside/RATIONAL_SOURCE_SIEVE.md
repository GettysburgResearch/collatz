# Infinitely many accurate dates exclude every competing rational binary source

**Proposed written theorem.** Fix an infinite shortcut script. Its true binary source \(b\in\mathbb Z_2\) exists and is unique by the parity-cylinder bijection. Write \(F_t(x)=A_t x+C_t/2^t\) for the prescribed composition on a real input, where \(A_t=3^{s_t}/2^t\). Suppose \(A_t\ge e^{-o(t)}\) on the dates under consideration. A single positive real trajectory tending to infinity under the script is sufficient for this slope condition, by its real logarithm identity.

* At infinitely many dates suppose an ordinary input \(x\) satisfies \(\|F_t(x)\|\le e^{-\sigma t}\), with fixed \(\sigma>0\). Then \(b=x\) or \(b\notin\mathbb Q\). In particular, if even one of its true starting bits disagrees with the script, every rational binary source, of either sign and any odd denominator, is excluded. **No spacing or density assumption on the dates is required.**
* More generally, allow a reduced rational probe \(p_t/q_t\), \(q_t>0\), and a positive endpoint denominator \(d_t\), with \(1+|p_t|+q_t+d_t=e^{o(t)}\). Suppose \(\|d_t F_t(p_t/q_t)\|\le e^{-\sigma t}\). If \(b\in\mathbb Q\), the probe equals \(b\) on *all sufficiently late such dates*. Variable positive finite witnesses of subexponential height that do not eventually equal one rational therefore also exclude every rational source. They need not have been chosen from a common arithmetic progression.
* For an algebraic constant \(\theta\notin\mathbb Z\), if \(\|F_t(x)-\theta\|\le e^{-\sigma t}\) infinitely often at a fixed ordinary \(x\), then \(b\notin\mathbb Q\). For a genuine positive escaping ordinary orbit, its own affine intercept \(C_t/2^t\) stays at distance greater than \(e^{-\sigma t}\) from each fixed algebraic phase modulo one at all sufficiently late dates.

The [regular-observation theorem](EXPONENTIAL_INTEGER_SHADOWS.md) recovers exact execution from one fixed ordinary input on a sufficiently regular schedule. Here the conclusion at arbitrary sparse dates is a rational-source alternative. No part identifies an arbitrary irrational binary source with a positive ordinary integer.

## Comparing rational candidates

If the binary source is rational \(a/b_0\) in lowest terms with odd positive \(b_0\), each of its genuine rational iterates belongs to \(b_0^{-1}\mathbb Z\). This follows directly from its legal parity divisions. Multiply the difference of the two affine outputs to obtain
\[
 h_t=d_t(b_0p_t-aq_t),\qquad
 \|h_t A_t\|\le b_0q_t\,\|d_t F_t(p_t/q_t)\|.                        \tag{1}
\]
When \(h_t\ne0\) its magnitude is \(e^{o(t)}\), so (1) violates the [uniform varying-clock lower bound](NATIVE_AFFINE_RECTANGLES.md#the-varying-clock-lower-bound). This proves the first two assertions. The same proof with two approximate probes instead of one exact rational source shows that, at any sufficiently large date, at most one distinct subexponential-height rational probe can have an exponentially accurate subexponential-denominator endpoint. More explicitly for probes \(p/q,p'/q'\) with denominators \(d,d'\) at the outputs, take \(h=dd'(pq'-p'q)\) and bound \(\|h A_t\|\) by \(qq'(d'\|dF_t(p/q)\|+d\|d'F_t(p'/q')\|)\). Uniformity is relative to any prescribed subexponential height envelopes.

## An algebraic fractional phase

For any fixed real algebraic \(\gamma\), on the indicated slope domain and for \(1\le h=e^{o(t)}\),
\[
                        \|h A_t-\gamma\|\ge e^{-o(t)}.                 \tag{2}
\]
Here logarithmic notation means that for each \(\sigma>0\) the distance eventually exceeds \(e^{-\sigma t}\). To prove (2), suppose instead that the distance is exponentially small along a sequence. Put \(U=h3^{s_t}\), \(V=k2^t\), \(W=2^t\), with \(k\) the nearest integer to \(hA_t-\gamma\). Use real forms \(U-V-\gamma W,U,W\) and coordinate forms at two and three. Their product is at most \(h\|hA_t-\gamma\|\); the height is at most \(e^{O(t)}\). The algebraic-coefficient Subspace Theorem gives one rational relation \(aU+bV+cW=0\) on a subsequence, allowing \(k=0\). Rational vectors in a proper algebraic subspace span a proper rational subspace. If \(b=0\) this fixes the rational ratio \(U/W=hA_t\), whose reduced denominator is at least \(2^t/h\to\infty\), a contradiction.

Otherwise substitute \(k=-(a/b)hA_t-c/b\). If \(1+a/b=0\), exponentially small distance forces \(c/b=\gamma\), and then \(hA_t=k+\gamma\) has bounded denominator, again impossible. In the remaining case \(hA_t\) is exponentially close to a fixed algebraic real \(\eta\). For \(\eta=0\) this contradicts the assumed lower slope. For \(\eta\ne0\) apply the two-dimensional theorem to \((U,W)\) with real forms \(U-\eta W,U\) and the finite coordinate forms. The product is at most \(h|hA_t-\eta|\), so the ratio \(U/W\) again lies on one fixed rational line. This proves (2), with an ineffective onset. The fixed-power fractional-parts results for \((a/b)^n\) are classical antecedents. [Bugeaud, *Quantitative versions of the Subspace Theorem and applications*, p. 38, algebraic real forms and rational exceptional subspaces](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.749.pdf) · [Corvaja–Zannier, *On the rational approximations to the powers of an algebraic number*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0403522)

Now suppose a rational \(a/b_0\ne x\) executes the script. Put \(h=|b_0x-a|\). Multiplying the observed phase by \(b_0\) gives an exponential approximation to one of \(\pm b_0\theta\) by \(hA_t\bmod1\), contradicting (2). If \(a/b_0=x\), the endpoint is already an integer, which also rules out a fixed noninteger phase. For the native intercept conclusion, physical integrality gives \(C_t/2^t\equiv-xA_t\pmod1\); apply (2) with multiplier \(x>0\).

## A single wrong script with accurate visits for every positive ordinary probe

The [positive-lower-density construction](POSITIVE_LOWER_DENSITY_SHADOWS.md) can use **one shared script for all positive ordinary starting inputs**. At stage \(j\ge1\) designate input \(x_j=1+v_2(j)\), and steer the one real state obtained from that positive integer under the entire prefix. The real interval argument is uniform in that state. Its growth reserve inequality is independent of the pair digits, so every fixed positive input simultaneously has positive lower real exponential growth. Each fixed integer \(x\) is designated once in every \(2^x\) stages; the time ratios of consecutive stages are bounded by \(4.3\). Its genuine rounded windows consequently give positive lower ordinary time density, with the density constant allowed to depend on \(x\). The same growing-modulus and real \(2^{-t/25}\) accuracy holds separately for each input. An arbitrary prescribed finite prefix may be inserted at the beginning.

For any two distinct fixed probes their good sets of that exponential precision intersect only finitely often, by applying (2) with \(\gamma=0\) to their fixed nonzero difference. In fact the common binary source here is irrational, by the rational-source sieve since more than one fixed probe has infinitely many good dates. Independently, the initial \(t\) consecutive ones of every stage would give any rational binary source \(a/b_0\) a state at time \(t\) whose numerator after adding \(b_0\) is divisible by \(2^t\). Its absolute value is \(O_{a,b_0}((3/2)^t)\), so that state would be \(-1\) and all future letters would be one, contrary to the pair words. The infinitely many good schedules at different source integers are therefore genuinely incompatible with any one rational execution. They supply no true escaping ordinary source.
