# Rational atoms in every finite-place projection of an ordinary time average

**PROPOSED, written proof.** Let \(T\) denote the shortcut map on the profinite integers \(\widehat{\mathbb Z}\). Select the letter from the binary component, then apply \(F_0(z)=z/2\) or \(F_1(z)=(3z+1)/2\) at every place. Division at 2 is on the prescribed integral branch. This is a continuous map. Each weak subsequential limit of the ordinary time averages of a single positive orbit is an invariant probability, by compactness and telescoping. The following applies to *every* invariant probability \(\mu\), whether or not it arises that way. Let \(\mu_p\) denote its marginal on \(\mathbb Z_p\).

1. For every prime \(p\), each atom \(u\) of \(\mu_p\) is a rational point on a periodic shortcut orbit on \(\mathbb Z_2\). Almost surely on the event \(z_p=u\), the binary coordinate is that very rational \(u\).
2. For a periodic binary point \(u\) whose period contains an odd letter, the events \(z_2=u\) and \(z_3=u\) agree up to measure zero. The rational is automatically integral at 3. At the all-even point \(u=0\), the implication from ternary to binary still holds.
3. At a binary atom \(u\), for each \(p\ge5\) the conditional \(\mu_p\)-law has at most one atom, at \(u\) if it is \(p\)-integral. Its remainder is absolutely continuous with respect to additive Haar measure. If the rational \(u\) has \(p\) in its reduced denominator, the law is exactly Haar within each of certain finite residue classes, as computed below. Those digits are jointly uniform at **all** primes dividing the reduced denominator, conditional on the corresponding finite residues.

Thus ternary atomic mass away from zero remembers a genuine rational cycle in both coordinates. At an auxiliary prime, an independent atomic rational center cannot carry any mass on that binary phase. The statements allow irrational, nonatomic components and signed nonordinary binary cycles.

## Recurrence identifies the rational and all its binary digits

Under an invariant probability, almost every point of the positive-mass event \(z_p=u\) returns to it at arbitrarily large times by Poincaré recurrence. A return with word \(w\), length \(d>0\), and \(s\) odd digits, gives \(2^d u=3^su+C_w\) in \(\mathbb Q_p\), hence
\[
                   u=\frac{C_w}{2^d-3^s}\in\mathbb Q .
\]
The denominator is odd and nonzero. Thus \(u\in\mathbb Z_2\), and a \(p\)-adic identity here is also an exact rational identity. Applied to \(u\), every one of the intermediate steps must be binary-integral. A wrong branch gives binary valuation \(-1\), after which each further branch subtracts one from that valuation. Hence \(w\) is part of the actual binary orbit of \(u\) and closes it. If \(x=z_2\) had the same word, then \(F_w(x)-F_w(u)=3^s(x-u)/2^d\) is binary-integral, so \(x-u\in 2^d\mathbb Z_2\). The arbitrarily late returns force \(x=u\). This proves statement 1, including \(p=2\). A nonperiodic ordinary center cannot be an atom at any prime in any such limit.

For the reverse implication at three, restrict to the event \(z_2=u\) of positive mass. Let \(d\) be the period. The first-return map \(T^d\) preserves the normalized restriction. Indeed the finite binary orbit is invariant, its preimage differs from it by a null set, and on its phases \(T\) acts as a cyclic permutation. On the ternary coordinate the return is \(z\mapsto u+r(z-u)\), \(r=3^s/2^d\). If \(s>0\), the rational formula has denominator prime to three and \(|r|_3<1\). Iterates converge uniformly on \(\mathbb Z_3\) to \(u\), so their stationary law is \(\delta_u\). If \(s=0\), the whole word is zero and \(u=0\); on that binary phase \(z\mapsto z/2\) at three preserves Haar, for example.

## The exact law at a prime omitted from binary and ternary

Fix a phase of positive binary mass, and keep \(r,u\) from the preceding proof. For \(p\ge5\), \(r\in\mathbb Z_p^\times\) and is not a rational root of unity. Translation by \(-u\) places the conditional \(p\)-law on \(\mathbb Z_p-u\) in \(\mathbb Q_p\). It is invariant under multiplication by \(r\), hence also by the compact group \(G_p=\overline{\langle r\rangle}\), by continuity. With \(m\) the order of \(r\) modulo \(p\) and \(e=v_p(r^m-1)\), we have
\[
              G_p=\bigcup_{j=0}^{m-1} r^j(1+p^e\mathbb Z_p).
\]
For completeness, for odd \(p\) and \(v_p(y)=e\ge1\), the binomial expansion gives \(v_p((1+y)^p-1)=e+1\). Raising to a positive power prime to \(p\) leaves that valuation at \(e\). Thus \(r^m\) has exact order \(p^k\) modulo \(p^{e+k}\), which proves the closure. Every nonzero orbit of \(G_p\) is a finite union of open balls in \(\mathbb Q_p\). On each such orbit the unique invariant probability is the Haar orbit average, and on each constituent ball it is normalized additive Haar, by the displayed description. The nonzero orbits are countable, since they are disjoint open subsets of a second-countable space. The only remaining candidate is the point zero in translated coordinates. This gives statement 3 for one prime. In particular it describes the *whole conditional law*, rather than just forbidding off-diagonal atoms.

Write \(\Delta=2^d-3^s\). If \(p\) divides the reduced denominator of \(u=C_w/\Delta\), put \(a_p=v_p(\Delta)\), \(b_p=v_p(C_w)<a_p\). Such a prime is at least five, including in the all-even case where the reduced denominator is one. Now \(m=1\), \(G_p=1+p^{a_p}\mathbb Z_p\), and \(v_p(z-u)=b_p-a_p\) for every \(z\in\mathbb Z_p\). Undoing translation, the orbit of \(z\) is exactly \(z+p^{b_p}\mathbb Z_p\). In particular if \(p\nmid C_w\) the conditional marginal is *full Haar at \(p\)*.

For the finite set \(P\) of these primes, the closure of the integer powers of the vector \((r)_{p\in P}\) is \(\prod_{p\in P}(1+p^{a_p}\mathbb Z_p)\). Indeed the orders at precisions \(a_p+k_p\) are the pairwise coprime integers \(p^{k_p}\). The Chinese remainder theorem realizes each prescribed tuple of powers at every finite precision. The conditional law given the joint residues \(z_p\bmod p^{b_p}\) is therefore product Haar on their joint class, whenever the conditioning class has positive mass. Arbitrary correlations between those low residues remain possible. For \(p\) not in \(P\), independence from such coordinates is not asserted.

## Finite ordinary version and limitation

During \(k\) successive copies of \(w\) in one positive ordinary trajectory, the states at the copy boundaries modulo \(\prod_{p\in P}p^{b_p+j_p}\) run periodically through the entire class of their low residues, once per \(\prod p^{j_p}\) copies. This holds before taking any empirical limit. To verify it, subtract the rational \(u\) locally, use \(z_l-u=r^l(z_0-u)\), and use the orders and CRT as above. The discrepancy in *counts for one residue* from \(k/\prod p^{j_p}\) is at most one when observing the \(k\) starting boundaries. When the denominator is one this assertion is empty.

Positivity of the original ordinary orbit does not rule out a negative rational as a local limit. The Dirac probability at the diagonal signed point \(-1\) is invariant, has every coordinate atomic, and gives odd frequency one. The binary cylinders of this point have ordinary positive representatives of every finite duration. For example \(x=2^j-1\) follows the all-odd word for \(j\) ticks. This constructs changing positive starts, not one such all-time orbit. For a genuinely escaping source every limiting odd frequency is at least \(\log_3 2\), by the telescoping formula in the [short-predecessor result](CONGRUENCES_TO_SHORT_PREDECESSORS.md). In particular a time-average limit supported on the positive \(1,2\) cycle is excluded. A diffuse law, a mixture, or a negative rational atom consistent with the drift bound is still possible at this level. Excluding these for one ordinary source needs further arithmetic.
