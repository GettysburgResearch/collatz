# Which purely atomic forward laws survive every odd-place equality?

**PROPOSED, written classification for a relaxation and a finite ordinary realization theorem.** Let \(T\) be the shortcut map, acting on \(\widehat{\mathbb Z}\) by choosing the binary parity and applying its rational affine branch at every place. The uniform probability on a signed ordinary shortcut cycle \(C\) is \(m_C\). Its odd frequency \(q_C\) and its mean linear drift \(g_C\) are
\[
 q_C=\frac{\#\{\text{odd inputs in one period}\}}{|C|},\qquad
 g_C=q_C\log3-\log2 .
\]
For a probability mixture \(\pi=\sum_C w_Cm_C\), let \(g(\pi)=\sum_C w_Cg_C\).

**Necessity on one positive escaping orbit.** Every purely atomic weak limit of its initial empirical probabilities on the *whole* profinite completion is a probability mixture of signed nonzero **integer** cycles and satisfies \(g(\pi)\ge0\).

**Sharpness with one local equality relaxed.** Conversely, take any such mixture with \(g(\pi)\ge0\), any fixed positive integer \(x\), and any \(N\ge0\). There is a single infinite shortcut script with the following properties:

- The positive real state starts at precisely \(x\). At **every odd prime**, the local coordinate is exactly the embedding of this same real rational at every time. The first \(N\) letters are the true shortcut letters of \(x\). The next letter is wrong, and the binary state driving all letters is a fixed \(2\)-adic integer congruent to \(x\) modulo \(2^N\).
- The resulting profinite time averages converge to \(\pi\) along the full sequence of times. The real rational orbit tends to \(+\infty\), and its exponential rate per shortcut tick is exactly \(g(\pi)\). The critical case \(g(\pi)=0\) is included.
- The script is aperiodic; the driving binary coordinate can be forced to be irrational. At increasing finite precisions one can also realize growing prefixes by genuine simple positive integer orbit segments above a growing floor, with a different initial integer permitted for each prefix. The construction does not produce an ordinary escaping seed. On the infinite script the original real value and original binary value disagree already modulo \(2^{N+1}\).

Thus the complete class of purely atomic laws allowed by stationarity, ternary units and nonnegative drift is attained with exact real and odd-prime agreement. An exclusion of these laws on true ordinary escape must use more than this agreement and any fixed amount of initial binary precision.

## Necessity and the distinction from atoms at one place

An atom of an invariant probability for a deterministic map returns to the same point by Poincaré recurrence and hence is periodic. At a profinite point of period \(d\), with \(s\) odd digits, the identity at *every* prime is \((2^d-3^s)z=C_w\) with the same nonzero integer denominator and the same integer numerator. The unique rational \(C_w/(2^d-3^s)\) is integral at every prime, hence is a signed integer. Invariance gives equal mass to all phases of its cycle. This is stronger than a statement about an atom of one marginal, which permits a rational with a genuine denominator and a nonatomic conditional law at its primes. [Marginal atoms and denominator-prime laws](ATOMS_OF_ORDINARY_TIME_AVERAGES.md)

A positive integer orbit has a first odd input. Its output is a ternary unit, and every subsequent odd or even step preserves ternary units. Thus a weak empirical limit has zero mass on the clopen congruence class \(0\bmod3\), excluding the all-even zero cycle. For a positive escaping orbit the correction \(\sum_{i<t,\mathrm{odd}}\log(1+1/(3x_i))=o(t)\). On a subsequence of times approaching \(\pi\), the odd frequency converges to \(\int1_{\mathrm{odd}}\,d\pi\), a clopen test. Telescoping \(\log x_t\) and using \(x_t\ge1\) gives \(g(\pi)\ge0\).

## Steering the physical residue coordinates

Write \(F_0(z)=z/2\), \(F_1(z)=(3z+1)/2\) without guard. At a modulus \(M\) coprime to six they are permutations. The two equally sloped compositions \(F_1F_0,F_0F_1\) differ by \(1/4\). Hence the group they generate contains translation by the unit \(1/4\) and acts transitively. The positive semigroup of permutations on this finite set contains the inverses, so **from every residue to every prescribed residue a positive word exists**. A shortest path uses at most \(M-1\) letters, by deleting cycles in a finite directed graph. The one-element case uses the empty word. This elementary commutator also appears in PR215's finite-extension mixing argument. [Pinned finite extension](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/4f33ecc2de6f0ccf605a68c28da5abea0348e47b/research/astra-nine-hour-20261128/machinery/PROFINITE_ANCESTORS.md)

At stage \(m\ge3\), impose modulus \(D_m=\prod_{p\le m}p^m\) and write \(M_m=\prod_{5\le p\le m}p^m\), with products over primes. The chosen real rational starts at \(x\) and stays positive dyadic. Its residue at every odd prime is therefore well-defined. Suppose we steer to \(a\bmod M_m\), where \(a\) is a selected phase of a signed nonzero integer cycle. Apply its true period word \(w\) repeatedly. The \(M_m\)-coordinate then follows the cycle exactly. At the starting phase, \(F_w(v)-a=3^s2^{-d}(v-a)\), \(s\ge1\), so after \(m\) periods the actual rational agrees with \(a\bmod 3^m\). Later letters preserve that agreement at the corresponding cyclic phases. At every date with at least \(m\) further matching letters, the controlling binary coordinate agrees at precision \(2^m\). Indeed the first \(m\) shortcut parity letters prescribe exactly one binary class modulo \(2^m\). For existence, successively append the unique upper bit giving the next parity, since the affine numerator's slope is odd. At the infinite level these compatible residues give one binary point for the complete chosen script.

Consequently, in any long run of copies of \(w\), all observations except at most \(m|w|+m\) of them are the exact prescribed cycle phases modulo \(D_m\). This conclusion holds uniformly in the incoming positive rational and in the later script.

## A positive-drift finite word at every precision

Choose finite mixtures of cycle laws \(\nu_m\) with rational individual weights, converging in total variation to \(\pi\), and with \(g(\nu_m)>0\). Such mixtures exist even at \(g(\pi)=0\): mix an amount tending to zero of the negative fixed point \(-1\), whose drift is \(\log(3/2)>0\), then truncate and approximate so accurately that the new positive margin survives. The same argument applies if \(\pi\) is already a point mass. All cycles in these finite mixtures are nonzero.

There is a finite word \(W_m\), of length \(B_m\ge1\), starting and finishing at residue zero modulo \(M_m\), with the following two properties for *each* copy of this word:

1. Once started at that residue, the time average of its \(B_m\) observations modulo \(D_m\) has total variation distance at most \(1/m\) from the projection of \(\nu_m\).
2. Its sum of the letter increments \(-\log2\) and \(\log(3/2)\) is strictly positive.

To make the word, steer from zero to the chosen phase of each cycle in turn. Append whole periods in counts chosen so that the relative durations in the long runs equal the rational weights of \(\nu_m\), and steer from the last phase back to zero. Repeat the period counts by one common large factor. The steering lengths are bounded independently of that factor, since each complete run finishes at the same residue as its beginning. The finite guards from the preceding section also have bounded total cost. The unguarded distribution of the whole-period phases has exactly the prescribed relative weights. Increasing the factor gives property 1 and preserves strictly positive total increment, proving property 2. The statement holds across successive copies and across stage changes, since property 1 was proved using only guaranteed future letters within each copy.

## Concatenation, every-time convergence and critical escape

Begin with the first \(N\) true letters of \(x\), then deliberately apply the opposite letter. At stage \(m\), append \(m\) zero letters and \(m\) one letters, then steer the resulting rational into zero modulo \(M_m\). The entrance length is bounded by \(E_m=2m+M_m\) regardless of the incoming residue. Follow with many copies of \(W_m\). The inserted runs imply that no eventually periodic binary sequence can be this script. The single wrong branch ensures from then on that the real dyadic denominator doubles at each tick; the script is genuinely off the ordinary diagonal.

All \(E_m,B_m\) are finite and can be chosen before choosing stage durations. Immediately before entering stage \(m\), arrange, for all but the first stage, that the elapsed time \(t_m\) exceeds \(m^2\sum_{j=3}^m(E_j+B_j)\), and that the accumulated sum of letter increments exceeds \(m+(E_m+B_m)\log2\). This can always be done by choosing enough copies in the preceding stage, each of which has positive total increment. During stage \(m\), the increment sum can lose at most \((E_m+B_m)\log2\) relative to the sum at entry: completed copies contribute positively, and there is at most one unfinished copy. Thus the total increment sum tends to \(+\infty\), including when the limiting slope is zero. Since the additive affine corrections are nonnegative and the real start is positive, the real value also tends to infinity.

For every fixed finite modulus \(D_j\), complete blocks from all sufficiently late stages approximate the projection of \(\pi\), by property 1, total variation approximation and divisibility \(D_j\mid D_m\). Up to any cutoff in stage \(m\), the aggregate lengths of all entrances and the one unfinished copy are \(o(t)\) by the choice of \(t_m\). Earlier finitely many stages have vanishing proportion. Therefore the empirical probabilities converge on all finite quotients, which proves weak convergence on \(\widehat{\mathbb Z}\). The parity mean tends to the prescribed value. The ordinary calculation of the log correction also holds for these positive real affine states with the *chosen* letter: their divergence implies that its time average tends to zero. Consequently \((\log x_t)/t\to g(\pi)\).

We may further insist that the consecutive \(W_m\) copies last more than \(m(1+b_m+B_m)\) ticks, where \(b_m\) is the date of the very first copy in stage \(m\). This forces the controlling binary coordinate to be irrational. If it were \(u=A/D\) in lowest terms with \(D\) odd positive, all its true iterates have denominator dividing \(D\), since each branch keeps binary integrality. As real rationals they satisfy \(|T^{b_m}u|+1\le (|u|+1)(3/2)^{b_m}\). The fixed rational phase \(v_m\) of \(W_m\) has a representation with numerator and denominator of magnitude at most \(3^{B_m}\), and odd denominator. Agreement of \(\ell\) parity letters implies \(T^{b_m}u-v_m\in2^\ell\mathbb Z_2\). If the difference is nonzero, its numerator over the product of the indicated denominators has magnitude at most \(2D 3^{B_m}(|u|+1)(3/2)^{b_m}\). This contradicts the chosen \(\ell>m(1+b_m+B_m)\) for large \(m\). Equality to \(v_m\) would force eventual periodicity, contrary to the prescribed runs. This bound uses the familiar rational phase match argument explicitly addressed in PR212.

## Finite positive witnesses

Choose lengths \(t_j\to\infty\) and precisions \(m_j\to\infty\), as slowly as desired. Take a positive integer \(n_j\) congruent to the constructed binary point modulo \(2^{t_j+m_j}\), and congruent to the original fixed integer \(x\) modulo the odd part of \(D_{m_j}\). CRT applies. It gives the genuine first \(t_j\) shortcut letters, agreement with the entire off-diagonal trajectory modulo \(D_{m_j}\) through that horizon, and an initial \(N\)-letter match with the fixed source \(x\) whenever \(t_j\ge N\). Choose from the first \((t_j+1)^2\) representatives above the full modulus \(Q_j\). Any two distinct prefix dates impose at most one forbidden starting value for equality of their endpoints: the two affine slopes are distinct, since an additional positive number of ticks cannot have slope one. We can therefore choose the whole ordinary segment simple. All its states exceed \(2^{m_j}\), since \(n_j>Q_j\ge2^{t_j+m_j}\). Along \(m_j\) sufficiently slow this has \(\log_2 n_j=t_j+o(t_j)\). For each fixed modulus the finite time averages thus have exactly the same limit \(\pi\); the ordinary log-correction formula and the growing floor give \(\log(T^{t_j}n_j/n_j)/t_j\to g(\pi)\) as well. The first wrong bit at the fixed original source precludes identifying these witnesses with \(x\).

PR212 excludes large logarithmic coverage by macroscopic short-period windows on a single simple positive ordinary path and proves a lower bound on nested power grammar size when the horizon exceeds the initial bit size by an unbounded factor. Its source hypotheses do not hold on the constructed infinite off-diagonal orbit, and the stated finite witnesses have horizon comparable to initial bit size. PR215's affine-overlap classification concerns components of a tilted ternary coding law. This construction uses deterministic ordinary residues and the full profinite forward-time law; it uses none of the tilted law's overlap estimates.
