## Recent machinery tested for a closing step

**Chudnovsky's logarithmic-form preprints.** The primary version of arXiv:2611.30820 was inspected, especially Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.2 and the formalization scope. For two fixed logarithms the stated homogeneous estimate is \(C H^{-2-\varepsilon}\); the paper does not claim the conjecturally optimal homogeneous exponent \(1+\varepsilon\). The improved cycle-width application and its qualification were already present in PR209. The combined rational construction in this packet has odd width tending to four, so it lies outside that narrow-band exclusion. The recent development was not rebuilt here, and no claim of an effective onset is added. [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.30820v1)

Its complete-row clock bound, source-preserving diagonal requirement and source-family calibration requirements were retained during this pass. No circuit lower bound, tensor price or LP solver was treated as a bound on a specified Collatz orbit. The rank-one obstruction at affine cuts and the common-gate identities in this packet explain why a proposed repeated-divisibility or independent-output argument still needs additional data. **Weaver's record increments.** Paper 224 v1, dated 25 November, bounds the spacing and counts of strict coefficient-record increments before original descent. The attempted application retained the same original trajectory. A divergent trajectory's coefficient tends to infinity after its bounded distortion correction, so it already has a last global coefficient minimum. Starting there supplies no pair of decreasing records to which the sparse-increment estimate can be applied. The missing future record is not implied by sparsity of differences between existing records. [Primary paper](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/224)

**Directed branch separation.** Paper 225 v1 supplies directed separation under positive predecessor-density hypotheses. The descent note reconstructs an incoming-basin amplification and keeps its actual sources and first crossing. Its mass does not become a lower count of distinct disagreement vertices. A family of ordinary observables with one failed invariant edge arbitrarily high is a control for this transfer; those observables are explicitly not globally invariant. ## The final global route

**Cohen–Helson–Rudin idempotent theorem.** The primary statement in Ben Green and Tom Sanders, *A quantitative version of the idempotent theorem in harmonic analysis*, Annals of Mathematics 168 (2008), 1025–1054, applies to bounded complex measures on a locally compact abelian group. On the circle, Boolean Fourier coefficients force their support into the coset ring of the discrete dual \(\mathbb Z\). The global note supplies the compactness argument from bounded Fejér integral norms and the arithmetic argument turning eventual periodicity into constancy. The latter is formalized in PeriodicColor; the analytic boundedness premise remains open. No quantitative bound on the coset decomposition is needed. [Primary statement](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2008/168-3/p09)

**Mixed equations versus independent Mahler equations.** Berg and Meinardus's analytic Collatz formulation yields a mixed dilation fixed-point equation. Schäfke and Singer's Theorem 1 needs two separate Mahler equations in multiplicatively independent bases. The mixed equation does not supply those hypotheses. Neklyudov's Hardy-space fixed-point criterion is also narrower: an actual nonzero bad indicator has infinitely many equal nonzero coefficients along each doubling ray and cannot lie in \(H^2\). The global note instead tests a symmetric Fourier–Stieltjes condition, which permits point masses. Root retrieved the primary theorem and author abstract for these scope checks. [Berg–Meinardus](https://www.math.uni-rostock.de/math/pub/romako/heft48/berg2.html) · [Schäfke–Singer](https://singer.math.ncsu.edu/papers/vshort2-5.pdf) · [Neklyudov](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11859)
