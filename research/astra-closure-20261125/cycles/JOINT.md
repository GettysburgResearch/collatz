# Local divisibility and both ancestor exclusions on the same wide rational cycles

**PROPOSED, with exact finite guards and the source-qualified inputs of the preceding arguments.** Fix any finite prime set $S$. On every sufficiently large upper convergent $h/p$ of $\log_2 3$, the positive rational cycles in [the prime-power theorem](README.md) can additionally satisfy the following at **every** odd phase:

1. Every legal inverse odd path of depths 1 through 17 has source $y\ge (3s+1)/4$.
2. Every legal inverse odd path of depths 1 through 16 with source $y=0\bmod3$ has $y\ge2s+1$.

Here $s$ is the actual rational minimum. A legal inverse path uses positive odd rational phases integral at 2 and 3 in the same denominator lattice as its target, with exact integer odd exponents $a\ge1$. Thus divisibility by three means a ternary-integral residue test. The ordinary least-disagreement branch needs these inequalities for its ordinary ancestors. This theorem imposes only the stated finite depths. In particular the rational seed does not represent an actual ordinary least disagreement.

The same examples have odd width tending to 4, both rotation orders, more than $p/40$ sorted octave mismatches at every nonzero shift, a positive proportion of high strict record phases, and common reduced denominator $>2^{p/10}$ prime to every prime in $S$. The variant with a prescribed unbounded canceled prime power on upper approximants also works. This strengthens the separate tests by imposing them jointly on one family.

## Place the free ranks away from the minimum

Use the same rotation and notation as in the prime-power proof, now with octave 1 as the default. Force octave 0 at temporal indices $-2,-1,0,1$ modulo $p$, retaining $j_0=0$ in rank coordinates. In rank coordinates these are $2(p-d),p-d,0,d$ for $d=h-p$. Lowering rank zero forces the other three: a lower rank forces the ranks reached by adding $d$ or $p-d$ whenever these additions remain below $p$. Their finite upward closure stops here for late convergents. Elsewhere permit both octave bits on the contiguous rank interval
$$
                     8/5\le 2^{r/p}<19/10.
$$
Delete from the free ranks the temporal indices $-2-2K$ through $1+2K$ modulo $p$, with $K=17$, reserving them for the fixed distinguished neighborhood. A free rank exceeds $\max(d,p-d)$: its normalized log rank is at least $\log_2(8/5)>0.67$, while $d/p\to\log_2(3/2)<0.59$. It admits lowering independently of all other free ranks, because no required upward extension is still below $p$. Thus $a_r=\lfloor(r+h)/p\rfloor+j_r-j_{R(r)}$ always belongs to $\{1,2\}$.

The remaining interval has $p\log_2(19/16)-O_K(1)$ bits and contains a consecutive subinterval of length $\gg_K p$ after the bounded deletion. Apply the signed prime-power switches there, with the other bits fixed. Formula (1) in the preceding proof allows any target for their binary sum. The same martingale and rotation-discrepancy proof applies to the field with a different piecewise smooth mean. With probability $1-o(1)$ the normalized phases before the switches have uniform error $o(p)$ around $2^{r/p}V$. Raising or lowering $p^{o(1)}$ bits changes the error bound only by $o(p)$ after recomputing the common $V$. Dyadic and ternary ordering follow as before; the ternary integer labels are below $3p<2h$. Rank zero is the actual minimum and rank $p-1$ has octave 1 at large $p$. Its physical height divided by $s$ tends to 4. In particular
$$
              x_r/s=2^{j_r+r/p}(1+o(1))
$$
uniformly. The unforced raw independent bits give expected mismatch count at least half the number of free ranks for each nonzero shift: every directed pair from a free source has chance $1/2$ of mismatch. Changing one random bit changes the count by at most two. The bounded-differences inequality and a union bound over $p-1$ shifts give $>p/40$ simultaneously with room for the switches. Off the finite distinguished region, the temporal translate of the free interval is disjoint from itself, so the temporal mismatch count is twice the number of lowered free ranks up to $O_K(1)$, exceeding $p/5$ with probability tending to one. A positive fraction of ranks have octave 1 and height $>2s$. The same strict-record argument applies. Since $\log_2(19/16)>1/5$, there are more than $2^{p/5+cp}$ resulting minima for some fixed $c>0$ despite the correction interval. The $O(p^6)$ height bound therefore again allows reduced denominator $>2^{p/10}$.

## Check all reverse words, including affine boundary cases

Use the exact 17-bit threshold $L(w)$ defined in [the ancestor count](BASIN.md). An ancestor with valuation sum $A$ at depth $k$ has $y=(2^A/3^k)(x-\eta)$ with $0<\eta\le(3/2)^k-1$. Therefore at bounded $k$ and large $s$, a uniform multiplicative slack in $x/s$ above all the limiting thresholds rules out the indicated smaller ancestors. For omitted totals $2^A>f3^k$ the first such power already gives a fixed strict margin since $k$ has a fixed bound and $f\in\{2,3/4\}$.

Away from the anchor the checker runs over all 17-gap histories allowed by the free interval. Write $z=2^{r/p}$. In the limiting rotation the preceding phase at temporal offset $i$ is the dyadic normalization of $3^i z$ into $[1,2)$. Both its wrap and its inclusion in the free interval are constant on each cell cut by the normalized values of $c3^i$ for $c=1,8/5,19/10$ in the stated finite range of indices. For every cell, [check_joint.py](check_joint.py) enumerates all subsets of the freely lowered positions among the 18 vertices of the history. It verifies that the left endpoint times $2^{j_r}$ is at least $(8/5)L(w)$ for every pattern. All relevant breakpoints with different base constants are distinct: their ratios involve 5 or 19 and cannot be a product of powers of 2 and 3. Within a single base constant distinct powers of 3 have distinct dyadic normalizations. Hence their ordering is stable when the rank rotation multiplier $2^{h/p}$ replaces 3 for all sufficiently large clocks. The finite histories on the corresponding half-open cells stay the same, with endpoints tending to their original values. Together with the uniform physical-height estimate this preserves strict slack. Fixing additional bits to one near the anchor only restricts the generic possibilities.

For a window meeting an anchor vertex, its terminal index $i$ is between $-2$ and $1+17$. All its non-anchor bits were fixed at one. The wrap list near rank zero stabilizes exactly because the finitely many nonzero multiples of $\log_2 3$ under consideration are nonintegral. Write the actual physical site as $x_i=A_i^*s+B_i^*$ with exact rational coefficients. Obtain the coefficients by iterating the given affine edges forward or backward from $x_0=s$. The checker finds either $A_i^*/L(w)\ge4/3$ or equality. Equality with the auxiliary threshold 1 alone imposes no affine ancestor condition. For each actual floor $f$ and each $k$, an equal leading threshold determines the only possible total by $2^A=f3^k/A_i^*$. The checker enumerates all positive exponent compositions of that total, keeps those with the matched terminal class modulo $3^{k+e}$, and maximizes their forward carry $C$. This gives the *smallest* ancestor intercept $(2^A B_i^*-C)/3^k$. In each of the twelve matched boundary totals it is at least 1 for the multiple-three floor or $1/4$ for the ordinary floor, as required. All strict leading comparisons at these finitely many sites persist for large $s$, since the tested small totals are finite and the uniform $\eta$ bound covers all larger ones. These checks use exact rational arithmetic.

This construction also leaves open the separate first-crossing branch.
