# Whole prime powers on ordered rational cycles

**PROPOSED theorem, with a source-qualified logarithmic-forms input.** Fix a finite set of primes $S$. For **every sufficiently large upper continued-fraction convergent** $h/p$ of $\alpha=\log_2 3$, there are primitive positive rational shortcut cycles with odd period $p$ and shortcut period $h$ satisfying all the following conditions simultaneously.

- Every phase is integral at every prime in $S$. In particular the physical carry cancels the **whole** $S$-primary factor of $D=2^h-3^p$, including its changing multiplicities.
- The reduced common denominator exceeds $2^{p/10}$. The ratio of largest to smallest odd phase tends to 4.
- Both the dyadic order of odd phases and the ternary order of all phases are rotations. Every nonzero rank shift changes the dyadic octave on more than $p/40$ odd ranks. More than $p/10$ odd phases exceed twice the minimum. A fixed positive fraction of physical phases there are strict all-pasts records at threshold $3/5$.

The prime set is fixed **before** the clocks grow. A separate clock construction below gives unbounded cancellation multiplicity at any one prescribed prime $\ell\ge5$. The result treats the actual prime powers on the supplied clocks, without choosing a subsequence that makes those primes absent. It leaves the other factors of $D$ untested. It gives an obstruction to a fixed-prime proof of nonintegrality from these ordering and record conditions alone. Positive ordinary cycle exclusion remains **OPEN**.

## The physical carry as a binary subset sum

We use the rational-cycle construction in [PR192, RATIONAL_ORDERS.md, frozen 570476be82f706122cdd23ebd7a00f3d4ff90cd8](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/570476be82f706122cdd23ebd7a00f3d4ff90cd8/research/astra-ultra-closure-20261125/cycle/RATIONAL_ORDERS.md). Put $I=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor p/3\rfloor\}$ and choose bits $j_r$ on $I$, zero elsewhere on $0\le r<p$. Starting at zero, ranks advance by $R(r)=(r+h)\bmod p$. The odd-step valuations are $a_r=\lfloor(r+h)/p\rfloor+j_r-j_{R(r)}$. They are 1 or 2 because every rank in $I$ is entered on a wrap modulo $p$ after adding $h-p$ and left without one.

To reach larger odd widths, put $d=h-p$, and additionally set the bit at $p-1$ to 1. For every newly raised rank $r$, also raise $r-d$ if nonnegative and $r-(p-d)$ if nonnegative, recursively. These are exactly the two possible requirements: an elevated target of a nonwrapping edge needs an elevated source, and an elevated source of a wrapping edge needs an elevated target. Each subtraction costs more than $0.4p$ at late convergents, so the forced set has at most seven ranks. None equals zero for large $p$: normalized by $p$, every descendant has the form $1-1/p-i(d/p)-j(1-d/p)$ for nonnegative $i+j\le2$. The only zero limit would have $i=j=1$ by irrationality, and that descendant would have value $-1$ before normalization. Hence all forced ranks stay a fixed positive fraction of $p$ away from zero. An initial reserved interval of $p^{o(1)}$ free ranks is untouched. Bits forced inside $I$ are no longer free. The gap legality remains valid for the entire union of raised ranks. Their sum is $h$. At time $i$ write $r_i=ih\bmod p$. Since $j_0=0$, the accumulated valuation at that time is $A_i=\lfloor ih/p\rfloor-j_{r_i}$. The physical fixed point is $C/D$ with
$$
 C=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}3^{p-1-i}2^{A_i}.
$$
Rotating the affine equations gives the prescribed valuations at all phases and one common reduced denominator. A shorter period would make the repetition count divide both $h$ and $p$.

Write $Q=\prod_{\ell\in S}\ell^{v_\ell(D)}$. It is prime to six. For $Q>1$, choose $a,b\in\mathbb Z$ with $ap+bh=1$. Then $u=2^a3^b$ in $(\mathbb Z/Q)^\times$ satisfies $u^p=2,u^h=3$. Write $v=u^{-1}$. Multiplying the carry by the unit $2\cdot3^{1-p}$ gives the exact target
$$
 Q\mid C\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{r=0}^{p-1} j_r v^r=2\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}v^r\pmod Q.                 \tag{1}
$$
Indeed $2^{\lfloor ih/p\rfloor}=u^{ih-r_i}$, and $2^{1-j}=2-j$ for a bit. Thus a consecutive interval of adjustable ranks supplies powers of one unit. The right-hand side is a particular target. The next argument reaches **every** target after the other bits are fixed.

## Reserving a short interval that attains every residue

All constants below may depend on $S$. Discard 2 and 3; they never divide $D$. The empty case $Q=1$ needs no corrections. Otherwise let $k_\ell=v_\ell(D)$, $K=\max k_\ell\ge1$ and $N=\lceil\sqrt K\rceil$. Let $m$ be the least common multiple of the numbers $\ell-1$ for the retained primes. Set $B=\max(1,\max_{\ell\in S} v_\ell(2^m-1))$ and $J=NB$.

We first prove that a consecutive reserved interval of length
$$
                        (1+K)\exp(O_S(\sqrt K))                         \tag{2}
$$
suffices. Translate it freely since every translation multiplies the available weights by a unit. Fix an integer representative $v>1$. For any $\ell$ with $k_\ell>B$,
$$
             1\le b_\ell:=v_\ell(v^m-1)\le B.
$$
Fermat gives the lower bound. For the upper bound, $(v^m)^p\equiv 2^{-m}\bmod\ell^{k_\ell}$, whose difference from 1 has valuation $v_\ell(2^m-1)<k_\ell$. Since $v^m-1$ divides $v^{mp}-1$, its valuation is no larger.

Take first $Q_0-1$ ranks where $Q_0=\prod\ell^{\min(J,k_\ell)}$. Their binary subset sums cover all residues modulo $Q_0$. Indeed whenever a nonempty attained set $A$ in a finite cyclic group is proper, adjoining one generator $w$ as an optional summand increases its size. Otherwise $A+w=A$, and iteration would give the entire group. Here each weight is a unit. The length of this initial segment is $\exp(O_S(N))$.

For each $\ell$ with $k_\ell>J$ and each $d=J,\ldots,k_\ell-1$ reserve $\ell-1$ mutually disjoint copies of the following binary alternatives. Define integers $a_0\le\cdots\le a_{N-1}$, differing by at most one, with sum $d-Nb_\ell$ by quotient and remainder. They are nonnegative and at most $N$ since $J\ge Nb_\ell$ and $d\le K\le N^2$. Put
$$
  t_i=m\,2^i\ell^{a_i}\prod_{s\in S,\ s\ne\ell}s^N,
  \qquad F(X)=\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}(X^{t_i}-1).
$$
The exponents satisfy $t_{i+1}\ge2t_i$. All their subset sums are distinct, and the positive and negative coefficients of $F$ are the indicator functions of two disjoint bit patterns. Each pattern fits in an interval of length $1+\sum_i t_i=\exp(O_S(N))$.

The difference between the alternatives in a copy starting at $c$ is $v^c F(v)$. Its valuation at $\ell$ is exactly $d$. Indeed for an odd prime $s$, $s\mid v^m-1$ gives $v_s(v^{m t}-1)=v_s(v^m-1)+v_s(t)$ by the binomial theorem (raise first to an exponent prime to $s$, then to $s$ repeatedly). Sum this identity over the factors at $\ell$ to obtain $Nb_\ell+\sum a_i=d$. At every other prime $s\mid Q$, the analogous sum is at least $N^2\ge k_s$. This computation holds independently of previous choices and includes arbitrarily unequal multiplicities.

Initialize all copies at their negative-coefficient patterns. Account for these patterns and every fixed exterior rank before solving the first congruence modulo $Q_0$. Next lift each active $\ell$ one digit at a time. At level $d$, the $\ell-1$ differences divided by $\ell^d$ are units modulo $\ell$. The same cyclic-group observation reaches any desired correction digit, while all other prime-power coordinates stay fixed. This gives (1) and proves (2).

For the required size, apply Kunrui Yu, *p-adic logarithmic forms and group varieties II*, Acta Arithmetica 89 (1999), consequence of Theorem 1 on p. 337, with $\Xi=2^h3^{-p}-1\ne0$. At each fixed $\ell\ge5$ the two bases are local units. Take $\mathbb Q$ if $\ell\equiv3\bmod4$, and $\mathbb Q(i)$ otherwise to meet (1.2); the odd prime is unramified, so its valuation on rational numbers is unchanged. The logarithmic heights, degree, ramification and residue degree are all bounded for this fixed prime. Equation (1.8) permits $\max(h,p,3)$ as the exponent bound. Consequently $k_\ell=O_\ell(\log(2p))$. Equation (2) is $p^{o_S(1)}$ and fits inside $I$ for every sufficiently large convergent. The theorem on local forms enters solely in this bound on the length of the reservation. [Yu, pp. 337–338](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa89/aa8944.pdf)

## Preserving the actual orders and high records

Start with independent fair bits on $I$. For each nonzero shift $t$ in $\mathbb Z/p$, consider edges $r\to r+t$ from $I$. Each edge meets at most three other edges (the degree of each endpoint, counting multiplicity, is at most two). Greedy selection supplies at least $|I|/4$ disjoint edges. The mismatch indicators on that matching are independent fair bits, including edges with a fixed outside endpoint. A binomial lower-tail bound makes the probability of fewer than $p/32$ mismatches exponentially small in $p$, since the mean is at least $p/24-O(1)$. Summing over all $p-1$ shifts gives the bound simultaneously with probability tending to one. The number of occupied bits exceeds $2p/15$ with probability tending to one by the same estimate.

For clarity, the stability of the frozen rational construction under changing the reserved ranks is necessary here. Its normalized recurrence is $v_{R(r)}^*=\lambda v_r^*+b_r^*$ with $\lambda=3\,2^{-h/p}<1$, $b_r^*=2^{-(r+h)/p-j_r}$ and physical odd values $x_r=2^{j_r+r/p}v_r^*$. The symbol $v^*$ here denotes a *real* normalized phase. For a fraction $1-o(1)$ of the bit strings the cyclic partial sums of $b_r^*-\bar b^*$ in rotation order are $o(p)$ uniformly up to a lap. The linked PR192 proof uses bounded geometric sums for the deterministic mean field and the maximal second-moment bound for the centered independent variables. Changing the interval and the at most seven forced bits, $T=p^{o(1)}$ changes in all, alters every such centered sum by at most $2T$. Therefore after the corrections it is still $o(p)$, and summation by parts around the periodic extension yields $v_r^*=V+o(p)$ uniformly, $V=\bar b^*/(1-\lambda)\gg p^2$.

The nominal spacings of $2^{r/p}V$ exceed a constant times $p$, giving the assigned strictly increasing dyadic mantissas and the minimum at rank zero. For the ternary ordering, label an odd phase by $r+p j_r$ and an intervening even phase by the odd successor's label plus $p$. These labels decline by $p$ modulo $h$ every shortcut step and lie below $2h$. Reducing a label by $h$ and dividing its phase by 3 changes the nominal normalized value by only $O(1)$ since $(2^{h/p}/3-1)V=\bar b^*/\lambda$. The same spacing orders all ternary mantissas, including the upper endpoint $3x_0$. All odd phases are below $4x_0$, while the raised rank $p-1$ has ratio $2^{1+(p-1)/p}(1+o(1))$ to the minimum. The perturbed bits preserve $>p/40$ mismatches at every shift (each changed rank affects at most two edges per shift) and $>p/10$ elevated odd phases. The temporal shift has more than $p/5$ mismatches because $I$ and $R(I)$ are disjoint and the finite forced set changes the count by $O(1)$, while the initial occupation exceeded $2p/15$. All physical phases lie between $x_0$ and $8x_0$. For the periodic score with increments $(e_i-3/5)\log3$ at physical parity $e_i$, the positive lap drift gives recurrent strict all-past records. From a record until the next one the logarithmic height after $j$ strictly intervening steps is at most its starting height minus $j(\log2-(3/5)\log3)/2$ for large $p$, since each positive log correction is at most $1/(3x_0)$. Thus a high phase lies a bounded number of steps after a high record. This gives the stated fixed positive fraction, on the same cycles.

There are at least $2^{\lfloor p/3\rfloor-o(p)}$ corrected patterns: outside the prescribed interval they are unchanged, with at most $2^T$ inputs per output. Each yields a different minimum because the literal rational dynamics and the realized order recover all its bits. The physical minimum is $O(p^6)$ by the lap identity and the source-qualified real logarithmic form in [PR192, cycle/README.md, same frozen head](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/570476be82f706122cdd23ebd7a00f3d4ff90cd8/research/astra-ultra-closure-20261125/cycle/README.md). Thus only $O(p^6Y^2+Y)$ such minima can have reduced denominator at most $Y$. Set $Y=2^{p/10}$ to finish. If a supplied subsequence has $h/p-\alpha\ll p^{-1000/479}$, then $V\gg p^{1000/479}$ on that subsequence. Existence of infinitely many such upper approximants is a separate assertion.

## Prescribing a genuinely growing canceled prime power

For any $\ell\ge5$ and any $k\ge1$ we can also supply arbitrarily large coprime clocks satisfying $\ell^k\mid D$. These clocks need only be upper approximants with $0<h/p-\alpha=O_{\ell,k}(p^{-2})$. This is the sole approximation property used in the construction, through $V\gg_{\ell,k}p^2$. All preceding conclusions persist if $S$ contains $\ell$. To construct the clocks, take discrete logarithms $a_2,a_3$ of 2 and 3 in the cyclic unit group modulo $\ell^k$, with representatives between 1 and its order $L=\varphi(\ell^k)$. With $g=\gcd(a_2,a_3)$, the primitive pair $(h_0,p_0)=(a_3/g,a_2/g)$ satisfies $2^{h_0}=3^{p_0}\bmod \ell^k$. It suffices to prescribe this pair modulo $L$.

Write $w_n=(h_n,p_n)$ for an upper convergent of $\alpha$ and $w_{n-1}$ for the preceding lower one. Their matrix is unimodular. Solve $Aw_n-B'w_{n-1}=(h_0,p_0)\bmod L$ for the two coefficient classes. They are jointly primitive modulo $L$. Choose a positive representative $B'$ of its class, then a representative $A>B'$ coprime to $B'$. At primes dividing both $L$ and $B'$ the prescribed class for $A$ is already a unit. At the other prime divisors of $B'$ this follows by imposing $A=1$ modulo each by CRT. The representatives can be bounded solely in terms of $L$ because there are finitely many class pairs. The resulting clock vector $(h,p)=Aw_n-B'w_{n-1}$ is primitive. Its denominator is comparable to $p_n$, and $0<h-p\alpha\ll_L 1/p_n$ by the opposite signs and the continued-fraction error bounds for both neighbors. This proves the claimed approximation and divisibility. Applying the theorem for each $k$ in turn and choosing the periods increasingly large yields $v_\ell(D)\to\infty$, $v_\ell(C)\ge v_\ell(D)$ throughout, with exponential remaining denominators. This last diagonal does not assert the prescribed congruence classes occur among the continued-fraction convergents themselves.

**First unsupported step toward ordinary exclusion.** These cycles pass the stated whole-prime-power tests simultaneously and violate full divisibility at a prime outside $S$. The argument giving them neither identifies such a prime for every remaining word nor establishes the least-disagreement basin hypothesis. The target for arbitrary primitive nontrivial positive words is still $\exists\ell\mid D$ with $v_\ell(C)<v_\ell(D)$, including words outside the ordered family. The fixed-local-minimum bounds of Simons and de Weger retain their dependence on the number of local minima. [Simons and de Weger](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa117-1-3)
