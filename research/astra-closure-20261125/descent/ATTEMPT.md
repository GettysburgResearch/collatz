# What a single basin disagreement forces upstream

**PROPOSED, source-qualified written deduction.** Let $b$ be a binary coloring invariant at every edge of the ordinary shortcut map $T$, and set $D(u)=b(u)-b(3u)$. Suppose $3\nmid x$ and $D(x)\ne0$. For every cutoff $K$ there is a fixed gate $z=2^jx$ with the following property. A positive lower density of starts reaching $z$ must cross an odd vertex $u>K$ with $D(u)=0$, $D(Tu)\ne0$, before reaching $z$. At that event $b(3Tu-1)\ne b(3Tu)$. This constructs crossings on actual orbits of the original population. The density constant may depend on $x,K$.

Invariance gives $b(2u)=b(u)$ and $D(2u)=D(u)$. If $u$ is odd, then $T(3u)=3Tu-1$, so
\[
 D(u)-D(Tu)=b(3Tu)-b(3Tu-1).
\]
Also $b(u)=b(3u+1)$ for odd $u$. The count of tripling disagreements on odd starts through $X$ is therefore at most the count of adjacent disagreements through $3X+1$. The latter is $o(X)$ by the ordinary binary coalescence theorem. Splitting all positive starts by their exact power of two gives $D=0$ at density one. For this last passage, fix the number of dyadic strata first and bound the remaining tail by its geometric density. [Adjacent membership changes, paper 12, Lemma 2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/12)

One starting value can encounter only finitely many distinct $2^j x$. Each such visit leads to $x$. Before the first hit the path is finite. A later return through any of these values forces $x$ onto a finite cycle. Choose $j$ so no start at most $K$ can ever reach $z=2^jx$. The shortcut predecessor basin of $z$ has positive lower natural density. In applying the published ordinary-map theorem, take the target $2z$, which is prime to three. Even if a shortcut skips that particular occurrence of $2z$, it lands at $z$. For a start in this basin with $D=0$, the path to $z$ must enter $D\ne0$ for the first time. The entering edge cannot depart an even state. Its source $u$ exceeds $K$ by construction. [Mazur, fixed-target theorem and qualification on multiples of three](https://www.proofatlas.ai/proofs/artifact.collatz.predecessors_positive_lower_density.v001.html)

The crossings need many odd departures from most originals in this positive-density population. Write $E$ for all odd $u$ with $D(u)=0$ and $D(Tu)\ne0$. The map $u\mapsto3Tu-1$ is injective on odd $u$, takes inputs up to $X$ into $[1,5X]$, and projects $E$ to adjacent disagreements. The fixed-gap square-root absorption bound therefore gives $\#(E\cap[1,X])=O(X/\sqrt{\log X})$. At clock $t$, every value has at most $2^t$ predecessors at that exact clock, and every positive start at most $X$ has reached at most $2^tX$. Thus the number of sources visiting $E$ by $h=\lfloor(\log\log X)/(4\log4)\rfloor$ is $O(4^hX/\sqrt{\log X})=o(X)$. All but $o(X)$ sources also have at least $h/4$ odd departures by that time, by the ordinary parity-word bijection, the binomial lower-tail bound and the incomplete-period error $O(2^h)$. Consequently a positive lower fraction of the original starts through $X$ must undergo $\gg\log\log X$ odd departures before their first such crossing on the way to $z$. Furthermore, a multiple of three has only dyadic predecessors. The union of the predecessor basins of $E\cap3\mathbb N$ has density zero by the fixed-stratum and geometric-tail argument above. The positive incoming mass can therefore be retained at crossings that are themselves ternary units. [Fixed-gap binary absorption and its complete-block counting form](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437/research/upper-bit-mergers/GROWING_ADDITIVE_GAPS.md)

The positive density belongs to the **union of predecessor basins** of the crossing vertices on these paths. A single ternary-unit vertex already has a positive-density predecessor basin. No bound on how many original paths concentrate through the same crossing follows from the adjacent-disagreement count. Pulling both original arms back to the prescribed targets $x,3x$ cannot fix this. An odd shortcut reaches a ternary unit, and all subsequent shortcuts preserve that property. The only predecessors of the multiple $3x$ are its dyadic multiples. Advancing the tripled arm to a unit gives two legitimate marginal predecessor estimates, with a joint estimate still missing.

## A one-edge control at arbitrarily large height

There are actual positive binary observables with **one** failed invariant edge, arbitrarily high, that have exact dyadic invariance, positive lower density of both colors and a concrete tripling disagreement. This tests reliance on the density theorems alone. It does not supply an exactly invariant nonconstant coloring.

Choose $e=7+54a$ for $a\ge0$, set $k=(2^e-20)/27$ and $n=32k+23$. Here $k$ is a positive integer and $3\nmid n$, because $2^e\equiv2^7\bmod81$. Both $n$ and $3n$ reach $27k+20=2^e$ at clock five. The three odd steps from $n$ give $48k+35,72k+53,108k+80$, then two halvings reach $2^e$. From $3n$ the first four values after departure are $144k+104,72k+52,36k+26,18k+13$, none equal to $n$. After the common endpoint both arms descend through powers of two to the cycle through one.

Take $b_n(u)$ to mean that the orbit visits $n$. This coloring fails forward invariance only on the edge out of $n$, has $b_n(2u)=b_n(u)$ for all $u$, and satisfies $b_n(n)=1$, $b_n(3n)=0$. The color one has positive lower density by target $n$. The color zero has positive lower density by the fixed target $85$. Its forward path through $128$ to one misses $n\ge151$, while the displayed path from $n$ misses $85$. Orbits visiting both targets are therefore impossible. The simplest small version takes the coloring of visits to $5$, for which $b(1)=0$ and $b(3)=1$, and the opposite color again contains every predecessor of $85$.

Paper 225 explicitly extends its directed lower bounds and its complementary upper estimate to finite-exception colorings with positive lower densities. Its lower estimate for distinct eligible affine branches is a subsequential multiple of $X/(\log X)^B$ for a comparison-dependent $B$, compatible with its upper $O(X/\sqrt{\log X})$. This family satisfies the hypotheses of that extension while the single failed edge can lie beyond any prescribed absolute cutoff. A proof for the true component coloring must use its exact invariance in an additional step. No estimate forcing positive density of the crossing vertices themselves is established here. [Paper 225, directed separation theorem](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/225)
