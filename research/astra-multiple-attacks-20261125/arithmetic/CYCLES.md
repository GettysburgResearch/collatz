# Two phases on a possible family of ordinary cycles

Suppose one had a sequence of pairwise distinct primitive positive integer cycles, with shortcut periods $h$ and minima $m$. Then $m\to\infty$ by determinism. The minimum is odd and its first successor is $b=(3m+1)/2$, so $D=b-m=(m+1)/2$. For **any** $N\to\infty$ with $N\le h$, partition the comparison from $m,b$ into at most $K$ time sets as above. Along the cycle sequence,
$$
 \liminf\frac{\log_2(m+1)+\sum_I\log_2H_S(Y_I)}{N}\ \ge\ 1.       \tag{6}
$$
Indeed each arm, through at most one lap of departed states, consists of distinct integer values at least $m$. The pinned uniform reciprocal bound above gives $\sum 1/x_j\le480m^{-1/80}$ for each such segment. The normalized corrections on both arms are consequently $o(m)$, whereas $D\sim m/2$. Under failure the height is again $\exp(O(N))$. Equation (4) and exactly the same rank reduction prove (6). If $N/\log h\to\infty$, the minimum term is $o(N)$: with $p$ odd departures in a lap, $0<h\log2-p\log3\le p/(3m)$ by the lap product, and the standard polynomial lower bound for the nonzero two-logarithm form bounds $m$ by a polynomial in $h$. [Lagarias, Lemma 2.2(1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0512006)

At a whole lap with one part, the discrepancy is exactly $(b-m)(2^h-3^p)/3^p$. The determinant itself can meet the prime-to-$S$ requirement. For shorter clocks and arbitrary parts, (6) requires mass in their own physical carry differences. This theorem about any hypothetical infinite family neither excludes an isolated nontrivial cycle nor identifies a missing prime in the denominator of an arbitrary periodic candidate.
