# Balanced replacements under physical block encodings

**PROPOSED written consequences of (5) in the [main note](README.md).** The statements are about the observable shortcut parities of an ordinary positive start. A fixed nonerasing encoding assigns a nonempty finite bit word to each abstract letter and concatenates the words. The lengths, numbers of ones, and slopes can all differ between letters.

## Substitution fixed points, including all $k$-Bonacci words

Let $u$ be the prolongable fixed point of a primitive substitution $\phi$ with expansion factor $\rho>1$. Suppose the balanced-pair algorithm for a nonempty return prefix terminates with coincidence as in Kebis, Luca, Ouaknine, Scoones and Worrell, *Transcendence for Pisot morphic words over an algebraic base*, §§4,7. For every fixed nonerasing encoding $h$ into physical bits, **an ordinary positive trajectory with parity word $h(u)$ is eventually periodic**.

The published hypotheses apply unconditionally to all $k$-Bonacci fixed words for $k\ge2$, to primitive irreducible Pisot substitutions on two letters under the paper's standing hypotheses, and to primitive irreducible Pisot substitutions on a finite alphabet with pure discrete spectrum. No general Pisot conjecture is assumed. This statement allows arbitrary blocks for each letter. If the encoded word is aperiodic, it has no ordinary positive realizer. A periodic encoded word by itself does not identify which positive cycle it belongs to. [2026 primary paper, Theorems 4.1, 4.2 and proof of 7.1](https://people.mpi-sws.org/~joel/publications/transcendence_pisot_morphic_words26.pdf)

**Proof.** If $u$ is eventually periodic then so is $h(u)$. A repeated infinite parity tail determines a repeated ordinary height: equal length-$j$ parity prefixes imply congruent starts modulo $2^j$ by induction through the two branches. Otherwise use the **actual tile lift** in the proof of Lemma 4.4, especially equations (4.3) and (4.4). The two projections of a tile word $w$ are $u$ and its shift by a fixed prefix $x$ of $u$. Every tile consists of a pair of words with identical letter counts, and a coincidence tile consists of identical letters. The lift $\psi$ commutes with projecting and applying $\phi$. For a chosen small $\eta>0$ we may freeze a level $m$ such that the upper density of mismatch tiles in $\psi^m(w)$ is at most $\eta$. This is the paper's use of coincidence before display (4.6). The two projections of $\psi^{m+n}(w)$ align $u$ with its shift by $|\phi^{m+n}(x)|$ in *letter* clocks.

For each tile $\gamma$, the physical length of $h\phi^n(\pi_1\gamma)$ belongs to $[c\rho^n,C\rho^n]$ for constants $0<c\le C$ independent of $m,n,\gamma$. This follows from primitivity, the finite tile alphabet, and the bounded positive lengths of the fixed encoding. The second projection has exactly the same physical length and odd count since its Parikh vector (the vector of all letter counts) is equal, and substitutions and encodings preserve this equality. Coincidence tiles give identical words.

Choose $\eta$ so small that $2\eta C/c<1/(4\log_2 3)$. Freeze $m$ as above and then take a finite prefix of $M$ tiles in $\psi^m(w)$ with at most $2\eta M$ mismatch tiles. Arbitrarily large $M$ work by upper density. For every subsequent $n$, the native shift is now $q_n=|h\phi^n(\phi^m(x))|$, with $q_n\le C_m\rho^n$ and $q_n\to\infty$. The native comparison length $N_n$ of the expanded tile prefix is at least $cM\rho^n$. Choose $M$ large enough that $N_n\ge2q_n$ throughout. The mismatch intervals are the expansions of the mismatched tiles from this **one fixed finite prefix**, giving at most $M$ intervals and total native length at most $2\eta M C\rho^n < N_n/(4\log_2 3)$. They have equal native grade per interval. These choices include any initial mismatch tile rather than discarding a moving initial clock.

On an escaping realizer, $(x_{q_n}+1)\le(x+1)(3/2)^{q_n}$. The expression in (5) is at most $\tfrac12\log_2(3/2)+\tfrac14+o(1)<1$. This contradicts (5). The finite lift and coincidence for each $k$-Bonacci substitution are verified in §7 of the primary paper. Its complex-base transcendence conclusion is not used. The earlier ICALP paper constructed the matching lift for the Tribonacci word. [2024 primary paper, §5](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2024.144)

## Several rotation channels with unequal native clocks

Fix $0<\theta<1$ irrational and finitely many intercepts $s_i$. At time $n$, channel $i$ emits one of two fixed nonempty physical bit words according to
$$
 a_i(n)=\lfloor(n+1)\theta+s_i\rfloor-\lfloor n\theta+s_i\rfloor .
$$
Concatenate the channels at each time. Their order may even be chosen by a finite partition into half-open intervals of a circle: a fixed permutation of the channels on each interval, evaluated at $\{n\theta+t\}$ for any fixed $t$. Every positive ordinary realization is eventually periodic.

Choose infinitely many convergents $p/q$ with $0<\delta=q\theta-p<1/q$. For each channel put $d_i(n)=\lfloor n\theta+s_i+\delta\rfloor-\lfloor n\theta+s_i\rfloor$. This is zero or one, and $a_i(n+q)-a_i(n)=d_i(n+1)-d_i(n)$. For large such $q$, $d_i(0)=d_i(q)=0$ for all channels, including an intercept at an integer, by the positive sign of $\delta$. Among $0\le n<q$ the set where $d_i(n)=1$ has bounded size independent of $q$. Indeed the test is occurrence in an interval of length $\delta$, and the orbit points are within $1/q$ on the circle of a permutation of the grid $\{j/q+s_i\}$. Only a bounded number of grid points can enter the enlarged interval. The same grid argument bounds the number of times the prescribed permutation differs between $n$ and $n+q$, using the finitely many partition endpoints.

For each $d_i(n)=1$ include indices $n-1,n$ in the changed region, and include every permutation mismatch as a singleton. Merge neighboring intervals. This region has bounded total length in abstract letters. At each boundary of a merged interval $[a,b)$ all $d_i(a),d_i(b)$ are zero. Consequently each channel has equal total numbers of each of its two labels on the two sides of that interval. Outside these intervals every emitted block matches. The native length and grade thus synchronize at every boundary, even for unequal-length blocks and time-dependent orders. The corresponding changed region has bounded total physical length. The two full windows at abstract clocks $0,q$ have equal native length $N$; the second starts at precisely the end of the first, so the native shift is also $N$. Apply (5) and $\log_2(x_N+1)\le N\log_2(3/2)+O_x(1)$. The one-channel rotation comparison has predecessors in the independent Sturmian literature. [Kebis et al., ICALP 2024, §4](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2024.144)

For example, use two channels at $\theta=\sqrt2-1$ with intercepts $0,3/4$. Emit a fixed marker **11110** first at each time, followed by **10** or **110** according to each channel. The marker can be implemented by a third channel whose two words coincide. Every macrostep expands, since even the shortest has six ones in nine bits, and each added one multiplies its slope by $3/2$. The unique runs of four ones identify the markers and the next two run lengths identify the two bits. Eventual physical periodicity would give an eventually periodic first rotation channel, impossible since its frequency of ones is irrational by telescoping the floors. This explicit varying-clock script therefore has no positive integer realizer.

The single mechanical parity-word classification, including the stronger identification of 1 and 2 for that narrower class, is separately developed by the msharpe248 Collatz repository. Its literal one-channel statement is not needed here. [Mechanical classification](https://github.com/msharpe248/collatz/blob/main/paper/mechanical_classification.pdf)

## Applying the criterion at the least bad ordinary source

The construction supplies long, cheap, native comparisons for specific symbolic systems. Prefix compactness of an arbitrary positive trajectory supplies a convergent subsequence of parity *windows*, but gives neither a bounded number of balanced replacement intervals nor the inequality between their native cost and the actual shift height. The source's ordinary realization already enters (3) in both places. To force a contradiction one still needs an upper bound on the entire prime-to-$S$ discrepancy cost for boundedly many parts at that same source. The PR192 Mahler criterion only covers a fixed series once its dilation equation is supplied. The PR210 critical-script theorem applies to a backwards limit with its own ternary coordinate. Neither provides the missing upper bound. The finite-prime cancellation and compressed-degree restrictions for positive *cycle* denominators on PR193/195 concern a different numerator and a different inference. No classification of all ordinary scripts or of the arbitrary high-degree cycle factor is obtained.
