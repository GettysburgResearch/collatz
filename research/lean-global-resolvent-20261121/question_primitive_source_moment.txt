PRIMITIVE SOURCE SERIES

For an integer q>=0 and a word w=(a_1,...,a_q) of positive integers,
define A_0=0, A_j=a_1+...+a_j, and

  C_w = sum_{j=0}^{q-1} 3^(q-1-j) * 2^(A_j).

Let r_w be the unique positive odd integer less than 2*3^q satisfying

  2^(A_q) * r_w = C_w (mod 3^q),

and set b_w=(2^(A_q)*r_w-C_w)/3^q.
For q=0 the empty word has r_w=b_w=1.

The following arithmetic facts have been proved and may be used:

1. b_w is a positive odd integer less than 2^(A_q+1).
2. For fixed q, the b_w are distinct.
3. Every positive odd integer has a unique representation
     n=b_w+2^(A_q+1)*k,
   with w of length q and k a nonnegative integer.
4. If Z=sum_{m>=1}m^(-4), then
     sum_w sum_{k>=1}(b_w+2^(A_q+1)*k)^(-4)
       <= (Z/16)*15^(-q).
5. The nonnegative series
     B_q=sum_{w of length q, r_w!=1} b_w^(-4)
   converges for each q and the real sequence B_q has a finite limit.

PROBLEM

Prove that lim_{q->infinity} B_q=0, or identify a rigorous obstruction.
Any explicit upper bound tending to zero would suffice; no particular
rate is requested. The sum includes every positive valuation, so an
argument using a finite alphabet must bound the entire omitted tail.

Do not infer the limit from boundedness, from convergence of the total
primitive-source sum, or from distributional statements in which the
integer source changes with q. A fixed source, if retained in the sum
at every sufficiently large depth, keeps its full b^(-4) weight.
