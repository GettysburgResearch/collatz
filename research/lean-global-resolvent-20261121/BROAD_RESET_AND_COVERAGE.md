# A broader unit sector and the exact remaining coverage gap

**Status:** the guarded arithmetic reset and certificate paths below are
unconditional and kernel-checked. Their universal applicability is not proved.
The guard allows arbitrary positive `P` divisible by 4. Powers of two form one
useful boundary specialization.

The source/target paths and odd-state bound are proved in
[OddReset.lean](lean/Collatz/OddReset.lean). The compatible certificate order is
proved in [OddCertificateMeasure.lean](mathlib/OddCertificateMeasure.lean).
The final [OddResetCertificate.lean](mathlib/OddResetCertificate.lean) ports
and rechecks the necessary core proof bodies, proves equality of the two
iteration conventions, and composes them into one theorem over every complete
supplied source certificate. No interface theorem is assumed.

All three final files compile with Lean 4.34.1 and warnings treated as errors.
[The execution receipt](../../reports/2026-11-21-lean-global-resolvent/checkpoint4-lean.json)
pins the exact sources and outputs. The valuation selector in Section 3 is a
separate written number-theoretic derivation, not an additional theorem in
those Lean modules.

## 1. Arbitrary-parent guard and exact paths

Let `l>=7`, let `P>0` be divisible by 4, and suppose positive integers `w,m`
satisfy

\[
 2P-1=3^{l+2}w,
 \qquad 27m+279=2^{l+9}w.
\tag{1}
\]

The three-odd-step source entrance `(1,2,5)` and target entrance `(6,1,1)`
both have shortcut clock 8. They send

\[
 (m,2m-1)\longmapsto(x_0,2x_0+1),
 \qquad x_0=2^{l+1}w-1.
\]

In fact `m=512t+331`, `2^l w=27t+18`, so each entrance is a genuine
ordinary path on an integer progression. Both arms then take `l` odd
shortcut steps. At that point, with `v=3^l w`, they are `2v-1` and `4v-1`.
Equation (1) says `2P-1=9v`; divisibility of `P` by 4 gives `v=7 mod8`.

The source tail is an odd step of division exponent 3, followed by one
odd shortcut step. Its endpoint is `(9v+1)/8=P/4`. The target tail has
division exponents `(1,2)` and endpoint `(9v-1)/2=P-1`. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{T^{l+12}(m)=P/4,\qquad T^{l+11}(2m-1)=P-1.}
\tag{2}
\]

The old source `P/2` reaches `P/4` in one even step. Consequently every
supplied ordinary certificate for `P/2` gives a certificate for `m` by a
concrete prefix replacement, retaining the supplied suffix. Conversely,
a certificate for `2m-1` yields one for `P-1` by removing its forced prefix.
Thus a closure failure at `P/2` transfers to an actual failure at `m`.

## 2. What decreases

The new source may have an even intermediate close to `2P/3`. The multiset
of all ordinary source states therefore need not decrease in this broader
grammar. Instead record every **odd** source state before the supplied hit
of 1, together with the initial source if that source is even.

Every inserted odd state is strictly smaller than the old initial source
`P/2`. The entrance's greatest odd-state leading coefficient, as an affine
function of `P`, is

\[
 \frac{2^{l+9}}{3^{l+4}}
 \le\frac{65536}{177147}<\frac12.
\]

The loop's greatest odd-state coefficient is `4/9`, and the last odd source
input has coefficient `1/6`. Their constant terms are negative. Thus (2)
replaces the old initial even entry `P/2` by finitely many smaller odd
entries, keeping the same supplied suffix from `P/4`. This is a strict decrease
in the multiset extension of natural order.

The same order decreases when a complete odd-to-odd accelerated source
prefix is consumed, and when an initial even source is halved. It also
admits the first reset grammar and the earlier C5 prefix replacements.
It does not assert that every arbitrary single shortcut step decreases
this filtered certificate measure.

The composed theorem `CollatzOddResetPort.complete_certificate_descent` has
an actual complete parent certificate as input and returns an actual complete
child certificate with strictly smaller measure. `complete_failed_reset`
also preserves failure of the target. Both statements are checked in one Lean
environment. The visible source port is regenerated by
[build_odd_reset_certificate.py](mathlib/build_odd_reset_certificate.py),
which verifies the three frozen core-source hashes before copying their proof
sections. The generated source is itself rechecked by Lean.


## 3. Valuation selector and unit sector

For arbitrary `P`, put

\[
 j=v_3(2P-1),\qquad u=(2P-1)/3^j.
\]

The second equation in (1) has left side `9(3m+31)`, of exact 3-adic
valuation 2. It therefore forces

\[
 l=j-4,\qquad w=9u.
\]

When `j>=11`, the guard `l>=7` holds. Integrality of `m` reduces to the
single unit condition

\[
 \boxed{\ 2^{j-1}u=1\pmod3.\ }
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed (1), after dividing by 9, reads
`3m+31=2^(l+9)u`; the exponents `l+9=j+5` and `j-1` differ by 6, so their
powers of 2 agree modulo 3. Positivity follows from `u>=1` and `l>=7`.

For `P=2^e`, `e` odd, let `h=v3(e+1)`. Then

\[
 j=h+1,\quad l=h-3,\quad
 \theta=2^h\frac{2^{e+1}-1}{3^{h+1}},
\]

and the entire guard is satisfied for `h>=10` and `theta=1 mod3`.
This covers a full normalized unit class for every such valuation; it is
not an assertion about the complementary unit class.

For completeness, the lifting identity used here has an elementary proof.
If `3` does not divide `a`, then `(4^a-1)/3` is `a mod3`, hence has no
additional factor 3. Also, when `X=1 mod3`, `X^2+X+1` has exact 3-adic
valuation 1. Factoring `4^(3a)-1` accordingly shows that each factor 3 in
`a` adds exactly one to `v3(4^a-1)`. Apply this to `a=(e+1)/2` to obtain
`v3(2^(e+1)-1)=1+v3(e+1)`.

## 4. Scope of the advance

In the power-of-two specialization, an odd exponent with `h>=1` already
gives `P-1=4 mod9`. The smaller odd integer

\[
 (8P-13)/9<P-1
\]

reaches `P-1` in two accelerated odd steps. Thus those Mersennes were
already excluded from being the globally least ordinary missing integer.
The contribution of the new grammars is the stronger **certificate
conversion**: the transformed source is proved to be in the basin using an
actually supplied certificate, and the replacement decreases a common
well-founded measure even though it increases the odd rank without bound.
It does not rely on assuming that every smaller source converges.

This distinction matters. Ordinary inverse ancestry alone does not provide
a universal proof program: there are arbitrarily large convergent inputs
with no smaller ordinary ancestor, as proved in PR179 at
`4bf2675bcc78b42a3f17c0dd826e1dfcc4c8f35b`. The even-exponent Mersenne fold
supplies a genuine forward comparison of another kind, but those two kinds
of moves have not been shown to cover every remaining state.

## 5. What the closing attempt still lacks

The new order and reset formulas do not provide an all-state selector.
The outstanding tasks are concrete:

1. Supply an entrance or a different decreasing transition for the
   complementary normalized unit in (3).
2. Handle the parents with small `v3(2P-1)` without assuming a smaller
   inverse ancestor exists.
3. Cover every nonterminal source/relation state arising when a source
   prefix is consumed. Closing only the power-of-two/Mersenne boundary
   would not prove that this all-state transition exists.

A finite search for paired entrances into `target=2 source+1` did find
some smaller unit cylinders in the complementary class. No complete cover
was obtained, so those isolated cylinders are not promoted here as a
closing result. A different tail changes the arithmetic root to
`32P-31`, but its source contains an odd input asymptotic to `2P/3`; it
does not satisfy the present decreasing order and has not supplied a
valid low-valuation transition. These are precise failures of the tested
completion mechanisms, not claims that other mechanisms cannot work.

The full theorem remains unproved in this checkpoint. The proved result
is an unbounded, explicitly guarded certificate reset, together with a
well-founded measure that supports it and the previously certified forms
of prefix consumption and replacement.

## 6. A precise obstruction to the tested different-root exit

The attempted `32P-31` exit has a stronger obstruction than a large peak.
Suppose `27v=32P-31` and `4|P`. The source `2v-1` first takes exact odd
valuation exponents `(4,1)` and reaches

\[
 H=\frac{9v+5}{16}=\frac{2P-1}{3}.
\]

Its next three ordinary states are `P`, `P/2`, and `P/4`. The target
`4v-1` takes exact odd exponents `(1,2,4)` to `P-1`. Thus this proposed
source replacement visits the **old source itself** before joining the old
suffix. The alternative `81v=32P-31` tail has the same obstruction:
its source prefix with division lengths `(2,2,1)` reaches
`(27v+5)/16=(2P-1)/3`, followed by `P`, `P/2`, `P/4`.

In either version the new certificate is an extra prefix followed by the
entire old certificate. Any order that strictly decreases when that prefix
is consumed must put the old certificate below the new one. It cannot also
put the proposed replacement below the old one. Increasing a weight to make
this reset appear smaller would therefore lose that normalization move.
These are written exact path calculations, not additional compiled modules.
They rule out these particular insertions as compatible decreasing resets,
not all possible exits or measures.
