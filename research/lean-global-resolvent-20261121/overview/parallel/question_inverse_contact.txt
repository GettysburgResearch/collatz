A FINITE-TIME CAPTURE QUESTION FOR A WEIGHTED INTEGER KERNEL

On the positive integers define

  (Pf)(q) = sum_{e>=1, 2^e q=1 (mod3)}
              3*2^(-e) f((2^e q-1)/3),
  g(q) = 1 if 3 does not divide q, and 0 otherwise,
  V_0=g, V_{j+1}=max(g, P V_j), V=sup_j V_j.

The following facts may be used as stated inputs:

1. V is finite at each positive integer, is the least nonnegative
   excessive majorant of g, and V=max(g,PV).
2. D_j=P^j1 satisfies D_j(q)<=(4j+2)(3/2)^j.
3. If C={q:3 does not divide q and V(q)<=2}, A={q:V(q)>2},
   and Qf=1_A Pf, then Q^n V(q) decreases to zero at each fixed q.

For q in A put

  K(q,x) = (3/2^e) V(x)/V(q)

when x=(2^e q-1)/3 is a positive unit, summing coincident branches if
necessary. Its row sum is one. Make C absorbing. Let tau be the first
entrance into C, including time zero. This defines an ordinary integer
Markov chain; tau is almost surely finite at each fixed starting unit.

Here tau and L count inverse odd steps, not the total sum of the exponents.
Every branch retains its exact weight 3*2^(-e).

For q sufficiently large put ell=log(q+2), with natural logarithm, and

  L(q)=floor((log q-3 log ell)/log(3/2)).

Restrict q to positive odd integers not divisible by three such that,
for the integer d with 2^d<=q<2^(d+1),

  (d+1)(q-2^d)<=70*2^d.

QUESTION

Prove or refute that there are explicit c>0 and q_0 such that all such
q>=q_0 satisfy

  Prob_q(tau<L(q)) >= c/log(q+2).

More generally, an effective lower bound p(q) with
p(q)*log(q+2)^2 tending to infinity on this same integer window would
suffice. The actual set C is defined using V, not a finite V_j. A fixed-q
almost-sure statement does not supply the requested moving-q rate.
No ordinary convergence theorem is an assumption of this question.
