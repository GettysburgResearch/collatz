# Complete-denominator paired-cycle attack

Date: 23 November 2026. **Status: a direct cycle-closing attempt, with an
exact cancellation result and an all-period control. This does not exclude
an unrestricted positive integer cycle.** The calculations below are
written proofs; no Lean theorem is claimed for this note.

The proposed new route was to pair an ordinary cycle with its parity
complement. Their product has a parity-independent multiplier `3/4`.
This produces the much larger cyclic denominator `4^A-3^A`, suggesting
that simultaneous integral closure could force an extra divisibility or
height loss. In fact the new product closure is exactly generated by the
two scalar closures. The calculation identifies where that approach
stops; it does not rule out using additional arithmetic information about
the original denominator being one.

## 1. Retain both complete numerical denominators

Let `e_0,...,e_(A-1)` be a nonconstant chronological shortcut word. Put

\[
p=\sum_i e_i,\qquad b=A-p,\qquad
s=2^A,\quad a=3^p,\quad c=3^b,
\]

\[
D=s-a,\qquad E=s-c,\qquad T=s^2-ac=4^A-3^A.
\tag{1}
\]

The two full affine carries are defined by

\[
C_0=C^*_0=0,\quad
C_{i+1}=3^{e_i}C_i+e_i2^i,\quad
C^*_{i+1}=3^{1-e_i}C^*_i+(1-e_i)2^i.
\tag{2}
\]

Write `C=C_A` and `C*=C*_A`. Throughout the positive-pair discussion,
assume `D>0` and `E>0`. The paired rational starting points are

\[
x=C/D,\qquad y=C^*/E.
\tag{3}
\]

If the original cycle is integral, its reduced denominator is exactly
one: `D|C`. The complementary reduced denominator is the complete value

\[
q=\frac{E}{\gcd(E,C^*)}.
\tag{4}
\]

Nothing below substitutes a factor of either denominator for (3)--(4).

Let `U_i,V_i` be the two full carries of the cyclically rotated words.
They satisfy

\[
2U_{i+1}=3^{e_i}U_i+e_iD,\qquad
2V_{i+1}=3^{1-e_i}V_i+(1-e_i)E.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus `x_i=U_i/D`, `y_i=V_i/E` are the actual paired rational
trajectories. Formula (5) follows either from carry composition or by
applying one shortcut step to (3). The resulting numerators have the
prescribed parities, because the denominators are odd. Both trajectories
close after the same complete word.

## 2. A parity-independent product recurrence

Put `P_i=x_i y_i`. Multiplying the two shortcut equations gives

\[
\boxed{4P_{i+1}=3P_i+(1-e_i)x_i+e_i y_i.}
\tag{6}
\]

The term quadratic in the two affine increments vanishes because
`e_i(1-e_i)=0`. Iterating (6) around the cycle gives

\[
T P_0=\sum_{i=0}^{A-1}3^{A-1-i}4^i
\big((1-e_i)x_i+e_i y_i\big).
\tag{7}
\]

Summing the one-step version, rather than iterating, gives

\[
\sum_i x_i y_i=\sum_{e_i=0}x_i+\sum_{e_i=1}y_i.
\tag{8}
\]

The scalar sum identities are

\[
\sum_{e_i=0}x_i=\sum_{e_i=1}x_i+p,\qquad
\sum_{e_i=1}y_i=\sum_{e_i=0}y_i+b.
\tag{9}
\]

Consequently the centered product has the exact average

\[
\boxed{\sum_i(2x_i-1)(2y_i-1)=3A.}
\tag{10}
\]

If `x_i` are integers and `y_i=w_i/q` are in lowest terms with their
common complete denominator, the retained lattice form is

\[
4x_{i+1}w_{i+1}
=3x_iw_i+(1-e_i)q x_i+e_iw_i.
\tag{11}
\]

The numerator `w_i` is coprime to `q`, and its parity is opposite to that
of the integer `x_i`. In particular (8) yields

\[
\sum_{e_i=0}x_i(q-w_i)
=\sum_{e_i=1}(x_i-1)w_i.
\tag{12}
\]

For a nontrivial positive integer cycle the right side is positive, so
some complementary odd numerator is below `q`. This by itself neither
bounds `q` from above nor forces enough distinct small numerators to
contradict the established capacity bounds.

## 3. The new closure has no independent equation

For arbitrary initial variables `X,Y`, the two full-word affine maps are

\[
X'=(aX+C)/s,\qquad Y'=(cY+C^*)/s.
\]

Define the scalar residuals

\[
f=DX-C,\qquad g=EY-C^*.
\]

The product closure `X'Y'=XY`, after clearing its complete denominator,
is

\[
H=TXY-aC^*X-cCY-CC^*=0.
\tag{13}
\]

There is the exact polynomial identity

\[
\boxed{H=sYf+sXg-fg.}
\tag{14}
\]

Indeed `aX+C=sX-f` and `cY+C*=sY-g`; expand their product. Once the
original scalar closure `f=0` holds, (13) becomes

\[
H=sXg.
\tag{15}
\]

For a positive original state `X`, its vanishing is precisely the
complementary scalar closure `g=0`. The apparent third denominator has
not supplied a third independent closing condition.

The same conclusion holds for fixed finite polynomial moments as
equations. For a monomial `X^rY^t`, its denominator-cleared full-word
closure is

\[
s^{r+t}X^rY^t-(aX+C)^r(cY+C^*)^t
=s^{r+t}X^rY^t-(sX-f)^r(sY-g)^t,
\tag{16}
\]

which belongs to the polynomial ideal generated by `f,g`. Finite linear
combinations have the same property after a common power of `s` is
cleared. This assertion concerns algebraic closing equations; it is not
a theorem that every use of heights, prime valuations, positivity, or
integral moments must fail. Such a use must supply an additional
inequality or integral constraint that is not already automatic from
the two scalar recurrences.

At the raw-carry level, the purported new divisibility reads

\[
T U_0V_0=\sum_{i=0}^{A-1}3^{A-1-i}4^i
\big(e_iD V_i+(1-e_i)E U_i\big).
\tag{17}
\]

This is true for **every** complete word, by multiplying (5) and
iterating. Divisibility of that right side by `T` therefore does not
single out the original integral zero fibre.

There is also an exact gcd identity, not merely an upper bound:

\[
T=sD+aE=sE+cD,\qquad
\boxed{\gcd(T,D)=\gcd(T,E)=\gcd(D,E).}
\tag{18}
\]

For the first equality of gcds, reduce `T` modulo `D` and use
`gcd(a,D)=1`; for the second use `gcd(c,E)=1`. These coprimality facts
hold because `D,E` are nonzero modulo three. Thus a large factor of `T`
cannot be counted as a new common factor of the two complete scalar
denominators without another argument.

**First unsupported inference in this attack:** interpreting (7), (13),
or (17) as an independent extra arithmetic demand after both scalar
closures have already been imposed. Equations (14)--(18) show exactly
why that interpretation fails. A claim about the lattice denominator
of `P_i` would additionally have to retain `q/gcd(q,x_i)`; replacing
`P_i` by an integer loses the complementary denominator.

## 4. High controls that also meet the new complementary bounds

The checkpoint17 one-rise family can test the combined proposed bridge,
including the **complete** complementary denominator. Take

\[
w=1^p0^b,\qquad A=p+b,\qquad
C=3^p-2^p,\qquad D=2^{p+b}-3^p>0.
\tag{19}
\]

The complementary word is `0^p1^b`, so direct iteration of (2) gives

\[
C^*=2^p(3^b-2^b),\qquad E=2^{p+b}-3^b.
\tag{20}
\]

Assume `p>b>=1`, so `E>0`. Because `E` is odd and `3^b-2^b` is
coprime to two, full gcd reduction gives

\[
\boxed{q=\frac{2^{p+b}-3^b}
{\gcd(3^b-2^b,\,2^p-1)}.}
\tag{21}
\]

To check this, subtract `(2^p-1)2^b` from `E`; the result is
`-(3^b-2^b)`. Thus (21) computes the actual reduced denominator and
does not rely on a selected factor.

Writing `d=p-b`, positivity of `D` implies

\[
E>3^p-3^b=3^b(3^d-1),\qquad
\gcd(E,C^*)\le3^b-2^b<3^b.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{q>3^{p-b}-1.}
\tag{22}
\]

In particular the complete complementary denominator is exponentially
large in the excess of original odd over even states in this family.

For any prescribed floor `H>=1`, take arbitrarily large `p` with
`A=ceil(p log_2(3))` satisfying

\[
1+\frac1{4H}<\frac{2^A}{3^p}<1+\frac1{2H},\qquad
(2/3)^p<1/2,\qquad 2^p>4H+1.
\tag{23}
\]

These exist because the fractional parts of multiples of the irrational
number `log_2(3)` are dense, as proved in checkpoint17. The minimum
state is

\[
x=\frac{1-(2/3)^p}{2^A/3^p-1},\qquad H<x<4H.
\tag{24}
\]

All shortcut states are at least `x`, and the word has least period
`A`: it has exactly one cyclic run of each symbol. Its odd states rise
strictly, followed by one descending halving chain. Nevertheless it is
nonintegral. Indeed

\[
x+1=\frac{2^p(2^b-1)}{D},\qquad v_2(x+1)=p.
\tag{25}
\]

If `x` were a positive integer, `x+1>=2^p`, contradicting (23)--(24).
The original complete denominator therefore does not divide its carry.

For `p>=5` in (23), `d=2p-A>=2`. The elementary bound
`log_2(3)<8/5`, obtained from `3^5<2^8`, also gives
`A<4d+5`, hence `A<=4d+4`. For `d>=2`, induction gives
`3^d-1>=3d` and `3^d-1>=2d+2`. Combining with (22) yields

\[
\boxed{q>A/2,\qquad q>3(2p-A).}
\tag{26}
\]

Thus these primitive rational cycles have arbitrarily high prescribed
floor, arbitrarily large period, both new complementary lower bounds,
and every exact paired product identity in Sections 2--3. The bound
(22) uses the full complementary denominator. The omitted condition is
identified explicitly: the original reduced denominator is not one.
These controls do not refute a theorem that actually uses original
integrality; they refute treating the assembled real identities and
complementary lower bounds as already sufficient for an exclusion.

## 5. Literature and scope of the attempted arithmetic transfer

The refreshed PR178, PR180, and PR182 results all retain the complete
denominators. The first two force large complementary denominators;
neither supplies a competing upper bound. The rotated-carry rigidity
in PR182 preserves the full original modulus but its root-count
normalization is exactly neutral.

Corvaja--Zannier's torus gcd theorem concerns fixed coprime polynomials
evaluated on a finitely generated multiplicative group, with exceptional
translated subtori. The carry polynomial varies with an unrestricted
parity word. The tensor construction above does not fix that polynomial
or produce the height saving needed to apply their result uniformly.
The repository's separate fixed-atom results already address a
restricted finite-dimensional recurrence family; this note does not
relabel that family as all words.

A newly indexed primary working note, *Cofactor budgets and
complementary-word capacity in integer Collatz cycles* (22 November
2026), proves common correction budgets for arc cofactors, an exact
determinant-absorption identity, and a repeated-block capacity bound.
Its closing section explicitly leaves a forced supply of favorable arcs
or shared content open. We did not obtain that supply from (6)--(18),
and no new cycle exclusion from that note is claimed here.
