Two complementary affine carries

For an integer m>=1, let bl(m) be the least positive E with m<2^E.
For r>=0, put F(r)=product_(0<=j<r)(1+3^j), with F(0)=1.

Question. Prove or refute the following statement.

For every fixed positive integer n, there exist integers K>=1, r>=0,
a in Z, and a binary word e_0,...,e_(K-1) such that the following hold.

Define two complete ordered carries and a count by

  C_0=D_0=s_0=0,
  C_(i+1)=3^(e_i) C_i + e_i 2^i,
  D_(i+1)=3^(1-e_i) D_i + (1-e_i) 2^i,
  s_(i+1)=s_i+e_i.

Then require simultaneously

  2^K divides 3^(s_K) n + C_K,
  2^K divides 3^(K-s_K) a + 3^r D_K,
  K >= 9 bl(n max(1,|a|) F(r)) + 1.

The integer n is fixed before choosing all the other data. A construction
that changes n with K does not answer the question. The numerator a may
have either sign or be zero. The second carry must use the same word as
the first, with the displayed complementary letters and their order.

For any chosen K, the first divisibility condition specifies exactly one
binary word of length K. It is therefore not legitimate to choose C_K and
D_K independently. Centered representatives solve the second congruence
without the final size condition; proving the simultaneous size condition
is the task. No assertion of uniform distribution is part of the premises.

Return a proof valid for every fixed n, or an exact counterexample with
proof that it excludes all possible K,r,a. Finite successful examples or
the decidability of a supplied candidate do not resolve the statement.
