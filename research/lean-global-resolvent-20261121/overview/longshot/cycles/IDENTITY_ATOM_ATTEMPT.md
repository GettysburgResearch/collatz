# Checkpoint 14: the exact affine identity atom

## Status

This attack did not prove positive-cycle exclusion. It tested whether the full-denominator zero fiber can be closed by positive affine-word return estimates, rather than by norms or conjugates. The exact operator identity below preserves the desired arithmetic. The available affine mixing theorem does not prove the required bound, and ordinary uniform mixing is incompatible with the support size at the relevant horizon. No Lean closure or new cycle exclusion is claimed.

## 1. The finite question

Let `A >= 3`, `1 <= p < A`, and

\[
D=2^A-3^p>0.
\]

For a binary word with its ones at `0 <= d_0 < ... < d_(p-1) < A`, write

\[
C_w=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}2^{d_i}3^{p-1-i},\qquad
N_r(A,p)=\#\{w:C_w\equiv r\pmod D\}.
\]

The direct universal target is

\[
N_0(A,p)<A. \tag{Z}
\]

This is a cycle-exclusion target, not a termination theorem for unbounded orbits. An actual primitive positive cycle of length `A` provides `A` distinct rotations in the zero fiber. Conversely, if no nontrivial positive cycle exists, the only zero-fiber words are the two repetitions of the itinerary of `1,2`, exactly when `A=2p`; there are two distinct words, regardless of the repetition count. Thus `(Z)` for all the indicated parameters is equivalent to absence of nontrivial positive cycles.

The exclusions in the parameter range matter. With `p>=1`, the case `D=1` is precisely `(A,p)=(2,1)`: for `A>=3`, reduction modulo `8` would require `3^p=7 mod 8`, which is impossible, and the smaller values of `A` are immediate. Here `N_0=2=A`, so the strict bound is false. If `p=0` were allowed, `(A,p)=(1,0)` would be another `D=1` case, outside the present domain.

For completeness, zero-fiber integrality is not merely a congruence surrogate. The affine composition attached to the word is

\[
x\longmapsto (3^p x+C_w)/2^A,
\]

whose unique fixed point is `C_w/D`. If this is a positive integer, the word is its genuine itinerary. At the first incorrectly chosen parity, the rational iterate acquires an even denominator in lowest terms. Every subsequent prescribed branch increases the power of two in that denominator, because its multiplier has odd numerator and its additive term is integral before the final division by two. It therefore cannot return to an integer. This proves the itinerary claim without a statistical assumption.

The stronger conjecture

\[
\max_rN_r(A,p)<A \tag{F}
\]

would also suffice. This attempt neither proved nor refuted `(F)`.

## 2. An exact trace coefficient

On `R = Z/DZ`, define the two affine permutations

\[
g_0(x)=2^{-1}x,\qquad g_1(x)=2^{-1}(3x+1).
\]

Both `2` and `3` are units modulo `D`. Let `P_0,P_1` be their permutation matrices. Matrix conventions can reverse the order of a word, which does not change the fixed-weight word sum. Then

\[
\boxed{[z^p]\,\operatorname{tr}(P_0+zP_1)^A=D\,N_0(A,p).}\tag{T}
\]

Indeed, the coefficient expands over all words of length `A` and weight `p`. Each corresponding permutation has affine multiplier `3^p/2^A = 1` modulo `D`, so is translation by `C_w/2^A`. A translation of `Z/DZ` has `D` fixed points exactly when its translation is zero, and otherwise has no fixed point. Summing its trace proves `(T)`.

Consequently the desired coefficient inequality is

\[
[z^p]\,\operatorname{tr}(P_0+zP_1)^A<AD.\tag{TC}
\]

This retains the distinguished zero residue at every prime power. It does not replace evaluation at `3` by a field norm, and it does not assume independent information from cyclic rotations.

In Fourier coordinates, the permutations move characters through the multiplicative action generated by `2` and `3`, with additive phase factors. An adjacent `01`/`10` swap in the word changes `C_w` by a unit of the form `2^i 3^j` modulo `D`. This suggests a trace cancellation problem on that graded walk. The suggestion is a research route, not a bound: taking absolute values or using the ordinary spectral-radius estimate discards the cancellation needed in `(TC)`.

### Marked rotations isolate zero

Let `N^b(r)` count words in the fiber `r` whose first bit is `b`. A left rotation sends

\[
C_w\longmapsto 2^{-1}3^b C_w\pmod D.
\]

Since rotation permutes all fixed-weight words,

\[
N(r)=N^0(2r)+N^1(2r/3). \tag{R}
\]

Both multiplicative branches fix zero and preserve every gcd stratum. At zero, `(R)` is merely `N(0)=N^0(0)+N^1(0)`. Thus the marked-rotation conservation law does not constrain the size of the zero atom: zero is an isolated component of its residue dynamics. This is a precise obstruction to deriving `(TC)` from rotation stationarity alone. The affine initial condition and the ordered numerator must remain in the argument. It does not obstruct a future argument using that additional information.

## 3. Why a uniform mixing theorem cannot be substituted

Let `mu` be the residue law of `C_w` when the word is uniform among the `binomial(A,p)` fixed-weight words, and let `u_D` be uniform measure modulo `D`. A direct support count gives

\[
\|\mu-u_D\|_{\rm TV}
\ge 1-\frac{\binom Ap}{D}.
\tag{S}
\]

Proof: take as the test set the support of `mu`, which has at most `binomial(A,p)` elements. Its `mu` mass is one and its `u_D` mass is at most that number divided by `D`.

At critical weight `p/A -> alpha = log(2)/log(3)`,

\[
\log\binom Ap = A h(\alpha)+o(A),\qquad
h(\alpha)=0.6584589953\ldots <\log 2,
\]

where `h(t)=-t log t-(1-t) log(1-t)`. Under the polynomial-loss full-denominator lower bound already used in PR175, the right side of `(S)` tends to one, with exponential rate difference

\[
\log 2-h(\alpha)=0.0346881852\ldots.
\]

Thus the relevant law is far from uniform on its actual modulus. This does **not** disprove `(Z)` or `(F)`: a very sparse support can avoid zero and have small fibers. It shows why a theorem that mixes the entire law at this horizon would be the wrong input.

The primary theorem checked was Breuillard–Varju, *Cut-off phenomenon for the ax+b Markov chain over a finite field* (2022), arXiv:1909.09053v2, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.09053 . Theorems 1–3 concern a fixed multiplier and i.i.d. translations; their strongest cutoff uses most multipliers and most primes, conditionally on Dedekind GRH. Theorem 5 gives a general high-order-multiplier bound on a longer polylogarithmic time scale. None supplies the correlated fixed-count identity estimate for every composite modulus `2^A-3^p`. Their equation (1.4) explicitly identifies the entropy lower barrier. Even granting their GRH hypothesis does not remove the parameter and horizon mismatches.

### Complete rational-denominator slices

For a positive divisor `d` of `D`, an exact slice can be counted without listing all words. Apply the shortcut rule

\[
T_d(x)=\begin{cases}x/2&x\text{ even},\\(3x+d)/2&x\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]

A positive period-`A` point with `p` odd steps satisfies `Dx=dC_w`. Thus integer `T_d` periodic points correspond exactly to words for which `D/d` divides `C_w`. Points congruent to `r mod d` count the full-word residue fiber `rD/d mod D`.

If `m` is the cycle minimum, the product identity gives

\[
\frac{D}{2^A}=1-\prod_{i=1}^p\left(1-\frac d{3x_i+d}\right)
\le\frac{pd}{3m+d},
\]

and hence the rigorous finite bound

\[
m\le\left\lfloor\frac{d(p2^A-D)}{3D}\right\rfloor. \tag{B}
\]

The minimum is odd and is not divisible by `3` when `gcd(d,6)=1`. Checking these candidate minima, rejecting a path as soon as it goes below its start, and accepting its first period-`A` return counts every cycle once. Each recorded pair has `gcd(A,p)=1`, so a matching cycle has primitive period `A`; no shorter-period bookkeeping is needed.

## 6. A positivity relaxation that fails

The cyclic linear system

\[
2n_{i+1}-(1+2w_i)n_i=w_i
\]

can be viewed as a cyclic M-matrix system when `D>0`. Replacing equality and exact parity by inequalities or rounded prescribed branches accepts spurious integer configurations. For example, the word `11010` has true affine fixed point `23/5`, yet its rounded prescribed branches admit

`1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 1 -> 2 -> 1`.

The second prescribed odd branch is applied at the even integer `2`, and the following prescribed even branch is applied at the odd integer `3`. A least-action or positive-subsolution argument that permits these rounding defects does not establish the exact zero atom. This route produced no exclusion.

## 7. What would count as a closing step

A universal proof of `(TC)`, or a genuinely zero-specific estimate equivalent to `(Z)`, would exclude every positive nontrivial cycle. A result with an unspecified exponential loss, a density-one set of moduli, independent translations replacing the conditioned words, or a norm in place of `C_w mod D` does not establish it. This attempt leaves exactly that estimate open.
