**General testing lemma.** An MSD automaton with leading-zero convention yields a finite stochastic transition matrix by feeding uniform independent digits. The probability of missing all closed classes after $M$ digits tends to zero uniformly over possible initial states, by repeated fixed-length trials with a positive lower chance to enter a closed class. For a closed communicating class of period $d$, $P^d$ converges on each cyclic subclass to its stationary distribution. Thus, after $L$ suffix digits, the expected output is uniformly close to the terminal cyclic subclass's mean once $L$ is sufficiently large. The current state's cyclic index plus the number of remaining digits determines that terminal subclass.

For $k^{J-1}\le X\le k^J$, a leading prefix of length $M$ defines an interval of length $k^{J-M}$ before truncation. The aggregate length of prefixes still transient is at most $\epsilon k^J\le k\epsilon X$. In a good prefix the terminal cyclic index is fixed across all its complete aligned suffix intervals of length $k^L$. With $J\ge M+L$, every prefix boundary is such a suffix boundary, so at most one final partial suffix interval incurs the $O(k^L)$ cost. On each complete suffix interval replacing $g=f-\delta$ by its leftmost value costs at most $k^L$ times its total variation, with a bounded factor for the outputs. Summing gives $o(X)$ for fixed $L$. Each resulting suffix expectation is within $\epsilon$ of the prefix's constant weight. Reinstating $g$ incurs the same variation cost. The Cesàro mean gives $\sup_{Y\le X}|\sum_{n<Y}g(n)|=o(X)$; bound the finitely many small $Y$ separately before taking the supremum. Thus all the $k^M$ prefix sums with constant weights contribute $o(X)$ for fixed $M$. Taking $X\to\infty$ and only then $\epsilon\to0$ proves the lemma, including nonconvergent digit distributions on the full integer cutoffs.

**Conditional detection clock.** Assume the quoted fixed-threshold first-passage estimate on odd sources. At fixed threshold $K\ge t$, a true hit of odd $t$ can follow the first odd landing below or at $K$ by at most the maximum hitting time over the finitely many successful odd seeds at or below $K$. The positive gap $3/\log 2-1/\log(4/3)$ absorbs that fixed constant and the sublinear clock error. Thus for sufficiently large successful odd sources outside the exceptional set, $j\le3\operatorname{bitlength}(m)$ odd iterations suffice. First take source cutoffs to infinity and then let $K\to\infty$ to obtain zero upper density of missed positives. For general input $n=2^v m$ with odd $m$, first truncate $v$ at a fixed bound and then let that bound grow; large-valuation shells have arbitrarily small density.

Each odd step of valuation $e_i$ uses exactly $e_i$ shortcut steps, including the first division within the odd shortcut. The identity $2^{e_i}x_{i+1}=3x_i+1\le4x_i$ gives $\sum_{i<j}e_i\le2j+\log_2 m-\log_2 x_j$. Including the $v$ initial halvings gives at most $2j+\log_2 n\le7\operatorname{bitlength}(n)$. The target is odd, so checking shortcut iterates through that bound detects each claimed hit. Intermediate positive integers grow by at most a factor of two per shortcut, leaving $O(\operatorname{bitlength}(n))$ bits per register and a quadratic bit-operation bound. No classification of the finite seeds is an input to the actual algorithm.
