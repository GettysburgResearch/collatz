# Variable-depth regeneration and the entropy cost of prescribing a shadow

Let $h_i$ denote ternary Haar probability on $U_i=i+3\mathbb Z_3$, $i=1,2$. A legal gap is $I_e(x)=(2^ex-1)/3$ with unit input and output. Write $L=1/\Theta_\infty$ for the infimum stationary finite-window mean gap (Weaver 127, Theorem 11). Its known entropy bound is $L\ge a_*$, where $H(a_*)=\log3$, $H(a)=a\log a-(a-1)\log(a-1)$.

## 1. Stop at the first failed prefix

Fix $a\ge1$ and credit $c\ge0$. Write $G_0=U$ and let $G_j=G_j(a,c)$ consist of those sources carrying a unit-legal $j$-letter word with $S_r\le ar+c$ for every $1\le r\le j$. These sets decrease. The condition is determined by the first $j+1$ trits, since only finitely many words can meet the budget and the terminal unit trit is included. For every $N\ge1$ and either $i$, if $c+6\ge a$ then
$$
 (L-a)\sum_{j=0}^N h_i(G_j)\le c+6-a.                    \tag{1}
$$
In fact that inequality also holds without the restriction $c+6\ge a$ by the same proof. If $a<L$, the full series is finite and $n h_i(G_n)\to0$. If either full series diverges, then $L\le a$. Thus for every $a>a_*$, divergence for one trit and one fixed finite intercept is sufficient for the critical endpoint $L=a_*$ when verified at slopes tending down to $a_*$. At the exact critical slope, every fixed-credit series already converges: Weaver 150/v2, Theorem 3, equation (7), bounds $h_i(G_n(a_*,c))$ by $O_c((1+\log n)n^{-3/2})$. Indeed survival implies $C_n\le a_*n+c$, and substituting $b=(3/2)\log(n+1)-t_*c\ge0$ in that equation gives the stated bound. Thus the positive endpoint test uses strictly supercritical slopes approaching $a_*$. [Weaver 150/v2, Theorem 3](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/150).

Start from $h_i$. Read successive trits and let $J$ be the largest $0\le j\le N$ for which the source lies in $G_j$. On a failure $j<N$, its stopping event is measurable at depth $j+2$; on reaching the cap read one further trit as well, giving the same depth $J+2$. This partitions $U_i$ into disjoint full ternary cells of those variable depths. On each such cell choose one surviving $J$-word; its intermediate states are units and its total is at most $aJ+c$, including $J=0$. The resulting state is known modulo nine. Choose a last exponent $1\le e\le6$ for which $2^e y\equiv1+3i\pmod9$, possible since 2 generates the six unit residues. This consumes one gap and returns to the original unit trit $i$. The whole plan on each cell of depth $J+2$ is fixed and its affine multiplier has ternary valuation $-(J+1)$. Its output on that cell, conditioned on the input Haar law there, is exactly $h_i$. Every plan therefore regenerates, including those stopped at the cap. Its length has expectation $\mathbb E(J+1)=\sum_{j=0}^Nh_i(G_j)$.

Sum the source/action occupations at all departure times of a block, divide by its expected length, then disintegrate by state. The equality of the endpoint laws telescopes to stationarity. Every state/action density is constant on cells of depth at most $N+2$ since we averaged finitely many fixed-word cells by expanding affine maps. The disintegration gives a finite-window rule with exactly this one-edge occupation and finite cap. Its cost is at most $(a\mathbb E J+c+6)/\mathbb E(J+1)$. The infimum property of $L$ proves (1). If a strictly positive finite rule is required in the cost characterization, mix this finite stationary flow with a positive reference flow at the same enlarged width and cap, and then send the mixing probability to zero. Finiteness and monotonicity of $h_i(G_j)$ imply $n h_i(G_n)\to0$ by summing over $n/2\le j\le n$.

This argument requires a coherent surviving word over each individual successful finite prefix. The selected words at different depths of the same trit stream need not be compatible. Only the word at the stopping depth is executed. It does not require a coupling that reads the unexposed Haar tail.

## 2. Exact exponential rate of corridor survival

For $a\ge1$ and $c\ge(r_i-a)_+$, where $r_1=2$ and $r_2=1$, one always has
$$
 \lim_{n\to\infty}{1\over n}\log h_i(G_n(a,c))
       =\min\{H(a),\log3\}-\log3 .                         \tag{2}
$$
A smaller intercept than that boundary gives no legal first letter in $U_i$. For the upper bound, count at most $\binom{\lfloor an+c\rfloor}{n}$ words, with at most one depth-$n$ source cell per word. Use the ambient measure bound one. When $a=1$, the word of initial gap $r_i$ and remaining ones is feasible and occupies a full $(n+1)$-trit cell after prescribing any one unit terminal. This gives the matching exponent $-\log3$.

For $a>1$ fix any $1<t<\min(a,a_*)$ and draw independent positive geometric letters of mean $t$. The random walk $S_j-aj$ has negative drift, hence its all-time maximum is finite almost surely. Prepend the fixed gap $r_i$ and sufficiently many ones so that the remaining credit makes survival for **all** subsequent prefixes have probability at least $1-\epsilon$. The finite prefix is itself legal at its coded source since its first trit is $i$ and every tail code is a unit. It consumes constant length $d$ for fixed $a,t,c,\epsilon$. Take a suffix of length $m=n-d$. Its ternary offset modulo $3^m$ has entropy $mH(t)+o(m)$ by Leen's Theorem C. The projected support of the surviving suffixes therefore has at least $\exp[mH(t)-\epsilon m\log3-o(m)]$ elements: split by the event of finite-prefix survival, pay $\log2$ for its indicator and the full $m\log3$ on its exceptional part. Distinct suffix offsets give distinct original depth-$n$ cells after appending the common initial prefix. For each retained suffix choose one word and prescribe terminal unit trit one, yielding one depth-$(n+1)$ cell in $G_n$ of the required initial trit. This proves the lower exponent after successively sending $n\to\infty$, $\epsilon\to0$, $t\uparrow\min(a,a_*)$. If $L>a_*$, then throughout $(a_*,L)$ these corridor probabilities must simultaneously be $e^{-o(n)}$ and summable at every fixed finite intercept. The exponent alone does not settle that possibility.

## 3. Why a single generic segment cannot be amplified at bounded cost

Consider any random plan of a common finite length $d=b+k$, started from $h_i$. Its $d$ executed gaps determine the initial source modulo $3^d$, so their joint entropy is at least $(d-1)\log3$. Suppose the $k$ prescribed shadow positions can be specified by an auxiliary finite or countable description of entropy at most $h_D$; the order inside and outside the positions is retained. Suppose the shadow vector belongs to a dictionary of size at most $M$ conditional on this description. Let $m$ be the average gap on the remaining $b>0$ positions. The entropy chain rule and concavity of the positive-geometric entropy give
$$
      (d-1)\log3\le h_D+\log M+b H(m).                       \tag{3}
$$
Indeed the description, shadow vector and ordered filler vector reconstruct every gap. One can drop their conditioning when bounding the filler entropy by the sum of its coordinate entropies and then $bH(m)$. The positions may depend on the input. In particular, choosing among all starting positions of one contiguous shadow costs at most $\log(d+1)$, and subexponentially many recursive schedule choices cost $o(d)$.

For a sequence with $k/d\to\rho\in(0,1)$, $h_D=o(d)$, $\limsup k^{-1}\log M\le s<\log3$, and shadow mean tending to $a_*$, (3) gives the lower total mean
$$
 \liminf {\mathbb E S_d\over d}\ \ge\ \rho a_*+(1-\rho)H^{-1}\!\left({\log3-\rho s\over1-\rho}\right)>a_* .       \tag{4}
$$
For a subexponential dictionary $s=0$. If its shadow weight tends to one, bounded total mean $C$ would give an upper filler entropy per total step $(1-\rho)H(C/(1-\rho))\to0$, contradicting (3). Thus the fixed-segment regeneration in `ADAPTIVE_WEAK_MARGINALS.md` cannot be purified by prescribing more and more of the block to fixed words, even nested according to a description of sublinear entropy. A critical dictionary that makes use of about $k\log3$ bits of source information is outside this obstruction. It is exactly the source dependence retained in the variable-depth construction of Section 1. The cost lower bound does not apply to unconstrained finite selectors merely because their stationary weak limit contains a critical component.

Weaver 127, Theorem 11, constructs stationary controllers by fixed-depth Haar regeneration. Weaver 229 proves the worst-root minimum cost per arrival tends to the same $L$. Weaver 135 proves an *upper* critical geometric Haar overlap $O(\log n/\sqrt n)$ and an entropy deficit of at least square-root size; the original-source corridor measure in (1) is a different quantity. The only tilted-law entropy used for (2) is Leen's Shannon-dimension theorem.
