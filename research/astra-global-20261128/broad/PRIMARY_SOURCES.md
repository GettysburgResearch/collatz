# Primary-source map for global approaches

**SOURCE-QUALIFIED, read November 27, 2026 UTC.** The pin approximation and its boundedness equivalence use elementary branch algebra. The fixed-arity theorem additionally uses the Subspace Theorem. This map records what other sources would actually supply in a transfer.

| Source | Reusable statement or mechanism | Missing hypothesis for a Collatz application here |
|---|---|---|
| Terras (1976) and Everett (1977) | For the shortcut convention, parity words of length $k$ parameterize initial classes modulo $2^k$. A random complete dyadic residue system therefore has fair, independent symbols through time $k$. | A fixed small ordinary source has only one branch at all future depths. Random words through a growing number of depths give no exceptional-point exclusion. [Terras](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-30-3-241-252), [Everett](https://doi.org/10.1016/0001-8708(77)90087-1). |
| Tao (2022), *Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values* | For every real-valued $f(n)\to\infty$, the minimum is at most $f(n)$ on a logarithmic-density-one set. Approximate transport of a first passage law uses characteristic-function estimates for a 3-adic random offset. | The result averages sources. It gives neither the finiteness of the maximal tilted multiplier at one prescribed integer nor the exclusion of a single exceptional component. [Author's preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03562). |
| Siegel–O'Dwyer (2026), *Almost-Boundedness of Collatz-Type Maps on Number Rings via Archimedean Methods* | The authors recover almost-boundedness through an archimedean method for their class of maps, under the dimension constraint $(p-1)^2\rho_H^d<1$ and their additional Hydra-map hypotheses. Their treatment includes the ordinary case. | This is a preprint about almost-boundedness. Its alternative proof has no asserted individual-integer conclusion. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33018). |
| Monks, Monks, Monks and Monks (2013) | Every ordinary positive shortcut orbit meets $2\bmod 9$. Every divergent orbit and nontrivial positive cycle meets $20\bmod 27$. Their sufficient-set framework permits testing a restricted goal whenever its forward or merging coverage is proved for that goal. | A compulsory visit to a class does not prove convergence of all members or their return to lower height. [Authors' paper](https://www.mathematicalgemstones.com/maria/papers/mmmm.pdf). |
| Yolcu–Aaronson–Heule (2023) | Encode a generalized Collatz function as a string system whose termination is equivalent to convergence. Natural/arctic matrix interpretations certify several partial systems. Their Theorems 3.8 and 3.10 exclude specified natural matrix interpretations for a **unary** system. Conjectures 4.11–4.14 ask for visits to selected mod-8 classes. | A termination certificate for the full system still requires a well-founded order respecting the actual rules. Their unary obstruction does not exclude all mixed-base interpretations. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14697). |
| Evertse–Ferretti (2013), quantitative Subspace Theorem | A fixed finite collection of systems of independent algebraic linear forms at fixed places converts a small product, relative to height with a fixed positive exponent saving, into containment in finitely many proper subspaces. Their theorem supplies quantitative counts under its height and parameter hypotheses. | The dimension, coefficient systems, saving and height matter. In the pin application the number of forms grows with the number of even events. Section 1 of our [sparse approximation proof](SPARSE_APPROXIMATION.md) handles the exceptional subspaces at fixed arity. [Annals paper](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2013.177.2.4). |
| K. C. Chim (2025), Theorem 2.1 | For two multiplicatively independent algebraic $p$-adic units with positive integer exponents, the explicit estimate for their nonzero power difference has linear dependence on the cutoff $H$ growing as the logarithm of the exponents. The other factors include the two logarithmic heights. At $p=2$ over the rationals these simplify as recorded in the [post-burst proof](MERSENNE_POST_BURST.md). | The second height grows after each new parity segment. The theorem does not give positive density of even steps from an unrestricted source. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2024.07.012). |
| Adamczewski–Bugeaud (2007) | They use repeated finite digit blocks and the Subspace Theorem to show that an algebraic irrational has superlinear block complexity in an integer base. | The Collatz parity stream is generally neither a base-$b$ expansion of a specified algebraic irrational nor a word known to admit a long repeating prefix. The term count in the pin sum can also grow. [Primary paper](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2007/165-2/p04). |
| Knight (2026), *Collatz high cycles do not exist* | Among rational cycles with specified period and number of odd entries, the high cycle maximizes the minimum. Knight excludes these high cycles on positive integers, supplementing the known circuit exclusion at the opposite extremum. | A putative intermediate positive cycle need not be either extremizer. [Publisher's abstract and paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2025.114812). |
| Vasilyev (November 2026), *Up or down at every integer* | For maps whose up/down sets are periodic modulo a power of two, a residue graph can give a uniform descent criterion. The author gives examples obtained by modifying the Collatz up-set for which finite verification succeeds. | A bound on average up-frequency in the residue chain is weaker than control of every path. Proving convergence for a modified map does not establish the original one. [Author's preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20344). |
| Elsenhans (November 24, 2026) | Presents an efficient method to check selected billion-digit inputs, with a near-linear bit-complexity conclusion conditional on quantitative convergence behavior. | Testing extraordinarily large selected inputs does not establish a uniform stopping law. [Research in Number Theory](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-026-00811-5). |

## Recent work closest to the present arguments

- Sodelin's unreviewed Theorem 6A.1 uses a repelling rational odd-map cycle, its ordinary integer shadows, and the last minimum of a corrected logarithm under an adaptive deadline. Its explicit family has exponents $(2,1,\ldots,1)$, corresponding to the same cyclic parity patterns as our local obstruction. The shortcut proof in [the ranking analysis](RANKING_OBSTRUCTIONS.md) gives a finite-prime root-avoidance construction and one bit length minus an additive constant for nonlinear joint local observations. [Author's note, pinned revision](https://github.com/Sodelin/Collatz-Conjecture-Work/blob/c3d1da2c5d/papers/round-6a/Theorem_6A1_Public_Review_Note.md).
- A November rStation preprint tests general binary digit-window sums, a finite linear combination of valuations at rational centers, and a bounded term. For positive mean digit score its Theorem A excludes an adaptive deadline of one hundredth of the bit length. Its proof uses a normal witness, concentration of window counts and a last minimum. Binary digit-window scores are outside the local class treated here. It credits Sodelin's last-minimum device and Nashida's prior staged obstructions. The statements here were read from the authors' texts and have not been independently revalidated in full. [rStation paper 208](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/208), [Nashida's preprint](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23081448).
- A public 2026 research manuscript gives an effective lower bound of order $\log t\log\log t$ on the number of exponents at least two among the first $t$ odd steps of every positive orbit, beyond a threshold depending on the start. Its Theorem 2 also bounds the maximum of a segment of fixed periodic-dictionary cost $r$ starting at $b$ by $K_{r,W,\sigma}b^\sigma$ for each $\sigma>\log_2 3$. The coefficient $K$ can be ineffective. These bounds constrain sparse defects and remain short of positive frequency. Our independent proof works directly with the inhomogeneous finite pin sums. [Primary manuscript, paper 15](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/15).
- The separate 2026 automaticity manuscript states that a positive rational 2-adic integer with an automatic parity sequence has an eventually periodic orbit. The authors specify substitution hypotheses for stronger classes. Even taking the claims as stated, computability of the Collatz orbit does not imply automaticity of its parity sequence. The argument here imposes no substitution hypothesis. [Primary manuscript, paper 16](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/16).

- Another November manuscript proves the following conditional short-time descent statement in its written argument. At the bit-length scale $n=\lfloor\log_2|x|\rfloor$, for fixed $A>0$, $0<\eta\le1$ and denominator exponent $b\ge0$, there are $K,X_0$ so that a signed rational start of magnitude at least $X_0$ and odd reduced denominator at most $|x|^b$ either descends below $|x|^\eta$ in $Kn$ steps or realizes more than $An$ length-$n$ parity factors there. The manuscript deduces superlinear factor complexity for an aperiodic rational-state orbit. It works with a bounded collection of short paths from a Rauzy graph and a Subspace Theorem argument. Low complexity of the parity word is an additional input. [rStation paper 173](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/173/v1).

- A November 19 manuscript proves that at least $1/\log_2 3$ of the rise steps of an aperiodic positive odd-map trajectory lie outside its $r$ longest rise groups, for each fixed $r$ in the limiting assertion. It gives separate quantitative exceptional-bin and larger-budget estimates. Our [macroscopic-mass proof](MACROSCOPIC_ODD_MASS.md) uses an unbounded count of marks selected by their own starting bit lengths and treats the entire last run; its concentration constant and a close fixed-budget theorem already appear in the manuscript. [rStation paper 174](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/174/v1).
- López and Stoll's Theorem 1 purports to identify the exact lower parity density of a divergent rational orbit. Its proof uses assertions about a real pseudo-orbit. An [explicit rational counterexample](REAL_BRANCH_AUDIT.md) violates the stated Lemma 24 under its literal hypotheses and shows that an accumulating real pseudo-orbit can start at an ordinary integer. The claimed 2-adic conclusion is not used as a theorem here. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12747).

## The Alpöge–Claude result and the proposed transfer

Alpöge's November 20 announcement describes a Lean development for RH and the Dirichlet version of GRH. The author describes iterated theta lifts as a means of obtaining increasing multiplicity. The Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative jointly certified the ordinary RH theorem statement and proof on November 21. Andrew Sutherland's checked archive gives the exact five challenge statements and independent verification records. Its scope includes all moduli and Dirichlet characters, with the conventional exclusion at the pole where required. These sources make no Collatz claim. [Author's note](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf), [joint verification statement](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/), [Sutherland's primary verification report](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/), [Sernau's shorter formal development](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean).

The analogous question asked here is whether accumulating local dyadic precision can outrun the archimedean height cost. Each new even departure contributes a summand with a strictly ordered dyadic valuation. The Subspace Theorem converts the accumulated precision into a fixed-arity bound; the unresolved step is controlling the constants and relations as arity grows. Neither the RH theorem nor a Dirichlet-character zero estimate enters this deduction.
