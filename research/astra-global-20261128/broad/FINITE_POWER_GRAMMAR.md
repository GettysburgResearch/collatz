# A superlogarithmic lower bound on arbitrary nested repetition trees

**Written theorem.** A power expression has leaves $0,1$, binary concatenation nodes, and nodes $V^q$ with arbitrary integer $q\ge2$. Its size counts nodes, even when the same phrase is used at different places in the syntax tree. A power itself contains only one copy of its child in that tree. Exponents have no assigned cost. Let $s(w)$ be the smallest such size for the binary word $w$.

Let $x_0,\dots,x_T$ be any finite positive ordinary shortcut path whose **input states** $x_0,\dots,x_{T-1}$ are pairwise distinct. Let $w$ be its $T$ input bits and $B=\log_2(x_0+1)$. Uniformly over such paths,
$$ \boxed{\qquad \frac{s(w)}{\log(1+T/B)}\longrightarrow\infty\quad\text{as }T/B\to\infty.\qquad} \tag{1} $$
The natural log is used for physical-time weights. This includes varying initial states and arbitrarily deep nesting. It also implies the same divergence for the *number* of terms in every partition into power expressions of fixed depth, since a fixed binary depth bounds the number of nodes per expression. Shared grammar nonterminals or arbitrary dictionary references are a different model.

## 1. Uniform logarithmic mass of macroscopic small-period windows

For path number $n$ use $T=T_n,B=B_n$ and the probability measure on $[0,T)$
$$ d\mu_{B,T}(s)=\frac{ds}{(B+s)\log(1+T/B)}. $$
Fix $\delta>0$. Suppose a finite family of periodic windows $[a,a+L)\subset[0,T)$ with integer endpoints obeys $L\ge\delta(B+a)$ and has period $p\le\eta_n L$, where $\eta_n\to0$ and $T_n/B_n\to\infty$. The words and periods may depend on the observed path. For the union $U_n$,
$$ \limsup_n\mu_{B,T}(U_n)\le\frac{c}{1+c},\qquad c=\log_2(3/2). \tag{2} $$
Here the assumption that the segment is simple replaces any hypothesis about the orbit after the cutoff.

**Exact extension and trimming.** Write $B_a=\log_2(x_a+1)\le B+ca$. A period-$p$ phase has rational fixed point $A/(2^p-3^k)$ of height at most $3^p$, including every rotation. Extend the match from $a$ to its first mismatch with that infinite repetition. There is a mismatch, because equality of the actual state to its period phase would imply $x_a=x_{a+p}$ with $a+p<a+L\le T$, since $p<L$ for all large $n$. Thus the extended length $\ell=v_2(x_a-\rho)$ is finite and $L\le\ell\le B_a+\gamma p$ with $\gamma=\log_2 3$. In particular $p\le\eta_n\ell$ and $\ell\le2(B+a)$ for large $n$.

Apply the farthest-right greedy covering of `OVERLAPPING_PERIODIC_WINDOWS.md` to these finitely many extensions. Order selected intervals $[a_i,b_i)$ by increasing start and end. They have multiplicity at most two, and every overlap $d_i=\max(0,b_{i-1}-a_i)$ obeys $d_i<p_{i-1}+p_i$ unless it is empty. Indeed an overlap of length $p_{i-1}+p_i$ gives equality of the two infinite periodic tails by cross-multiplication of their rational generating functions, contradicting $b_i>b_{i-1}$. Trim $d_i$ symbols from each left end and discard the entire remainder if $d_i>\ell_i/2$. At any cutoff $t$ the total lengths of selected intervals starting before $t$ are $O(B+t)$ even when one crosses $t$, since endpoints are $O(B+t)$ and multiplicity is two. The length of discarded coverage up to $t$ is $O(\eta_n(B+t))$. Integrating this cumulative inequality by parts shows its logarithmic mass is $O(\eta_n)(1+1/\log(1+T/B))$.

The retained fragments are disjoint and extend exactly to first mismatch. Write their starts and lengths as $a_i,\ell_i$ afresh. Every $p_i\le2\eta_n\ell_i$. There is a fixed $\delta'>0$ depending only on $\delta$ such that $\ell_i\ge\delta'(B+a_i)$. For the original start $a$, its trimmed start is $a+d$, with $d\le\ell/2$ and $B+a+d\le B+a+\ell\le 3(B+a)$, while the new length is at least $L/2\ge\delta(B+a)/2$. One can take $\delta'=\delta/6$. In particular the number of retained intervals starting before $t$ is $O_{\delta'}(1+\log(1+t/B))$, because successive starts grow the quantity $B+a_i$ by at least $1+\delta'$.

**Uniform finite arithmetic.** Use the finite fixed-dimensional inequality (2) of `RATIONAL_MATCH_MASS.md`. Its Evertse constant $K_{r,\epsilon}$ is independent of the source, windows and period data. At each retained phase its $D_i=\log_2(2H(\rho_i))$ and $R_i=\log_2(2+|\sigma_i|)$ are $O(1+\eta_n\ell_i)$. Fix $m>2/\delta'$, $0<\epsilon<1$, put $K=\max_{r\le m}K_{r,\epsilon}$ enlarged to at least one, and sum the finite inequality over the $m$ residues of block cuts as in `MACROSCOPIC_ODD_MASS.md`. If $a_j\le t<a_{j+1}$, $G_j=\sum_{i\le j}\ell_i$, $M(t)$ is marked length up to $t$, and $U(t)$ is the count of outside odd inputs up to the integer cutoff, then
$$
 (m-(1+\epsilon)/\delta')G_j\le c(m-1) U(t)
 +O_{m,\delta'}\!\left((B+a_1)+(\eta_n+\epsilon)(B+t)
 +(1+K)\,[1+\log(1+t/B)]\right). \tag{3}
$$
For (3), every start height is charged once to $\sum B_{a_i}\le G_j/\delta'$, except $a_1$, charged $m-1$ extra times. All gap spans in any one partition are disjoint before $a_j$. Each height and terminal cost occurs $O_m(1)$ times, and $G_j=O(B+t)$. The total number of blocks is $m+j-1$. These are finite estimates, including the potentially incomplete last mark since $M(t)\le G_j$. The bounds for real $t$ differ only by $O(1)$ through integer parts. One may replace $U(t)$ by $t-M(t)+1$. Before $a_1$ one has $M(t)=0$. If there are no marks, there is nothing to prove.

Use $\int_0^T dM(t)/(B+t)=M(T)/(B+T)+\int_0^T M(t)\,dt/(B+t)^2$. The first-mark error integrates only from $a_1$ onward, so $(B+a_1)\int_{a_1}^Tdt/(B+t)^2\le1$. The $K$ error also contributes $O_{m,\epsilon,\delta'}(1)$ before normalization, uniformly for $B\ge1$. Divide by $\log(1+T/B)\to\infty$. First take $n\to\infty$, then $\epsilon\downarrow0$ for the fixed $m$, then $m\to\infty$. Equation (3) yields (2), including any original overlap by the trimming estimate. This order of limits requires no uniformity in Evertse's constants as the dimension varies.

## 2. Extraction from bounded-depth expressions

Here is the purely combinatorial step. On a sequence with $D_n=\log(1+T_n/B_n)\to\infty$, let a forest of $N_n\le C D_n$ pairwise disjoint pieces be represented by power expressions of depth at most $d$, with fixed $C,d$. It need only cover part of the prefix. After passing to a subsequence, for each $\varepsilon>0$ there is a fixed $\delta>0$ and periodic windows inside those pieces with $L\ge\delta(B_n+a)$, maximum $p/L\to0$, and total $\mu_{B_n,T_n}$-mass at least the forest's mass minus $\varepsilon+o(1)$.

**Proof by depth.** To cost at most $\zeta>0$ in mass, discard any current piece of length $L<\delta_0(B+a)$ with $\delta_0=\zeta/(1+C)$. Each contributes at most $\log(1+\delta_0)/D_n$. For the rest, collect leaves as period-one windows. The union of leaves with $L=1$ and $L\ge\delta_0(B+a)$ lies below $1+1/\delta_0$ and has mass $o(1)$, so it can instead be discarded. Split every concatenation into its two children. For power nodes $V^q$ take the finite measures on $\mathbb N_{\ge2}\cup\{\infty\}$ giving mass $\mu(I)$ to the exponent of its interval. Their total mass is at most one. Along a subsequence all atom masses and total mass have limits (ordinary diagonal Bolzano–Weierstrass). Given $\zeta$, select fixed $R$ so the sum of all limiting finite masses above $R$ is at most $\zeta$. On a further subsequence choose $r_n\to\infty$, $r_n>R$, so that the mass at $R<q<r_n$ is at most $2\zeta$ for all sufficiently large $n$. This last choice follows by convergence on each finite set $R<q<K$, then letting $K$ increase sufficiently slowly.

Keep powers with $q\ge r_n$ as periodic windows. Their periods are at most $L/r_n$ and their length threshold is unchanged. Unroll powers with $q\le R$ into at most $R$ disjoint children. All remaining children, including the concatenation children, form a forest of size at most $(R+2)CD_n$ and depth at most $d-1$. Induct with this new fixed constant; the minimum of the finitely many positive length thresholds works for the final collection. Each stage loses at most $3\zeta+o(1)$ in mass, so one may take $\zeta<\varepsilon/(4(d+1))$. There are at most $d+1$ diagonal subsequences, and all collected periods satisfy $p/L\to0$ uniformly. The depth-zero case consists only of leaves and follows from the same discard estimate. This proves the extraction claim.

## 3. Arbitrary nesting depths and consequences

Suppose (1) fails on a sequence and choose trees with at most $C D_n$ nodes. Flatten every top-level concatenation down to its first leaf or power. These maximal pieces partition the full input; their syntax subtrees have disjoint node sets. Every nontrivial power $W^q$ of period $p=|W|$, starting at $a$, obeys
$$ (q-1-c)p\le B_a. \tag{4} $$
Indeed the distinct integers $x_a,x_{a+p}$ share at least $(q-1)p$ initial parity bits, so $2^{(q-1)p}$ divides their nonzero difference. Both states are positive and $x_{a+p}+1\le (3/2)^p(x_a+1)$, proving (4). Thus its length is at most $2B_a/(1-c)$ for every $q\ge2$. A leaf has length one. Every maximal piece therefore carries at most $C_0/D_n$ logarithmic mass for an absolute constant $C_0$, since $B_a\le B+ca\le B+a$.

For any fixed integer $D$ there are at most $C D_n/D$ maximal pieces with more than $D$ nodes. Their total mass is at most $CC_0/D$. Choose $D$ sufficiently large to make this smaller than $(1-c/(1+c))/4$, and keep the other maximal pieces as a depth-$D$ forest. Their mass is then at least $1-(1-c/(1+c))/4$. By §2 on a subsequence one can cover all but another $(1-c/(1+c))/4$ of that mass by a single family to which (2) applies, a contradiction. This proves (1).

For a general fixed starting integer first move to its first even input, which takes $v_2(x+1)$ initial odd steps. This fixed change of origin does not affect the asymptotic logarithms. Take $g_j$ to be the ordinary odd gaps between successive even inputs from that point. On events $0,\dots,J$ a constant-gap plateau has word $(01^g)^q$ of bounded expression size independently of $g,q$ (zero repetitions inside the formula are simply deleted). Cylinders from each plateau concatenate without loss or overlap. On any nonperiodic positive ray the number of plateaus among these $J$ gaps divided by $\log t_J$ therefore tends to infinity. In particular $\sum_{i<J-1}|g_{i+1}-g_i|/\log t_J\to\infty$. If the gaps are nondecreasing then $g_J/\log J\to\infty$. This does not force a positive even density or control a sufficiently rough gap schedule.

A primitive positive cycle gives a simple-input segment of its full length $P$ from its least state $x$. The repeated terminal state is allowed; every comparison in (4) is inside the input segment because $q\ge2$. For an odd input $z$ the multiplicative factor is $(3/2)(1+1/(3z))$. If $O$ is the number of odd inputs, multiplication over the cycle gives
$$ 0<P\log2-O\log3=\sum_{z\ {\rm odd}}\log(1+1/(3z))\le P/(3x). $$
The standard two-logarithm bound $P\log2-O\log3\gg(P+1)^{-C}$ shows $x\ll(P+1)^{C+1}$. Hence along a family of primitive cycles with $P\to\infty$, a whole lap cut at its least state has superlogarithmic power-expression size. No conclusion on a cut with $\log x$ comparable to $P$ is required.

## Published overlap and formal scope

Weaver's paper 15/v2, Theorems 1 and 9, gives a stronger effective $\log t\log\log t$ scale for counted periodic fragments in the indicated fixed and charged adaptive Syracuse dictionaries. The adaptive bound charges the total division lengths of the templates and gives that complexity scale, for instance, when this budget is at most $t^\theta$ for fixed $\theta<1$ and fixed source. It does not give (1) directly when the budget is $t^{1-o(1)}$. Paper 147's binary fragment duration result fixes its dictionary before the path varies. Paper 173 proves superlinear *factor complexity* for every aperiodic odd-denominator rational orbit and a finite descent criterion at the moving scale $\lfloor\log_2|x|\rfloor$. These are distinct definitions of complexity. Equation (1) is qualitative and retains free expanded periods and exponents. The shared-prefix dyadic divisibility used for (4) is classical and is also used in Wren's repetition bound in post 6. This manuscript does not infer (1) for shared straight-line grammars or universal programs.
