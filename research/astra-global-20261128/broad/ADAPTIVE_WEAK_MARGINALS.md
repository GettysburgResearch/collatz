# Stationary adaptive policies can retain any prescribed ternary gap law with positive weight

Let $U=\mathbb Z_3^\times$ and $A_e(y)=2^{-e}(1+3y)$ for $e\ge1$. Write $P$ for any stationary ergodic process of positive integer gaps with finite mean $a$. Its ternary source and its first executed inverse gap have joint law $J_P=(X,E_1)_*P$, where
$$
 X=\sum_{j\ge1}3^{j-1}2^{-(E_1+\cdots+E_j)}\in U.
$$
The source projection is $\nu_P$. A finite stationary policy draws legal inverse actions from a finite cap according only to the current residue of its state; after applying $I_e(x)=(2^e x-1)/3$ the next exposed trit is uniform. We use its locally Haar realization on $U$, including the whole $3$-adic source in each stationary occupation measure.

There is an absolute $\rho>0$ independent of $P$ such that a sequence of these finite policies has stationary source/action occupations converging weakly along a subsequence to
$$
       \rho J_P+(1-\rho)J', \qquad
       \text{mean costs before passage to the limit}\longrightarrow
       \rho a+2(1-\rho).                                           \tag{1}
$$
The residual $J'$ is itself a stationary legal edge flow. In particular the state marginal dominates $\rho\nu_P$. This applies to the singular full-dimensional entropy-critical geometric Syracuse law, and to the atom at the signed fixed ray $-1$. The original-source finite policies in (1) may choose incompatible high-digit marginals from one width to another. Their true stationary state measure at each finite width is locally Haar. For the critical iid law the limiting path process itself contains that iid law as an ergodic component of weight at least $\rho$, as proved in Section 3. The construction controls the joint law of the current state and action. Averaging a stored plan into a state-dependent Markov rule need not preserve longer path correlations.

## 1. Route to a generic critical or noncritical segment

Weaver–Wren, Theorem 8 and equations (80)–(83), give constants $\delta_*>0,b_*$ and a common integer length
$$
 b=b_*+1+\lceil(k+1)/\delta_*\rceil.
$$
Any supplied positive odd unit can reach any chosen unit class modulo $3^{k+1}$ in exactly $b$ legal steps of total gap exactly $2b$. The largest letter is $O(1+\log(k+1))$. The same finite assertion applies to any $3$-adic unit source cell modulo $3^{b+k+1}$: choose a positive odd representative and apply its supplied route. The affine image of that cell under the word has the same final class at the desired precision, and intermediate steps stay in the same legal residue classes. The universal constant in (1) is $\rho=\delta_* /(1+\delta_*)$. No numerical size beyond positivity is needed. [Weaver and Wren, *Hierarchical Syracuse renewal and linear valuation budgets for modular routing*, Theorem 8](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/48).

Take a $P$-generic infinite gap sequence with both the ergodic means of $E_1$ and all finite source/action cylinder indicators convergent to their expectations. Keep its first $k$ labels $w$. Fix a unit trit $u$ and set $t=A_w(u)\bmod3^{k+1}$, where $A_w=A_{e_1}\circ\cdots\circ A_{e_k}$. Every starting point in $t+3^{k+1}\mathbb Z_3$ follows the legal inverse gaps $w$ and ends in $U_u=u+3\mathbb Z_3$; each intermediate point is a unit since it is the corresponding composition of remaining contractions applied to a unit.

Start with Haar on $U_u$. On each initial $(b+k+1)$-digit cell, fix one length-$b$ route to $t$ and append $w$. Conditional on this entire initial cell, the plan is fixed. Its affine multiplier after $i$ steps has ternary valuation $-i$, so the phase-$i$ state is exactly Haar on a cell of depth $b+k+1-i$. In particular the final state after $b+k$ steps is Haar on $U_u$ on every initial cell. The complete block regenerates the initial law.

Average the joint state/action occupations at the $b+k$ departure times. The source and target marginals coincide by the two endpoint laws. At every phase each state/action density is constant on cells of depth at most $b+k+1$. Thus disintegrating the average joint occupation by its state supplies a rule depending only on those finitely many trits. Its cap is the larger of the finite route cap and the maximum letter in $w$. The unvisited rows may be filled by any legal bounded choice. Its stationary source, and its one-step joint law on the actual space $U$, are exactly the just-computed averages. Reading any additional top trit if necessary puts this rule in the stated uniform-fresh-top finite model. No uniform positivity assumption is imposed on all legal actions.

On the appended $w$ segment, at any nonterminal location with at least $m$ subsequent letters the $m$-trit state is determined by those $m$ letters alone. It agrees with the ternary source of that shift of the original generic sequence. Only the last $m$ phases may be lost when testing a fixed $m$-trit cylinder. Hence the averaged shadow edge law tends to $J_P$. Its mean gap tends to $a$. The remaining route-edge laws are tight because their means are exactly two; take a weakly convergent subsequence. Invariance of the full flow passes to the limit by testing bounded continuous state functions, discarding uniformly small mass at large letters. The same holds for the limiting shadow flow by stationarity of $P$, so subtracting the two gives residual stationarity. The residual mean in the limit can be smaller than two if a tail loses first moment. These facts prove (1) as stated.

## 2. The cost of loading recurrent signed atoms

Let $C<\infty$ be the limit inferior of the finite-policy means along any weakly converging sequence of source laws. Pass to a subsequence attaining $C$ and a further subsequence on which the two-sided stationary gap law converges. Such a subsequence exists because the one-coordinate means are bounded, the path laws are stationary, and a countable product of sufficiently large coordinate cutoffs has arbitrarily high probability. Write $Q$ for the resulting stationary gap process. The ternary coding is continuous: the first $j$ positive exponents determine the source modulo $3^j$. Its state marginal is therefore the prescribed weak limit. Its mean $a_Q$ is at most $C$.

Every finite locally Haar stationary policy has gap entropy rate at least $\log3$: its length-$n$ gap word determines its initial residue modulo $3^n$, and local Haar density gives the latter entropy $n\log3+O_W(1)$. Subadditivity of stationary block entropies makes every block entropy at least $n\log3$. This finite-law argument is the known entropy ceiling. It persists for $Q$: at fixed block length, bounded mean gives uniform control of entropy lost by clipping large coordinates. For one coordinate with tail probability $p_R\le C'/R$, the tail entropy conditional on its occurrence is at most the entropy of a nonnegative geometric variable of mean $C'/p_R$, hence clipping loses at most $p_R[1+\log(1+C'/p_R)]$; sum this bound over the coordinates. Finite clipped entropies are continuous under weak convergence. Thus $h(Q)\ge\log3$. [Weaver, *Critical barriers and memory costs in Collatz inverse dynamics*, Section 4, equations (18)–(19)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/150).

All atoms of the limiting state law lie on periodic cycles of odd rational units (odd means $2$-adic valuation zero). To see this without a Markov assumption, the image of a source atom under a positive-probability action is an atom. Conversely the event of reaching an atom can only come from a countable set of points $A_e(y)$, so receives zero contribution from diffuse states. The atomic event is invariant modulo null sets. Almost surely on it the path returns to its starting point by recurrence to a positive singleton. A return of length $n$ and total $s$ gives $x=C_w/(2^s-3^n)$ with
$$
 C_w=\sum_{j=1}^n 3^{j-1}2^{s-(e_1+\cdots+e_j)}
$$
odd and prime to three. Both numerator and denominator are odd; hence every visited atom has dyadic valuation zero. Given its descendant $y$, the unique possible previous gap is $v_2(1+3y)$, as $2^e x=1+3y$ and $v_2(x)=0$. Thus the reversed evolution on the countable atom set is deterministic. Recurrence forces its invariant laws to be mixtures of finite periodic cycles; the mass on each cycle is uniform. The atom-conditioned gap law has entropy rate zero, by ergodic decomposition and finite one-letter entropy. The recurrence step is related to the fixed-cap atomic argument of Weaver, while the policy law in this section may vary arbitrarily with width. [Weaver, *Nonatomic recurrent cores for capped Collatz inverse dynamics*, rational recurrence argument](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/116).

Write $p_{\mathcal C}$ for the limiting weights of these cycles, $a_{\mathcal C}$ for their average gaps, $p=\sum p_{\mathcal C}$, and $H_{\rm geom}(a)=a\log a-(a-1)\log(a-1)$ for $a\ge1$, with $H_{\rm geom}(1)=0$. Finite $C$ forces $p<1$. The entropy decomposition over the invariant atomic event, and the maximum entropy of a positive integer of prescribed mean, give the necessary bound
$$
 (1-p)H_{\rm geom}\!\left(\frac{C-\sum_{\mathcal C}p_{\mathcal C}a_{\mathcal C}}{1-p}\right)\ \ge\ \log3.                 \tag{2}
$$
Indeed the complementary entropy rate does not exceed the entropy of its one-gap law, whose mean is at most the displayed argument. In particular $C\ge p+(1-p)H_{\rm geom}^{-1}(\log3/(1-p))$. For $p=0$ this recovers the known Shannon lower bound $a_*=1/\theta_*$. Costs required by (2) diverge as $p\uparrow1$. Equation (1) with the constant gap-one law shows that some positive atom weight really is achievable at a uniformly bounded mean, even though total convergence to that atom is impossible. Neither estimate determines the limiting minimum finite-policy cost.

## 3. The true critical gap law survives Markovization as a component

Let $\mu$ be the ternary source of an iid gap law $P$ of full support, with finite mean, finite entropy, and with the first gap $e=f(x)$ determined almost surely by its coded state $x$. This applies to the critical geometric law. Indeed the length-$n$ gap word determines $X\bmod3^n$ and the critical one-gap entropy is $\log3$. Leen's full entropy dimension makes $H(E^n\mid X\bmod3^n)=o(n)$. Under iid coding $H(E^n\mid X)=nH(E\mid X)$: after conditioning on the preceding gaps, the remaining iid suffix is independent and the known prefix affine map identifies the original and suffix states bijectively. Thus $H(E\mid X)=0$. This use of critical decoding is an application of the primary dimension theorem and the posterior entropy identity. [Leen, *Tilted 3-adic Syracuse laws*, Theorem C](https://zenodo.org/records/23268832) · [Weaver, critical dimension normalization](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/135/v1).

Set $B_e=\{x:f(x)=e\}$. The measures $(A_e)_*\mu$ are each absolutely continuous with respect to $\mu$, since $\mu=\sum p_e(A_e)_*\mu$ and $p_e>0$ for every $e$. Each is concentrated on $B_e$, by iid independence of the tail and recovery of the first gap.

Now consider **any** stationary inverse-path law $Q$ on the same alphabet, with source and target marginal both $\lambda$. In the terminal marginal at $y$, split $\lambda=g\mu+\lambda_s$. The part with terminal law $g\mu$, transported backward to $x=A_e(y)$ using the actual conditional label under $Q$, has source law absolutely continuous with respect to $\mu$. Indeed each individual $e$-piece is dominated after transport by the image of $g\mu$, itself absolutely continuous with respect to $(A_e)_*\mu$. The $e$-piece is concentrated on $B_e$. The total absolutely continuous source mass thus obtained already equals the full absolutely continuous mass of $\lambda$. Therefore this part has initial marginal exactly $g\mu$, and no singular terminal mass can contribute to the absolutely continuous source. Membership in the absolutely continuous part (off a fixed $\mu$-null carrier of $\lambda_s$) is invariant along $Q$ paths.

On this part the action is $f(x)$ almost surely, so $g\mu$ is invariant under $x\mapsto I_{f(x)}(x)$. This deterministic map is ergodic for $\mu$ because the iid coding is ergodic and the source recovers the word. Its invariant absolutely continuous subprobabilities are scalar multiples of $\mu$ by Birkhoff. Consequently $\lambda=t\mu+\lambda_s$ with $t=\int g\,d\mu$, and the path process on the part of weight $t$ is the original iid process, since its state and current action recursively determine the full forward gap word.

Apply this to any weak path-process subsequence from Section 1 with $P$ critical. Tightness, coding and stationarity are as in Section 2, and its state law dominates $\rho\mu$. Therefore its genuine critical iid component has weight $t\ge\rho$, irrespective of possible additional critical mass in the route filler. This conclusion uses the disjoint decoded branch images. For a general $P$ Section 1 only asserts the state/action occupation result. No conclusion of total variation convergence at the working precision or of critical mean for the whole finite policy follows.
