# Effective bounds after a long initial odd run

**PROPOSED, written argument.** The map is the shortcut map. The large parameter $K$ counts the first $K$ odd inputs of $n=2^K q-1$ where $q$ is positive odd. The number of subsequent even inputs may grow with $K$. All logs in asymptotic ratios are natural unless the base is displayed.

## 1. What already happens at one even event

For $K\ge1$, the $K$ odd steps take $n$ to $y=q3^K-1$. One even step then takes it to $(q3^K-1)/2$. The next odd run has length $v_2(q3^K+1)-1$, so the time of the second even input is exactly

$$
 D_1(n)=K+v_2(q3^K+1).
$$

If $q\equiv1,3\bmod8$, the extra valuation belongs to $\{1,2\}$ for every $K$. If $q\equiv5,7\bmod8$, there are arbitrarily large $K$ with $v_2(q3^K+1)\ge\log_2 K-O(1)$. To see this, the subgroup generated by 9 modulo $2^m$, $m\ge3$, contains all units $1\bmod8$. Its order is $2^{m-3}$, from $v_2(9^{2^t}-1)=t+3$, which follows by squaring and observing that the other factor has valuation one. Hence the powers of 3 cover both cosets $1,3\bmod8$. The target $-q^{-1}$ lies in this union. Choose $K<2^{m-2}$ in the relevant residue class with $3^K\equiv-q^{-1}\bmod2^m$. The choices tend to infinity as $m$ tends to infinity, since no positive $q3^K+1$ is zero. Discard the finitely many levels at which $K=0$.

For fixed $q\equiv5,7\bmod8$, Theorem 2.1 of Chim, specialized as below with bases $3$ and $-1/q$, also gives $v_2(q3^K+1)\ll_q\log(K+2)$. Multiplicative independence holds because such $q$ has a prime factor different from 3. Thus for fixed $q=7$, for example, $\log\log n$ is the order of the largest excess over $\log_2(n+1)$ along subsequences. The universally valid fixed-arity slope $1+\epsilon$ cannot be replaced even at arity one by $1$ and a bounded error.

For the actual Mersenne inputs $q=1$, arity two also exhibits this obstruction. Take $H$ with $3^H\equiv-7\bmod2^m$, $m\ge4$, and $0<H<2^{m-2}$, using the same subgroup. Then $H\equiv2\bmod4$. Put $K=H-1$. After the initial $K$ odd steps from $2^K-1$, the next three branches are even, odd, even, ending at $(3^H-1)/8$. The ensuing odd length is $v_2(3^H+7)-3$. Consequently $D_2(2^K-1)=K+v_2(3^H+7)\ge K+m$. These exponents are unbounded with $m$.

## 2. A two-logarithm estimate that permits growing arity

Start at $y=q3^K-1$, immediately after the initial odd run. Let $z_t=T^t(y)+1$ and let $s_t$ count the odd inputs in these $t$ subsequent steps. Unrolling gives

$$
 2^t z_t=3^{K+s_t}q+E_t,\qquad 0\le E_t\le 6^t .
$$

Indeed a shifted state $z$ goes to $3z/2$ at an odd input and to $(z+1)/2$ at an even input. The offset is the sum of $2^i3^{s_t-s_{i+1}}$ over even inputs $i<t$. Thus $E_t\le 3^t(2^t-1)<6^t$ for $t>0$. If $g=v_2(z_t)$ is the ensuing odd-run length, then

$$
 t+g=v_2(3^{K+s_t}q+E_t).
$$

There is an absolute effective $C$ such that

$$
 t+g+1 \le C\,(1+t+\log_2(2q))\,\log_2(K+t+2). \tag{1}
$$

If $E_t=0$ or is even the valuation on the right side of the preceding identity is zero. Otherwise put $\alpha_1=3$, $\alpha_2=-E_t/q$, $b_1=K+s_t$, $b_2=1$, and $p=2$. These are nonzero rational 2-adic units, and $b_1,b_2$ are positive integers. In the multiplicatively dependent case $E_t/q=3^j$ for some integer $j$ (prime valuations force this). The valuation of $3^{K+s_t-j}+1$ is at most two, also for negative exponents, by reduction modulo eight.

Otherwise use Chim's Theorem 2.1 in the rational field, with $e=f=D=g_{\rm unit}=1$, $C_{\rm Chim}=4250$ and $Z_1=4$. Here one may take $A_1=3$ and $\log A_2=\max(\log 2,\log\max(E_t,q))$. Passing to lowest terms can only reduce this height. The theorem bounds $v_2(\alpha_1^{b_1}-\alpha_2^{b_2})$ by an absolute constant times $\log A_2\max(1,\log b')$, where $b'=b_1/\log A_2+1/\log3$. The harmless constant also covers the two explicit fixed lower cutoffs for its parameter $H$. Since $\log A_2\ll1+t+\log q$ and $b'\ll K+t+2$, this gives (1). Its valuation is unchanged on multiplying by odd $q$. The source proves the needed linear dependence on $\log b'$ via an interpolation determinant. [K. C. Chim, Journal of Number Theory 266 (2025), Theorem 2.1, equations (2.1)–(2.6)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2024.07.012).

## 3. The quantitative separation

Let $t_0=0$ and $t_{e+1}=t_e+v_2(z_{t_e})+1$, the time just after $e+1$ subsequent even inputs. Put $Q=1+\log_2(2q)$. Increasing an absolute effective constant if needed, while $t_e\le K$, (1) yields

$$
 t_{e+1}+Q\le C'(t_e+Q)\log(K+2).
$$

Fix $\beta\in[0,1)$ and suppose $\log Q\le(\beta+o(1))\log K$ along a family with $K\to\infty$. For every fixed $\eta\in(0,1-\beta)$, every

$$
 0\le e\le\eta\frac{\log K}{\log\log K}
$$

satisfies $t_e+Q\le K^{\beta+\eta+o(1)}$. Induction is legitimate since $Q(C'\log(K+2))^e\le K^{\beta+\eta+o(1)}<K$ throughout the asserted range.

Consequently, for each fixed $\gamma>0$, the first $\lfloor\gamma K\rfloor$ subsequent shortcut steps contain at least

$$
 (1-\beta-o(1))\frac{\log K}{\log\log K}
$$

even inputs. In particular the coefficient is one for ordinary Mersennes $q=1$ and for any fixed odd $q$. This theorem is uniform in a growing number of even events while the **starting integer varies** and begins an unusually long all-odd run. The resulting proportion of compulsory even steps can still tend to zero. The theorem does not give descent below $2^Kq-1$ or control the complete Mersenne orbit.
