# Complete mixing across separated prime-factor scales under ordinary RH

Use the independent geometric valuations $v_p$ with $\Pr(v_p=a)=(1-1/p)p^{-a}$, their finite signatures $S_z=\prod_{p\le z}p^{v_p}$, and $\mathcal A_w=\sigma(v_p:p\le w)$. For any fixed union of eventual Collatz components or fixed odd-target visit event $F$ with ordinary density $d$, if $z\to\infty$ and $\log(2w)=o(\log z)$, then
\[
          \mathbb E\left|\Pr(F(S_z)=1\mid\mathcal A_w)-d\right|\to0.     \tag{1}
\]
This theorem invokes **ordinary RH** for the prime estimates and the same counted-pair synchronization input as the preceding work. The relative order of the two prime scales has no fixed logarithmic-power restriction. With the source-qualified full ordinary component law $\pi$ in total variation, the conditional component law itself tends to $\pi$ in expected total variation. There is no hypothesis of independence between the prime factors of an actual integer beyond the full subpower-signature approximation already proved. [Ordinary signature transfer](SMOOTH_DIVISOR_FATES.md) · [Ordinary component TV and its analytic input](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/7902faf17d50851b46cdcd420ae479d19f0c845c/research/astra-nine-hour-20261128/escape/phase5/SMOOTH_SOURCE_CLOCK_GROUPS.md)

The [typical-band and empirical occupancy theorem](PRIME_BAND_OCCUPATION.md) now proves (1) without RH by retaining a smaller prime factor on a set of probability tending to one. Its tagged-prime argument also gives an exact empirical density along all actual prime links. The present all-box proof supplies a separate single-prime statement, used below for prescribed consecutive links.

The proof averages all the large prime factors together. Holding the other large primes fixed and using an estimate for just one variable prime can leave a multiplier polynomial in that prime, which is too large for a vanishing native fusion cost. The one-sided positive-fraction theorem in [canonical prime variation](CANONICAL_PRIME_VARIATION.md) avoids RH by retaining only an arithmetic portion of a factor band. Equation (1) instead controls the whole conditional probability.

## Smoothed products of prime intervals

Fix $r\ge1$. In logarithmic size coordinates choose a nonnegative smooth partition of unity with translates of a fixed bump supported on intervals $[j/r,(j+2)/r]$. For example normalize the translates of a smooth bump on $(0,2)$ bounded below on an interval covering one period; dilate the resulting partition by $r$. Every factor interval then starts at some $P_i$ and has log width $2/r$. Give a prime $p$ weight proportional to $W(r\log(p/P_i))/p$, where $W$ is the fixed normalized bump. Its continuous log-coordinate reference on $0\le s\le2/r$ has density proportional to $W(rs)/(\log P_i+s)$. Let $Y=\prod_{i=1}^rP_i$, assume every $P_i\ge Y^\theta$ for some fixed $\theta>0$, and take $Y\to\infty$. Let $\nu$ be the law of $\sum_i\log(p_i/P_i)$ for independent choices in the intervals; let $\rho$ be the convolution of the corresponding reference densities, supported on $[0,2]$.

For a smooth probability kernel $\phi_\Delta$ supported on $[0,\Delta]$, obtained by scaling one fixed bump, put $\Delta=Y^{-1+\gamma}$ with fixed $0<\gamma<1/2$. Under ordinary RH we have
\[
                  \| (\nu-\rho) *\phi_\Delta\|_{L^1(\mathbb R)}=o(1).  \tag{2}
\]
The constants in this asymptotic may depend on $r,\theta,\gamma$, which are fixed before the large-scale limit. No independence of the orbit marks is used here.

For each factor, the characteristic functions of its normalized prime and continuous laws differ by $O_r(P_i^{-1/2}\log^C(2P_i(2+|\xi|)))$ for a fixed absolute $C$. To see this apply the smoothed von Mangoldt explicit formula to
\[
   W(r\log x)\frac{\log P_i}{\log(P_i x)} x^{-1-i\xi}
\]
at scale $P_i$, dividing the resulting formula by $P_i\log P_i$. The main term is the continuous integral before normalization. Each nontrivial zero $1/2+i\beta$ gives absolute contribution
$O_{r,N}(P_i^{-1/2}(\log P_i)^{-1}(1+|\beta-\xi|)^{-N})$ by integration by parts in the log variable. The zero count on a unit interval is $O(\log(2+|\beta|))$ by Riemann–von Mangoldt. The trivial zeros give a summable smaller bound (the bump has support on $x\ge1$). Removing all prime powers of exponent at least two costs $O_r(P_i^{-1/2}\log^C(2P_i))$ before adjusting logarithmic factors, by elementary counting of powers in this interval. Finally the total unnormalized prime and reference masses both equal $(1+o(1))(r\log P_i)^{-1}\int W$, obtained also by setting $\xi=0$. This proves the characteristic-function estimate. The reference transform has magnitude $O_r((1+|\xi|)^{-1})$. [Ben Green, *The prime number theorem*, Theorem 7.1, smoothed explicit formula](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/primenumbers.pdf) · [Riemann–von Mangoldt unit-interval bound, Exercise 17](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2021/02/12/246b-notes-4-the-riemann-zeta-function-and-the-prime-number-theorem/)

To prove (2) expand the difference of the $r$ factor products. A term indexed by a nonempty set $J$ of error factors contributes, on $|\xi|\le Y^{1-\gamma/2}$, at most
\[
 O_{r}\!\left((\log Y)^{O_r(1)}\prod_{i\in J}P_i^{-1}
      \int_0^{Y^{1-\gamma/2}}(1+u)^{-2(r-|J|)}du\right)
\]
to its squared $L^2$ norm. Proper $J$ give $o(1)$ since the integral is bounded and $\min_i P_i\ge Y^\theta$. For $J$ the full set, the product $Y^{-1}$ pays for the length $Y^{1-\gamma/2}$. On the complementary frequencies use the trivial transform bound two and rapid decay of $\widehat\phi(\Delta\xi)$, since $\Delta Y^{1-\gamma/2}=Y^{\gamma/2}$. Choosing enough integrations by parts makes this squared integral $o(1)$ as well. Plancherel bounds the density in $L^2$, and its spatial support has length at most three, proving (2).

## Return to the true product sources

Write $M=\prod_i p_i$, so $Y\le M\le e^2Y$. Suppose $D$ is odd with $\log(2D)\le\lambda\log Y$. For any fixed orbit event $F$ of ordinary density $d$, and more generally uniformly for the whole component unit ball when the physical ordinary law converges in TV, equations (2) and the counted-fusion input give
\[
     |\mathbb E F(DM)-d|
             \le C\{\sqrt{r(\lambda+\gamma)}+r\sqrt\lambda\}
                     +o_{r,\theta,\gamma}(1).                            \tag{3}
\]
Here the absolute $C$ is independent of $r,\lambda,\gamma$. Uniformity of this leading constant will matter as the number of allowed large primes increases.

Take the binary comparison precision $t=\lfloor(\log_2Y)/(4r)\rfloor$. The law of $M$ in each binary class modulo $Q=2^t$ has an upper bound $C/Q$. Indeed fix all but a prime factor with largest lower endpoint $P_i\ge Y^{1/r}$. Their product permutes the odd classes; the sampled prime has normalized weight at most $Cr\log P_i/P_i$. Brun–Titchmarsh in an interval of length $O(P_i/r)$ gives at most $C P_i/(rQ\log P_i)$ primes in the specified odd class: $Q\le P_i^{1/4}$ and $r$ is fixed while $Y$ grows. Thus no exponential-in-$r$ loss occurs. The shortened log intervals were chosen to keep the entire product in $[Y,e^2Y]$. [Montgomery–Vaughan, explicit Brun–Titchmarsh](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300004708) · [Arbitrary-offset counted binary cylinders, Paper 209/v1, Theorem 4.2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/209)

Smooth the source $M$ to $\lfloor Me^v\rfloor$, with $v$ drawn independently from $\phi_\Delta$. For each integer $j\ge0$ the conditional chance that this displacement equals $j$ is at most $C/Y^\gamma$, uniformly in $M$, and the displacement is at most $CY^\gamma$. For each *fixed* $j>0$, comparison of sources $DM,D(M+j)$ fails on at most $O(\sqrt{(1+\log(Dj))/t})$ of the binary cells, because $D$ is odd. Multiplying by the class upper bound and summing with the preceding conditional displacement bound gives $C\sqrt{r(\lambda+\gamma)}+o(1)$. For a fixed odd visit target, all intervening states before the comparison date exceed it for large $Y$. This argument does not assume that the source-dependent rounded displacement has an independent cell law.

By (2) we may now take the smoothed reference density $g=\rho*\phi_\Delta$ in place of the actual smoothed product. It has total mass one, support in $[0,3]$, $\|g\|_\infty\le Cr$ and $\|g'\|_1\le Cr$; one normalized factor has these bounds and convolution cannot increase them. For $u=\lfloor Ye^s\rfloor$ under $g(s)ds$, its point probabilities are at most $Cr/Y$ and their total variation as a sequence is $O(r/Y)$ (integrate the variation of $g(\log(x/Y))/x$). Compare $F(Du)$ with $F(Du+i)$, $0\le i<D$, this time using $t'=\lfloor(\log_2Y)/10\rfloor$. Periodicity and the weight upper bound give error $Cr\sqrt\lambda+o_r(1)$, with the harmless $\log 2/\log Y$ retained when $D$ is bounded. The averaged blocks tile an interval of size $O(DY)$ with weights of total variation $O(r/(DY))$. Partial summation and the ordinary density of $F$ give $d+o_r(1)$ there, uniform for all $D$ and all component tests with the stated TV input. This proves (3).

## Removing the artificial prime boxes

Fix an error tolerance and auxiliary constants $K_0>1$, $0<a<1$. Choose $0<\delta<a$ (all chosen before $z\to\infty$). Under the preceding signature law, discard histories with $\log S_w>K_0\log(2w)$, at cost $O(1/K_0)$. Add the independent band $(w,z^\delta]$ to the earlier multiplier and discard increments with log larger than $K_0\delta\log z$, also at cost $O(1/K_0)$. This is permitted since $w<z^\delta$ eventually. Remove the dyadic part of the resulting multiplier, which preserves every event used in (1). On all retained conditional histories $\log(2D)\le3K_0\delta\log z$ for large $z$.

The expected number of distinct primes above $z^\delta$ is $\log(1/\delta)+o(1)$. Exponents larger than one there have total probability $o_\delta(1)$. Discard outcomes with more than $K$ such primes at cost $O((1+\log(1/\delta))/K)$. Also discard those with no prime above $z^a$, whose probability $V(z)/V(z^a)\to a$. Conditional on $r\le K$ large prime factors, their squarefree product has weights proportional to $1/(p_1\cdots p_r)$ on unordered distinct tuples. Order them at random. Partition each prime smoothly into the logarithmic intervals used above, discarding crossing intervals at $z^\delta,z^a,z$. Such intervals have total reciprocal prime mass $o_\delta(1)$ by the prime number theorem/Mertens at any of the three fixed powers (or just the upper sieve in proportional intervals). In each ordered list of full intervals the distinct-prime restriction changes the independent product distribution by $o_{K,\delta}(1)$ uniformly: each atom of a factor is at most $C K\log z/z^\delta$, and there are finitely many pairs. Uniform errors over boxes survive mixing with their actual total probability.

For every retained box list one lower endpoint is $\gg z^a$, all lower endpoints are $\gg z^\delta$, and $Y\le z^K$. Thus $\theta=\delta/(2K)$ works for large $z$, and $\lambda\le 4K_0\delta/a$. Apply (3) with $\gamma=\delta$ (taking $\delta<1/2$). The leading error is
\[
 O\!\left(\sqrt{K(K_0\delta/a+\delta)}+K\sqrt{K_0\delta/a}\right),
\]
with an absolute constant. First take $K_0$ large and $a$ small, then take $\delta$ sufficiently small and $K=\lceil(1+\log(1/\delta))/a\rceil$. The discarded mass and the remaining error are arbitrarily small since $\delta(1+\log(1/\delta))^2\to0$. Taking $z\to\infty$ with those fixed choices proves (1), uniformly on the original retained histories. All tests in the full-component version may themselves depend on that earlier history; the absolute constants and the ordinary-law TV input are uniform. No passage to the full infinite prime factorization of an ordinary source is used.

## Exact cutoff frequencies and prescribed consecutive prime additions

Take any fixed increasing sequence $\log z_{j-1}=o(\log z_j)$, $z_j\to\infty$. The conditional estimate (1) and bounded martingale differences give empirical frequencies along the deterministic cutoffs $S_{z_j}$: for every fixed finite word of prescribed positive-density orbit events, the fraction of consecutive cutoff windows realizing it tends in $L^1$ to the product of the indicated ordinary densities. For a proof multiply the final event error after conditioning on all previous bands by the indicator of the earlier word; its mean absolute conditional error tends to zero, and the centered terms have variance at most the number of summands. Induct on the word length. In ordinary octaves use all cutoffs through the last $k$ with $\log z_k\le\log X/\log\log X$, then project the single full signature at that cutoff. For example $\log z_j=j!$ gives $k\sim\log\log X/\log\log\log X$. The no-RH full-band proof previously required $\log z_{j-1}=o((\log z_j)^{1/200})$ for this **exact frequency** limit.

There is a different consequence at individual, consecutive prime additions. Given any finite word $E_1,\ldots,E_h$ of component or odd-target events with ordinary densities $d_i>0$, a density-one set of ordinary integers has at least $c_{E_1,\ldots,E_h}\omega(n)$ mutually disjoint runs of **consecutive individual prime additions** realizing it. Here a run of $h$ prime additions has $h$ marked resulting sites. Each of its added primes occurs to exponent exactly one. In particular each directed transition between the known disjoint predecessor events of $5$ and $85$ occurs at a positive fraction of the actual individual prime links on density-typical ordinary integers. Under a second-cycle hypothesis this also holds between the two distinct cycle basins. This result asserts positive frequencies for actual consecutive factors, without equating their frequencies to independent products.

To prove it fix $K_0=10$ and take $\log z_j=R^j$ for a sufficiently large **fixed** $R$ depending on the finite word, as in the no-RH positive-fraction proof. In each band $(w,z]$, $w=z^{1/R}$, impose the event that its increment is *exactly* one prime $p\in(\sqrt z,z]$. Its probability is
\[
                \frac{V(z)}{V(w)}\sum_{\sqrt z<p\le z}\frac1p
                         \longrightarrow \frac{\log2}{R}>0.
\]
Given this event the prime population has harmonic weights $1/p$. Smoothly box it at fixed ratio as above, discarding endpoint intervals of conditional mass $o_R(1)$. At any retained history $\log d_0\le K_0\log w$, remove the dyadic part of $d_0$ and apply the single-factor case $r=1$ of (3) with $\lambda\le3K_0/R$ and $\gamma=1/R$. Taking $R$ sufficiently large leaves a conditional probability at least $d_i/2$ for any prescribed $E_i$, relative to the one-prime event. Each such update keeps the next history good by $\log(d_0p)\le(1+K_0/R)\log z$. In $h$ consecutive bands, the desired word of actual individual additions therefore has conditional probability bounded below by a positive constant on all sufficiently late good block starts. For disjoint blocks the good-start frequency follows from the covariance estimate in the no-RH proof, and the conditional block errors obey the bounded martingale-difference law. Transfer the resulting number of successful blocks by the single full-signature TV approximation; the available number of blocks is $\asymp_{R,h}\log\log X$. Since there are no intervening prime factors inside any of the $h$ bands, the selected factors are consecutive on the true canonical chain. The elementary size restriction $\omega(n)\sim\log\log n$ in probability finishes the claim.

The prior RH source theorem already samples a future mark at a prime, and Paper 233 also gives independence along the ordered factors of squarefree integers with a **fixed** factor count. The results here concern a complete geometric band with arbitrarily many earlier prime factors, and a positive fraction of consecutive links of an ordinary integer whose factor count itself grows.
