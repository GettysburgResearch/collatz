# Coalescing Brownian grade paths at the diffusive smooth-multiplier scale

Let $N\to\infty$. Fix finitely many positive odd base multipliers $A_i$, normalized to $3\nmid A_i$ by absorbing their fixed powers of three into the exponents. Let $e_{i,N}\ge0$ be integers with $e_{i,N}/\sqrt N\to u_i$ and let $b_{i,N}$ be ordinary integers satisfying $\max_i\log(2+|b_{i,N}|)=o(N)$. At a common Haar $n\in\mathbb Z_2$ use the genuine shortcut inputs
\[
                   x_i=A_i3^{e_{i,N}}n+b_{i,N}.
\]
Interpolate the paths $Z_i^N$ defined at dates $m/N$ by
\[
           Z_i^N(m/N)=\frac{2(e_{i,N}+k_m(x_i))-m}{\sqrt N}.
\]
On every fixed bounded time interval these paths converge jointly to the following finite system. Each particle starts at $2u_i$ and runs as a standard real Brownian motion. Distinct currently unjoined particles have independent Brownian increments. Within a common base $A_i=A_j$, two particles join at their first meeting and subsequently have the identical path. Particles of different bases remain independent even when their real values cross. Particles at the same initial point with the same base begin joined in the limit. Equivalently this is an independent collection, indexed by the distinct bases, of finite systems of coalescing Brownian motions. One can construct it by independently running all representatives to their first within-base collision, identifying collided particles, then repeating with independent Brownian increments for the representatives.

For a common base and $u_i\ne u_j$, let $T_{ij}^N$ be the first true equal-state date of the two physical inputs, allowing unequal initial grades. Then for any $s>0$,
\[
            \Pr\{T_{ij}^N\le sN\}\longrightarrow
                  2\Phi\!\left(-\frac{\sqrt2\,|u_i-u_j|}{\sqrt s}\right).    \tag{1}
\]
For equal $u_i=u_j$ the limit is one. Different normalized bases have probability zero of equal-state fusion at any finite date under Haar. For a uniform ordinary source $n\in[X,2X)$, the path law through $0\le m\le N$ and (1) for $s\le1$ hold if $2^N=o(X)$. For another fixed horizon $s$ require $2^{\lfloor sN\rfloor}=o(X)$ when it exceeds $N$. These conclusions concern the genuine date and states on the initial binary source horizon; paths starting from enormous smooth exponents outside the diffusive range require different normalization. Positive ordinary sources at all affine coordinates are understood, as holds automatically under the stated octave growth and subexponential offsets.

[Paper 13, Theorems 3–8](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/13/v1) studies physical durations of individual interior mixed-clock excursions and moving $3^{h+1}x+c$ pairs under gap survival or its complement. It proves strong conditional clock deviations, couplings across offsets, and a Brownian lower comparison for simultaneous families of consecutive starts, explicitly crediting Leen's affine-line occupation rigidity and physical fusion rates. Here both centred ordinary grade paths are kept jointly through true equal-state fusion, including an arbitrary finite collection and the independent nonsmooth classes. We use the published moving-*translation* counted synchronization estimate in the passage from aligned slopes to true state equality.

[PR 214, ordinary affine interval profile](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/1e0fb541d2439dcb2d7162cce6462405b66e1b47/research/astra-critical-rigidity-20261128/literature/ORDINARY_AFFINE_INTERVAL_PROFILE.md) already gives exactly the scalar probability (1) for the pair $x,3^s x+D$, including the full binary cutoff on every interval and subexponential normalized offset. Its [tower-family theorem](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/1e0fb541d2439dcb2d7162cce6462405b66e1b47/research/astra-critical-rigidity-20261128/literature/CRITICAL_TOWER_FAMILIES.md) also treats growing marked sets of tower exponents and the mean actual tower fibre size. Our scalar formula is recorded as a consequence and consistency check. The further statement here is the full joint path-valued limit through coalescence for finitely many genuine physical grades, with independent families from nonsmooth base classes.

## The native clocks up to a first slope resonance

Let $K_i(m)=e_{i,N}+k_m(x_i)$. With the common binary source prefix filtration the next increments of the centred grades are a predictable sign vector or its negative, with equal probability, since every native affine coefficient is odd. Therefore the centred $Z_i^N$ are martingales at the physical grid points. Their diagonal predictable bracket increments are $1/N$ and their off-diagonal increments are $1/N$ times the actual product of the departure signs. Individual fair-walk fourth-moment bounds give joint tightness and allow all stopped martingale/bracket identities to pass to weak limits, using bounded past tests first and a monotone class. The jumps vanish uniformly.

For different base multipliers the [quantitative block joining](PRECRITICAL_PAIR_WORDS.md) applies on Haar directly with initial slope ratio $(A_j/A_i)3^{e_{j,N}-e_{i,N}}$. Its estimates are uniform in this power shift and the intercepts. It gives vanishing off-diagonal brackets uniformly in physical time on each compact rescaled interval. For example apply it to finitely many deterministic grid endpoints bounded away from zero and use the $1$-Lipschitz bound on the rescaled brackets, then refine the grid.

For a fixed common base define $\tau_{ij}=\inf\{m:K_i(m)=K_j(m)\}$, the first slope resonance, allowing zero. The off-diagonal bracket up to $m\wedge\tau_{ij}$ is $o_{\Pr}(1)$ after the $1/N$ scaling, uniformly on compact rescaled time intervals. Here is the stopped occupation argument; a nonsmooth fixed-slope theorem cannot simply be applied at the resonant level. Before resonance, each mixed departure moves the exponent difference $d=K_j-K_i$ by a fair sign, and at all other physical dates $d$ stays fixed. Complete the simple walk with independent signs after the first zero. A live nonzero level has its physical hold bounded in conditional mean by $2+v(d)$, with centered fluctuation a bounded-exponential-moment geometric or zero, as in the native calculation, where
\[
                         v(d)=v_2(3^d-1),\quad d\ne0.
\]
Set $v(0)=0$. Over $O(N)$ mixed steps starting at $O(\sqrt N)$ it is bounded by $C\log(2+N)$, by the order of three and the lifting-of-exponent identity. At nonzero levels its high valuation events are the residue classes $d\equiv0\bmod2^{l-2}$ when $l\ge3$, with zero itself omitted. For upper bounds reinsert zero into the truncated periodic majorants and their high-class counts.

Fix $0<\eta<K$ and any physical horizon $m$ with $\eta N\le m\le KN$ and $m\le\tau_{ij}$. The live time spent in a given direction cell before $m$ is bounded by the sum over at most $\lceil KN\rceil$ mixed visits of its nonnegative live holding times. Centered live fluctuations stopped at resonance have exponential tail. Upper bound their conditional means by the corresponding truncated periodic sums over the *completed* walk plus its high-level excess. As in the quantitative joining proof, use binary direction levels $l$ fixed, valuation cutoff $5\log_2\log(3+N)$, a period-polylogarithmic Poisson solution and Azuma. Uniformly over all the above $m$, with probability tending to one each depth-$l$ direction cell has empirical mass at most
\[
                    C(K/\eta)(l+3)2^{-l}+\delta
\]
for any fixed $\delta>0$ and finitely many tested levels. The division by the prefix length costs only $1/(\eta N)$. No holding time at the resonant slope has been charged. The bound covers an unfinished interval because its nonnegative complete live holding time is included; if its endpoint is the resonance itself, the departure just before that resonance is still in the preceding live interval.

At every fixed binary residue depth the empirical pair-state law through such a prefix is uniformly close to the mixture of its true conditional line laws. Split the fixed-length lookahead deviations into their residue classes of dates as in the earlier proof. The maximal bounded-martingale inequality controls all partial sums up to $KN$, even when only a random subset of prefix lengths is later retained. The underlying physical paths exist after resonance, so no fictitious continuation of their state-cylinder tests is needed. The dyadic line incidence bound and finite stationarity-of-window argument give full two-symbol pair-word frequencies up to an arbitrary fixed tolerance on every retained prefix; the constant $K/\eta$ in the cell cap adds an $O(\log(K/\eta))$ loss to long-block entropy, removed by taking a large fixed block. In particular the mean product of the two departure signs tends to zero uniformly along these prefixes. For prefixes shorter than $\eta N$, their normalized bracket has magnitude at most $\eta$. Send $\eta$ down to zero. This proves the stopped bracket statement.

## True integer state fusion at resonance

At the random physical prefix $\tau=\tau_{ij}\le KN$, both slopes in the common unread Haar tail are equal. Their state difference $h$ is thus a fixed ordinary integer on that prefix. Write $k_i=k_\tau(x_i)$. In the native identity
\[
 T^m(A_i3^{e_i}n+b_i)=
    2^{-m}A_i3^{K_i(m)}n+2^{-m}3^{k_m(x_i)}b_i+D_{i,m},
 \qquad D_{i,m}=2^{-m}3^{k_m(x_i)}R_m(x_i)
\]
the common $n$-coefficient cancels at resonance. The nonnegative real $R_m$ uses just the actual finite parity word as in [the native fibre proof](ORDINARY_SOURCE_FIBRES.md). As identities one may evaluate them first at an ordinary lift of the fixed source prefix. Under the unconditioned Haar source each individual coordinate has fair iid parities despite the moving odd multiplier. The reversed-drift fractional moment estimate from that proof gives $\max_{m\le KN}D_{i,m}\le N^C$ outside a probability tending to zero, with a suitably large fixed $C$. Also $\max_{m\le KN}2^{-m}3^{k_m(x_i)}\le N^C$ with high probability: a small positive fractional power has mean decaying geometrically in $m$ by the negative fair real log drift, so sum the tail bounds. The uniform bounds apply to the stopped prefixes without conditioning on the value of $\tau$. Thus at any observed first resonance $\log(2+|h|)\le o(N)$ with probability tending to one, as the fixed intercept bound is subexponential in $N$. More explicitly choose a deterministic $r_N=o(N)$ larger than twice the input intercept log and $C'\log N$. The chance of a first resonance by $KN$ with $\log(2+|h|)>r_N$ tends to zero.

Conditioned on each resonant cylinder, one current state is uniform Haar because its slope in the fresh common tail is odd. The other is its fixed translate by $h$. For every integer $h$, the ordinary counted synchronization input [arbitrary-displacement estimate used in the component proof](ALL_SEMIPRIME_COMPONENTS.md) bounds failure of equal-state, equal-*additional*-grade fusion within $u$ further physical dates by $C\sqrt{\log(2+|h|)/(1+u)}$, capped by one. Applying this on the good resonant prefixes with $u=\lfloor\epsilon N\rfloor$, and summing their conditional probabilities, gives an $o(1)$ failure for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Once such a fusion has occurred both physical states and formal grades $K_i,K_j$ agree forever. Hence the time from first resonance to permanent joint fusion is $o_{\Pr}(N)$ on every bounded observation horizon. The centred grade increments agree after fusion, so the off-diagonal predictable bracket satisfies, uniformly for $s\le K$,
\[
     \langle Z_i^N,Z_j^N\rangle_s
                =(s-\tau_{ij}/N)_++o_{\Pr}(1).                           \tag{2}
\]
A resonance at the observation boundary creates at most the length of the short unfinished interval in this equation. Equality of states at unequal slopes occurs at only one two-adic point per prefix, so the true state meeting date is almost surely between first resonance and the subsequent counted fusion, unless all three dates are infinite.

## Identification of the joint limit

Take a subsequence along which all the tight paths, their brackets and the capped times $\tau_{ij}/N$ converge jointly; use successive compact horizons if needed. Denote a limiting common-base resonance time by $\rho$. If the initial exponents are ordered, their difference cannot cross zero before the first resonance, since each step changes the integer gap by at most one. If limiting starts are equal, choose a further subsequence with a fixed initial ordering. In the limit the stopped gap accordingly stays nonnegative until $\rho$, and is zero there when $\rho$ is finite. After $\rho$ it remains zero: the physical delay is $o_{\Pr}(N)$ and the uniform modulus of continuity of the tight grade martingales makes their extra separation during that delay $o_{\Pr}(1)$. This modulus argument is needed; a mere bound on the *number* of delayed steps would not bound a square-root-scaled difference by itself.

The continuous limiting difference is a nonnegative martingale, by its pre-resonance order and post-resonance equality. Its bracket has rate $2$ until $\rho$ and zero afterward by (2). In particular it cannot reach zero at a strictly earlier time with positive probability: a nonnegative martingale stays at zero after its first zero by optional sampling and conditional nonnegativity, which would contradict the positive bracket rate on that extra interval. This also forces $\rho=0$ for an equal limiting initial level. The martingale assertions may be taken in the natural joint path and bracket filtration: their finite conditional identities involve only past paths/brackets and pass under fourth-moment uniform integrability. The possible inclusion of additional stopped times as coordinates does not require anticipating their future values; the bracket process in (2) already identifies the time as its change point and the path argument identifies it with the ordinary first zero.

Thus $\rho$ is exactly the first limiting path meeting for each common-base pair, and all its coordinates stay together thereafter. Different bases have vanishing cross brackets for all times. Up to the first possible within-base meeting, the distinct representatives therefore have identity covariance bracket: by Lévy's characterization they are independent Brownian motions stopped on meeting. One justification is to append independent standard Brownian continuations from that stopping time before invoking the usual characterization. Conditional on the stopped history, the remaining distinct representatives have the same characterization up to their next within-base meeting. There are at most $r-1$ identifications after collapsing coincident initial levels, so induction gives uniqueness of the stated coalescing system and hence convergence of the full sequence. If meetings occur simultaneously, identify every resulting cluster at that time before the next step.

Finally two independent standard Brownian motions starting distance $2|u_i-u_j|$ apart have a difference of variance rate two. The reflection principle gives their probability of meeting by $s$ as (1); its distribution has no positive-time atom. Convergence of the resonance and negligible physical fusion delay proves the formula for true ordinary state equality. At Haar-different bases actual equality requires a single exceptional common-tail value at some finite date and has mass zero. In an ordinary octave binary-word tests and counted fusion through date $N$ transfer by prefix discrepancy $O(2^N/X)$. To check *accidental* unequal-slope integer meetings as well, apply the final-date affine-line identity: every such meeting leaves the two physical states equal at date $N$ and keeps their slope ratio different forever, since both subsequent grades increase together. At most one ordinary source per depth-$N$ cell can do this, for total mass $O(2^N/X)$. This completes the physical transfer without substituting a fixed-displacement estimate at the moving resonance.
