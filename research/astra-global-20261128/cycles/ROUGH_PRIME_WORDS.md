# Prescribed consecutive prime-factor fates from ordinary character sums

Fix a finite word $F_1,\ldots,F_h$ of eventual-component unions or fixed odd-target visit events with ordinary natural densities $d_1,\ldots,d_h>0$. List the distinct prime factors of an integer in increasing order and form the partial products of their entire powers. For some $c>0$, with natural probability tending to one there are at least $c\omega(n)$ disjoint strings of $h$ consecutive prime additions whose successive resulting sites satisfy the prescribed word. Each prime in a selected string has exponent one. The result uses the counted-pair synchronization and the indicated ordinary-density premises, and uses **no Riemann hypothesis**. In particular the two directed $5,85$ visit patterns already exhibited by the RH proof both have positive lower frequency on genuine prime links of typical integers.

The proof uses an auxiliary probability law dominated by a fixed multiple of the true geometric-band law. Its preparatory factors are rough integers. They allow binary equidistribution deeper than the binary length of any individual appended prime. At each appended prime, earlier orbit labels can act as an arbitrary bounded weight on the preparatory product. The unrestricted smoothing estimate for a prime with its earlier product fixed is stronger and still uses RH in the all-box paper.

## A binary upper bound for products of rough integers

Write $Z\to\infty$. Fix a real $s>3$ and set $Q=2^{\lfloor s\log_2 Z\rfloor}$. There is a constant $c_s>0$ with the following property. Fix $K\ge1$. Take any smooth nonnegative rescaled bump supported on $[P,e^{2/K}P]$, with lower endpoint $Z^{1/3}\le P\le Z^{1/2}$, shape $W(K\log(n/P))$ for a fixed compactly supported smooth $W$ of positive integral. Restrict harmonic weight $W(K\log(n/P))/n$ to odd integers with all prime factors exceeding $w=Z^\varepsilon$. Let $\mu_P$ be the resulting probability measure. For any fixed $R$, all sufficiently small fixed $\varepsilon>0$ give a nonnegative dominating measure $\mu_P^+$ on odd residue classes such that
\[
      \|\mu_P^+\|\le1+1/R,\qquad
      |\widehat{\mu_P^+}(\chi)|\ll_{K,R,s,\varepsilon} Z^{-c_s}
                \quad(\chi\ne1\text{ on }(\mathbb Z/Q)^\times).
                                                                  \tag{1}
\]
Here the exponent can be fixed before choosing $K,R,\varepsilon$.

Use upper and lower fundamental-lemma sieve weights at level $Z^{1/20}$ against the odd primes up to $w$. Their coefficients have absolute value at most one and the upper divisor sum majorizes the rough indicator pointwise. Restrict all measures to odd integers. The harmonic mass of a box of actual rough integers is between $(1\pm e(\varepsilon)+o(1))V(w)(\int W)/K$, where $e(\varepsilon)\to0$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The majorant using the upper divisor sum has the same upper bound. Indeed, summing the rescaled bump harmonically over multiples of an odd sieve divisor $d$ and odd inputs gives the ordinary integral divided by $2d$ with an error $O_K(1/P)$, uniformly up to the sieve level. The odd-prime product is $2V(w)$. The support-summed error is $O_K(Z^{1/20}/P)$ and the fundamental-lemma ratio is $(20\varepsilon)^{-1}$. Normalize the upper measure by the exact true box mass. This is positive and gives the mass claim in (1) on choosing $e(\varepsilon)$ small relative to $1/R$. [Ford, *Sieve methods*, Theorem 3.6(a)](https://ford126.web.illinois.edu/sieve2023.pdf)

For the second assertion of (1), use Banks–Shparlinski's Theorem 2.1 with zero polynomial phase on the primitive character inducing $\chi$. Its conductor $q$ is a power of two, hence its core is two. When its exponent is sufficiently large it meets the prime-power hypothesis (2.1). For every $Z^{1/10}\le N\le q$ and $q\le Z^s$, the theorem gives $|\sum_{I}\chi(m)|\ll_s N Z^{-c'_s}$ on every interval of length $N$, with $c'_s>0$ depending only on $s$. Indeed $\log q/\log N\le10s$ and the lower bound in (2.8) is a constant power of the core. The character induced to $Q$ agrees at every integer with the primitive character since both have precisely the even integers as their nonunits. For intervals longer than $q$, complete periods cancel. The last remainder of length below $Z^{1/10}$ is paid trivially; this also handles all bounded conductors. Thus every partial interval inside the full $m$-range of a divisor $d\le Z^{1/20}$ gives
\[
       \left|\sum_{m\in I}\chi(m)\right|
           \ll_{K,s} (P/d)Z^{-c''_s}
\]
for some $c''_s>0$. The trivial-bound transition uses $P/d\ge Z^{1/3-1/20}$. Partial summation and the factor $1/d$ in $1/(dm)$ now give $O_{K,s}(Z^{-c''_s}/d)$ per divisor in the original unnormalized harmonic character sum. Summation costs $O(\log Z)$ and normalization another $O_K(\log Z)$. Decreasing the exponent gives (1). [Banks and Shparlinski, Theorem 2.1, equations (2.1) and (2.8)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.07553)

Choose $R>6s+10$ so large that $R c_s>2s$. For any $R$ independent such rough-integer boxes the chance that their product belongs to any specified odd residue modulo $Q$ is at most $C/Q$ for an **absolute** $C$, for all sufficiently large $Z$ with the fixed parameters. Indeed, convolve the $R$ positive majorants multiplicatively and use character inversion. The trivial-character contribution is at most $(1+1/R)^R/\varphi(Q)$. The remaining contribution is $O_{K,R,s,\varepsilon}(Z^{-Rc_s})=o(1/Q)$. Convolving with independent odd variables preserves this upper, and weighting a sample by any earlier indicator bounded by one only decreases each class mass.

## An honest subpopulation of the geometric band

Choose disjoint fixed prime ranges $[Z^{a_j},Z^{b_j}]$ for $1\le j\le h$ with $1/2<a_1<b_1<a_2<\cdots<b_h<1$ (for one range just require $1/2<a_1<b_1<1$). The full true increment on primes in $(w,Z]$ has probability $V(Z)/(V(w)m)$ at every integer $m$ composed of these primes, including repeated factors. Independently choose $R$ preparatory rough integers in $[Z^{1/3},Z^{1/2}]$ and one tagged prime from each of the $h$ ranges, using the following probability measures. Set $K=R+h$. In each range choose uniformly a grid box $[e^{l/K},e^{(l+2)/K}]$ fully contained in that range, then sample by the normalized harmonic bump inside it, restricted to rough integers or primes as appropriate. There are $\asymp_{K,h}\log Z$ such boxes per range, and each integer belongs to at most three boxes. The normalizing rough-box mass is $\gg_K1/\log Z$ by the sieve estimate above. The prime-box mass is $\gg_{K,h}1/\log Z$ by the ordinary PNT. Consequently each unconditioned atom is at most $C_{K,h,\varepsilon}/n$ for an eligible integer $n$.

The resulting auxiliary product law satisfies
\[
             \mu_{\rm aux}(m)\le C_{R,h,\varepsilon}/m
                  \le C'_{R,h,\varepsilon}\Pr(M_{w,Z}=m).               \tag{2}
\]
Every prime in an auxiliary product lies in $(w,Z]$, and $\Omega(m)\le(R+h)/\varepsilon$. The number of ordered representations into the prescribed $R+h$ slots is therefore bounded by $(R+h)^{(R+h)/\varepsilon}$, by assigning labelled prime occurrences to the slots. Multiplication of the atom uppers gives the first inequality. Mertens gives $V(Z)/V(w)\to\varepsilon$ for the second. The constant need only be finite after all parameters are fixed. All preparatory prime factors precede the first tag. The tags are exactly the last $h$ distinct prime factors of the increment, occur to exponent one, and are consecutive on the true ascending prime-power chain independent of which representation of the preparatory product is used.

## Retaining the earlier marks while appending one prime

Let $D_0$ be the original geometric signature through $w$. Work on a good earlier history $\log D_0\le 10\log w$. Remove the dyadic part of $D_0$, which preserves the stated events. Condition on all auxiliary grid box choices. For the $j$th tag let $N$ be the product of the lower endpoints in the $R+j-1$ earlier boxes, $P$ its own lower endpoint, and $Y=NP$. Thus $Z^{R/3}\le N$, $P\le Z$, and all partial product supports have upper-to-lower ratio at most $e^2$.

Weight the previous samples by the product of the first $j-1$ prescribed orbit indicators at their actual partial products times $D_0$. The resulting subprobability of the earlier product has weights $b_n\ge0$, $\sum b_n=B\le1$, on $[N,e^2N]$, satisfying
\[
                \max b_n\ll_{R,h,\varepsilon}(\log Z)^{R+h}/N .
\]
Indeed after fixing the boxes each individual atom costs $O_{K,h,\varepsilon}(\log Z)/n$ and ordering multiplicity is bounded exactly as in (2). No independence from the earlier marked path is imposed.

Convolve the earlier log-product subprobability with the harmonic prime law in the current box. Its smoothed density on log scale, with width $\Delta=Z^2/Y$, differs in $L^1$ by $o_{R,h,s,\varepsilon}(1)$ from replacing **only the new prime** by the smooth continuous log reference, then applying the same kernel. To see this, take frequency ceiling $T=Y/Z^{3/2}\le N$. The elementary Dirichlet mean-value estimate for $\sum b_n n^{-it}$ on $[-T,T]$ is $O_{R,h,\varepsilon}((\log Z)^{R+h+1})$, by the atom upper and the harmonic off-diagonal sum in the occupation paper. At $|t|\le(\log Z)^A$ the ordinary PNT compares the prime with its continuous reference with arbitrary logarithmic accuracy. From there up to $T$, the new normalized prime transform and the reference transform have an arbitrary fixed logarithmic saving by increasing $A$, using the Vinogradov–Korobov estimate. Here $P\ge Z^{1/2}$, $T\le Z^{R+h}$ and all parameters are fixed before $Z\to\infty$. Thus their squared difference times the earlier polynomial has integral $o(1)$. Beyond $T$, $\Delta T=Z^{1/2}$ and arbitrarily rapid kernel decay pays the whole integral. Plancherel and the uniformly bounded log support prove the claim. The new continuous density has mass $B$, sup norm and total variation $O(K)$, even for an arbitrarily labelled earlier submeasure. [Prime transform and elementary mean value](PRIME_BAND_OCCUPATION.md) · [Matomäki et al., Lemma 3.2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-026-01408-6)

The initial source smoothing displaces the integer product by at most $CZ^2$, each rounded displacement with conditional probability at most $C/Z^2$, uniformly in its value. Apply the genuine counted-merger estimate at $t=\lfloor s\log_2 Z\rfloor$ to each fixed displacement, using the absolute class upper just proved for the **unweighted** full product. The original source multiplier $D_0$ is odd. The total error is $O(\sqrt{(2+10\varepsilon)/s})+o(1)$, with absolute constant. The least source is $\gg Z^{R/3}$, so the cleared merger applies before a visit to any fixed target. For the smoothed continuous comparison tile the $D_0$ residue offsets as in the preceding prime-band proof, with binary precision $\lfloor(\log_2Y)/10\rfloor$. The density variation $O(K)$ and $\log D_0\le10\varepsilon\log Z$ give $O(K\sqrt{\varepsilon/R})+o(1)$. Ordinary natural density and partial summation evaluate the tiled law at $B d_j+o(1)$. All limiting errors are uniform over good $D_0$, choices of boxes, and the particular earlier label submeasure. Consequently the new word weight differs from $d_j$ times the previous word weight by at most
\[
            C\left(s^{-1/2}+K\sqrt{\varepsilon/R}\right)
                       +o_{R,h,s,\varepsilon}(1),                         \tag{3}
\]
for $\varepsilon\le1/10$, with an absolute $C$. [Physical displacement and full component or odd-target comparison](PRIME_BAND_MIXING.md)

Choose $s$ so large that $hCs^{-1/2}<\prod d_j/8$, then choose $R$ as above and $K=R+h$. Finally choose $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small both for (1) and for $hCK\sqrt{\varepsilon/R}<\prod d_j/8$. Induction in (3) gives conditional auxiliary success probability at least $\prod d_j/2$ for all large $Z$ and all good previous histories. Equation (2) therefore gives a fixed positive lower success probability on that same event for the true geometric band. In particular the Fourier step has not asserted a short-interval prime estimate under an exact fixed previous product.

## From individual bands to the ordinary chain

Use stages $\log Z_j=(1/\varepsilon)^j$ so the preceding cutoff is $w=Z_{j-1}$. The fraction of good starting histories stays bounded below with probability tending to one. In detail $L_j=\log S_{Z_j}/\log Z_j$ has mean $1+o(1)$, bounded second moment, and $|\operatorname{Cov}(L_i,L_j)|\ll \varepsilon^{j-i}$ for $i<j$ by independence of increments. Thus stage averages converge to one in $L^2$, and at most a fraction $(1+o_{\Pr}(1))/10$ can have $L_i>10$. Bounded martingale differences show that the proportion of successful bands differs from the average of their conditional probabilities by $o_{\Pr}(1)$. Each success uses disjoint true prime additions within its own band. Transfer at the last cutoff $\log Z_j\le\log X/\log\log X$ by the full-signature TV approximation. There are $\asymp_\varepsilon\log\log X$ stages; $\omega(n)/\log\log X\to1$ in ordinary octave probability. This proves the stated positive lower fraction. [Good histories and signature transfer](CANONICAL_PRIME_VARIATION.md)

The proof gives lower frequencies depending on the prescribed word. It does not evaluate the exact joint distribution on adjacent links or the distribution of a single unmultiplied prime. The single-site empirical limit on the whole canonical chain follows independently from the typical-band occupation theorem.
