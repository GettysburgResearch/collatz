# A bounded-height parity law with all local integral parent tests

The positive-entropy rotation example in [the annulus theorem](ANNULUS_SPECTRAL_FACTOR.md) extends to the dyadic **and** ternary arithmetic of the ordinary shortcut map. In fact it is a local limit of packets of true positive integer trajectories with all their actual parent branches. This identifies a global arithmetic condition still missing from an argument based on those local data.

Let $M>1$ be arbitrary. There are heights $H_L\to\infty$ and packets of disjoint positive integer shortcut segments of $L$ steps each. Write $P_L$ for the total number of selected dated states, omitting the terminal state of each segment from the root sampling. They have the following properties.

* All selected states lie in $[H_L/4,4H_L]$ for large $L$, and $H_L\le C_M P_L^M$.
* Root the packet uniformly. Its limiting parity process is stationary and ergodic, has strictly positive entropy, and has the irrational rotation factor of [equation (1)](ANNULUS_SPECTRAL_FACTOR.md). Here the empirical words are read from the genuine segment around each internal date; boundary dates have vanishing proportion in a fixed window.
* At every fixed distance from the root one may also observe the full directed ordinary graph, including every positive odd or even parent, finite residues at **both** primes, and each vertex height divided by $H_L$. All these observations converge jointly to the model below.

The individual segments are of logarithmic order in $H_L$ and have free endpoints. The packet does not produce one closed or similarly long connected segment of length $P_L$. In particular this construction does not supply a full-depth counterexample to the return-moment bound.

## An explicit stationary process

Take $t$ uniform mod one, $\alpha=\log_3 2$, and $t_i=\{t+i\alpha\}$. Set $b_i=\lfloor t_i+\alpha\rfloor$. At each occurrence $b_i b_{i+1}=01$ independently mark it with probability one half; write $E_i\in\{0,1\}$ for its mark, zero at other dates. The $01$ pairs are disjoint, so
\[
      u_i=b_i+E_i-E_{i-1}\in\{0,1\},\qquad
      F_i=-t_i+E_{i-1},\qquad F_{i+1}-F_i=u_i-\alpha.
\]
Thus $r_i=3^{F_i}\in[1/3,3]$ and $r_{i+1}=3^{u_i}r_i/2$ exactly. The process is an ergodic factor of the product of an irrational rotation and a Bernoulli shift. One elementary verification of product ergodicity uses the Fourier modes of the rotation: any invariant square-integrable function gives Bernoulli-shift eigenfunctions at the opposite rotation frequencies, and mixing leaves only the constant mode. Conditional on $t$, a block containing $k$ complete eligible pairs records $k$ independent fair choices. Their mean density is $1-\alpha>0$, hence the parity entropy rate is at least $(1-\alpha)\log2$. Bounded $F_i$ gives (1) by conditional medians on the parity process. No assertion that $t$ itself is read off from a prescribed finite word is needed.

## Both arithmetic coordinates and all parent branches

For every such two-sided word there is a unique $x_i\in\mathbb Z_2$ with forward shortcut parities $u_i,u_{i+1},\ldots$. For finite lengths the parity itinerary map is a bijection from residues modulo $2^n$ to binary words of length $n$: equality of first bits forces equality modulo two, and equal first bits give an image difference of $3^{u_i}(x-x')/2$. Induct on $n$, then take the compatible limit. This is the classical dyadic parity coding of the shortcut map. [Bernstein and Lagarias, *The 3x+1 conjugacy map*](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1996-060-x)

There is also a unique bounded ternary state $z_i\in\mathbb Z_3$ generated by the entire past. Compose $v\mapsto(3^{u_j}v+u_j)/2$ from any point of $\mathbb Z_3$ from time $i-n$ to time $i$. Two such outputs differing only before that start agree modulo $3^{\sum_{j=i-n}^{i-1}u_j}$. This exponent tends to infinity, since the perturbed word differs from the mechanical count on any interval by at most two. The limit exists, is independent of initialization, and is equivariant. Thus the model on triples $(x,z,r)\in\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_3\times(0,\infty)$ has forward law
\[
 (x,z,r)\longmapsto
   ((3^b x+b)/2,(3^b z+b)/2,3^b r/2),\qquad b=x\bmod2.
\]
Its even parent $(2x,2z,2r)$ is always present. Its odd parent $((2x-1)/3,(2z-1)/3,2r/3)$ is present exactly when $z\equiv2\bmod3$. These are all branches of the ordinary shortcut map; iterating gives the whole labelled backward tree. The real coordinate is a large-height normalization, so its affine correction is zero in the limit. The root distribution given by $(x_i,z_i,r_i)$ is invariant.

## Realization by ordinary integer segments

Fix a generic realization of this stationary process for its finite parity and residue cylinders and continuous bounded-height tests. The future dyadic coordinate is nonatomic: an atom in an ergodic stationary one-sided parity law would give a finite periodic orbit and entropy zero. Thus the generic realization may also be chosen with no dyadic coordinate equal to a rational number at any date.

Choose $C>\log(12)/(1-1/M)$ and $H_L=\lceil e^{CL}\rceil$, increasing $C$ if necessary so $H_L/4>6^L$ for large $L$. We realize many copies of the same generic prototype on dates $0,\ldots,L$. Choose the starting integer $s$ in $[H_Lr_0,H_Lr_0+H_L/L]$ with
\[
           s\equiv x_0\pmod {2^{2L}},\qquad
           s\equiv z_0\pmod {3^L}.
\]
The notation for the residues denotes the indicated finite arithmetic digits. The Chinese remainder theorem yields $\gg H_L/(L12^L)$ distinct candidates. Each has the prescribed first $L$ parity bits. At its $t$th iteration, $0\le t\le L$, its dyadic coordinate agrees with $x_t$ modulo $2^{2L-t}$ and its ternary coordinate agrees with $z_t$ modulo $3^L$. Throughout the segment,
\[
     T^t(s)=A_t s+B_t,\qquad
     A_t=3^{\sum_{i<t}u_i}2^{-t}=r_t/r_0\in[1/9,9],\qquad
     0\le B_t=A_t\sum_{i<t}u_i/(2A_{i+1})\le(81/2)L .
\]
Consequently $T^t(s)/H_L=r_t+O(1/L+L/H_L)$ uniformly in the root and the date.

For the prescribed prefix every $s\mapsto T^t(s)$ is injective. A previously used state excludes at most $L+1$ new candidate starts if every new segment is to remain disjoint from every previous one. There are no internal repeats on a candidate segment for large $L$: a return after $1\le l\le L$ steps would fix a positive integer of an affine word. Clearing its denominator gives $(2^l-3^k)y=A$ with $0\le A<6^l$, forcing $y<6^L$, whereas all candidate states exceed $H_L/4$. Take $\lfloor H_L^{1/M}/L\rfloor$ disjoint segments greedily. The total loss of candidates from those choices is $O(LH_L^{1/M})=o(H_L/(L12^L))$ by the choice of $C$. Thus $P_L=L\lfloor H_L^{1/M}/L\rfloor\asymp H_L^{1/M}$. Endpoint vertices can also be kept disjoint by the same count.

For a fixed radius, a finite ordinary graph test uses only boundedly many dyadic and ternary digits: each forward step can lose one dyadic digit, and each odd inverse step can lose one ternary digit. All its permitted parent and child vertices remain positive at large height, and all their normalized real affine constants are $O(1/H_L)$ for that radius. Any accidental coincidence of two distinct affine expressions at a finite radius can occur only at one rational root per pair; expressions equal as maps represent the same forced vertex in both graphs. At large ordinary roots the accidental finite collection is avoided, and the prototype's nonrational dyadic coordinate also excludes it. Thus the full labelled balls agree together with any fixed residues, and the normalized real coordinates converge uniformly there. Birkhoff's theorem on the generic prototype, with $O(1/L)$ boundary loss for any fixed itinerary window, gives the joint convergence asserted above.

The construction permits an exact stationary limit on the full local arithmetic graph even for a bounded radial process with positive parity entropy and a rotation factor. Ordinary integer injection for a *single full period*, and estimates uniform through that full period, remain available constraints outside what this local approximation preserves. The binary-window $\times2,\times3$ rigidity theorem concerns a further average over digit heights. Its authors explicitly exhibit independent biased parity processes with Lebesgue binary-window limits. The two arithmetic coordinates above satisfy the native shortcut recurrence; they carry no proved separate $\times2$ and $\times3$ invariance of the ordinary parity law.
