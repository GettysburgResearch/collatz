# Exact affine overlap of biased parity laws and ordinary rare-survivor patterns

Let $x_i=A_i n+b_i$ with fixed positive odd $A_i$ and integer $b_i$ for $0\le i<r$. For $p\in(0,1)$, write $\mu_{p,i}$ for the source law on $n\in\mathbb Z_2$ that makes the complete actual parity sequence of $x_i$ iid Bernoulli$(p)$. A *cylinder fusion* of $i$ to $0$ occurs if at a finite date their true affine states agree on the entire current binary source cylinder. Equivalently their current states and their current slopes $A_i3^{k_i}$ agree. Let $F_i$ be the union of these cylinders and $F=\bigcap_i F_i$. The notation $k_i$ at a stated date denotes its true odd count through that date. Countably many accidental equalities of states at an unequal slope have probability zero under each of these nonatomic source laws.

For $p\ne1/2$, on $F_i$ the two measures $\mu_{p,0},\mu_{p,i}$ are exactly proportional, and on its complement their restrictions are mutually singular. More precisely if $A_i/A_0=3^{e_i}$, $e_i\in\mathbb Z$, then
\[
        \mu_{p,0}|_{F_i}
           =\left(\frac p{1-p}\right)^{e_i}\mu_{p,i}|_{F_i}.            \tag{1}
\]
If $A_i/A_0\notin3^{\mathbb Z}$, $F_i$ is empty. For unequal bias parameters $p,q$ the two pullbacks are mutually singular for arbitrary slopes and offsets, including when one parameter is fair. Consequently among the maps $n\mapsto A n+b$ with $A$ a positive odd integer and $b$ an integer, the only one preserving the measure class of a fixed nonfair biased parity law is the identity.

[PR 215, biased binary fusion](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/18e1e6316453a6cb05b4fed9a62e49b8a50cb3ea/research/astra-nine-hour-20261128/machinery/phase8/BIASED_BINARY_FUSION.md) already proves the same-bias common-part and quantitative translation overlap statements, in the more general setting of arbitrary two-adic affine maps, by a finite-cylinder likelihood-ratio martingale. The conclusions just stated for a common bias and fixed ordinary affine inputs are special cases. Here the strict grade deficit on the unabsorbed part and the bounded-density binomial bridge supply the relative ordinary counts below.

There is a corresponding coefficient for actual integers. Fix $p>1/2$, $t=t(X)\to\infty$, $2^t=o(X)$, and $K=K(X)$ with $K/t\to p$. For uniform $n\in[X,2X)$,
\[
 \frac{\Pr\{ k_t(x_i)\ge K\ \text{for every }i\}}
      {\Pr\{ k_t(x_0)\ge K\}}
   \ \longrightarrow\ \int\min_i d\mu_{p,i}.                            \tag{2}
\]
The integral means the total mass of the greatest common submeasure, equivalently integrate the minimum of the densities against their sum. It vanishes when any pair of slopes is nonsmooth. Otherwise it equals
\[
       \mu_{p,0}(F)\left(\frac{1-p}{p}\right)^{\max_i e_i},
                   \qquad e_0=0.                                       \tag{3}
\]
Under the existing counted synchronization input for smooth affine pairs, $F$ contains a genuine finite cylinder in the latter case, making this coefficient positive for every fixed finite smooth family. Given that all counts exceed their common threshold, almost every source in that case has already physically synchronized all its coordinate paths by the tested date. The nonsmooth pair's stronger near-critical conditional upper estimate is in the [grade-repulsion theorem](BIASED_GRADE_REPULSION.md).

For ordinary shifts $x_h=n+h$, the limit of the finite-dimensional field of events $k_t(n+h)\ge K$, conditioned on $k_t(n)\ge K$, is the random set of integers $h$ whose actual forward path synchronizes with that of $n$ under $\mu_{p,0}$. In particular for each $h\ne0$ its inclusion probability $c_p(h)$ satisfies
\[
                     0<c_p(h)\le1-|2p-1|<1.                            \tag{4}
\]
At fair bias the counted synchronization input instead gives almost-sure fusion for every fixed integer translation. The upper bound in (4) uses only native affine digits and the biased walk.

## Fusion, transience and the two singular parts

For $i$ fixed write $D=k_i-k_0$. Under $\mu_{p,0}$ the embedded signs at successive mixed departures form a nearest-neighbor walk with probability $1-p$ of $+1$ and $p$ of $-1$, while the mixed dates are predictable in the first-source parity filtration. For a nonsmooth pair the [preceding proof](BIASED_GRADE_REPULSION.md) gives strict contraction of the companion's asymptotic distance from grade $1/2$. For a smooth pair the sole resonant grade is $D=-e_i$, at which the two slopes agree. There the state difference is a fixed ordinary integer for this cylinder (use a nonnegative residue for the shared source and run the integer formulas). If this integer vanishes, the cylinder is fused forever. If nonzero, a mixed departure follows exactly its finite two-adic valuation in common steps. At every other grade the same conditional geometric wait envelope as in the previous proof applies, now with $v(D)=v_2(3^{D+e_i}-1)$, since the fixed common odd factor has valuation zero. Clearing negative exponents and the order of three give $v(D)\le C\log(2+|D+e_i|)$. Its level-$k$ sets again occupy at most one exponent class modulo $2^{k-2}$ for $k\ge3$.

Complete the sequence of mixed signs after possible absorption by independent Bernoulli signs on an auxiliary probability space. This produces a full walk with the stated bias: a next finite predictable mixed date uses a fresh independent first-source parity, and a prior fusion is decided by the existing prefix. Equivalently one can sample each successive waiting experiment, then use a fresh independent biased sign if the mixed date occurs. This makes the unabsorbed embedded signs an initial segment of an iid walk. Possible future absorption, which depends on subsequent histories, has not been conditioned away. In particular the resonant grade is visited only finitely often on any nonfused path, almost surely, by biased transience.

Set $v(-e_i)=0$ in the occupation calculation. The potential/Azuma bound from the grade-repulsion proof applies with this definition: (4) there follows from the level counts and the logarithmic cap, with at most a deleted singleton in each level set. For nonresonant dates the positive excess of the waiting lengths above $1+v(D)$ has bounded conditional exponential moment. Ignore the finitely many resonant holding intervals on each nonfused sample. Each of them has finite length. Consequently on $F_i^c$ a positive lower proportion of all physical dates are mixed almost surely; the upper away from one follows from the same three-date common-child argument, unaffected by smooth slopes. The biased signs and the first source's ordinary strong law give
\[
 \limsup_{t\to\infty}|k_t(x_i)/t-1/2|<|p-1/2|\quad\mu_{p,0}\text{-a.s. on }F_i^c.    \tag{5}
\]
The constants bounding the two proportions may be chosen deterministic after discounting a sample-dependent finite initial delay. This argument also covers the reversed pair under its own tilted source.

On each minimal fusion cylinder the length agrees and $k_0-k_i=e_i$, while all later coordinate parities agree. The cylinder likelihood ratio is exactly (1), on every subcylinder as well. On the complement, simultaneous typicality under two biased measures would by (5) require reciprocal strict inequalities for the two absolute biases. If one measure is fair, the Haar source makes the other coordinate's grade fair almost surely. On the fusion cylinders different limiting own biases are incompatible. This proves the singularity and common-part claims. For the finite-family minimum, each measure dominated by all must be supported on every $F_i$ and (1) applies simultaneously; this gives (3). If two affine measures out of a finite collection have nonsmooth ratio the whole common part vanishes.

For translations by nonzero $h$ the first mixed departure follows exactly $v_2(h)$ common dates at the initial resonant grade zero. The ensuing biased nearest-neighbor walk has probability $|2p-1|$ of never returning to zero (condition on its first step towards its drift and use the elementary gambler's ruin probability for the one reverse hit). There can be no cylinder fusion on that event. Every finite fair-source fusion cylinder has positive mass under any $p\in(0,1)$; the Haar synchronization theorem supplies at least one for each fixed shift. This proves (4). For a smooth ratio $A_i/A_0=3^{e_i}$ and equal biases, measure-class equivalence would force both probabilities of $F_i$ to be one by the singular complement. Equation (1) then forces $e_i=0$. The translation argument for equal slopes still applies to any nonzero offset difference, since the initial physical displacement is a fixed integer and the first-source parity map remains a binary isometry. This proves the stated measure-class stabilizer. [Counted native synchronization inputs](MULTIPLICATIVE_MEETING_TIME.md)

## Exact grade slices detect fusion at the tested date

We record the required conditioning argument instead of equating arbitrary late true meetings with finite-prefix meetings. Let $K_t/t\to p$ and use the ordinary binary slice $k_t(x_0)=K_t$. It is the same as conditioning $\mu_{p_t,0}$ on that count, $p_t=K_t/t$. Every depth-$t$ word in it has equal mass. For each fixed prefix the slice law tends to $\mu_{p,0}$ by elementary sampling without replacement. On the first $s=\lfloor t/2\rfloor$ parities its density relative to $\mu_{p_t,0}$ is bounded by an absolute constant on compact subintervals of $0<p<1$. Indeed the ratio equals the mass of one residual binomial count on $t-s$ trials divided by $\Pr(\operatorname{Bin}(t,p_t)=K_t)$; the numerator is $O(t^{-1/2})$ uniformly and the denominator is $\gg t^{-1/2}$ by Stirling. On the full $t$ dates the density bound is $O(\sqrt t)$.

Here is a uniform version of the nonresonant tail estimate at the half time, for $p_t\to p\ne1/2$ and a fixed smooth pair. Write $H_s$ for the number of mixed dates by $s$. There is $c_1>0$ such that the tilted probability of *no fusion by $s$ and $H_s<c_1s$* tends to zero. To see this, first choose a fixed $J$. The completed biased walk has probability $O(e^{-cJ})$ of revisiting its fixed resonant grade at any embedded index $j\ge J$, uniformly in $p_t$ near $p$, by Hoeffding and summation. The physical time to a fusion or the $J$th mixed date is finite almost surely at parameter $p$ by the waiting argument. Its probability of exceeding a sufficiently large fixed $M$ can therefore be made arbitrarily small, uniformly for large $t$, because the event through $M$ is a union of fixed finite cylinders and $p_t\to p$. Beyond the first $J$ mixed dates, except for the excluded late resonance, the previous truncated potential bound gives over the first $m=\lfloor2c_1s\rfloor$ embedded positions total nonresonant cost $O(m)$ off probability $o(1)$, including the geometrically bounded excess in nonresonant holds. To apply it after absorption, set unrealized excesses to zero; the completed iid walk supplies all $m$ valuations and dominates this nonnegative sum. Taking $c_1$ small, $M+O(m)<s$ for large $t$. Thus nonfusion by $s$ forces at least $c_1s$ mixed dates outside the stated exceptions. Send first $M$ and $J$ to their required fixed large values and then the arbitrary error to zero. This proves the uniform assertion.

For definiteness let $p>1/2$; reversing the drift signs covers $p<1/2$. Off probability $o(1)$, every unfused sample at half time now has $D_s<-c_2t$ for some $c_2>0$, since the completed biased walk stays below a negative linear slope at all sufficiently large embedded indices outside exponential probability. Bounded half-prefix density transfers this conclusion to the slice. The slice probability of an unfused half-time sample overcoming that negative gap to reach $D\ge-c_2t/2$ at any later mixed date by time $t$ is $O(\sqrt t e^{-c_3t})$, by the future biased walk's gambler's ruin bound before absorption and the full-slice density bound. If the pair is nonsmooth use the stronger previous estimate directly, or its identical half-time drift argument. Thus all but $o(1)$ of the slice either fused by half time or end with companion grade below first grade by a *fixed linear* amount. An accidental unequal-slope equality does not enter any of the counted-fusion arguments.

Finally the slice probability of fusion by $s$ tends to $\mu_{p,0}(F_i)$. A lower bound follows from each fixed finite fusion horizon. For the upper bound, on a nonfused prefix at any fixed horizon $M$ the later first fusion requires another visit to the resonant embedded grade. Uniformly off $O(e^{-cJ})$, it must occur before the $J$th mixed date. The probability that neither fusion nor the $J$th mixed date has occurred by fixed $M$ can be made arbitrarily small as above. This event and any fusion before $s$ are half-prefix events, so use the bounded half-prefix density for the upper error. First approximate the finite horizon under $p_t$, then take the two fixed cutoffs large. A finite union bound gives the same result for simultaneous fusion of all $i$ by $s$, with limit $\mu_{p,0}(F)$. If the slopes are nonsmooth the simultaneous coefficient is zero. This also shows the true finite ordinary slice detects the relevant fusions. Each final uniform binary residue contributes $X/2^t+O(1)$ starts in the original octave, so every relative conditional probability transfers with factor $1+O(2^t/X)$ when nonzero. If an actual final-state coincidence at unequal slopes is queried, its affine equality singles out at most one ordinary start per final residue and costs at most $O(2^t/X)$ conditionally on any slice or upper tail.

## Upper tails and physical nondecrease

Under the fair binary law, conditional on $k_t(x_0)\ge K$, the overshoot $J=k_t(x_0)-K$ converges to the nonnegative geometric law $\Pr(J\ge j)=((1-p)/p)^j$. This follows from the adjacent binomial probability ratios $(t-k)/(k+1)$, uniformly bounded away from one on the upper tail for large $t$. At every *fixed* overshoot $j$, the preceding exact-slice result shows that off $o(1)$ probability every remaining coordinate above $K$ has fused. In a simultaneous fused family the grades there equal $K+j-e_i$. They all exceed or equal $K$ precisely when $j\ge\max_i e_i$. The fusion coefficient at each fixed overshoot is the same $\mu_{p,0}(F)$. Truncate the uniformly geometric tail and pass to the limit. This proves (2)–(3), their conditional synchronization conclusion, and the stated additive field by applying finite inclusion-exclusion to its joint inclusion probabilities.

At $p=\alpha=\log 2/\log3$ take $K=\lceil\alpha t\rceil$. If $t\le(1-\epsilon)\log_2 X$ for any fixed $\epsilon>0$ (or only $2^t t^C=o(X)$ for a sufficiently large absolute $C$), the grade event is exactly $T^t x_i\ge x_i$ at every ordinary source in the octave for large $X$. Indeed every intermediate value $z$ is $\gg X2^{-t}$, and the sum of actual odd log corrections $\log(1+1/(3z))$ is $O(t 2^t/X)$. The nonzero linear form $k\log3-t\log2$ has absolute value at least $c(t+1)^{-C_0}$ uniformly for $0\le k\le t$ by the classical fixed two-logarithm estimate. The sign therefore determines nondecrease. Alternatively $t\le(1/2-\epsilon)\log_2X$ suffices elementarily: when $3^k<2^t$ their difference is at least one, yielding logarithmic separation at least $2^{-t}$. Thus (2) gives a relative ordinary count of genuine simultaneous failure to decrease at the tested date. It is a property of that finite date, not a condition on the least stopping time or on eventual integer convergence.
