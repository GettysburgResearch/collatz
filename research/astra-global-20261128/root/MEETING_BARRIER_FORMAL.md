# A native finite barrier for multiplicative meetings

[MeetingBarrier.lean](MeetingBarrier.lean) proves the elementary, multiplier-uniform part of [the meeting-time theorem](../cycles/MULTIPLICATIVE_MEETING_TIME.md) on the actual shortcut map imported from `CoreBranching`.

For positive multipliers $A,B$, the division-free predicate `TwoThreeRelated A B` means exactly
$$
 A2^a3^b=B2^c3^d
 \quad\text{for some nonnegative integers }a,b,c,d.
$$
Thus it is equivalent to $A/B$ belonging to the multiplicative group generated by two and three. The formal statement uses the displayed integer predicate, so neither division nor logarithmic-form estimates enter the proof.

The endpoints establish the following facts.

- `affine_identity` gives $2^sT^s(x)=3^{k_s(x)}x+E_s(x)$, with the offset defined from the actual native parity choices.
- `offset_budget` gives $E_s(x)+2^s\le3^s$ for every finite source and clock.
- `meeting_power_relation` says that if $T^s(An)=T^u(Bn)$ and $n\ge3^{\max(s,u)}$, then $A3^{k_s(An)}2^u=B3^{k_u(Bn)}2^s$. Both dates vary independently.
- `meeting_source_bound` therefore gives $n<3^{\max(s,u)}$ for any meeting of unrelated multipliers.
- `disjoint_prefixes` gives the complete finite-prefix conclusion: if $n\ge3^h$ and the multipliers are unrelated, then $T^s(An)\ne T^u(Bn)$ for every $s,u\le h$.

The key arithmetic comparison clears the smaller binary clock first. The two offsets are then both strictly below the common source factor. An equality between two multiples of that factor, each perturbed by one such offset, forces equality of their integer coefficients. Clearing a larger common denominator without first removing the smaller clock would lose the stated uniform bound.

The Lean file has strict implicit-variable and warning settings and was compiled in the pinned Lean 4.34.1 environment. The checkpoint replay audits all its public, private and generated declarations. This finite result neither asserts eventual meeting nor formalizes the source-qualified critical square-root meeting window, the Matveev improvement, or a universal Collatz statement.
