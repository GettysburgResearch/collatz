# Incoming parity and the actual last exit of a positive ordinary orbit

**Source-qualified written theorem.** Work with the limiting first odd landing $Y_B$ at floor $B$, or an actual odd source octave with $\log(X/B)\to\infty$. There is an absolute $c>0$ such that the following holds for each fixed finite partition $\mathcal I$ of $[0,1]$ into intervals, $B\to\infty$, $k\ge1$, $m\ge0$, and $k+m\le c\log B$. Record
\[
      A_B(y)=(\mathcal I(y/B),\ y\bmod3^m,\ y\bmod2^k),\qquad
      r=\lfloor \sqrt B\rfloor .
\]
The last entry equivalently records the first $k$ shortcut parities *after this first landing*, including its initial odd parity. Let $\Gamma_B$ consist of the first two projected coordinates of $G$ and an independent uniform odd binary residue. Let $F_B$ be **any** map on the possible finite or cemetery values of the smaller first landing $L_r$. Then, in sum norm,
\[
 \mathcal L\big(A_B(Y_B),F_B(L_r(Y_B))\big)
          \;=\; \Gamma_B\otimes (F_B)_*\mu_r+o(1),                     \tag{1}
\]
where cemetery conventions on the left may be arbitrary at a cost tending to zero. The error is uniform over $F_B$, its target cardinality and all tests of the displayed pair. It includes the complete smaller label on taking $F_B$ to be the identity. This uses the **earlier** growing original-source digit comparison [PR213, source suffix law, (1)](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/5ac2b4a7ab9fee19d2e1b1c2e4aadecd08413128/research/astra-diagonal-frontier-20261127/linear/source_suffix_law.md), in addition to [the all-physical-label first-landing template](LOCAL_LANDING_PROFILE.md). The conclusion concerns the incoming label's digits and an unrestricted later physical event. It is stronger in that respect than the longer unconditioned binary-word estimate in [the near-full conductor theorem](GROWING_LANDING_PHASES.md).

## Passing an unrestricted later label through the first-landing profile

It suffices to take $F_B$ the identity. For $\epsilon>0$, choose one fixed finite interval–ternary template $g$ for the first-landing grid, with sum-norm error $<\epsilon$ in the limit and $\|g-G\|_1<\epsilon$. Sending each original label $y$ to $(A_B(y),L_r(y))$ contracts the error even when the displayed test depends on the entire subsequent positive orbit.

For uniform original odd starts $y$ in any fixed proportional interval $[dB,eB)$ with $0<d<e$, PR213 gives the joint distribution of $y\bmod 2^k3^M$ and $L_r(y)$ within $o(1)$ in sum norm of uniform odd source residues times $\mu_r$, for $k+M\le 2c\log B$ and sufficiently small absolute $c$. Indeed its stated error is relative $C(\log(Y/r))^{-1/9}$ plus an arbitrary fixed inverse power in every event on sharp octaves. Here $\log(Y/r)\sim(\log B)/2$ on each fixed octave. For the indicated interval first obtain the result on $[1,dB)$ and $[1,eB)$ with raw normalization $2/B$ by summing the fixed number of leading dyadic octaves and bounding the remaining inputs in absolute mass by their total normalized length. Increase the number of included octaves and subtract the cumulative sums. This works also for a fixed interval starting at zero. The resulting main factor for a raw interval is its length divided by $B$. All estimates hold for arbitrary tests on the joint output space, and the allowed $c$ can be fixed using one sufficiently large error power.

Refine $\mathcal I$ by the fixed cuts of $g$ and take $M=\max(m,m_g)$, where $m_g$ is its ternary depth. Multiply the raw joint comparison on each interval by its bounded $g$ as a test of those $M$ trits. The finite number of such intervals is fixed before $B$ grows. Sum and push forward to the advertised observation. The resulting measure is the projection of $g(u,z)\,du\,d\lambda_3(z)$ on the measured coordinates, times the uniform odd binary law and $\mu_r$. Its distance from the expression in (1) is at most $\|g-G\|_1$. Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ finishes. For an actual distant octave, the first-landing grid law is also within $o(1)$ of $\mu_B$ in total variation, and its initial first passage to $r$ agrees with the one from $Y_B$ when finite. This proves (1).

## Specializing to the last exit and its harmonic weight

On an escaping orbit that has visited $[1,B]$, denote its last odd vertex there by $M_B$. Let $E_B$ be the set of all such ordinary last-exit vertices, and give bounded orbits or missing landings a single default label. When the orbit also reaches $r$, its entire forward sequence beginning at $L_r$ is fixed by that smaller landing, which itself lies at or below $B$. Thus $M_B$, its escaping component, and the entire strict future of $M_B$ are functions of $L_r$. This statement does not claim that the native time from $Y_B$ to $L_r$ is a function of the latter. The smaller-floor cemetery has probability $o(1)$ by [the first-landing bound](FIRST_LANDING_ENTROPY.md). Consequently (1) factors the entering observation from the actual last exit and any function of its strict future, even if the measured exit sets vary with $B$. Conditional factorization follows on any such events with probabilities bounded away from zero. In particular it holds on each escaping component if that component exists, since an ordinary unit predecessor basin in it has positive natural density.

Put $w(x)=3x/(3x+1)$, $F(x)=\prod_{i\ge0}w(U^i x)$, $\kappa=D/2$, and $a_y=h(y)/y$ as in [the stationary last-exit theorem](SPATIAL_TRACE_TAIL.md). Let $\mathbb E_{\rm nat}$ denote the limiting odd-source octave law, at fixed $B$; bounded test sets of first/last labels have limits by their finite values and the earlier first-hit identities. Then the positive escaping flux has the product formula
\[
 \left\|\kappa\,\mathbb E_{\rm nat}\big[F(x)1_{M_B\in E_B}
             \delta_{(A_B(Y_B),M_B)}\big]
          -\Gamma_B\otimes\sum_{z\in E_B} a_z F(z)\delta_z\right\|_1
                   \longrightarrow0 .                                \tag{2}
\]
At each $z\in E_B$, the natural weight $\kappa\,\mathbb E_{\rm nat}[F(x)1_{M_B=z}]$ equals $a_zF(z)$ by the fixed-target weighted first-arrival identity: visiting this nonperiodic $z$ makes it the last low, and $F(x)$ is the first-arrival product into $z$ times $F(z)$. For the joint assertion first replace $F(x)$ by $F(L_r(x))$ when the smaller landing exists. The positive segment before that landing is simple, all departed states exceed $r$, and the uniform upper bound on its logarithmic correction is $O(r^{-\kappa_0})$ for a fixed $\kappa_0>0$ from reciprocal packing. Thus the replacement costs $o(1)$ uniformly; the cemetery costs its $o(1)$ mass. Now the weight and exit mark on the finite part are functions of the smaller landing, with weight at most one. Apply (1) and use the exactly identified marginal to obtain (2). The same limiting joint statement can be defined using limsup over the original distant source scale at fixed $B$: for finite $B,r$, the asserted joint natural weights exist by conditioning first on the finite $Y_B$ and on the first-arrival product into each such label, whose limiting weighted law is part of the pinned stationary interface.

Equations (1)–(2) show that the entering observations, including an increasing genuine forward binary word, are asymptotically independent of the exact outgoing last-exit label under the natural and harmonic mass laws. The outgoing marginal in (2) can still have a positive total supported on the exceptional binary-sparse exits. Its mass is the unpriced term in the escape problem.

## The entire inward clock is independent of the eventual last exit

Now take any $r=r(B)\to\infty$ with $v=\log(B/r)\to\infty$; $r$ may grow arbitrarily slowly. After the first odd entrance $Y_B$ record the elapsed shortcut clock $T$ until the first odd entrance $Y_r$ at $r$. Let $O_B$ denote any function, possibly varying with $B$, of the even smaller first landing at $s=\lfloor\sqrt r\rfloor$. In particular it may record the physical last exit at $B$ on escaping orbits, with a default for the bounded population. In the latter interpretation smaller-floor failures can be given a default at a cost tending to zero. The inbound exact clock and the outgoing mark obey
\[
   \sum_{t,o}\left|\Pr(T=t,O_B=o)-
       f_v(t) C(tb-v)\Pr(O_B=o)\right|\longrightarrow0.               \tag{3}
\]
The cemetery probability is $o(1)$ and can be included separately. This holds under the limiting first-landing law at $B$, and also for every actual distant starting octave with $\log(X/B)\to\infty$. Use $P=\log3$, $b=\log2$, the Gaussian $f_v$ of [the global clock theorem](GLOBAL_LANDING_CLOCK.md), and probability Haar measure $m_P$ on $\mathbb R/P\mathbb Z$. Let $\lambda(u)=\int G(u,z)\,d\lambda_3(z)$ be the bounded real density of an odd landing height relative to its floor. Extend it by zero outside $(0,1]$. Define
\[
  W_\lambda(a)=P\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{a+jP}\lambda(e^{a+jP}),\qquad
  C(\theta)=\int W_\lambda(a) W_\lambda(a+\theta)\,dm_P(a).            \tag{4}
\]
Thus $W_\lambda$ is the bounded probability density of $\log u\bmod P$, and $C$ is its circular autocorrelation. Formula (3) is an asymptotic in the exact **integer** time. The nonconstant periodic factor persists after averaging over the first and second height labels. Its shape is the same after conditioning on any such future events of uniformly positive probability.

To prove this, approximate the grid law at $B$ by a fixed bounded step template $g$ in the full mass norm. For the subsequent joint clock and label $(T,Y_r)$ one can replace $g(u,z)$ as an incoming weight by $\lambda_g(u)=\int g(u,z)\,d\lambda_3(z)$ with $o(1)$ mass-norm error. Indeed for each of its fixed real intervals and fixed ternary modulus, (2)–(4) of [PR213, growing original source digits](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/5ac2b4a7ab9fee19d2e1b1c2e4aadecd08413128/research/astra-diagonal-frontier-20261127/linear/source_suffix_law.md) first retain the complete physical short-time endpoint **and its exact accumulated total**. On their good short paths above $r$, send this marked endpoint to $(T,Y_r)$ by adding its remaining physical clock, exactly as in the proof of (2) of [the global clock theorem](GLOBAL_LANDING_CLOCK.md). This deterministic operation contracts the sum norm, including the cemetery. The same bounds therefore hold for this joint pair. Subintervals of the incoming grid reaching zero can be handled by discarding $u<\delta$ at cost $O_g(\delta)$ before taking $B\to\infty$, then decreasing $\delta$. The total change from the initial template replacements can be made arbitrarily small.

For this fixed real step weight, the full labelled joint law under the raw grid weights $(2/B)\lambda_g(y/B)$ is, up to an error tending to zero in sum norm,
\[
        \mu_r(z) f_v(t) W_{\lambda_g}(tb+\log(z/r)-v),\qquad
                                 1\le z\le r\text{ odd}.              \tag{5}
\]
For one octave $[aB,2aB)$ at fixed $a>0$ use the global clock theorem and note its leading density may equivalently use the **actual** marginal $\mu_r(z)$ in place of the grid template $(2/r)g'(z/r,z)$. This follows by a common arbitrarily accurate template from [the local profile](LOCAL_LANDING_PROFILE.md), since the periodic factor is bounded and the sum of the Gaussian weights is bounded. The difference between $f_{v+\log a}$ and $f_v$ has vanishing sum norm. With raw weights $2/B$ this octave has total mass $a+o(1)$ and its wrapped factor is exactly $aW(tb+\log(z/r)-v-\log a)$. This is the wrap in (4) for $1_{[a,2a)}$. Split any initial real interval into its dyadic octaves, truncating at a fixed small $a$ at first and then removing the cutoff. The wrapped contribution from this discarded tail is uniformly $O_g(a)$ by its geometric series. Differences of initial intervals and linearity establish (5). Its derivation uses the arbitrary-separation clock theorem, even when $r$ is small compared with every fixed power of $B$.

Map $z$ in (5) to $O_B(L_s(z))$, retaining its exact time. On the $\mu_r$ distribution first entry at $s$ follows first entry at $r$, including the common default convention up to an $o(1)$ cemetery mass. Apply (1) with upper floor $r$ and lower floor $s$ to the pair (scaled height $z/r$, later mark); no fine binary observation is necessary here. This factors the sum with its clock-dependent weight $W_{\lambda_g}(\theta+\log(z/r))$ uniformly in $\theta$. For details, $W_{\lambda_g}$ is bounded and piecewise smooth with finitely many jumps on the circle because $\lambda_g$ is a fixed interval step function. On $z/r\ge\delta$ the translates composed with the logarithm can be replaced by tests on a fixed fine real partition with uniformly small integral error: away from the jumps the derivative is bounded in terms of $g,\delta$, and the number of jump positions there is bounded independently of the translate. The real marginals of $\mu_r$ converge to the bounded continuous-distribution law $\lambda(u)du$, uniformly on intervals by monotonicity of distribution functions, so the small neighborhoods of those finitely many jumps also cost uniformly little. The part $z/r<\delta$ has asymptotically $O(\delta)$ mass. This justifies the factorization even if the tests in the sum norm vary with $t$ and $o$. The sum over $t$ of $f_v(t)$ stays bounded.

The real factor after this comparison is $\int W_{\lambda_g}(\theta+a)W_\lambda(a)\,dm_P(a)$. Wrapping contracts the $L^1$ distance between densities, and $W_\lambda$ is bounded, so replacing $\lambda_g$ by $\lambda$ costs uniformly at most $\|W_\lambda\|_\infty\|g-G\|_1$. The last-exit marginal may be replaced by its actual value by the unmarked lower-label approximation or by first-landing consistency. Send the original template error to zero. This proves (3).

The Fourier coefficients of $C$ are $|\widehat W_\lambda(j)|^2$. For $j\ne0$ and $\tau_j=2\pi j/P$, the record formula with record increment $L$ gives, in the $e^{i\tau_j a}$ convention,
\[
       \widehat W_\lambda(j)
            =\frac{1-\mathbb E e^{-i\tau_j L}}{i\tau_jD}.              \tag{6}
\]
Each coefficient is nonzero. In fact the independent past partial sums beyond the outermost gap have positive probability to stay nonnegative: their future infimum is finite by positive drift, and a sufficiently large initial gap offsets any fixed bounded negative infimum of the independent remainder with positive probability. On this event the positive record increment is $(E_0 b-P)_+$. The two positive values for $E_0=2,3$ have positive probability and differ by $b$. They cannot both be multiples of $P/j$ since $b/P$ is irrational. Thus $e^{-i\tau_jL}$ is not almost surely one. Equation (6) gives $|\widehat W_\lambda(j)|=O(1/|j|)$; consequently $C$ has an absolutely convergent Fourier series, mean one, and strict maximum at $0\bmod P$ since all nonconstant cosine coefficients are positive. The actual exact inward-clock law has these same periodic fluctuations on positive escaping or bounded outcome classes. The harmonic-weighted statement follows by weighting by $F(L_s)$ and applying the same uniform packing error as for (2).

Finally, an escaping orbit that first went to $r$ must climb back above $B$ before its final exit. In shortcut time $j$, the elementary bound $T^j x+1\le(3/2)^j(x+1)$ holds on all positive integers. Therefore the time from $Y_r$ to a state above $B$ is at least $\log((B+1)/(r+1))/\log(3/2)$. If the date is assigned to the last odd state at or below $B$, subtract at most one shortcut step to reach a state above $B$: the shortcut image after this odd departure is at least its next odd endpoint, which exceeds $B$. Equation (3) shows that, on any positive-probability escaping classes, the inward time divided by $v$ tends in probability to $2/D$. Hence the first-to-last duration divided by $v$ is at least $2/D+1/\log(3/2)-o(1)$ in probability. This lower bound prices a long recrossing interval on a genuine escaping trajectory. Its expense is elapsed time, and no upper bound eliminating these orbits follows from it.
