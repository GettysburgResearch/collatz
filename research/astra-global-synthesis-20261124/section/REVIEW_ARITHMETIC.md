**M1 and M2.** On each unit interval, the lift before reduction modulo one is strictly increasing. At an integer seam, the extra numerator one on the right gets multiplied by two, whereas the incoming left limit has an unmultiplied one. This gives the positive jump stated in the source. The degree-one identity survives composition even across these jumps. For any two starts in a half-open unit interval, every prefix floor increases by at most one. A new complete clock word changes at least one prefix floor, so summing those $k$ floors can make at most $k$ strict increases. The complete-word carry is independent of the initial source. The terminal odd parity supplies the extra factor two in the difference of sources, since the factor $3^k$ is odd. The positive carry, lower initial height, and strict upper terminal height give $2^{A_k+1}>3^kH_0/H_k$. The maximum number of points of a lattice class in a half-open interval of real length $h$ is $\lceil h/\text{spacing}\rceil$, including integral ratios. These facts give both factors of (1). Successive windows of a confined injective path have distinct starting points, so the bound on $L-k+1$ gives $L\le2k$ in one octave at the stated choice of $k$. At a suffix minimum the first exit must be upwards, and the upper endpoint is even.

I supplied and checked the sharpness argument added in the reviewed revision. Each fractional cut $-j\log_2 3$ has a different position for $1\le j\le k$. Successive cuts change a cumulative clock and hence the gap word. Interior points have a positive distance from all endpoints along the full finite rotation word. The terminal-odd parity cylinder supplies an actual ordinary source at bounded distance from each scaled interior point. The fixed-word affine carries then give an $O_k(1/H)$ error after normalization, proving occurrence of all $k+1$ words for the same stationary octave. Joint sharpness of the separate lattice-count factor is not established.

**One-octave full denominator.** Scaling all cycle phases to their numerators gives a strictly ordered set on the multiplicative cover, with exactly $p$ points in any half-open octave starting at a point of that cover. The raw affine images are consecutive because they are distinct, span less than one octave and exhaust its $p$ rays. The internal gaps are tripled. At the seam the difference of additive contributions is exactly $d$. Each gap is multiplied by the actual power of two of the source marking its left endpoint. The underlying permutation is one cycle, so its full circuit multiplies the homogeneous coefficient by $3^p$ and the denominator by $2^A$. The single inhomogeneous term is $d$ times a product of powers of 2 and 3. This proves divisibility by the entire $2^A-3^p$ after cancellation and forces $d=2^A-3^p$. This is restricted to the stipulated ordinary-width octave.

**Affine-permutation sorting.** Periodic index displacement is bounded and only bounded-distance pairs can be inverted. Swapping a descent and all its translates changes the number of inversions per period by exactly one: truncate to many consecutive periods and divide by their number, with bounded boundary error. This also covers period two, where the adjacent pairs are disjoint and cover the whole line. The average displacement survives a seam swap because the entering and exiting copies differ by opposite period shifts. The sorted cover must be a translate, whose permutation of residues has $\gcd(n,R)$ cycles. One transposition either joins two cycles or splits one. Starting from a single residue cycle proves the lower bound and the parity constraint.

**Witness directions.** For a forward dyadic inversion the starting lifts have $\lambda u<\mu v$. Inverting raw order after the step requires $3(\mu v-\lambda u)<\lambda-\mu$, so $\lambda/\mu=2^s>1$. The unit integer gap forces $s\ge3$. Changing the common scale does not change a triple. Distinct $s$ cannot give the same ordinary pair, since the upper end of the interval for one $s$ lies below the next lower end for $u\ge1$. On the triadic cover use the inverse equation $2x-e(x)=3^{e(x)}\operatorname{prev}(x)$. The new inequality is $2(\mu v-\lambda u)<\mu e(v)-\lambda e(u)$ and forces $e(v)=1$. If $\lambda/\mu$ were a nonnegative integral power of three, dividing by $\mu$ would require twice a positive integer to be less than or equal to one. Thus $\mu/\lambda=3^s$ with $s\ge1$, giving (3). Reversing each calculation produces the corresponding inversion. Distinct odd numerators define distinct dyadic rays and distinct ordinary shortcut states on the cycle are all prime to 3, so the two covers really are permutations. The winding numbers are the sums of the scale exponents, since the choice of ray representatives telescopes under permutation. They are respectively $A$ and $p$. No claim of distinct simultaneous witnesses across the two different lists is needed.
