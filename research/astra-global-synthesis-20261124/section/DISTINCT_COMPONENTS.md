# The comparison paths of an escaping input belong to different ordinary components

**PROPOSED written conditional theorem.** An escaping ordinary input in the mixed singleton/pair language forces at least two disjoint escaping ordinary forward orbits, even when intersections are tested at unequal clocks. An escaping input in the paired-only language forces at least three such orbits. These are statements about either hypothetical escaping input; existence of such an input remains open.

Let $U$ be the odd Collatz map on positive odd integers, and put $e(x)=v_2(3x+1)$. For any escaping ordinary odd orbit define
$$
M(x)=x\,2^{\sum_{j\ge0}\log_2(1+1/(3U^j(x)))} .
$$
An escaping ordinary orbit is injective. Reciprocal summability on **each** such orbit follows, for example, from PR193's origin-centered counting estimate, by summing over dyadic octaves. Thus $M(x)$ is finite, $M(U^j x)/U^j x\to1$ along that orbit, and
$$
M(Ux)=3M(x)/2^{e(x)}.
$$
If two escaping odd sources $x,y$ eventually intersect, say $U^r x=U^s y$, iteration gives
$$
M(y)/M(x)\in\{2^a3^b:a,b\in\mathbb Z\}.
$$
This necessity permits unrelated odd clocks $r,s$. A shortcut-orbit intersection also implies an odd-orbit intersection: advance their common positive state until its next odd state.

**Mixed-language comparison.** Take one fixed escaping ordinary input in the language of [the mixed theorem](../merger/mixed.md). Start at a free light or the beginning of a complete three-light preparation. Such an anchor is reached after finitely many blocks from every allowed initial partial phase. From then on, group the input into single free lights, complete $LLLP$ tiles and complete $LLLPP$ tiles. At each resulting boundary the original value $q$ and shadow $y$ are odd and $y=(183q+1)/256$. The literal target table gives two odd steps on a free light, $3+2+1+2=8$ on a singleton tile, and $3+2+2+1+2=10$ on a pair tile, matching the input in every case. The total numbers of shortcut clocks likewise match, respectively $3,14,19$. Between odd endpoints the shortcut duration is the total of the odd valuations.

At these infinitely many anchors the given $q$ tends to infinity. Consequently the corresponding ordinary shadow values $y$ tend to infinity. The complete shadow trajectory cannot be eventually periodic; it is itself an escaping ordinary orbit, and its own reciprocal correction is summable. Between any two selected anchors both corrected heights acquire the same factor $3^{\#\mathrm{odd}}/2^{\#\mathrm{shortcut}}$. Their ratio is constant. Taking the limit on the two escaping trajectories therefore gives its **exact** value
$$
\frac{M(y)}{M(q)}
=\lim_{\text{anchors}}\frac{y}{q}
=\frac{183}{256}=\frac{3\cdot61}{2^8}.
$$
The prime 61 rules out intersection at any clocks. This assertion uses the actual shadow trajectory, not the phase-specific auxiliary smaller source sometimes chosen from the input itself to beat both floors.

**Paired-only comparison.** This input also qualifies for [the paired theorem](../merger/proof.md). Its second shadow $\widetilde y$ stays odd at all block boundaries and has two odd steps and identical shortcut duration on every input block. Its affine numerator is $3123q+K$ with one of finitely many fixed offsets $K$, and its denominator is 4096. As before it escapes, its own correction is summable, and the constant corrected ratio to the input is $3123/4096=9\cdot347/2^{12}$. Start all three paths at a mixed free or begin anchor; the older paired shadow can be taken at that very same odd block boundary, regardless of its phase. The two shadow ratios there differ by
$$
\frac{3123/4096}{183/256}
=\frac{3\cdot347}{16\cdot61}.
$$
Neither shadow ratio nor their quotient belongs to the multiplicative group generated by two and three. (In particular, 347 is coprime to $2,3,61$.) Thus no two of the three actual forward trajectories share an ordinary integer. This does not require a common choice of the smaller partners used in the separate least-source deductions.

The proof applies only to escaping input paths. On a bounded cycle the infinite correction series used here diverges. Neither comparison theorem says its own output again obeys the input grammar, so repetition to generate arbitrarily many components does not follow.
