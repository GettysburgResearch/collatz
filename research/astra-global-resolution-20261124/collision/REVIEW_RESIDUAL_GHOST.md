# Independent review of the signed limit on the modulus-36 family

Reviewed [RESIDUAL_GHOST.md](../arithmetic/RESIDUAL_GHOST.md), SHA-256 f5258e750f83ea5c1874fb81ebac95365996395dec317d1b4853b6f60de2a47d, and the simultaneous-fusion input in [PR182 §1](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/4f226f3b278ffe4559110a6ca952ff4a7c56ceba/research/high-variance-closure-20261124/ordinary-arithmetic/PERIODIC_BOUNDARIES.md) at commit 4f226f3b278ffe4559110a6ca952ff4a7c56ceba. This is an independent written review of the new theorem.

1. **Interpolation on every prescribed cylinder.** For $k=r+2^ht$ the physical clocks $h+2$ on $A=36k+8$ and $h+3$ on $B=72k+17$ consume exactly the available initial congruences. Each resulting affine form has coefficient $9\cdot3^p$ in $t$, by the parity-prefix identity with the odd count of the respective arm. The endpoint on the left can equal $(4^j-1)/3$ at arbitrarily large positive lifts because $4$ has order $3^{a}$ modulo $3^{a+1}$ for $a=p+2$. This realizes *all* residues modulo $3^a$, including multiples of three, and each of the chosen endpoints reaches 1. For both arms orienting the smaller slope gives $z,3^sz+c$. The cited §1 supplies an actual fixed common clock on a binary subresidue, and the odd coefficient of $t$ in $z$ makes its pullback a nonempty binary class. Reapplying the left-arm construction there gives infinitely many parameters at which both arms reach 1. The proof needs no independence or assertion of positive density.
2. **Separate clocks.** The two signed traces at $k=-1/3$ project modulo 16 to $\{12,14,15\}$ and $\{9,6,11\}$. In a class $3k+1=0\bmod 2^h$, initial precisions are at least $h+2$ and $h+3$ respectively. Each shortcut step costs at most one bit, so for every $i,j\le h-2$ both residues still match their respective signed traces modulo 16. Hence the first possible value of $\max(i,j)$ is unbounded even along pairs proven to meet. Different pairs can have different subsequent meeting clocks.
3. **Companion interpretation.** The fixed odd tail at $-1$ gives $D(-1)=-3$. Two even prefixes give $D(-4)=-4/3$. At $-7$ the physical period $101$ gives the affine return $d\mapsto17/9+(32/27)d$, with unique fixed point $-51/5$. The positive pairs in item 1 have triadic companions by eventual arrival at 1. Continuity gives the displayed signed limit and refutes the implication $D(A)\in\mathbb Z[1/3]\Rightarrow D(B)\in\mathbb Z[1/3]$ on all dyadic parameters. The separate physical claim about positive parameters remains open. The first signed point has $A=-4$, which never reaches 1.
4. **Literal signed obstruction.** At $k=-34/81$, the affine substitution yields $A=-64/9$ and $B=-119/9$. Six halvings followed by two odd steps send the left arm to 1. The right prefix has parities $1010$, reaches $-7$ after four steps, and then traverses the negative period $101$. Backward companion splitting from 1 gives $D(1/3)=13$ and $D(-1/9)=55/3$. Six even prefixes give $3520/2187$. Splitting backwards from $D(-7)=-51/5$ on the right gives $-34/5,-121/15,-242/45,-833/135$, respectively. Thus the all-dyadic physical implication fails at this second point with its antecedent satisfied.

The assertions above follow at the specified pins. The lower bound concerns both physical clocks bounded by one common deadline. It gives no lower bound on the smaller of the two clocks.
