# Where independent Collatz trajectories first meet

**PROPOSED, source-qualified ordinary-integer theorems. Universal convergence is OPEN.** Use the shortcut map $T(2n)=n, T(2n+1)=3n+2$. All times count shortcut steps. Write $O(x)=\{T^i x:i\ge0\}$, and $x\sim y$ if the two forward orbits intersect. A component is an equivalence class for this relation. The component masses $d_C$ are ordinary natural densities and sum to one under the inputs below. A cyclic component has least shortcut period $p_C$. In every other component all forward paths are injective.

Draw $U,V$ independently and uniformly from $[X,2X)\cap\mathbb N$. Fixed positive-ratio source intervals with possibly different endpoints on the two arms work as well. The claims here do not use independent draws to represent the actual head child and its parent. Logs without subscripts are natural.

**Results independent of any Gaussian clock theorem.** On the event that the two independent paths meet, their lexicographically first common ordinary vertex is tight as $X\to\infty$. Its two hitting clocks differ from the respective times to the global orbit minima by tight errors. On escaping components the probability of a meeting with any prescribed $b$ clock differences is $O(b/\sqrt{\log X})$. At the single deterministic clock
$$
 H_X=\left\lceil \mu\log X+B\sqrt{\log X\log\log X}\right\rceil,\qquad
 \mu=2/\log(4/3),
$$
for a sufficiently large fixed $B$, the probability of equal endpoints tends to $\sum_{C\ {\rm cyclic}}d_C^2/p_C$. This holds for every later deterministic clock as well. With unrestricted time and lag in any fixed finite set $D\subset\mathbb Z$, the limit is $\sum_{C\ {\rm cyclic}} d_C^2\,\lvert D\bmod p_C\rvert/p_C$. Thus these observables distinguish cycles from escaping components. Their proof imports the specific passage, sparsity and fusion estimates below.

**Additional conditional results assuming the joint minimum-clock CLT.** Define $h_X(t)=\lfloor\mu\log X+\sigma t\sqrt{\log X}\rfloor$ for the shortcut-clock coefficient $\sigma$ in the explicit additional assumption below, and write $\Phi$ for the standard Gaussian distribution function. We did not retrieve the full primary Brownian manuscript. These formulas are deductions from the displayed extra assumption.

| Observable | Limiting mass under the additional CLT |
|---|---|
| Meeting by the two separate Gaussian clock quantiles $s,t$ | $\Phi(s)\Phi(t)\sum_C d_C^2$ |
| Equality at the single deterministic clock of quantile $t$ | $\Phi(t)^2\sum_{C\ {\rm cyclic}}d_C^2/p_C$ |
| Eventual meeting with lag at most $L_X\to\infty$, $L_X/(\sigma\sqrt{\log X})\to\lambda\in[0,\infty]$ | $\sum_{C\ {\rm cyclic}}d_C^2 + [2\Phi(\lambda/\sqrt2)-1]\sum_{C\ {\rm escaping}}d_C^2$ |

## 1. A whole prescribed trace is rarely reached before a fixed low passage

Let $a_M(n)$ be the first shortcut clock at an **odd** vertex at most $M$, with infinity on a miss. For every $A>0$, uniformly in the full prescribed ordinary orbit $O(y)$,
$$
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}\Pr\{a_M(U)=\infty\ \text{or}\quad
       T^iU\in O(y)\text{ for some }i\le a_M(U)\}
       \ll_A(\log M)^{-A}.                                      \tag{1}
$$
The bound on the left also holds with the supremum in $y$ **inside** the limsup. It has no sum over the clocks of the prescribed orbit.

Here is the required small-set estimate at the fresh source octave $Y=M^{1.001}$. Put $d=\log_2 M, h=\lfloor .014d\rfloor, b=\lceil .004d\rceil$. The first $h+b+1$ shortcut parity bits of a uniform odd source in $[Y,2Y)$ have total-variation distance $O(2^{h+b+1}/Y)$ from the fair word with its first bit prescribed odd. This is the finite parity bijection and division with remainder. Except on $O(M^{-c_0})$ for some absolute $c_0>0$, at most $.55h$ of the first $h$ bits are odd, and there is no run of $b$ even inputs starting at any of clocks $0,\ldots,h$. Use the binomial tail and $(h+1)2^{-b}$, with a fixed constant for the prescribed first bit. If the trajectory stays above $M$ up to $h$, its exact affine product bounds its terminal height by
$$
  2M^{1.001}3^{.55h}2^{-h}\exp(h/(3M))<M
$$
for large $M$, since $1.001+.014(.55\log_2 3-1)<1$. Thus it enters $[1,M]$ by $h$, and the subsequent halvings give an odd landing by $h+b$.

At most $2^{h+b+1}$ original integers can yield one specified landing with this clock cap. Each length-$k$ word, $0\le k\le h+b$, is affine with positive slope and admits at most one preimage of a fixed endpoint. There are $O(M^{.97})$ vertices of $O(y)$ below $M$. Dividing the combined count by the $\asymp M^{1.001}$ available odd sources gives $O(M^{.97+.018-1.001})$, in addition to the power-small exception. Now use the **single** mark $g=1_{O(y)\cap[1,M]}$ in the relative landing estimate at the fresh and far octaves. Eliminate the common positive functional using the fresh upper bound. The additive error is paid once, even if $y$ changes with the size of the far source. Add the cited miss probability. Forward invariance of $O(y)$ identifies a visit before the landing with membership of the landing itself.

To pass from odd to all sources, split $n=2^s u$ with $u$ odd. On each octave with $X/2^s\ge M^{1.001}$ use the same odd estimate. On the remaining sources use divisibility by a power $\gg X/M^{1.001}$, of mass $O(M^{1.001}/X+1/X)$. A visit in the initial halving segment also persists to the first odd landing. For a fixed bounded-ratio change in the original source interval cover by a bounded number of octaves of comparable total length. This proves (1).

On independent $U,V$ apply (1) in either orientation, conditioning on the entire partner. Except on that same small total probability, both arms visit odd roots $v,w\le M$ before **any** common iterate. From then on the remaining problem is on one of finitely many ordered pairs of ordinary integers. For each pair with intersecting futures, take the lexicographically earliest meeting clocks (minimize the first clock, then the second); take the maximum of both coordinates over all such root pairs. This finite constant may be ineffective. Likewise the time from any root $v\le M$ to its own global minimum is bounded by a finite number depending on $M$. Before its first odd $M$-landing any earlier even hit below $M$ halves down to that odd landing, so the first global minimum is attained at or after this landing for sources that start above $M$. The lexicographically earliest meeting for the original pair is the corresponding earliest meeting for the two roots, shifted by the actual passage clocks. In particular its **ordinary height is tight on the meeting event**, and each of its two clocks differs from that arm's own $\tau_*$ by a quantity tight on that event. On the same good event *all* witnesses have their arm clocks at least $\tau_*(U)-b(M),\tau_*(V)-b(M)$ for a finite $b(M)$.

Thus existence of a rectangular-deadline meeting is squeezed between the same-component events with both minimum clocks below the specified deadlines minus or plus $b(M)$, with limiting error $O_A((\log M)^{-A})$. At fixed $M$, $b(M)/\sqrt{\log X}\to0$. Use the joint one-arm input, independence, and the summable component masses before letting $M\to\infty$. This proves the rectangular-deadline formula under (G). In particular the Gaussian profile multiplies the *entire* meeting mass, including any escaping components. The absolute-error proof does not condition on a vanishingly rare class.

## 2. Physical phase and exact offsets on escaping components

Choose one reference $r_C$ in each component. If $C$ is acyclic, any relation $T^i n=T^j r_C$ gives the same integer $s(n)=i-j$: advancing two such equalities to a common first-arm time and using injectivity of the reference orbit proves uniqueness. In a cyclic component choose the reference on its cycle and take the same quantity modulo $p_C$. In both cases $s(Tn)=s(n)-1$. On one component the full set of feasible differences $i-j$ for common iterates of $n,n'$ is the singleton $s(n)-s(n')$ in the escaping case and its full residue class modulo $p_C$ in the cyclic case. Indeed choose meetings arbitrarily far along the common reference tail; on the cycle arbitrarily many laps can be appended to either arm.

For an absolute estimate keep the record $(C,s(n))$ on all escaping components and give every other start one neutral symbol. Write $S$ for adding one to the integer coordinate, fixing the neutral symbol. On any source interval $I$ of length comparable to $X$ in $[X,6X)$, let $\nu_I$ be the source law with weights proportional to $1/n$. The PR201 one-digit couplings give
$$
 d_{TV}(\nu_{2I},S\nu_I)+d_{TV}(\nu_{3I},\nu_I)\ll (\log X)^{-1/2}.
$$
Use integer intervals with integral endpoints. At $2n+d$, the first step followed by finite-grid synchronous fusion with $n$ adds exactly one to the phase. At $3n+d$, common-clock fusion preserves phase. The fraction of failed comparisons is $O(1/\sqrt h)$ with $h=\lfloor(\log_2X)/2\rfloor$, by the pinned input. Averaging the finitely many digits and replacing $1/(cn)$ by $1/(cn+d)$ costs $O(1/X)$. The proof compares all components simultaneously, including unbounded labels.

For the base-three octave $J=[X,3X)$, its double is the disjoint union $[2X,3X)\cup 3[X,2X)$. Tripling the second piece preserves its unnormalized logarithmic weight up to $O(1/X)$ and preserves its record up to the fusion error. This gives $d_{TV}(\nu_J,\nu_{2J})\ll(\log X)^{-1/2}$, hence the same bound for $d_{TV}(\nu_J,S\nu_J)$. For each escaping component, the two-sided variation of its summable mass sequence is at least twice its largest atom. Sum over components to obtain
$$
 \sum_{C\ {\rm escaping}}\sup_k\Pr_J(C,s=k)\ll(\log X)^{-1/2}.  \tag{2}
$$
Domination by a bounded multiple of this source law extends (2) to the ordinary octave; finitely many octaves suffice for general fixed-ratio windows. Conditional on an independent partner, a chosen list of $b$ allowable differences costs $O(b/\sqrt{\log X})$ for an escaping-component meeting. The list may even be chosen by that partner, with no dependence on the first arm.

For fixed cyclic $C$ and $p=p_C$, every phase has ordinary limiting mass $d_C/p$, also jointly with the Gaussian first-hit-clock mark used in section 3. Here are localization details, since a full logarithmic octave by itself does not establish uniformity on its subintervals. Set $n(u)=\lfloor X3^u\rfloor, 0\le u<1$. For a nontrivial $p$-th root of unity $\zeta$, put $F_X(u)=1_C(n(u))\zeta^{s(n(u))}$. With $\theta=\log_3 2$, the digit comparisons just proved give
$$
   \|F_X(\cdot+\theta)-\zeta F_X\|_{L^1(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)}=O((\log X)^{-1/2}).
$$
Before wrapping the circle, the new integer is a rounded double. On the wrapped interval, first compare the unwrapped rounded double to the triple of the wrapped integer plus its digit. The exceptional cell weights are $O(1/X)$, multiplicities bounded, and the tests use the full fixed-digit exceptional sets irrespective of the rounding digits. Every Fourier coefficient of $F_X$ therefore tends to zero: no $e^{2\pi i k\theta}$ is a nontrivial root of unity. Density of trigonometric polynomials and uniform boundedness give weak convergence to zero against every fixed $L^1$ test. A bounded source weight and subinterval follow by integration over the source cells, with $O(1/X)$ endpoint error for ordinary counts. Finite Fourier inversion on the cycle, and component density for the trivial character, give the claim. This yields the fixed-lag formula in the opening statement using (2) and independence; truncate the component list before taking the limit. The formula is for existence of a meeting, and counts a pair once even when several differences coincide modulo $p$.

For a growing consecutive lag window the cyclic factor tends to one on each fixed cycle. On an escaping component, $s(n)=\tau_*(n)+s(m(n))$ exactly. The minimum has a tight, countable limiting law, so $s(m(n))/\sqrt{\log X}\to0$ in probability on escape (truncate to finitely many minima). The difference of two independent normalized phases, on any specified common escaping component, therefore has limiting Gaussian variance two, with unnormalized mass $d_C^2$. This proves the last table entry, including its two limiting cases by tightness and continuity. No assertion about a Gaussian *lattice* local limit is used.

## 3. Single-clock energy and the remaining global inequality

Let $p_{X,h}(z)=\Pr(T^hU=z)$ and $E_X(h)=\sum_zp_{X,h}(z)^2$. This is the probability of equality at that single clock on iid sources. Fix a cyclic component $C$, its reference point $a$ on the cycle, and let $t_a(n)$ and $e_C(n)$ be its first arrival at $a$ and at the cycle. They differ by at most $p_C-1$. On $\{C,m(n)\le M\}$ the difference between $t_a(n)$ and $\tau_*(n)$ is bounded by a constant depending only on $C,M$: after the minimum its forward path is fixed, or, if $a$ was visited first, the minimum is on the cycle and reached within one lap. Thus the centered clock $t_a$ has the same unnormalized Gaussian limit on $C$.

The phase-equidistribution proof of section 2 also permits multiplying $F_X$ by any fixed bounded Lipschitz function of $(t_a(n)-\mu\log X)/(\sigma\sqrt{\log X})$ on $C$. In the digit comparisons all prefixes through $h+1\le(\log_2X)/2+1$ exceed the fixed $a$ for large $X$. Hence the first-hit clock changes by precisely one for doubling and zero for tripling on their successful comparisons. The extra Lipschitz error is $O(1/\sqrt{\log X})$. The zero Fourier mode in the *phase* variable is identified by the Gaussian limit; use continuity to approximate a quantile indicator. It follows for every point $z$ of this cycle that
$$
  p_{X,h_X(t)}(z)\longrightarrow d_C\Phi(t)/p_C.
$$
The point is determined by the initial phase and $h_X(t)\bmod p_C$ once $e_C\le h_X(t)$; replacing $e_C$ by $t_a$ changes the threshold by at most one period. There are finitely many residues, so the variation of $h_X(t)\bmod p_C$ is harmless.

For a transient $z\in C$, the event $T^hU=z$ forces $t_a(U)=h+t_a(z)$, because a trajectory which already visited $a$ stays on its cycle. The largest atom of $t_a(U)$ on this component is $o(1)$ by the continuous limiting distribution after normalization (use a finite grid of small-mass Gaussian intervals and truncate the tails). On an escaping component, (2) bounds even the probability of an all-future synchronous collision. In passing to the sum of squares, first retain finitely many components carrying all but an arbitrarily small mass: the total contribution from the complement is at most its ordinary mass. This proves the single-clock Gaussian formula under (G). For $H_X$ or any later clock the fixed-floor deadline of PR193 and finiteness of the remaining route on each of finitely many floor roots shows that each fixed cyclic component has already entered its cycle except on $o(1)$ total mass (allow one more fixed unit in $B$). Hence its transient contribution vanishes and its phases are uniform by section 2 without requiring the Gaussian input.

There is an explicit positive arithmetic expression for the missing global lower bound. For the ordinary uniform octave of size $X$, $E_X(0)=1/X$. At any physical step the bucket at $y$ receives mass from $2y$ and, exactly when $y\equiv2\pmod3$, from the odd positive integer $(2y-1)/3$. Expanding the squares and summing yields the exact finite formula
$$
 E_X(H)=X^{-1}+2\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\sum_{y\equiv2\ (3)}
       p_{X,h}(2y)p_{X,h}((2y-1)/3).                              \tag{3}
$$
At every finite horizon the sums have finite support; the two summands in a bucket account for *independent* original samples. This also gives a central clock profile of the cumulative sum under the Gaussian input. The shortcut has no positive fixed point (check both branches), and its known cycle has period two. Therefore $\sum_{C\ {\rm cyclic}}d_C^2/p_C\le1/2$, with equality only when the full mass is on a single period-two component. The fixed-target positivity input gives positive mass to the component of one and to any other component that exists (strip twos and take an odd transition). Under that input the inequality
$$
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}\sum_{h<H_X}\sum_{y\equiv2\ (3)}
       p_{X,h}(2y)p_{X,h}((2y-1)/3)\ \ge\ 1/4                    \tag{4}
$$
is equivalent to the full positive Collatz conjecture. The matching upper limit follows above. The lower bound is **OPEN**. Formula (3) replaces experiments about eventual merging with a finite two-branch sum, but the branch-pair masses inside it have not been made sufficiently large. Neither independence of two separate starts nor marginal equidistribution gives a uniform lower bound on these products at the paired preimages of a specified $y$.

A period-sensitive finite observation recovers more information than (3). For each fixed integer $k\ge1$, intersect the two events $T^{H_X}U=T^{H_X}V$ and $T^{H_X+k}U=T^{H_X}V$. Their common endpoint must be in $\operatorname{Fix}(T^k)$, and this is sufficient. There are at most $2^k$ such ordinary integers, since each parity word gives an affine map with slope $3^p/2^k\ne1$. Consequently the limiting simultaneous probability is
$$
  D(k)=\sum_{C:\ p_C\mid k}d_C^2/p_C.
$$
Ordinary divisor inversion gives the sum of squared masses divided by period for each *exact* period. For $k=2$, the only positive fixed points of $T^2$ are $1,2$ (the four pairs of branches, also proved in Lean), so the limit is $d_{C(1)}^2/2$. This conclusion uses no Gaussian assumption. It does not determine the arithmetic size of any of these probabilities.

## 4. What synchronized long blocks contribute

Apply [PR202, 3b10b24d12d4b349cb20f4cbc83086c8a09f807e, sections 2–4](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/3b10b24d12d4b349cb20f4cbc83086c8a09f807e/research/synchronized-long-basin-blocks-20261124/README.md), with its synchronization, routing, and fixed-target-density inputs. Fix an odd unit $a$ in a hypothetical escaping component and a fixed macroscopic interval comparable to $[X,2X)$. For $M_X=\lfloor\exp((\log X)^\delta)\rfloor, 0<\delta<1/2$, the construction selects distinct roots $q$ that reach $a$, with a block $B_q$ of $M_X$ consecutive starts satisfying $T^H x=q$ for **every** $x\in B_q$. The same $H=o(\log X)$ works for every selected root. Before the greedy thinning in PR202, its fixed-ratio root count is $\gg_a Y$ in an interval comparable to $Y=X/K=X^{1+o(1)}$. All blocks for distinct $q$ are already disjoint: an integer in two would have two values at the same clock.

Choose a point uniformly from the union of these constructed blocks. Its root is thus uniform over the selected $q$'s, without a weight depending on the root. On this union $s(x)=H+s(q)$ exactly. Domination of the root selection by $O_a(1)$ times an ordinary fixed-ratio source law at scale $Y$, **before** transport, now proves
$$
  \sup_k\Pr(s(x)=k\mid x\in\bigcup_q B_q)\ll_a(\log X)^{-1/2}.
$$
It costs no reciprocal of the possibly vanishing fraction of the original $x$-interval covered by the blocks. In particular this constructed family in an escaping component comprises at least $\gg_a\sqrt{\log X}$ different physical phases, even though every single block meets simultaneously. Two independent uniform members of the union have all-future synchronous collision probability $O_a(1/\sqrt{\log X})$; their collision probability with unrestricted lags is exactly one. Thus the synchronized blocks themselves do not give the lower bound in (4) through treating different blocks as one physical phase. For a shrinking root interval the positive-proportion domination used here requires its own density loss, and we claim this conditional bound only for fixed macroscopic intervals. PR195's directed actual-head transition and PR201's killed-profile bounds concern the dependent head coupling. No tensor product estimate for that coupling enters (1)–(4).
