## One prescribed ordinary source

On a length-$i$ physical prefix, the formal trial and the true value $-D(x_i)/3$ differ by $(D(n)-a/b)/(3A_i)$. Thus agreement of order $2K$ gives order at least $2(K-i)$ at time $i$. For $i<K$ there are two bits left, sufficient to distinguish the only legal branch residues (even or $1\bmod4$). The trial remains dyadically integral at $i=K$. Since its sole odd-denominator operations are multiplication by three and addition of an integer before division by a power of two, the factor $3^{h+1}$ in the initial denominator is gone by time $h+1$. Multiplication by $m$ then clears all denominators throughout the remaining legal interval. Nothing requires the candidate numerator to be nonnegative.

If no physical value below 20 has been seen, the exact multiplier with positive increments gives $2^ix_i\le n(61/20)^{P_i}$ and $\log A_i\ge i\log(256/243)/5-(3/5)\log n$. The telescoping formula for $B_i$ gives $w_i\ge1/4$ at every sufficiently late index through the legal horizon. Multiplication of positive trial steps starting at that index forces at least $ct-O(N)$ visits to one fixed bounded interval before time $t$. The upper bound at time $J=O(N)$ controls the subtracted logarithm, and the lower bound at the final time controls its added logarithm.

Consider a bounded endpoint with a $k$-step past lying entirely in the cleared grid, where $3^k>Bm$. Reduction of the full affine equation modulo $3^k$ specifies its numerator $mw_i$ from the ordered word. Its allowed positive interval contains at most one representative. The positive source $w_{i-k}\ge1/4$ also forces $u\ge(\log_2 3-1)k-O_B(1)$ ones. This lies strictly above $k/2$ at large $k$. The ratio of binomial coefficients in that tail is bounded by a fixed number less than one and Stirling supplies the stated square-root logarithmic saving. The initial $k$ discarded samples cost $O(N)$, already allowed. Choosing the absolute multiplier of $N+F(m)$ sufficiently large yields an actual repeat.

On a trial repeat, the same rational value has the same permitted next physical bit; both paths are legal for the asserted comparison length. Equal physical starts at the two repeat times give an all-time repetition, and the residual term vanishes dyadically because $v_2(A_i)\ge i$ and the periodic phases are integral. Different starts give $2^{K-j}\mid x_j-x_i$ by subtraction of the two physical affine equations. Their positive sizes bound this nonzero difference by $(3/2)^j(n+1)$, as used. If a small value occurred before the repetition argument, direct shortcut iteration from $1,\ldots,19$ reaches $1$ in at most 14 steps. The exact two-cycle gives $D(1)=1+(8/9)D(1)=9$. The cross numerator for two unequal rationals is then nonzero, and its binary logarithm yields the claimed bound. This establishes the arbitrary-denominator statement for incorrect candidates, including all signs and the small-horizon alternative.

## One primitive physical cycle or one fixed escaping source

For a closed word with positive companion gap $\Gamma$, the common odd denominator divides $2^{L+p}-3^L$ and is coprime to six. The least positive phase has an odd incoming bit because the even branch increases a positive phase. Thus $4w_{\min}\ge3w_{\min}+1$. Repeated rational phases would have identical future bits, contradicting primitivity within a lap. The product of the companion correction factors supplies a positive fraction of the $L$ distinct phases in a fixed bounded interval whenever $\Gamma\ge\eta L$. Periodic extension supplies an arbitrary past in the same grid at every such phase; there is no discarded initial segment, even if $k>L$. Equation (4) now proves (5), and taking logarithms before inverting retains the factor $(\log L)^{1/(2s)}$.

For a physical cycle the primitive parity period agrees with the primitive point period: a proper repeated prefix would have an affine iterate of positive slope with a periodic real point, which its single block must already fix. A nontrivial positive cycle stays at least 20, giving $\Gamma\ge\delta L$ on the lap. On a fixed escape a proper-power prefix of length $L$ and block size $r\le L/2$ would give $2^{L-r}\le |x_r-n|<(3/2)^r(n+1)$. Hence $(4/3)^r<n+1$, bounding $r$ and then $L$ in terms of the fixed start. Every sufficiently long prefix therefore qualifies *after periodization*. This reasoning does not equate its auxiliary rational companion with the genuine escaping scalar.

## Physical fixed clocks and the two full divisors

For the physical divisor, the least absolute phase is odd. The logarithmic product bounds it by $(1+p/g)/3$ for both signs. In the negative case each odd magnitude exceeds $1/3$, and the signed logarithmic correction uses $-\log(1-1/(3y))$. At a fixed denominator, the binary prefix fixes the signed minimum numerator modulo $2^k$; the ordered terminal odd gaps fix the same numerator modulo $3^r$. The whole interval of possible numerators has one sign and magnitude between 1 and $YQ$, so equality of the residue gives a unique rational minimum, including equality at the cutoff. Each minimum fixes its entire marked orbit up to at most $A$ rotations.

The Bernoulli probability of the whole marked word at the exact weight is $e^{-Ah(u)}$. Dividing this by the largest possible missing suffix weight gives the lower mass of each extendible binary prefix. The corresponding geometric probability of all $p$ gaps of total $A$ is also $e^{-Ah(u)}$. Each missing gap has mass at most $u$, giving the required lower bound on each terminal tuple's mass. This is a counting argument in two auxiliary laws. There is no sampling hypothesis on the orbit of one physical integer. Lagarias's Lemma 2.2(1), (2.4), gives the quoted polynomial bound after multiplication by $A\log3$. For interior densities, the unused bit or gap horizon is at least an $\epsilon$-dependent positive multiple of $A$ less $O(\log A)$. The divisor count and the cost of conditioning on the exact clocks are subexponential. The imprimitive fraction is exponentially small since $h(u)$ is uniformly positive. [Lagarias, Lemma 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0512006)

For the companion divisor the past fixes the numerator modulo $3^r$ with $r$ physical bits, since every numerator multiplier is three. On the other side the first crossing of the clock $j+P_j$ gives disjoint prefix cylinders with overshoot at most one, leaving at least $(A+p-k-1)/2$ unused physical bits. For negative companion phases with least magnitude at most $1/3$ the root-height bound is immediate; otherwise all its logarithmic factors in the product comparison are well defined. This proves the companion estimate at exact clocks. A union bound suffices for the simultaneous conclusion.

For the raw gcd, the two rational units have exponent vectors $(A,-p)$ and $(A+p,-A)$. A vanishing determinant would make $p/A$ the irrational positive root of $u^2+u-1$. Corvaja and Zannier's Proposition 2, (2.8), has exceptional multiplicative subgroups through the identity when both targets are one. Multiplicative independence excludes them all. The sum over primes outside $2,3$ of the negated local contribution is precisely the logarithm of the complete positive gcd. The remaining places only enlarge the upper estimate, and the two heights are $O(A)$. The uniform $o(A)$ follows by taking each fixed positive error first. The statement does not give an effective threshold for all independent points. [Corvaja and Zannier, Proposition 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0311030)

## Checks and boundary

An independent exact-rational enumeration checked the two physical and companion return equations, unchanged reduced denominators, and all literal phase residues on the 4,072 nonconstant marked words of lengths 2 through 11. A separate enumeration on all 8,166 nonconstant marked words of lengths 2 through 12 checked uniqueness of rational minima for 22,697 binary-prefix and 3,413 terminal-gap encodings whenever the respective modulus was at least the actual numerator magnitude. No physical or companion denominator estimate depends on these checks.

No mathematical correction to the pinned claims was required. The two separate uses of primitivity and the order of inversion in (5) are made explicit above. The individual lower bound concerns the companion denominator and allows it to grow. The exponentially small exceptional *fraction* for the physical divisor can still contain the full set of representations of a hypothetical positive integer cycle. This review does not certify universal descent, rationality of any fixed escaping scalar, or a Lean proof of the asymptotic inequalities.
