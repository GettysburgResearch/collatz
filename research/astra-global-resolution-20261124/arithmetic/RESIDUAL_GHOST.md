# A signed limit of proven convergent pairs on the last affine condition

**PROPOSED written theorem.** Write \(A(k)=36k+8\), \(B(k)=72k+17\), \(k\ge0\). The [single-return reduction](../rewriting/SEVEN_RETURN.md) asks for \(A(k)\in C\Rightarrow B(k)\in C\) for the ordinary basin \(C\) of 1. Every binary residue class of \(k\) contains infinitely many positive parameters with a certified convergent left arm. Using the pinned elementary pair-fusion result below, the same holds with **both arms certified convergent**. Even among the latter pairs, there is no uniform finite deadline for their first meeting with separate shortcut clocks. Their dyadic companions have a limit with a triadic first coordinate and a nontriadic second coordinate. These are properties of a sequence of changing ordinary parameters.

**Elementary interpolation of the left arm.** Fix \(k=k_0+2^h t\), \(0\le k_0<2^h\). Put \(v_0=9k_0+2\), \(u=T^h(v_0)\), and let \(p\) be its odd count through those \(h\) steps. The parity-cylinder affine identity gives
\[
 T^h(9k+2)=u+3^{p+2}t .
\]
For \(j\ge1\) the odd positive integer \(w_j=(4^j-1)/3\) reaches 1, since \(3w_j+1=4^j\). These \(w_j\) realize every class modulo \(3^{p+2}\) infinitely often: \(4\) generates the subgroup \(1\bmod3\) modulo \(3^{p+3}\). Indeed \(v_3(4^{3^m}-1)=m+1\) by induction, cubing \(1+3^{m+1}z\) with \(3\nmid z\). This gives the full subgroup order, and one may add arbitrarily many such orders to \(j\). Choose \(w_j\equiv u\bmod 3^{p+2}\). For all sufficiently large such \(j\), take \(t=(w_j-u)/3^{p+2}>0\). Now \(A(k)=4(9k+2)\) has a finite certified path to 1. This construction requires neither a density estimate nor a universal convergence assumption.

**Optional simultaneous interpolation.** For the fixed original binary class, \(T^{h+2}A(k)\) and \(T^{h+3}B(k)\) are affine integer forms in \(t\), of slopes \(9\cdot3^{p_1}\) and \(9\cdot3^{p_2}\), respectively. This follows from \(36=4\cdot9,\ 72=8\cdot9\) and the same parity-cylinder identity. Denote the lower-slope form by \(z\); the other is \(3^s z+c\) with \(s=|p_1-p_2|\) and an integer \(c\). The exact interface in PR182 §1 gives a binary sub-residue class of \(z\) and a fixed common clock at which these two forms fuse for all sufficiently large positive lifts. The coefficient of \(t\) in \(z\) is odd, so pulling back that class gives one nonempty binary sub-residue class of \(t\), hence a refinement of the original class of \(k\). Apply the elementary left-arm construction anew on that refinement. Its arbitrarily large convergent left arms share a future with their right arms, proving the simultaneous assertion. The two original clocks can differ by one. [PR182, frozen §1 and elementary transition proof](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/4f226f3b278ffe4559110a6ca952ff4a7c56ceba/research/high-variance-closure-20261124/ordinary-arithmetic/PERIODIC_BOUNDARIES.md)

**Limits on these actual positive pairs.** For each \(h\), prescribe \(3k+1=0\bmod2^h\). Along any such choices \(k_h\to-1/3\) in \(\mathbb Z_2\) and
\[
 (A(k_h),B(k_h))\longrightarrow(-4,-7).
\]
The signed forward traces are \(-4,-2,-1,-1,\ldots\) and \(-7,-10,-5,-7,\ldots\). Their residues modulo 16 are disjoint. A pair of inputs congruent modulo \(2^m\) remains congruent modulo \(2^{m-i}\) through step \(i\le m\), by the common-prefix affine identity. Here \(A(k_h)+4=12(3k_h+1)\) and \(B(k_h)+7=24(3k_h+1)\). In particular for \(h\ge4\), *no* ordinary meeting is possible with both clocks at most \(h-2\). Choosing the simultaneous interpolation at every \(h\) still guarantees eventual ordinary meeting and convergence.

For the continuous dyadic companion \(D\) in the [pointwise note](README.md), splitting gives \(D(x)=(2/3)D(Tx)\) on an even bit and \(D(x)=1+(4/3)D(Tx)\) on an odd bit. The all-odd ray gives \(D(-1)=-3\), hence \(D(-4)=-4/3\). The period \(101\) from \(-7\) gives \(D(-7)=1+8/9+(32/27)D(-7)\), hence \(D(-7)=-51/5\). Thus companion pairs from **positive inputs on which both arms converge**, each pair belonging to \(\mathbb Z[1/3]^2\), approach \((-4/3,-51/5)\). Triadic membership on the right is not closed under these limits even when the left limit stays triadic. This precludes extending the triadic-companion implication to all dyadic parameters. It also rules out one parameter-independent physical meeting cutoff under the convergent-left-arm antecedent. The signed input -4 does not physically reach 1, so the signed limit is not a counterexample to the literal basin implication on all dyadic inputs. It supplies no fixed positive counterexample. The rational comparison bound at a fixed source still has a source-height cost; the 291-leaf reduction supplies no compatible successful rational candidate for the right child in this construction.

**A signed parameter whose left arm reaches 1.** At \(k=-34/81\in\mathbb Z_2\), the two arms are \(-64/9\) and \(-119/9\). The first takes six even steps to \(-1/9\), followed by the two odd steps \(-1/9\to1/3\to1\). The second follows
\[
 -119/9\longrightarrow-58/3\longrightarrow-29/3\longrightarrow-14\longrightarrow-7\longrightarrow-10\longrightarrow-5\longrightarrow-7 .
\]
It therefore stays on the negative three-cycle after its fourth step. The companion recursion, starting from \(D(1)=9\) and \(D(-7)=-51/5\), gives respectively \(D(-64/9)=3520/2187\) and \(D(-119/9)=-833/135\). This parameter disproves the literal implication “if \(A(k)\) reaches 1, then \(B(k)\) reaches 1” on all of \(\mathbb Z_2\). The modulus-36 criterion concerns positive ordinary inputs, so this signed obstruction leaves its stated implication open.
