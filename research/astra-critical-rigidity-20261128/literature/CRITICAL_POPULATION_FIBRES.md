# Macroscopic endpoint fibres for a whole critical family of Collatz inputs

Fix \(K>0\), \(t=\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor\), \(m=1+\lfloor K\sqrt t\rfloor\). For each \(0\le k<m\) take \(x_k=3^k x+D_{N,k}\) with integer offsets satisfying \(\max_k\log(2+|D_{N,k}|/3^k)=o(t)\). Choose \(x\) uniformly on any interval of \(N\) consecutive positive integers on which all arms are positive. Give the labels \(k/\sqrt t\) equal masses \(1/m\). Write \(W_k,Y_k\) for the adjusted grade and actual log-height paths in the [finite-family theorem, (1)](COALESCING_ORDINARY_HEIGHTS.md). At a fixed native fraction \(u\in(0,1]\), let \(P_N(u)\) be the decreasing sequence of the cardinalities of the distinct **numerical** endpoint fibres of \(k\mapsto T^{\lfloor ut\rfloor}x_k\), divided by \(m\) and padded by zeros.

Consider coalescing Brownian motions \(V(a)\) of variance rate \(1/4\) from the interval \([0,K]\). Use normalized Lebesgue measure \(da/K\) there. The empirical measure assigning mass \(1/m\) to each \((k/\sqrt t,W_k,Y_k)\) converges **in distribution as a random probability measure** to the pushforward by \(a\mapsto(a,V(a),V(a))\). Jointly, at any fixed finite collection of observation times \(u>0\), the vectors \(P_N(u)\) converge in distribution in \(\ell^1\) to \(P(u)\), the decreasing masses of the Brownian endpoint fibres at those times. All limits hold along arbitrary interval placements.

For example the limiting mean probability that two independently sampled labels have the same actual endpoint is
\[
   \lim_N\mathbb E\sum_i P_{N,i}(u)^2
      =\frac{2}{K^2}\int_0^K (K-y)\operatorname{erfc}(y/\sqrt u)\,dy.        \tag{1}
\]
The limiting fibre law has finitely many positive masses almost surely. The expected number of its positive masses is \(1+2K/\sqrt{\pi u}\). The convergence is for masses; the discrete systems may still carry additional singleton or other vanishing-mass fibres.

## Construction and sampling proof

Construct a consistent family of coalescing Brownian paths from a nested countable dense set containing 0 and \(K\), using the finite-dimensional construction. For any deterministic \(a\), the paths from rational approximants just below and above \(a\) meet before \(u>0\) with probability approaching one: the nonmeeting probability equals \(H_u(h)=\operatorname{erf}(h/\sqrt u)\) for initial gap \(h\). Sandwiching and monotonicity provide the measurable version at Lebesgue-almost every initial label. More explicitly, at every rational time \(u>0\) take the monotone left and right limits; they coincide at all except countably many initial points. For a fixed initial label use nested approximants and a summable sequence of pair nonmeeting probabilities at times decreasing to zero. Thus its path is well defined for positive times almost surely and extends continuously to its given starting point: on each sufficiently small interval it is sandwiched between continuous paths from fixed nearby rational starts. Fubini gives the pushforward on the interval. Its fixed finite samples at distinct deterministic points have the classical coalescing law, by the same squeezing or the standard Brownian web construction. [Fontes, Isopi, Newman and Ravishankar, *The Brownian web: characterization and convergence*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0311254)

At a fixed positive time the number of different values on the dyadic subdivision of \([0,K]\) of size \(2^j\) intervals has expectation
\[
                 1+2^j H_u(K/2^j)\longrightarrow1+2K/\sqrt{\pi u}.
\]
Indeed order identifies the count as one plus the number of unequal consecutive pairs. The numbers increase on refinement. Hence the dense set of dyadic labels has only finitely many endpoint values, with this expected count. Every one of these values has positive Lebesgue preimage almost surely. For each of the countably many dyadic starts, one-sided dyadic neighbors in the interval meet it before \(u\) almost surely for some positive distance, since their nonmeeting probability tends to zero. Countable intersection applies also at 0 and \(K\) with the appropriate one-sided neighbor. Monotonicity makes the intervening interval part of the same fibre. The a.e. extension above introduces no further positive-mass value.

Draw finitely many independent uniform real labels on \([0,K]\), independently of the source. If \(U\) is such a label, assign it index \(\min(m-1,\lfloor mU/K\rfloor)\). This produces independent uniform arithmetic indices, whose scaled values converge to their real labels and are eventually distinct almost surely. For each fixed realization of these distinct limiting labels the finite-family theorem applies, with convergence of marked joint paths and all pairwise equalities at the chosen dates. Integrating bounded tests gives convergence for every fixed finite sample. This argument permits an arbitrary triangular choice of all offsets satisfying the uniform assumption.

Here are details sufficient to identify the distributions of the random measures and the ranked masses. The law of one sampled triple of label and paths is tight by the preceding convergence. The random empirical measures are tight in the space of probability measures with weak topology. Indeed take compact sets with expected outside masses at most \(\varepsilon 2^{-2j}\), then use Markov's inequality at thresholds \(2^{-j}\) and the compact-containment criterion for probability measures. Products of integrals of bounded continuous tests are expectations of products evaluated at conditionally independent labels. Such moments determine the distribution of a random probability measure, using a countable convergence-determining class of tests and bounded real moments. Their limits are exactly the Brownian pushforward moments.

For ranked masses use the compact product-topology simplex \(p_1\ge p_2\ge\cdots\ge0,\ \sum_i p_i\le1\). Each power sum \(S_r(p)=\sum_i p_i^r\), \(r\ge2\), is continuous there since \(p_i\le1/i\) gives uniformly vanishing tails. Conditional on a source, it is the probability that \(r\) uniform labels share one endpoint. Independent groups of sampled labels give all products of power sums at different observation times, together with arbitrary path-integral moments. Thus all those moments converge. The power sums distinguish points of the product simplex: they determine the finite measure \(\sum_i p_i^2\delta_{p_i}\) on \([0,1]\) by its polynomial moments, hence every positive mass with its multiplicity. These bounded separating functions determine the joint law by Stone–Weierstrass on the compact ranked coordinates and the countable bounded path tests above. The limiting Brownian vectors have sum one. Coordinate convergence to a mass-one vector from vectors of sum one implies \(\ell^1\) convergence, by choosing a finite initial segment of near-full limiting sum. Applying this on a subsequence representation gives the asserted stronger topology in distribution. Formula (1) follows by integration of the Brownian pair hitting probability.

## Two actual towers and the small-scale interface count

The grade-path and numerical-fibre conclusions also hold on each fixed tower \(X_n^\pm=c+M3^{\pm n}\), with \(c\) integral, \(M\) a positive integer, and \(n\) chosen uniformly from any \(N\) consecutive nonnegative indices. Use the ascending family \(x_k=X_{n+k}^+\) or the reversed descending family \(x_k=X_{n+m-1-k}^-\). They start as \(x_k=3^k x_0+(1-3^k)c\). In the ascending case one also has the real log-height conclusion centred at \(\log_2 x_0\), discarding finitely many nonpositive starts. We assert grade paths and physical equality partitions for the descending rationals.

For the tower transfer the base \(x_0\) runs periodically through two depth-\(d_0=v_2(M)+3\) cosets, each once at every finer complete period. Condition a Haar base on either coset. Reveal its first \(d_0\) bits. Each individual residual arm on that coset is Haar since its affine slope on the remaining binary variable is odd. For a pair with exponent gap \(h\) and fixed first words with grades \(g_i,g_j\), the new signed gap is \(s'=h+g_j-g_i=h+O(d_0)\). Its normalized offset equals
\[
    2^{-d_0}\{3^{g_i}(1-3^{-h})(-c)+C_j/3^{s'}-C_i\},
\]
which is bounded for a pair of separated limiting labels. This follows from the exact two prefix affine equations in the original, correctly oriented pair. The \(d_0\) initial native increments change the scaled paths by \(O(d_0/\sqrt t)\). All the conditional future martingale and bracket arguments now apply to each fixed distinct sample. The discrepancy for any shortened joint history at \(j=t-\lceil2\log_2(t+2)\rceil\) is \(O(2^j/N)\), since the allowed cosets have one common period \(2^{j-v_2(M)-2}\). The full-period majorant handles the terminal equality indicator as in the finite-family theorem. The [unequal-slope tower equation (4)](TERNARY_TOWER_SYNCHRONIZATION.md) excludes accidental coincidences apart from \(O_{c,M}(t+1)\) exponent indices per pair. For ascending real heights, remove the \(O_{c,M}(t)\) smallest exponent indices so that \(x_0\ge N^{3/4}\); the same prefix carry estimate under the fixed cosets gives (1) of the finite-family theorem. Applying the sampling proof gives the tower claims.

There is a measurable difference between resolving a whole ascending or descending power tower and resolving its macroscopic Brownian interfaces. Write \(B_N\) for the number of consecutive labels with unequal actual endpoints at date \(t\). For \(r\ge1\) inspect only the \(r+1\) equally spaced labels rounded to the discrete mesh, and denote their unequal-neighbor count by \(B_{N,r}\). By the uniform [adjacent tower tail](TERNARY_TOWER_SYNCHRONIZATION.md), the finite critical pair law, and linearity of expectation,
\[
  \lim_{r\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb E(B_N-B_{N,r})
      =K\left(\kappa(c,M)-\frac2{\sqrt\pi}\right)\ge0.                  \tag{2}
\]
Here \(B_{N,r}\le B_N\) deterministically. The limits count boundaries with multiplicity. They do not identify the probability of an isolated hole from its expected count.

For detail on the excess, let \(D^*_v=1+g_2(v)-g_1(v)\) be the signed adjusted grade difference for the adjacent oriented tower pair under its two-coset Haar law. Both arms have the same marginal law because multiplication of the power part by three preserves those cosets. Thus \(\mathbb E D^*_v=1\) at every date. At \(v\ge d_0\) canonical orientation has level \(|D^*_v|\) and all fresh labels are fair. The exact drift of that absolute level is one at a level-zero departure and zero otherwise. Leen's capacity \(h(s,D)=s+\mathbb E M\) counts all remaining departures. Telescoping expected drift from \(d_0\), then taking the monotone limit of the expected departure counts, gives
\[
  \kappa(c,M)=\frac2{\sqrt\pi}\lim_{v\to\infty}\mathbb E|D^*_v|
   =\frac2{\sqrt\pi}\left(1+2\lim_{v\to\infty}\mathbb E(-D^*_v)_+\right). \tag{3}
\]
This supplies computable monotone lower bounds after date \(d_0\), and in particular \(\kappa(c,M)\ge2/\sqrt\pi\) for every tower. [Leen, *Exact rates in Collatz synchronization*, harmonic capacity and its one-step drift](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23268751)

For the unshifted powers \(c=0,M=1\) the two equiprobable residual states at depth three are \((1,-2),(1,-1)\). From \((1,-1)\) an odd lower base gives a down-step to the scalar offset \(-1\), which departs at the next date. From \((1,-2)\) an even lower base gives \((1,-1)\) with fresh residual base, followed by the same event. The future expected departure counts are therefore at least \(1/4,1/2\), respectively. In particular
\[
                     \kappa(0,1)\ge 11/(4\sqrt\pi)>2/\sqrt\pi.
\]
The macroscopic Brownian endpoint masses and a strictly positive expected excess of fine boundaries in (2) coexist in this actual power family.
