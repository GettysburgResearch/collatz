# Independent Collatz path fluctuations for arithmetically distinct multipliers

Write \(T\) for the shortcut map, \(\gamma=\log_2 3\), \(b=1-\gamma/2\), and \(t=\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor\). Fix positive integers \(A_1,\ldots,A_d\). Choose \(x\) uniformly from any interval of \(N\) consecutive positive integers. The actual paths
\[
    Y_i(k/t)=\frac{\log_2 T^k(A_i x)-\log_2(A_i x)+bk}{\gamma\sqrt t},
          \qquad 0\le k\le t,                                              \tag{1}
\]
linearly interpolated, converge jointly in \(C([0,1])^d\). The limit places one Brownian motion of variance rate \(1/4\) on each equivalence class of multipliers modulo \(2^{\mathbb Z}3^{\mathbb Z}\). Every coordinate in the same class reads that same path. All the class motions are independent. The convergence holds along every sequence of interval positions. For example \(x,5x,25x,125x\) give four independent paths in the limit, under one ordinary integer sample.

The following stronger version explains the distinction between multipliers. Choose fixed odd positive representatives \(c_b\) with distinct classes modulo \(3^{\mathbb Z}\), and form a fixed finite family of arms \(y_i=c_{b(i)}3^{s_i}x+D_i\). Here \(D_i\in\mathbb Z\), \(s_i\) are nonnegative integers with \(s_i/\sqrt t\to a_i\ge0\), and \(\max_i\log(2+|D_i|/3^{s_i})=o(t)\). Use any positive interval as above on which all arms are positive. The adjusted grades \((s_i+g_i(k)-k/2)/\sqrt t\) and the true log heights \((\log_2 T^k y_i-\log_2 x+bk)/(\gamma\sqrt t)\) converge jointly to the same finite system. Within each group \(b\) one has coalescing Brownian motions started at the \(a_i\), including immediate identification of coincident starting points. The different groups are independent. At each fixed positive observation time through 1 the actual equality partition within each group has its coalescing Brownian limit; different groups have no numerical meetings through the full deadline with probability tending to one. The following proof of vanishing cross-covariation for different groups holds for arbitrary offsets in \(\mathbb Z_2\) under Haar.

## The compact pair kernel for an odd unit slope

Let \(\alpha\) be a fixed odd dyadic unit and set \(X(s)=\alpha3^s\) for \(s\in\mathbb Z_2\), with the continuous power \(3^{2j}=9^j\), \(3^{2j+1}=3\cdot9^j\). On \(E=\mathbb Z_2^2\) use coordinates \((s,D)\) for the oriented affine pair \(z,X(s)z+D\). At a fresh fair lower parity \(e\), set \(e'=(e+D)\bmod2\). The direct affine update is
\[
 s'=s+e'-e,\qquad 2D'=3^{e'}D+e'-eX(s').
                                                                            \tag{2}
\]
Orientation stays fixed. This defines a Feller two-branch kernel \(P_\alpha\) on the compact state space. Product Haar \(\Lambda\) is invariant under each individual branch: splitting \(D\) into its even and odd halves makes the new offset an odd affine bijection of the free tail, and the level is translated by a constant on each half.

The stationary probability measures of this kernel are exactly \(\Lambda\) if \(1\notin X(\mathbb Z_2)\), and the convex combinations of \(\Lambda\) and \(\delta_{(s_*,0)}\) if \(X(s_*)=1\). The latter \(s_*\) is unique since the continuous power is injective. In the latter case translation of the level gives literally Leen's original “Full raw stationary classification.” We detail the extension to the other coset using his Section 3 proof, whose energy argument concerns individual offset Fourier modes. [Leen, *Exact rates in Collatz synchronization*, stationary rigidity of the raw kernel](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23268751)

First the level projection of the odd-offset part of a stationary law is constant times Haar: its Fourier coefficients satisfy the nearest-neighbor averaging identity. Any Haar-null level set therefore has zero odd-offset mass. From \(D\ne0\), exactly \(v_2(D)\) successive lower zeros yield an odd offset at the same level. Branch domination transfers the null statement back. From \(D=0\), a lower one gives \((1-X(s))/2\), nonzero whenever \(X(s)\ne1\). Thus in the other coset the entire stationary level marginal is absolutely continuous.

Here are the ingredients identifying a stationary measure \(\mu\) with absolutely continuous level marginal. On a partial affine graph \(D=A+BX(s)\), (2) sends the slope coefficient to \(B/2\) or \((3B-1)/2\) with equal probabilities, according to the lower bit. The constant coefficient is \((3^{e'}A+e')/2\). These are Leen's coefficient maps unchanged. Distinct graphs have at most one level of intersection. The argument closing a countable family under these maps, restricting the stationary measure to its forward-invariant union, and passing to its almost surely unique slope therefore excludes any positive mass on such a union. Indeed the fair slope chain escapes to infinite dyadic norm: from an integral coefficient at most one branch remains integral, and thereafter both decrease its valuation by one.

For a lower word \(w\) of length \(n\), grade \(q_w\) and carry \(C_w\), its endpoint on any fixed second-arm parity history of relative grade change \(l\) has offset
\[
       2^{-n}(3^{q_w+l}D-3^l C_w X(s)+C'),
\]
where \(C'\) is the second word's carry. A collision between different words either has unequal end levels, lies on one of the excluded graphs, or, for equal \(q_w,l\), fixes a single level because the distinct words have different \(C_w\). The usual parity peeling recovers \(w\) from \((q_w,C_w)\). Hence the full \(2^n\) word endpoints are distinct almost surely. They occupy at most \(2n+1\) levels. Stationarity therefore gives zero mass to any measurable set with at most \(J\) points in each level fibre, using the upper bound \(J(2n+1)2^{-n}\). Apply this to measurable conditional atoms of mass at least \(1/J\): the conditional offset distributions are almost surely nonatomic.

Finally take \(f=c(s)\exp(2\pi i kD/2^v)\), \(k\) odd, \(v\ge1\), with \(c\) a fixed bounded level cylinder of precision \(L\). In Leen's high-bit calculation choose \(\Delta=2^{n+v-3}\) at sufficiently large \(n\), ensuring \(2^L\mid\Delta\). Translate the starting level by \(\Delta\) and follow the same lower word. The ratio of the two multipliers is exactly \(3^\Delta\). Its difference from 1 has valuation \(n+v-1\). As long as the two offset parities agree the level difference stays \(\Delta\), and the normalized gap \(\eta_j=2^j(D'_j-D_j)/(3^\Delta-1)\) satisfies
\[
        \eta_{j+1}=3^{e'_j}\eta_j-e_j2^jX(s_{j+1}),\qquad \eta_0=0 .
\]
Integrality inductively preserves those parities up to and including the last departure. Since \(X\) is odd, \(\eta_n\equiv e_0\bmod2\). The translation fixes the endpoint test for a lower first zero and negates it for a first one. The two corresponding functions are orthogonal in product Haar. Each branch is a Haar isometry, so \(\|P_\alpha^n f\|_2^2=\tfrac12\|P_\alpha^{n-1}f\|_2^2\) from that threshold onward. The pullback precision is at most \(q=n+\max(L,v,1)\) in both coordinates; each division consumes only one additional bit.

For the restriction \(\nu\) of \(\mu\) to level density at most \(J\), joint precision-\(q\) cells satisfy \(2^q\sum_C\nu(C)^2\to0\). Indeed Cauchy--Schwarz on each level cell leaves at most \(J\) times the average conditional collision probability on the offset partition, which tends to zero by nonatomicity. Cauchy--Schwarz on joint cells and the energy bound then give \(|\nu(P_\alpha^n f)|\le C_f(2^q\sum_C\nu(C)^2)^{1/2}\to0\). Remove the density cutoff and use stationarity: all nonzero offset Fourier modes vanish, even multiplied by any level character. The odd-offset flux now identifies the full level marginal with Haar, so \(\mu=\Lambda\). This proves the classification used in both cosets. The compact proof here adapts Leen's graph, collision and energy lemmas; it does not assume raw uniqueness for a different multiplier as an external theorem.

## A fixed rational multiplier excludes the exceptional stationary atom

Take a positive rational odd unit \(\alpha\notin3^{\mathbb Z}\), initial ordinary level \(h_0=O(\sqrt t)\) of either sign and arbitrary dyadic integral offset. The empirical occupation measure of the raw chain (2) to time \(t\) converges in probability, in weak topology, to \(\Lambda\), uniformly along these starting data. In the coset without an absorbing level this follows directly from the stationary classification. For completeness, for every continuous \(f\) the empirical integral of \(P_\alpha f-f\) tends to zero in probability by bounded martingale telescoping. Compactness and a countable dense family of tests imply that each subsequential random limit is stationary almost surely. It remains to exclude mass at an inaccessible but arbitrarily close absorbing point in the other coset.

Write \(e(h)=v_2(\alpha3^h-1)\) for integer \(h\). It is always finite. The fixed-rational binary logarithm bound gives
\[
                   e(h)\le C_\alpha\log(2+|h|).                           \tag{3}
\]
In Yu's Theorem 2 take the fixed binary units \(3,\alpha\) with exponents \(h,1\); for negative \(h\) one may invert the fixed rational \(\alpha\) and change the exponent to \(|h|\), preserving the valuation. The expression is nonzero by hypothesis. The fixed extension \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\) meets the auxiliary dyadic condition, and the exponent of the last base is a unit. All heights are fixed, giving (3); handle \(h=0\) separately. [Yu, *p-adic logarithmic forms and group varieties II*, Theorem 2](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa89/aa8944.pdf) · [Fixed-prime specialization with moving heights, equation (8)](../ordinary/FIXED_NUMERATOR_LANDING_COMPLEXITY.md)

During a visit to level \(h\) the expected number of native dates through its next mixed step is at most \(e(h)+2\), uniformly in the offset. Indeed for even offset the two possibilities are \(D/2,(3D+1-X(h))/2\). Below valuation \(e(h)\) both drop the valuation by one; at or above that threshold a fair choice reaches \(e(h)-1\) with probability at least one half at each attempt. At an odd offset the very next step changes the level by a fair sign. The successive direction levels are therefore exactly \(h_0+S_j\) for ordinary simple symmetric random walk \(S_j\). In \(t\) physical dates the number of directions is at most \(t\).

Suppose \(X(s_*)=1\). For \(h\equiv s_*\bmod2^r\) with \(r\ge1\), the binary lifting identity gives \(e(h)=2+v_2(h-s_*)\). Among the possible levels \(|h-h_0|\le t\), (3) bounds all these valuations by \(O_\alpha(\log(2+t))\), uniformly for \(|h_0|\le C\sqrt t\). On each interval of \(B=\lceil\sqrt t\rceil\) consecutive levels, restricted to this possible range, each congruence of precision \(l\) has at most \(B2^{-l}+1\) representatives. Summing valuation layers yields
\[
   \sum_{h\equiv s_*\ (2^r)}(e(h)+2)
       \ll_{\alpha,C} (r+1)B2^{-r}+r+\log(2+t)                           \tag{4}
\]
on that interval. The fixed \(r\) is taken before \(t\to\infty\).

For the first \(t\) directions the expected local time of \(S_j\) at \(z\) is at most \(C\sqrt t\exp(-cz^2/t)\). This follows by summing the binomial mass bound \(C(j+1)^{-1/2}\exp[-cz^2/(j+1)]\), obtained from Stirling's bounds in the central range and the binomial exponential tail outside it. Multiplying (4) by this local-time bound on successive length-\(B\) blocks at distance \(lB\) from \(h_0\), then summing the Gaussian bounds over both directions, gives
\[
 \frac1t\mathbb E\#\{k<t:h_k\equiv s_*\bmod2^r\}
   \ll_{\alpha,C}(r+1)2^{-r}+
                         \frac{r+\log(2+t)}{\sqrt t}.                     \tag{5}
\]
Here \(h_k\) denotes the level at native time \(k\); condition the cost of each whole visit on its incoming state and use the uniform holding bound. This estimate does not condition the direction increments on the lengths of their holds. Each tested level residue is clopen. Taking the subsequential occupation limit in expectation, then \(r\to\infty\), excludes mass at \((s_*,0)\) almost surely. The random stationary limit is now uniquely \(\Lambda\), as asserted.

## Independent Brownian groups on ordinary sources

Take a pair from different groups and use the first arm as the dyadic base. Its initial multiplier is \(\alpha3^{h_0}\) with \(\alpha=c_{b(j)}/c_{b(i)}\), \(h_0=s_j-s_i\). Its initial offset is \(D_j-\alpha3^{h_0}D_i\in\mathbb Z_2\). The sampled source initially Haar yields exactly the ordinary fair lower labels for (2), conditional on the common binary filtration. In (2) a parity disagreement occurs exactly when \(D\) is odd. This is a continuous cylinder test with product-Haar mean one half. Consequently its empirical native fraction tends to one half in probability. The same argument at every deterministic positive fraction of the horizon has \(h_0=O(\sqrt{ut})\); a finite time mesh and bounded increments give convergence uniform in the time fraction. The exact pair bracket in (3) of the [coalescing path theorem](COALESCING_ORDINARY_HEIGHTS.md) therefore tends uniformly to zero.

Within the same group the first-return and post-return argument of that theorem applies whenever the limiting starting points differ. If two levels have the same limiting point, orient to a nonnegative initial gap \(h=o(\sqrt t)\). For \(h>0\) the original uniform physical return domination gives \(\Pr(R>\eta t)\le C(h+1)/\sqrt{\eta t}\) for fixed \(\eta>0\); if \(h=0\) take \(R=0\). The normalized offset has log magnitude after adding two of order \(o(t)\), since every initial exponent is \(O(\sqrt t)\). By [the post-return bound](ORDINARY_AFFINE_INTERVAL_PROFILE.md), canonical absorption occurs in \(o_{\mathbb P}(t)\). Tight moduli of the individual walks identify the pair in the path limit. For each pair of distinct initial positions in the same group the limiting cross bracket is the elapsed coalesced time divided by four. Across groups it is zero even when the limiting paths cross. The stopped martingale characterization now gives precisely independent groups: before the next within-group meeting all distinct representatives have the diagonals and zero cross-brackets of independent Brownian drivers, by Lévy's characterization after independent continuation. Identify the within-group meeting coordinates and iterate. No identification occurs at an inter-group crossing.

Transfer to any given integer interval uses the common binary residue up to \(t-\lceil2\log_2(t+2)\rceil\). All arms have odd integer slope. The exact real carry conversion of the finite-family theorem adds only the fixed term \(\log_2 c_{b(i)}/(\gamma\sqrt t)\) to its scaled height. The finite-deadline accident estimate applies across different groups as well. The nonzero real slope difference \(c_i3^{s_i+g_i}-c_j3^{s_j+g_j}\) has absolute value at least a fixed positive fraction of the larger term because \(c_j/c_i\notin3^{\mathbb Z}\). Centering at the offset contribution leaves an interval of length \(O(2^{3t/4})\) per pair of typical terminal grades, with exponentially few low-grade exceptions. An earlier numerical meeting would persist to the deadline. These facts prove the stronger version, including its partition assertions.

For the fixed multipliers in (1), write each \(A_i=2^{r_i}3^{s_i}c_{b(i)}\), with fixed nonnegative \(r_i,s_i\) and a positive representative prime to six. The first \(r_i\) steps of \(A_i x\) may always be taken even and leave exactly \(3^{s_i}c_{b(i)}x\). The difference in the two grade clocks shifted by \(r_i\) is at most \(r_i\) at every date, so their normalized grade paths are uniformly close. For real log heights, each shortcut changes the logarithm of a positive integer by at most one in absolute value, and the fixed centering shift is harmless. All \(s_i/\sqrt t\) tend to zero. This proves (1), with its equivalence classes including powers of two. The numerical partition assertion above was for odd aligned multipliers: \(x\) and \(2x\) can follow the same orbit one date apart.

Leen's original raw stationary classification, affine-graph and high-bit energy proofs, and its separate physical return estimate are the substantive probability inputs. Classical binary linear forms exclude concentration at the single inaccessible stationary atom for a fixed non-power rational slope. All populations are sampled, and the joint law does not determine any one integer's unlimited future.
