# Small residues on periodic rational phases and recovery of an ordinary trajectory

Write \(T(x)=x/2\) on an even numerator and \(T(x)=(3x+1)/2\) on an odd numerator, with the rational in lowest terms and its denominator odd. Write \(\gamma=\log_2 3\). For a nonzero integer \(c\), put \(c^\circ=c/(2^{v_2(c)}3^{v_3(c)})\), retaining the sign.

**Proposed results.** A positive primitive periodic rational orbit with reduced denominator \(q\) and native period \(L\le K\log_2 q\) has at most one odd numerator below an explicit positive power of \(q\), for all sufficiently large \(q\). Two such positive orbits with a short integral scaled relation at their starting phases and a sufficiently small odd numerator on each orbit must actually be the same cycle. Their possible phase shift and slope exponent obey an exact discrepancy equation. If both calendars have all their odd dates in single intervals of length \(o(L)\), any noncollapsed relation requires a slope exponent \(\omega(L)\), even for unbounded odd grades.

For the signed rational periodic closure of the first \(t\) letters of any fixed ordinary positive source, all sufficiently smooth cross-errors of small rational references follow one exact multiplicative rule at all phases. At every sufficiently large nonreturn either the reduced endpoint displacement has an exponentially large part outside \(2,3\), or on a consecutive interval of \((2-\gamma-\epsilon)t\) dates every competing small rational reference has an exponentially large such error. Precise quantifiers and an all-central-dates conclusion for subexponential ordinary orbits appear below.

## The arithmetic input and an exact lifting lemma

Let \(q>1\) be prime to six and assume \(2^L\equiv3^p\pmod q\), with \(L,p\ge1\). A modular exponent pair \((a,b)\) acts by multiplication by \(2^a3^b\), including negative exponents. Put \(l=\log_2q\). Fix an integer \(K\ge1\) and \(\eta=1/(200K)\). Suppose
\[
 \max(L,\gamma p,|a|,\gamma|b|)\le Kl,\qquad
 2^a3^b u\equiv v\pmod q,\qquad
 0<|u|,|v|\le q^\eta,\quad \gcd(uv,6)=1.                            \tag{1}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(q\) depending only on \(K\),
\[
                    Lb=-pa,\qquad |u|=|v|.                           \tag{2}
\]
If in addition
\[
                2q^\eta\max(2^{L/2},3^{p/2})<q,                      \tag{3}
\]
then \(u=v\) with sign and \((a,b)\) is an **integer** multiple of \((L,-p)\).

Varjú's Theorem 2 says that in the indicated exponent box, every vector mapping a signed lift prime to six of size at most \(q^{1/(147K)}\) to another such lift belongs to one line. Its modulus threshold is ineffective. The zero vector and \((L,-p)\) are among these vectors using lifts \(1,1\). This gives the first part of (2). This is the small-lift strengthening of the earlier pure lattice relation. [Varjú, *On the multiplicative group generated by two primes in* \(\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z\), Theorem 2, 2022 revision, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.04367)

For the second part take \(R=3K\). The pigeonhole principle supplies \(1\le k\le R\) and \(m\in\mathbb Z\) with \(|ka-mL|\le L/R\). Put \(a'=ka-mL\) and \(b'=kb+mp\). Then \(b'=-pa'/L\), so the reduced positive numerator and denominator of \(2^{a'}3^{b'}\) are each at most \(q^{K/R}\). The congruence \(2^{a'}3^{b'}u^k\equiv v^k\pmod q\) lifts to a rational equality, as the cleared difference has absolute value at most \(2q^{K/R+R\eta}<q\) for sufficiently large \(q\). The valuations at two and three force \(a'=b'=0\). Thus \(|u|=|v|\). Under (3) take \(k=1\) and choose \(m\) to make \(|a-mL|\le L/2\). The same proof now gives \(u=v\), \(a=mL\), \(b=-mp\). The lifting argument uses no coprimality of \(u,v\) with \(q\).

The concurrent moving-residue theorem at PR 215 is a direct qualitative precursor. For a positive physical determinant, a positive rational unit residue of subexponential height and an exponentially large divisor of that determinant, its equations (1)–(2) already place the residue in the two-three group and determine the primitive clock direction and order. Applied to the ratio of two small reduced odd numerators, this gives their equality in the subexponential regime. For each bounded clock-to-modulus ratio, a nonexplicit positive power threshold also follows by a diagonal contradiction. Consequently the qualitative small-numerator separation, the ensuing positive phase classification and its clustered-calendar specialization can also be deduced from that result. Here Varjú supplies the explicit simultaneous exponent in (1), with signed representatives, either sign of the physical determinant, and no unit requirement modulo the chosen modulus. The fixed-source deductions (7)–(11) use these properties directly. ## Scaled phases of arbitrary odd grade

Use least native periods \(L_i\) and odd grades \(p_i\) for two positive rational cycles with starting phases \(z,w\). Assume
\[
 w=3^jz-a,\quad j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\quad a\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
 L_1,L_2,j\le B\log_2q.                                               \tag{4}
\]
Here \(q\) is the common reduced denominator. Every positive periodic phase has a complete-word denominator dividing \(2^{L_i}-3^{p_i}>0\), coprime to six. Consequently (4)'s integral relation preserves the reduced denominator. The numerator at every odd input is prime to six by the native rotation of its complete-cycle equation, whose carry is nonzero modulo three.

Let \(K=3\lceil\max(1,B)\rceil\). Suppose each cycle has an odd input with numerator at most \(q^{1/(200K)}\). For all sufficiently large \(q\) the cycles are identical up to rotation. To see this, let \(t_i\in[0,L_i)\) be the respective forward times to the two chosen inputs, put \(h_i\) for the intervening odd grades, and denote the numerators there by \(y_i\). Modulo \(q\) every native step multiplies the numerator by \(1/2\) or \(3/2\). It follows that
\[
                   2^{t_1-t_2}3^{j+h_2-h_1}y_1\equiv y_2\pmod q .
\]
Since \(\gamma p_i<L_i\), these exponents and the first full clock satisfy (1). The positive numerators agree by (2). Their phases coincide exactly, giving a single primitive cycle of some period \(L=L_1=L_2\) and grade \(p=p_1=p_2\).

Write \(w=T^s z\), \(0\le s<L\), and let \(h_s\) count the odd departures in those \(s\) steps. Comparing the actual numerator residues with (4) gives \(2^s3^{j-h_s}\equiv1\pmod q\), since the starting numerator is a unit modulo \(q\). This vector is in the same box, so
\[
                j=h_s-\frac{ps}{L}.                                   \tag{5}
\]
Put \(d=\gcd(s,L)\), including \(d=L\) for \(s=0\). Bézout's identity for the two clock vectors gives \((d,-pd/L)\) in the relation lattice. In particular \(pd/L\) is an integer and
\[
                  q\mid 2^d-3^{pd/L},\qquad
                  \frac Ld\le\frac{L}{\log_2(q+1)}.                  \tag{6}
\]
If \(L<2\log_2(q+1)\), this forces \(s=j=a=0\) and \(z=w\).

On a **single** primitive positive cycle of \(L\le B\log_2q\), any two odd numerators at most \(q^{1/(200K)}\) agree by (2) applied to their time and grade difference. Distinct phases cannot have the same numerator. Thus at most one such date occurs in the primitive period. An elementary gap estimate alone bounds their number in terms of \(B\), as every small odd input needs enough preceding divisions by two. The lifting argument gives one for every fixed \(B\).

For a direct variable-grade specialization, suppose the odd dates in each primitive calendar are contained in one cyclic interval of \(b_i=o(L_i)\) steps and \(\max L_i\to\infty\). At the first odd input of the interval, the carry for the whole rotated word is the carry accumulated in these \(b_i\) steps, since the remaining letters are even. It is positive and at most \(3^{b_i}-2^{b_i}<3^{b_i}\), by the all-odd maximal carry. Hence its reduced numerator is below \(3^{b_i}\), and
\[
       \frac{2^{L_i}-3^{p_i}}{3^{b_i}}\le q<2^{L_i},\qquad p_i\le b_i.
\]
Both \(L_i\sim\log_2 q\), both selected numerators are \(q^{o(1)}\), and (6) precludes a noncollapsed (4). Thus on **every** such noncollapsed sequence \(j/\max(L_1,L_2)\to\infty\). Here collapse means \(z=w,j=a=0\). The prior [bounded-grade phase obstruction](../ordinary/BOUNDED_GRADE_PAIRED_PERIODS.md) allows arbitrary locations of a bounded number of odd letters. This argument instead allows an unbounded number in a short cyclic interval. Neither hypothesis contains the other.

## Exact cross-errors for signed closures of actual prefixes

Fix a positive integer \(n\), write \(x_i=T^i n\), and let \(g_i\) count the odd departures up to date \(i\). Fix a nonreturn \(x_t\ne n\). Close the first \(t\) actual binary letters periodically and denote its signed rational phase at date \(i\) by \(z_i=u_i/q\), always with the same positive reduced denominator. Put
\[
 g=g_t,\quad D=2^t-3^g,\quad f=\gcd(|D|,|x_t-n|),\quad
 q=|D|/f,\qquad d=\operatorname{sgn}(D)(x_t-n)/f .
\]
Then for \(0\le i\le t\)
\[
     u_i-qx_i=2^{t-i}3^{g_i}d,\qquad
     q\ge 2^t/(C_n t^{14}).                                          \tag{7}
\]
The initial identity follows from \(z_0-n=2^t(x_t-n)/D\), then the first \(i\) common native branches multiply the difference by \(3^{g_i}/2^i\). The denominator estimate holds at **every** nonreturn by the [physical denominator theorem](PREFIX_CLOSURE_DENOMINATORS.md). Its explicit finite version uses only the observed prefix. In particular \(l=\log_2q\ge t-O_n(\log t)\). For all sufficiently large \(t\), \(g>0\), \(q\) is prime to six, and (3) holds for \((L,p)=(t,g)\) and any \(\eta\le1/200\), since \(\max(2^{t/2},3^{g/2})\le3^{t/2}\).

Fix \(B\ge1\), \(K=\lceil 2B+16\rceil\), and \(\eta=1/(200K)\). For rational references \(r_i/v_i,r_j/v_j\) at any two dates \(i,j\in[0,t]\), take \(v_i,v_j>0\) and height \(\max(|r|,v)\le2^{Bt}\) in each representation. Their nonzero cross-errors are
\[
                  E_i=v_i u_i-r_iq,\qquad E_j=v_j u_j-r_jq.
\]
Assume \( |v_j^\circ E_i^\circ|,|v_i^\circ E_j^\circ|\le q^\eta\). At every sufficiently large nonreturn, uniformly in these choices, there is \(k\in\mathbb Z\) with
\[
 \frac{E_j/v_j}{E_i/v_i}=
          2^{i-j}3^{g_j-g_i}\left(\frac{2^t}{3^g}\right)^k.          \tag{8}
\]
Indeed \(v_i E_j\equiv2^{i-j}3^{g_j-g_i}v_j E_i\pmod q\). Strip the powers of two and three from both sides, apply (1) and the signed integer-multiple conclusion under (3), and restore those powers. To check the uniform exponent box, \(x_i\le n2^t\), \(|d|\le (n+1)2^t\), \(q\le3^t\) and \(2^{t-i}3^{g_i}\le3^t\) give \(|u_i|\le (2n+1)6^t\). Hence the base-two logarithm of either product \(|v_i E_j|,|v_j E_i|\) is at most \((2B+3)t+O_n(1)\). For the signed exponent difference between their removed powers, the larger of the two logarithm bounds suffices. Adding the physical date or grade difference costs at most \(\gamma t\). Equation (7) supplies the stated \(K\) for fixed \(B\) and large \(t\).

For a single reference \(r/v\) at \(i\), set \(E=v u_i-rq\ne0\). A direct comparison with (7) yields the source-specific version. If
\[
         |v^\circ d^\circ|,|E^\circ|\le q^\eta ,
\]
then at every sufficiently large nonreturn
\[
          E=v d\,2^{t-i}3^{g_i}(2^t/3^g)^k
                    \quad\text{for some }k\in\mathbb Z.               \tag{9}
\]
This follows by the same congruence proof using \(E\equiv vd2^{t-i}3^{g_i}\pmod q\). The preceding exponent box bounds still suffice. In particular, integrality at two and three forces
\[
        i>v_2(vd),\quad g-g_i>v_3(vd)
                      \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r/v=x_i.              \tag{10}
\]
The first strict inequality excludes all \(k<0\) and the second excludes all \(k>0\). With \(k=0\), (7) and (9) give equality to the ordinary state. These guards also explain the appearance of a neighboring lap at an endpoint. At date zero both \(n\) and \(x_t\) give smooth multiples of \(d\), with residuals \(2^td\) and \(3^gd\), respectively.

## A phasewise dichotomy at every ordinary nonreturn

For each \(0<\epsilon<2-\gamma\) choose
\[
 K=18,\quad \eta=1/(200K),\qquad
 \sigma=\min(\eta/8,\epsilon/8).
\]
For **every** fixed positive integer \(n\), at every sufficiently large nonreturn \(t\) at least one of the following holds.

* \(|d^\circ|>2^{\sigma t}\), so the reduced terminal displacement itself contains this much multiplicative mass outside \(2,3\).
* There is an interval of at least \((2-\gamma-\epsilon)t\) consecutive native dates \(i\) on which \(x_i\) has primitive cross-error \(|d^\circ|\le2^{\sigma t}\). At each of these dates **every** rational \(r/v\ne x_i\), \(v>0\), with \(\max(|r|,v)\le2^{\sigma t}\) has \(|(v u_i-rq)^\circ|>2^{\sigma t}\).

In the second alternative \(x_i\) supplies the low-error integer reference irrespective of its archimedean size. The competing height cutoff may be smaller than \(x_i\). The statement uses the product of prime powers outside two and three with multiplicity.

To prove it, the [physical correction product](PREFIX_CLOSURE_DENOMINATORS.md) gives, uniformly at nonreturns of the fixed source,
\[
 \log_2|d|\le\max(0,\gamma g-t)+O_n(\log(2t)).
\]
For distinct departure states this uses the harmonic upper bound on their reciprocal sum. After a repeated state every actual displacement is bounded in terms of the fixed source. Also \(1\le x_t\le n2^{2g-t}\), since an odd positive departure multiplies a state by at most two. Thus \(g\ge(t-\log_2 n)/2\). In particular
\[
 g-v_2(d)-v_3(d)\ge
 \min\{g,\ t-(\gamma-1)g\}-O_n(\log(2t))
 \ge (2-\gamma)t-O_n(\log(2t)).                                     \tag{11}
\]
Take all integer dates \(v_2(d)+\sigma t<i<g-v_3(d)-\sigma t\). They satisfy both guards (10), since \(g_i\le i\) and \(v_2(v),v_3(v)\le\sigma t\). Their number has the required lower bound by (11). If the first alternative fails then \(|v^\circ d^\circ|\le2^{2\sigma t}<q^\eta\) at the stated heights. Any competing reference with \(|E^\circ|\le 2^{\sigma t}\) is excluded by (10). The case \(E=0\) also cannot occur at these heights, since an exact representation of the closure requires \(q\mid v\) whereas \(v<q\).

If \(\max_{j\le t}\log(2+x_j)=o(t)\) along any sequence of nonreturns, then for every fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\) the second conclusion holds on **all** dates \(\delta t\le i\le(1-\delta)t\) with \(\sigma=\min(\eta/8,\delta/8)\). Here \(\log_2 |d|=o(t)\), while \(1\le x_t\le x_i2^{2(g-g_i)-(t-i)}\) yields \(g-g_i\ge(\delta t-\log_2 x_i)/2\). Both strict guards hold at every such date. In this case the actual ordinary state has subexponential height as well. If only \(\log(2+x_t)=o(t)\) is known on the sequence, the same reasoning using \(g_i\le i\) still covers \(\delta t\le i\le(1/2-\delta)t\).

The every-nonreturn dichotomy includes exponentially growing positive orbits if any exist. The modulus onset imported from Varjú is ineffective. All residue identities concern the canonical signed periodic closure of the supplied positive trajectory. They do not force that trajectory to repeat or to have a cheap competing reference.
