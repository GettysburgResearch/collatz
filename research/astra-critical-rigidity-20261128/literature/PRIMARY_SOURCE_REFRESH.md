# Primary-source refresh for the November 28 research pass

A search through the late November 27, 2026 UTC snapshot returned a new finite computation, a restricted first-crossing theorem, and recent machine-assisted work on other problems. None of the sources examined supplies an all-source Collatz termination theorem. The chosen mathematical input is the fixed-form rational subspace theorem; it applies to the actual original source with its complete dyadic precision.

## AI announcements and what was actually checked

The November 21 joint Lean FRO/Mathlib account reports checking Alpöge's Riemann-hypothesis proof against the reviewed statement and multiple kernels on an inert proof export. The November 24 OpenAI matrix-multiplication manuscript reports a matching lower bound for exponent 9/4. Its companion Lean scope explicitly covers division-free circuits computing formal identities. These sources address different mathematical statements. Neither source gives an estimate on the dyadic residue of an individual Collatz source, on its first crossing, or on its full cycle denominator. The July AI-generated purported Collatz disproof itself was invalidated by the Lean kernel maintainer's August 1 postmortem. This pass uses no purported Collatz disproof. ## Which existing result the new argument uses

The fixed-form rational subspace theorem is applied with the three places \(\infty,2,3\). The significant choice is the real source as a retained coordinate and the **complete original-source form at 2**. The cancellation across changes of reference is an exact identity. Moving low-height arithmetic coefficients become factors of the remaining coordinates. Multiplicity at every prime outside those three places is retained in the primitive scaling. The [integer result](POWERED_WORD_HEIGHT.md) and the [open rational result](OPEN_ARC_HEIGHT.md) give the required products and resolve every exceptional subspace by a finite descent in dimension. [Schlickewei, original quantitative theorem](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1992__82_3_245_0/) · [Evertse, Theorem 1.1 normalization](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~evertsejh/dio2011-padicsubspace.pdf)

**Priority within this repository.** PR212's MOVING_ARCS already proves a stronger theorem for *closed* cycles, including rational accumulation rates, the coprime-clock exponent 1, and the rate \(1/K\) for bounded small-reference arc counts. The sharp open-source bound and its rational-source cost address a different endpoint problem. Paper 15 and PR212's elementary arc argument already imply that a single fixed small source cannot have unboundedly long prefixes with a fixed number of sufficiently cheap pieces. The first-order bit-length threshold for a moving actual window or an original least positive realizer is the substantive target here.
