# The scale at which a block of actual powers samples Brownian Collatz paths

Fix an integer \(a\ge2\), a positive integer \(M\), and a nonnegative integer \(c\). Consider the fixed sequence of positive integers \(z_n=c+Ma^n\), \(n\ge0\). Choose \(n\) uniformly from any interval of \(N\) consecutive nonnegative exponents. Put \(t=\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor\). For each arm form its entire actual centered log-height path
\[
 X_{n,k}(h/t)=\frac{\log_2 T^h z_{n+k}-\log_2 z_{n+k}
                     +(1-\tfrac12\log_2 3)h}{(\log_2 3)\sqrt t},
                   \qquad 0\le h\le t ,
\]
with linear interpolation. Let \(\nu_{n,N,m}=m^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\delta_{X_{n,k}}\), a random probability measure on path space. Write \(\mathcal W\) for the deterministic law of one Brownian motion of variance rate \(1/4\) started at zero. The following limits hold along arbitrary placements of the exponent interval, with the weak topology on probability measures.

* If \(a\) is odd and has a prime factor other than three, every fixed list of different offsets \(k\) has independent Wiener paths in the joint limit. For **every** \(m=m_N\to\infty\), \(\nu_{n,N,m}\to\mathcal W\) in probability. The block can grow arbitrarily slowly. In particular its largest terminal fluctuation tends to \(+\infty\) in probability and its smallest to \(-\infty\), in the normalization displayed above.
* If \(a=3^l\), \(l\ge1\), the transition scale is \(\sqrt t\). For \(m/\sqrt t\to0\) the empirical measure converges in distribution to \(\delta_B\), a random point mass at one path \(B\) with law \(\mathcal W\). For \(m/\sqrt t\to K\in(0,\infty)\), it converges in distribution to the pushforward of normalized Lebesgue measure on \([0,lK]\) under \(y\mapsto V(y)-y\), where \(V\) is the coalescing Brownian family of variance rate \(1/4\). For \(m/\sqrt t\to\infty\) it converges in probability to the deterministic measure \(\mathcal W\).
* If \(a\) is even, all \(m\) grade paths equal that of the fixed shift \(c\) through date \(t\), outside \(O_{a,M}(t+1)\) exponent indices in any interval, uniformly in \(m\). Outside another \(O_{a,M,c}(t+1)\) indices, all displayed paths differ by \(o(1)\) in supremum norm from the deterministic mesh path \((g_c(h)-h/2)/\sqrt t\), where \(g_c(h)\) counts odd departures from \(c\). For \(c=0\) they are exactly the all-even paths there. No limiting distribution of the particular deterministic path for general \(c\) is assumed.

The first two conclusions also hold for the block \(a^kx\), \(0\le k<m\), when the single base \(x\) is chosen uniformly in any interval of \(N\) consecutive positive integers. In this formulation the deterministic Wiener limit describes the empirical distribution across the dilates of most individual sampled ordinary seeds, even when \(m_N\to\infty\) very slowly. None of these populations is obtained by taking successive time shifts on one Collatz orbit.

## Fixed rows and arbitrary starting exponents

For odd \(a\) put \(v=v_2(M)\) and \(d=v_2(a^2-1)\). The binomial lifting identity shows that \(a^2\) generates \(1+2^d\mathbb Z_2\) topologically; modulo \(2^j\) its order is \(2^{j-d}\) for \(j\ge d\). In detail \(v_2((a^2)^{2^i}-1)=d+i\) follows by repeated squaring since \(a^2\equiv1\bmod8\). The residues of \(a^n\) are one coset for even \(n\) and one different coset for odd \(n\), given by \(1,a\bmod2^d\). Distinctness follows from \(v_2(a-1)<d=v_2(a-1)+v_2(a+1)\). Thus \(c+Ma^n\) traverses exactly once per period all refinements of two fixed depth-\(v+d\) cosets. At depth \(j\ge v+d\) the period is \(2^{j-v-d+1}\). The shortened joint word law through \(t-\lceil2\log_2(t+2)\rceil\) is consequently within \(O_{a,M}(t^{-2})\) of Haar conditioned on their union, for every permitted interval location.

Condition on either initial coset and reveal the fixed number \(v+d\) of initial bits. On each arm the remaining base is Haar since its derivative in the residual binary variable is odd. For each ratio of arm multipliers the finite initial words change it by a power of three and change the offset to a dyadic integer. If the ratio has a prime factor away from three, [the independent multiplier theorem](INDEPENDENT_MULTIPLIER_PATHS.md) applies to the residual pair: the direction law for an arbitrary offset places no real-size requirement on this cross bracket. In particular every fixed distinct list of powers of an odd \(a\) with a prime factor away from three has independent scaled grade walks in the joint limit, all started at zero after centering. The initial finite histories and the short terminal interval change scaled paths by \(o(1)\). The real carry correction is negligible uniformly off \(O_{a,M,c}(t+1)\) initial exponents, since the base there is at least \(N^{3/4}\) and the normalized initial affine offsets for \(z_{n+k}=a^k z_n+(1-a^k)c\) are bounded. Centering at each arm's true starting height gives the displayed process. These finite-list bounds are uniform in a common additional exponent shift: use \(z_{n+j}\) as the initial base, which only translates the exponent sample, and keep the list of relative offsets fixed.

For ordinary dilates \(a^{j+k}x\) the same uniformity in \(j\) follows by taking \(a^jx\) as base. Odd multiplication permutes all residues of the relevant binary precision, so the discrepancy from Haar is uniform in \(j\); real size \(a^jx\ge x\) handles the carry simultaneously in every fixed relative group. For both populations at most \(O(N^{3/4})\) ordinary bases or \(O(t+1)\) tower indices, respectively, need be deleted for the real-size comparison.

## Passing from a finite sample to a whole block

For a bounded continuous path test \(f\), divide a long block into adjacent groups of fixed size \(r\). In the independent case the expected absolute difference of each group's empirical average from \(\mathcal W f\) has limsup at most \((\operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal W} f/r)^{1/2}\), uniformly in its origin. This follows from the finite joint convergence to independent paths and Cauchy--Schwarz. The incomplete group costs at most \(2\|f\|_\infty r/m\). The triangle inequality, followed by \(r\to\infty\), proves convergence in \(L^1\) for every such test. Taking a countable, bounded convergence-determining family metrizing weak convergence gives the random-measure convergence in probability. Fixed open upper and lower half-lines for the terminal observation have positive Wiener mass, which proves the two extreme-value statements without a claimed growth rate.

When \(a=3^l\), a finite list with critical initial exponent gaps has the coalescing path limit under the same two-coset conditioning, by [the tower and finite-sampling proof](CRITICAL_POPULATION_FIBRES.md). For clarity, the relative normalized offset for an exponent gap \(h\) is initially \((3^{-h}-1)c\), bounded independently of \(h\). After fixed initial words with grades \(g_1,g_2\) its expression is \(2^{-v-d}(3^{g_1}(3^{-h}-1)c+3^{-s'}C_2-C_1)\) with \(s'=h+g_2-g_1\) if no reorientation is needed. For bounded \(h\) the finitely many possible resulting offsets are uniformly bounded after the necessary orientation; for \(h\to\infty\) the large-exponent formula applies. The same-group small-gap extension of the independent multiplier theorem gives identical limiting paths whenever \(h=o(\sqrt t)\). For ordinary bases these facts use the unconditional Haar version.

If \(m/\sqrt t\to0\), draw any fixed number of independent uniform labels on the discrete block (repeats allowed). All their gaps are \(o(\sqrt t)\), hence their paths are a single common Wiener path in the joint limit. If \(m/\sqrt t\to K>0\), the scaled exponents of these samples converge to independent uniform positions on \([0,lK]\); they are almost surely distinct in the limit. Their scaled grade system then converges to the corresponding coalescing motions. With each path centered at its **own** source log height, subtract its initial spatial coordinate from the common-origin normalization. The random empirical measure in both cases is determined by these finite-sample laws: one sampled path gives tightness by compact containment and products of bounded test integrals give all mixed moments. This is the sampling argument of the cited population theorem, including its treatment of dense dyadic starting labels.

Finally for \(m/\sqrt t\to\infty\), divide into blocks of size \(\lfloor K\sqrt t\rfloor\), first keeping \(K\) fixed. The critical limit applies uniformly at every block origin. For the centered Brownian path average over an interval of length \(lK\) the mean is \(\mathcal W f\); its variance is \(O(\|f\|_\infty^2/(lK))\). Indeed a pair at initial distance \(d'\) can be coupled to independent Brownian paths until their first meeting. Its covariance for two bounded path tests is at most \(2\|f\|_\infty^2\operatorname{erfc}(d')\) on the unit time interval. Integrate this bound over the two spatial variables using the integrability of \(\operatorname{erfc}\). The incomplete group costs \(O(K\sqrt t/m)\). Letting \(K\to\infty\) finishes the same \(L^1\) test argument. The use of contiguous blocks matters: a specific very large power gap can itself be highly divisible dyadically.

For even \(a\), \(z_{n+k}\equiv c\bmod2^t\) for every \(k\ge0\) as soon as \(v_2(M)+nv_2(a)\ge t\). Through every \(h\le t\) the exact output difference from the orbit of \(c\) is \(3^{g_c(h)}Ma^{n+k}/2^h\). The carry of the prefix of \(c\), after division by \(3^{g_c(h)}\), is bounded by \(2^t\). Delete the indices where \(Ma^n<2^{2t}\), only \(O(t+1)\) possibilities, and the carry divided by the starting size tends uniformly to zero. When \(c=0\), the common carry vanishes. This proves the final case.
