# Reduced denominators attached to every nonreturn of one ordinary source

**Proposed theorem.** Fix an arbitrary positive integer \(n\), let \(x_t=T^t n\) for the shortcut map, and write \(g_t\) for the number of odd departures before time \(t\). Close the **actual first \(t\) binary letters** periodically, denote its signed rational starting phase by \(z_t\), and write \(q_t\) for its reduced positive denominator. For every \(t\ge1\) with \(x_t\ne n\),
\[
 q_t\ \ge\ \frac{2^t}{4800\,M(n,t)\,t^{14}}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Here \(M(n,t)=n\) if \(x_0,\ldots,x_{t-1}\) are distinct. Otherwise take their maximum. This parameter can be read from the supplied prefix and is bounded over all \(t\) for each fixed \(n\). At an actual return \(x_t=n\), one has \(z_t=n\) and \(q_t=1\).

For every fixed \(n\), as \(t\) tends to infinity through its nonreturns, the subgroup generated by \(2,3\) modulo \(q_t\) has order \(\omega(t^2)\). In particular, every unequal-slope rational merger of this center by a source and its triple has a slope exponent gap \(\omega(t)\). Here \(f(t)=\omega(v(t))\) means \(f(t)/v(t)\to\infty\). A separate effective bound gives primes larger than \(c\sqrt t\) in the full reduced denominator at every sufficiently large nonreturn. These assertions hold regardless of whether the given positive source eventually enters a cycle.

## An explicit finite denominator bound

Write \(D_t=2^t-3^{g_t}\), \(\lambda=3^{g_t}/2^t\). The ordinary word has nonnegative carry \(C_t=2^t x_t-3^{g_t}n\). Its rational center is \(z_t=C_t/D_t\). The exact parity cylinder gives this signed rational its prescribed infinite periodic word. Since \(D_t\) is odd,
\[
 q_t=\frac{|D_t|}{\gcd(|D_t|,\,x_t-n)}.                                            \tag{2}
\]
For \(g_t>0\), both \(C_t\) and \(D_t\) are units modulo three, as seen from the last odd-letter summand in \(C_t\). In the all-even case \(z_t=0,\ q_t=1\). Formula (2) also holds there. [Physical closure and signed reference construction](SELF_COPIED_REFERENCE_COVERS.md)

Lagarias's explicit inequality, with \(p=g_t,\ q=t\), gives \(|\ln\lambda|\ge(\ln3)/(1200\,t^{13.3})\), including all nonreduced exponent fractions. Consequently
\[
 \frac{|D_t|}{2^t\max(1,\lambda)}
    =1-e^{-|\ln\lambda|}\ge \frac{1}{2400t^{13.3}},
\]
using \(1-e^{-s}\ge\frac12\min(1,s)\) for \(s\ge0\). The exponent here is convenient for an all-length numerical constant. [Lagarias, *Ternary expansions of powers of 2*, Lemma 2.2(1), equation (2.4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0512006)

The actual correction product is
\[
 x_t=n\lambda\prod_{\substack{j<t\\x_j\ {\rm odd}}}
                       \left(1+\frac1{3x_j}\right).
\]
When the departure states are distinct, their positive-integral reciprocals sum to at most \(1+\ln t\). Hence \(|x_t-n|\le2n\,t^{1/3}\max(1,\lambda)\). If a departure state has already repeated, the entire future is contained in the states visited before the first repetition and \(|x_t-n|\le M(n,t)\). Dividing the determinant bound by the appropriate bound on this nonzero difference gives (1). Either no repetition ever occurs, in which case \(M(n,t)=n\) always, or it occurs at a fixed finite date, after which the maximum stabilizes. In particular the finite estimate only needs a replayed prefix. Eventual use of a constant depending only on \(n\) does not require an algorithm deciding which alternative occurs.

## Which modular relations can be short?

Drop finitely many initial all-even lengths. Let
\[
 \mathcal L_t=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:2^a3^b\equiv1\pmod {q_t}\}.
\]
Since \(q_t\) is prime to six this includes negative exponents. The physical clock \((t,-g_t)\) belongs to \(\mathcal L_t\). For any fixed \(B\), all lattice points with \(\max(|a|,|b|)\le Bt\) belong to the rational line spanned by that clock, at all sufficiently large nonreturns.

Indeed, let \(U=2^t3^{-g_t},\ V=2^a3^b\). A point outside that line gives a multiplicatively independent pair of rational \(S\)-units for \(S=\{\infty,2,3\}\). Both are congruent to one at the complete modulus \(q_t\). The sum over all places of \(\log^-\max(|U-1|_v,|V-1|_v)\) is at most \(-\ln q_t\), by the primes dividing \(q_t\) and the nonpositive remaining terms. Its comparison height \(\max(h(U),h(V))\) is \(O_B(t)\). Equation (1) provides a fixed positive fraction of this height in \(\ln q_t\). Proposition 2 of Corvaja and Zannier then leaves only finitely many such multiplicatively independent pairs. Distinct lengths give distinct \(U\), since \(v_2(U)=t\). The proposition identifies exceptional subgroups effectively, but does not bound the heights of the finitely many independent exceptions here. [Corvaja and Zannier, *A lower bound for the height of a rational function at S-unit points*, Proposition 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0311030)

In fact the entire intersection of the clock line with the lattice consists of **integer** multiples of the physical clock, at all sufficiently large nonreturns. Otherwise its generator would be \((t,-g_t)/d\) for an integer \(d\ge2\). Then \(q_t\mid 2^{t/d}-3^{g_t/d}\), implying \(q_t\le3^{t/2}\) against (1). This primitive-generator assertion has an effective threshold from (1). Together with Proposition 2 it gives exact classification of every modular relation in each fixed linear-size box, with an ineffective onset for eliminating off-line relations.

Every nonzero relation has \(\|(a,b)\|_\infty\ge\log_6 q_t\), as \(q_t\) divides the nonzero numerator of \(2^a3^b-1\), whose absolute value is at most \(6^{\|(a,b)\|_\infty}\). The first successive minimum of the lattice in the sup norm is thus \(\gg t\) (indeed it eventually equals \(t\)). Its second minimum is \(\omega(t)\) by the preceding result. Minkowski's second theorem for the unit square gives its index at least half the product of the two minima. The index is exactly \(|\langle 2,3\rangle\bmod q_t|\). Also \((0,\operatorname{ord}_{q_t}3)\) is independent of the clock, so \(\operatorname{ord}_{q_t}3/t\to\infty\).

The [rational-merger classification](../ordinary/UNIVERSAL_RATIONAL_MERGERS.md) realizes each of these actual-prefix centers for \(g_t>0\). Any such unequal-slope intersection satisfies \((3^j-1)z_t\in\mathbb Z\), and therefore \(j\) is a positive multiple of \(\operatorname{ord}_{q_t}3\). Thus while the centers are attainable, their required slope gaps outgrow the lengths of their physical defining words, even if those words contain an unbounded number of odd departures. The earlier [bounded odd-grade shadow obstruction](../ordinary/RATIONAL_PERIODIC_SHADOWS.md) uses the same gcd theorem for an arbitrary bounded-grade cycle; the additional input here is (1) at **every nonreturn of one fixed ordinary source**. Neither result asserts an intersection between the ordinary source \(n\) and its triple.

## Effective prime mass above the square-root scale with a small constant

For \(5\le p\) prime and \(t\ge g_t\ge1\), the \(p\)-part of \(q_t\) divides that of \(2^t-3^{g_t}\). Apply Bugeaud–Laurent with field \(\mathbb Q\), \(D=f=1,\ A_1=A_2=p,\ b_1=t,\ b_2=g_t\). The bases \(2,3\) are multiplicatively independent \(p\)-adic units. Corollary 1 to Theorem 3 gives, using natural logarithms,
\[
 v_p(q_t)\le\frac{24p}{(\ln p)^2}
       \max\{\ln(2t)+0.4,\ 10\ln p,\ 10\}^2.                                 \tag{3}
\]
The first term comes from \(b'=(t+g_t)/\ln p\). For any fixed \(p\), Chim's later Theorem 2.1 improves the right-hand exponent dependence to \(O_p(\ln(t+1))\), so the part supported on any fixed set of primes is polynomial in \(t\). The earlier estimate (3) has the useful uniform dependence on a growing prime cutoff. [Bugeaud and Laurent, Theorem 3 and Corollary 1](https://irma.math.unistra.fr/~bugeaud/travaux/logpadicdef.pdf) · [Chim, *Lower bounds for linear forms in two p-adic logarithms*, Theorem 2.1, JNT 266 (2025)](https://tugraz.elsevierpure.com/ws/portalfiles/portal/92346766/1-s2.0-S0022314X24001793-main.pdf)

For \(t^{1/4}\le Y\le\sqrt t\), summing \(v_p(q_t)\ln p\) over \(p\le Y\) gives
\[
 \ln \prod_{p\le Y}p^{v_p(q_t)}\ \le\ C\big(t^{1/2}(\ln(2t))^2+Y^2\big)             \tag{4}
\]
with an absolute effective constant. Below \(t^{1/4}\) use (3), \(1/\ln p\le1\), and sum \(p\) over all integers up to that cutoff. Above it the two logarithms in the maximum divided by \(\ln p\) are bounded by an absolute constant, so (3) is \(O(p)\). Now \(\sum_{p\le Y}p\ln p\le Y\sum_{p\le Y}\ln p=O(Y^2)\) by the elementary Chebyshev bound. For example, summing the primes in \((m,2m]\) using their product's divisibility into \(\binom{2m}{m}\le4^m\) and iterating the dyadic bound supplies an effective absolute constant.

Together with (1), (4) says that the part of \(q_t\) supported on primes **above** any \(Y=o(\sqrt t)\) has base-two logarithm at least \(t-o(t)\), at every nonreturn of a fixed source. For \(Y<t^{1/4}\) use monotonicity. For a sufficiently small absolute fixed \(c>0\) the part above \(c\sqrt t\) still has logarithm at least \(t/2\) in base two at all sufficiently large nonreturns. The finite estimates (1) and (3) are effective even when the source is supplied as a parameter. The square-root estimate concerns the reduced denominator after all numerical cancellations.

## A common slope gap over a short band of physical lengths

For \(d\ge1\) the exact clock congruences imply
\[
 \gcd(q_t,q_{t+d})\mid |2^d-3^{g_{t+d}-g_t}|\le 3^d.                     \tag{5}
\]
In particular consecutive reduced denominators are coprime, for both possible next parities. To see (5), a common divisor is prime to six when it exceeds one. Divide the two congruences \(2^t\equiv3^{g_t}\) and \(2^{t+d}\equiv3^{g_{t+d}}\) modulo that divisor. An initial all-even prefix has \(q_t=1\). Also \(q_t\le |2^t-3^{g_t}|\le3^t\). Consequently at any nonreturn \(v\) the portion of the new denominator left after dividing out its gcd with **any single earlier denominator** satisfies
\[
 \frac{q_v}{\gcd(q_v,q_t)}\ge\frac{(2/\sqrt3)^v}{4800 M(n,v)v^{14}},\qquad 1\le t<v.
\]
Here \(\min(t,v-t)\le v/2\). Every sufficiently late nonreturn has a reduced denominator distinct from every earlier one.

Take \(m=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor\) and the \(m\) lengths \(N,\ldots,N+m-1\). At least \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) are nonreturns, because consecutive returns would give a positive fixed point of the shortcut map. Let \(Q\) be the lcm of the reduced denominators in this band, and \(M_n=\sup_t M(n,t)<\infty\). Prime by prime the log lcm is at least the sum of individual logs minus the sum of pairwise log gcds. Indeed, for a sorted list of valuations all but the largest occur among the pairwise minima with a largest entry. Equations (1) and (5) therefore give
\[
 \log_2 Q\ge \lfloor m/2\rfloor [N-\log_2(4800 M_n)-14\log_2(N+m)]
                  -(\log_2 3)\frac{m^3-m}{6}                              \tag{6}
\]
for large \(N\); one can use the positive part of the square bracket for all \(N\). In particular
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log_2 Q}{N^{3/2}}
       \ \ge\ \frac12-\frac{\log_2 3}{6}>0 .
\]
If the source itself never returns, \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) can be replaced by \(m\). In any particular supplied band replace \(M_n\) by the largest \(M(n,t)\) encountered. Any one positive exponent \(j\) admitting merger lines with that gap for every center in the band must satisfy \(Q\mid 3^j-1\), hence \(j\ge\log_3(Q+1)\). Unlike the arbitrary individual second-relation bound, this common-gap cost has the effective finite formula (6).

The denominator dichotomy holds on the ordinary orbit of \(3\), including the tail after it reaches \(1\), since \(3\) itself never returns. Thus high reduced denominators here do not distinguish convergence from divergence. They obstruct a route that would assign compressed full-period closures to arbitrarily long nonreturn prefixes, or reuse a common short merger gap across many such lengths. Forced low-cost chronological matches on an arbitrary fixed orbit remain open. Weaver's [Paper 15/v2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/15/v2) proves height obstructions for small periodic dictionaries, and [Paper 236/v1, Proposition 5.3](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/236/v1) treats a single exact fractional power. Neither result is needed for the numerical denominator formula.

It checks (2), (5), the explicit finite bound (1), 337,392 overlap pairs, 528 lcm bands and the exact group-index computation on 924 small-modulus instances.
