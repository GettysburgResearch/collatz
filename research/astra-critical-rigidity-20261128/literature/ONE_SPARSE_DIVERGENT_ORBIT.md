# A single sparse change orbit preserves complete descents and carries positive divergent mass

Let \(T\) be the positive shortcut, \(m_T,\tau_T\) its global minimum and first date there, and \(p_1\) the density of its actual basin of one. We construct a computable positive integer map \(U\) whose entire change set lies on one divergent **\(U\)-orbit**. Every even branch remains exact. There are two choices.

* **Exact minimum and complete stopped path.** The rule can have \(\sup_n|U(n)-T(n)|\le4\), with \(O(\log X)\) changed sources through \(X\). For uniform \(n\le X\), the genuine and modified paths stopped at their own global minima, including the starting integer and all intermediate integers, are identical with probability \(1-O((1+\log X)^2/X)\). The new divergent component has density \(p_1>0\). On the complement of an explicit set of \(O((1+\log X)^2)\) starts through \(X\), every original path that never hits one is unchanged forever. Every original path that hits one agrees exactly through that first visit and then escapes along the inserted orbit. This choice changes the outgoing edge at one.
* **Preserved cycle of one and arbitrarily small positive divergent mass.** The rule can instead fix \(1\leftrightarrow2\) and satisfy the sharper global bound \(\sup_n|U(n)-T(n)|\le2\). All changes may lie beyond any prescribed finite verification bound. The inserted divergent mass \(w\) is positive and may be made arbitrarily small. Off the same size exceptional set, every affected source follows its true trajectory to one fixed odd target \(a\) in the original basin of one, then escapes. Its modified path stopped at its own minimum is an actual prefix of its genuine stopped path, shorter by at most a constant depending only on \(a\), for every such start. All other orbits remain unchanged forever. In particular the two centered height processes through their respective minima have supremum-distance \(O_a(1/\sqrt{\log n})\) off the exceptional set.

These statements concern literal coupled paths. The total variation error for the *entire stopped-path law* in the second choice tends to \(w\), which can be as small as desired. In both choices the phase-independent Brownian minimum-clock and height limits survive as statements about the respective stopped statistics (with the possibly changed minimum law in the second choice). The divergent component is genuine for the modified map. We do not propose that its edges satisfy the genuine shortcut equation at the changed sources.

Wren's November 19–20 discussion asks how a positive-density divergent component could arise by a sparse change, and distinguishes preminimum descent from re-ascent; its example uses polynomial dynamics over a finite field. The construction here uses the ordinary shortcut's exact exclusion of multiples of three after the first odd departure. That elementary exclusion also appears in the sibling-density literature. This is a calibration for stopped path observables; no assertion for arbitrary ensemble statistics or arbitrary error scales follows. ## The changed edges

An odd multiple of three has the form \(s=6k+3\). From any such \(s\ge9\), use
\[
       J(s)=
       \begin{cases}T(s)+1=9k+6,&k\text{ odd},\\
                     T(s)-2=9k+3,&k\text{ even}.
       \end{cases}
\]
It is another odd multiple of three, strictly larger than \(s\). Indeed \(J(s)\ge(3s-3)/2\ge(4/3)s\) for \(s\ge9\). Starting from a given \(s_0\ge9\), the sequence \(s_{j+1}=J(s_j)\) is strictly increasing and has \(O(1+\log X)\) members through \(X\). Membership in it is decidable by generation until passing the tested argument.

For the first choice set \(U(1)=3,\ U(3)=9\), begin the sequence at \(s_0=9\), and set \(U(s_j)=s_{j+1}\). All other inputs take their true \(T\)-edge. The first two errors are one and four. Write \(S=\{3,s_0,s_1,\ldots\}\) for the high changed odd sources, excluding the anchor one. The exceptional set is
\[
                         D=\bigcup_{s\in S}\{2^v s:v\ge0\}.
\]
The above counting bound gives \(|D\cap[1,X]|\ll(1+\log X)^2\).

After the first odd departure of a true trajectory, its state \((3y+1)/2\) is prime to three; even halving and all subsequent odd departures keep this property. A true trajectory therefore meets \(S\) if and only if its initial odd part belongs to \(S\). This is exactly membership in \(D\). For \(n\notin D\), a change is possible only on first reaching the anchor. At one both maps have already attained the same global minimum; the new tail is strictly increasing. For \(n\notin D\) never reaching one there is no change at any date. The inserted component is precisely \(\operatorname{Pred}_T(1)\cup D\); all other \(U\)-trajectories outside it retain their true infinite futures. Its density is \(p_1\), positive by the genuine predecessor theorem. The pointwise equality of complete stopped paths off \(D\) implies the stated total variation estimate. [Mazur, Theorem 1.1 and the shortcut conversion (1.3)](https://www.proofatlas.ai/papers/positive-lower-density-collatz-predecessors/paper-v1.pdf)

For the second choice take an integer \(\ell\ge2,\ 3\nmid\ell\) and the odd anchor \(a=(4^\ell-1)/3\). The numerator modulo nine shows \(3\nmid a\). Now \(T(a)=2^{2\ell-1}\), and \(T^{2\ell}a=1\), with only powers of two after \(a\) on the way. Set \(s_0=T(a)+1\), an odd multiple of three at least nine, \(U(a)=s_0,\ U(s_j)=J(s_j)\). Leave all other edges genuine. The support is on the single \(U\)-orbit from \(a\). In this case \(S=\{s_0,s_1,\ldots\}\), and \(D\) is again its dyadic closure. The inserted basin is exactly \(\operatorname{Pred}_T(a)\cup D\); all other paths remain unchanged. For distinct such anchors the genuine predecessor sets are disjoint, since no anchor reaches another before or after the cycle at one. Each has a positive natural density \(w_\ell\), by Mazur's positivity and Leen's density of sets invariant above a fixed value. Their sum is at most \(p_1\), hence \(w_\ell\to0\). One may choose \(a\) larger than any prescribed finite list of verified trajectory values. [Leen, invariant-set and terminal-label theorems](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23269488)

For \(n\in\operatorname{Pred}_T(a)\setminus D\), let \(t_a(n)\) be the first true arrival at \(a\). Its modified minimum \(r\le a\) lies in this genuine initial segment, and the path thereafter rises from \(a\). The target \(a\) does not recur in its true trajectory. Thus
\[
  \tau_T(n)=t_a(n)+2\ell,\qquad
  \tau_U(n)=t_a(n)-t_a(r),\qquad
  0\le\tau_T(n)-\tau_U(n)\le2\ell+
                       \max_{\substack{r\le a\\r\in\operatorname{Pred}_T(a)}}t_a(r).
\]
The final maximum is finite for the fixed anchor. These modified minima exceed one, because a true path cannot leave the \(1,2\) cycle to reach \(a\). Consequently the stopped-path laws differ by \(w_\ell+o(1)\) in total variation: the laws include the distinct starting coordinate \(n\), so their distance is precisely the proportion of starts on which the finite paths differ. A deterministic change of at most this many dates changes the two log heights, compared at the same fractions of their respective durations, by \(O_a(1)\) before normalization since each true log increment has absolute value at most \(\log2\). For the joint minimum law, mark each \(n\in\operatorname{Pred}_T(a)\) by its least true state through \(t_a(n)\), and mark its complement separately. The mark is unchanged on the next genuine step whenever \(n>a\). Leen's terminal-label theorem gives independence of this fixed mark from the true Brownian process jointly with the genuine minimum. Off \(D\) the modified minimum is a function of these labels and the genuine minimum. This justifies the corresponding joint stopped-process assertion.

The required edit size two is optimal for the particular mechanism of an infinite path of successive odd multiples of three with no intervening states and additive changes of at most two: if the permitted change is at most one, the only candidate multiple of three at each odd source is \(T(s)+1\). Its repeated use gives \(s_j+3=(3/2)^j(s_0+3)\), which fails to remain integral. This observation concerns that mechanism alone. Alternatively, without an additive error restriction one may choose the successive odd multiples of three arbitrarily far apart, putting the support below any prescribed nondecreasing, unbounded counting budget eventually.

## Exact effect at the common critical date

For \(k\ge2\) independent uniform starts through \(X\), put \(h=\lfloor\mu\log X+\sigma u\sqrt{\log X}\rfloor\) using the true parameter values of [the genuine dispersion formula](../ordinary/SYNCHRONOUS_PHASE_DISPERSION.md). Define \(w=p_1\) in the first choice and \(w=w_\ell\) in the second. If \(C\) runs over the genuine positive cycles other than \(1,2\), with original basin mass \(d_C\) and least period \(P_C\), then
\[
   \lim_{X\to\infty}\Pr(U^h n_1=\cdots=U^h n_k)=
    \Phi(u)^k\left(\frac{(p_1-w)^k}{2^{\,k-1}}+
                            \sum_C\frac{d_C^k}{P_C^{k-1}}\right).         \tag{1}
\]
In particular the exact effect of the sparse edits is visible in this common-date test, even though the marginal true minimum clock is preserved to \(O_a(1)\) off \(D\).

Surviving original components other than the basin of one keep their full original paths outside \(D\). The probability of sampling any member of \(D\) is \(o(1)\). A start in the inserted component outside \(D\) reaches the anchor at its original \(\tau_T\) minus the fixed offset \(2\ell\) (zero for the first choice). For these starts equality at any common date implies identical first arrival dates at the anchor: before the anchor synchronized paths have the same future arrival, afterward the increasing spine is injective, and a state on the prefix outside the anchor cannot also lie on the spine. The largest atom of the unconditional original \(\tau_T\)-law is \(o(1)\) by its Gaussian scaling, using finitely many narrow central bins and tight tails. Hence two independent samples from this component contribute \(o(1)\) even to eventual synchronous equality, in absolute probability.

For the remaining part \(B\) of the original basin of one the genuine and modified orbits coincide. Leen's terminal-label theorem for the true mask \(\operatorname{Pred}_T(a)\), or the empty residual in the first choice, gives
\[
     X^{-1}\#\{n\le X:n\in B,\ \tau_T(n)\le h\}\longrightarrow(p_1-w)\Phi(u).
\]
The parity of \(\tau_T\) bisects this mass. Here are details for the additional restriction by \(B\). Use the signed weight \((-1)^{\tau_T(n)}\) on the displayed set, zero otherwise. Its neighboring values agree on density one: first truncate genuine synchronization of \(n,n+1\) to a large fixed number of dates. For large \(n\) all those dates precede any visit to \(1,a\); meeting there then gives identical mask, minimum and arrival date. The successful truncation densities tend to one. Consequently every fixed residue class has the same signed mean up to \(o(1)\). Doubling a sufficiently large source increases its true arrival at one by one and preserves the two masks. For odd \(n\), \(n\) and \(3n+1\) have the same true state after one step, hence identical masks and arrival dates. Applying the residue comparison shows that the signed mean \(A_X\) satisfies \(A_{2X}=-A_X+o(1)\), \(A_{3X}=A_X+o(1)\); the bounded clock shifts and the changes between \(h_X\) and \(h_{\lambda X}\) for fixed \(\lambda>0\) have negligible mass by continuity of the clock distribution. Choose fixed positive odd \(p\) and positive \(q\) with \(2^p/3^q\) arbitrarily close to one, possible by irrationality of \(\log_3 2\) on odd multiples. The two iterated relations and the elementary difference bound for means on nested cutoffs, first using the same clock threshold, show \(\limsup_X2|A_X|\ll|2^p/3^q-1|\). This proves bisection. Whether the last true cycle entry occurs one date before hitting one makes \(o(1)\) difference. A collision of residual starts still outside the cycle forces a common later \(\tau_T\) and has probability \(o(1)\). With the unchanged contribution of the other original components this proves (1).
