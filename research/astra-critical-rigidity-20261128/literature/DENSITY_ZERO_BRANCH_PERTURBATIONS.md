# Density-zero odd-branch changes can prescribe the entire synchronous phase quotient

Write \(T(n)=n/2\) for even \(n\) and \(T(n)=(3n+1)/2\) for odd \(n\). Fix \(m\ge2\) and any distinct positive odd integers \(q_0,\dots,q_{m-1}\), for instance \(q_0=1\). There is a deterministic map \(U:\mathbb N_{>0}\to\mathbb N_{>0}\) with the following properties.

* Every even branch is exactly the shortcut branch. The odd sources where \(U(n)\ne T(n)\) have natural density zero.
* Every \(U\)-trajectory enters one of the prescribed two-cycles \(\{q_j,2q_j\}\). Each basin has natural density \(1/m\). Its two classes for merger at a common date each have density \(1/(2m)\), also within every fixed arithmetic progression when measured relative to that progression.
* For \(k\ge2\) independent uniformly sampled positive integers through \(X\), the probability of an eventual common-date merger under \(U\) tends to \((2m)^{1-k}\). Consecutive starts under \(U\) merge at a common date with density one.
* On density one the entire true trajectory of every one of a fixed finite number of positive affine test inputs \(an+r\) agrees with its \(U\)-trajectory for any fixed number of dates. This also holds up to some diagonal deadline tending to infinity with \(X\). No logarithmic rate for that deadline is asserted.

The [ordinary synchronous dispersion theorem](../ordinary/SYNCHRONOUS_PHASE_DISPERSION.md) uses the actual shortcut map and the masses of its actual components. This construction shows that density-one tests of the branch equation and phase dilation laws, even when accompanied by exact halving on every even integer and all fixed affine reindexings, cannot on their own determine the universal-convergence value of the independent-start merger statistic. The counterexample changes the map.

## Balanced labels compatible with every fixed affine dilation in density

We construct \(F:\mathbb N_{>0}\to\mathbb Z/(2m)\) so that
\[
  F(2n)=F(n)+m\text{ for every }n,\qquad
  F(an+r)=F(n)+m v_2(a)\text{ on density one}                            \tag{1}
\]
for each fixed positive integer \(a\) and fixed integer \(r\). All labels have natural density \(1/(2m)\). The construction uses elementary simultaneous approximation of logarithms. Multiplicative stability of integer sets and the separate stronger notion of uniform distribution in short intervals are discussed by Tao and Teräväinen. Here even doubling has an exact prescribed phase action. [Tao and Teräväinen, Definitions 1.4–1.7](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2019.28)

Write \(p_1,p_2,\dots\) for the odd primes. Choose increasing positive integers \(Q_j\), with \(Q_1=1\), \(Q_{j+1}/Q_j\equiv1\bmod 2m\), such that for every fixed \(d\),
\[
                  \operatorname{dist}(Q_j\log_2 p_d,2\mathbb Z)\to0 .
\]
At each stage the numbers \(1,\log_2 p_1,\ldots,\log_2 p_j\) are rationally independent by unique factorization. Kronecker's theorem on the torus applies also to multipliers restricted to \(1\bmod2m\) after scaling each frequency by \(Q_j/2\). One can thus make all the new distances for \(d\le j\) smaller than \(1/j\). Each \(Q_j\) is \(1\bmod2m\).

For \(Q=Q_j,\ Q'=Q_{j+1}\), put \(L_Q(u)=\lfloor m Q u\rfloor\bmod 2m\). To pass from \(L_Q\) to \(L_{Q'}\), use \(L_{Q'}(u)\) on \(\{\{m Q u\}<s\}\) and \(L_Q(u)\) elsewhere, for \(0\le s\le1\). Call the result \(G_{j,s}\). Translation by \(1/(mQ)\) increases either label by one and preserves the selector. Thus the pattern has period \(2/Q\) and spends the same Lebesgue length at each label in that period. Translation by one increases the label by \(m\). Changing \(s\) by \(\Delta\) changes the pattern on a fraction at most \(|\Delta|\) per period. On every interval of bounded length the number of discontinuities is \(O_m(Q'+1)\).

For each nonnegative integer \(l\) fix one such pattern \(G_l\). Start at \(L_{Q_1}\). Beginning at a sufficiently late integer \(l_j\), pass from \(L_{Q_j}\) to \(L_{Q_{j+1}}\) in \(j\) equally spaced parameter steps, then keep \(L_{Q_{j+1}}\) until the next transition. Choose \(l_j>l_{j-1}+j\) so large that \(Q_{j+1}\le2^{l_j/4}\). The index \(j\) in use tends to infinity. Each pattern has a balanced period tending to zero; its number of discontinuities on an interval of bounded length is \(O_m(2^{l/4}+1)\) for large \(l\). On every fixed-length real interval, uniformly in its location, the Lebesgue measure where \(G_l\) and \(G_{l+1}\) differ tends to zero: partition into full periods and at most two partial periods. The same holds with \(l+1\) replaced by \(l+h\) for bounded integer \(h\).

For a fixed odd positive integer \(a\), translation by \(\log_2a\) changes \(G_l\) on a set of Lebesgue measure \(o(1)\) in each unit-length interval, uniformly in location. The exceptional fraction for each of the two floor patterns is \(O_m(\operatorname{dist}(Q\log_2a,2\mathbb Z))\) at its own period, and similarly for \(Q'\). For the selector use \(Q\). The incomplete periods have total cost \(O(1/Q)\). The approximation for odd prime factors gives the assertion for \(a\). A factor \(2^v\) supplies the exact shift \(m v\).

Initially define \(F(n)=G_{\lfloor\log_2(n/2^{v_2(n)})\rfloor}(\log_2 n)\). Exact doubling follows since the pattern index is unchanged. Here are details transferring the real estimates to natural density. On \(2^J\le n<2^{J+1}\), discard \(v_2(n)>V\), at most \(O(2^{-V}2^J+1)\) values. The index is now \(J-v_2(n)\), within \(V\) of \(J\). On this shell replacing it by \(J\) has relative error \(o_V(1)\). Indeed for an exceptional set of logarithmic length \(o(1)\) with \(O_V(2^{(J+O(V))/4})\) interval components, the number of integer samples in its exponential image is at most \(O(2^J)o(1)+O_V(2^{J/4})\). This also bounds partial shells by the full-shell normalization. For \(an+r>0\) discard the same large-valuation event there; with fixed \(a,r\) its relative size is \(O_a(2^{-V})+o(1)\) for sufficiently large \(V\). Its pattern index lies within \(O_a(V+1)\) of \(J\). The term \(r\) perturbs \(\log_2(an)\) by \(O_{a,r}(2^{-J})\), at a cost \(o(1)\) after thickening the discontinuities of \(G_J\). These reductions and the fixed translation estimate give (1) on every dyadic shell, then at all cutoffs by summing a geometric series and sending \(V\to\infty\).

On any full or partial late shell the normalized count of a fixed label of \(G_J(\log_2 n)\) differs by \(o(1)\) from \(1/(2m)\) times the normalized sample size. Use the same discretization and integrate the bounded smooth density \((\log2)2^{u-J}\) on \(J\le u<J+1\) over full periods of length \(2/Q\), plus at most two partial periods. Hence each \(F\)-label has the stated natural density. The case \(a=1,r=1\) in (1) also gives uniform label frequencies on every fixed residue class, by comparison in full blocks of the fixed modulus.

## Turning the labels into actual components of a modified map

On each dyadic ray \(2^v q_j\) prescribe \(F(2^v q_j)=j+mv\bmod2m\). These disjoint changes have density zero and preserve (1), since a fixed affine map takes values in the changed set on at most \(O(\log X)\) starts through \(X\). For each label \(l\), the designated cycle has a unique vertex \(b_l\) with label \(l\).

First impose \(U(q_j)=2q_j,\ U(2q_j)=q_j\). On all other even sources use \(n/2\). For the other odd sources keep \(T(n)\) whenever \(F(T(n))=F(n)-m\); otherwise set \(U(n)=b_{F(n)-m}\). This initial map \(U_0\) satisfies the label equation everywhere. At odd \(n\), \(2T(n)=3n+1\); (1) and exact doubling show that the changed odd set has natural density zero.

Every other cycle of \(U_0\) is a genuine cycle of \(T\), since a redirected edge would already enter a designated cycle. At its largest odd element redirect to \(b_{F(n)-m}\). These added changes also have zero density. Indeed a true shortcut cycle with largest odd point at most \(X\) is entirely below \(2X+1\): evens decrease and the step out of an odd point is \((3n+1)/2\). For every fixed \(R\) there are finitely many cycles of length at most \(R\), by their finitely many affine words with slope different from one. Longer cycles with this bound on their largest odd point are pairwise disjoint and consume more than \(R\) integers each below \(2X+1\). Send \(R\to\infty\). The resulting map \(U_1\) has only the designated cycles.

Finally enumerate the starts \(j=1,2,\ldots\). Follow the current path from \(j\) until it reaches a designated cycle or encounters an odd \(y>2^j\). In the second case redirect \(y\) straight to \(b_{F(y)-m}\). This search terminates: an infinite path avoiding the cycles and all such odd points would be bounded, since even steps decrease and only finitely many smaller odd outputs are available. Redirecting directly into a cycle creates no additional cycle. Only \(O(\log X)\) of these edits occur at inputs through \(X\). The final map reaches a designated cycle from every \(j\): its finite successful path at stage \(j\) is retained, or the first later edit on that path goes directly to the same designated cycle. All even branches remain exact.

Under the final map \(F(U(n))=F(n)-m\) everywhere. The residue \(F(n)\bmod m\) selects the eventual cycle. Two starts synchronize if and only if their initial labels agree: after both have entered, the designated label specifies the same vertex, and unequal labels stay unequal at every common date. This gives both the basin densities and the claimed independent collision probabilities directly.

For the finite-path comparison, let \(E=\{n:U(n)\ne T(n)\}\), of natural density zero. For each fixed \(k\), \(T^{-k}E\) also has natural density zero. There are finitely many native words of length \(k\); on each cylinder \(T^k\) is an injective affine map bounded by \(2^k n\) on positive integers. The same reasoning handles fixed affine test inputs and finite unions. Thus all their first \(J\) dates agree off density zero for each fixed \(J\), and a slowly increasing deadline follows by diagonalization. Consecutive true shortcut starts have a synchronous merger on density one by the published counted shortcut result. Truncate that result at a large fixed time and apply the finite-path comparison to both starts; their eventual \(U\)-merger density is also one. [Leen, exact balanced-shortcut dictionary and absorption tail](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23268751)

The conclusion preserves local finite-orbit tests and fixed affine density transport while changing the complete ordinary future. It neither changes the genuine Collatz map in the research packet nor supplies its critical-clock law.
