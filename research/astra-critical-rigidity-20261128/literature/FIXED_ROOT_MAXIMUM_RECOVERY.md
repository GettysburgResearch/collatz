# Large relative recoveries on the true inverse tree of one fixed integer

**Proposed, source-qualified written theorem.** Fix a positive transient integer \(a\) prime to three. Let \(V_0=a,V_1,\ldots\) be a genuine native inverse ray sampled by natural predecessor densities, so \(T(V_{i+1})=V_i\). Define
\[
               G_k=\max_{0\le i\le j\le k} V_i/V_j .
\]
This ratio is a forward gain from ancestor \(V_j\) toward the fixed root. For every \(\epsilon>0\), almost surely, at all sufficiently large \(k\),
\[
       c\,k/\log\log k\ \le G_k\ \le C_\epsilon k(\log k)^{1+\epsilon}.
                                                                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(\log G_k/\log k\to1\) almost surely. Moreover \(G_k/k\) is tight and bounded away from zero in probability. These conclusions apply to a tree ending at one prescribed actual integer, including an eligible integer whose forward fate is unknown.

The [fixed-root inverse ray theorem](../ordinary/NATURAL_INVERSE_RAY_EQUIDISTRIBUTION.md) supplies this measure, the almost-sure growth \(\log V_k\sim\lambda k\) with \(\lambda=1/\mu=\log(4/3)/2\), and asymptotic odd frequency one half. We use its source-counted finite-prefix identification together with an all-height estimate for real, chronological excursions. A functional Brownian limit for the heights alone does not imply (1), whose logarithmic gain scale is smaller than the Brownian normalization.

## Tests between two physical gates

Write \(h(a)>0\) for the natural predecessor density of \(a\). For \(Z>w>a\), the high and low gates on a forward path are its first odd states at most \(Z\) and \(w\), respectively. On the inverse path the corresponding indices are the **last** visits to those sets of odd integers before reaching the root in chronological direction, that is, the largest outward indices of such visits, when present. These are finite because all inverse vertices are distinct. For fixed \(Z\) the tests of a segment between two such gates that occur before arrival at \(a\) are determined by a finite inverse prefix. Indeed every odd \(v\le Z\) on that ray reaches \(a\) after at most
\(\max_{v\le Z,\,v\ {\rm odd},\,v\leadsto a}\tau_a(v)\) steps, a finite maximum over fixed genuine paths when nonempty. Thus, for each fixed choice of thresholds, probabilities of these tests on the inverse ray, multiplied by \(h(a)\), are limits of subprobabilities from unrestricted uniform odd sources in \([b,2b)\) as \(b\to\infty\), retaining only paths reaching \(a\). The fixed-depth identification and odd-octave mass limits are proved in the cited companion. The bounds below may drop this success restriction on the physical source side. In particular no uniform numerical lower bound for a moving vertex's predecessor density is needed.

Suppose \(Z=e^{C_1 k},\ w=e^{C_2 k}\), where \(C_1>C_2>0\) are fixed. On the event that both gates are present before the root, the probability of a gain \(A\ge2\) in that segment is at most
\[
              C_{a,C_1,C_2}(k/A+k^{-c_0})                               \tag{2}
\]
for some fixed \(c_0>0\). Start at the outer source \(b\) and use the bounded first-landing test at \(Z\); Leen's uniform Transport comparison puts it on the reference source \(Z^\alpha,\ \alpha=1001/1000\), with error \(O((\ln Z)^{-c})\). Equivalently, apply that comparison directly from the outer source at every gate of the descending grid \(Z,Z^{1/\alpha},\ldots\) through the first threshold at most \(w\). There are \(O_{\alpha,C_1,C_2}(1)\) pieces, all with log height comparable to \(k\). First odd gates are real record lows. Hence a cross-gate gain is dominated by one commencing at the latest such gate. The local native martingale bound (3) in [Maximum preminimum recovery](MAXIMUM_PREMINIMUM_RECOVERY.md) applies on each new reference, including the starting odd landing, and the transport errors sum to \(O(k^{-c_0})\). Extending the last piece to the next lower grid gate can only increase the tested maximum. If that lower grid gate is never met, the same local comparison counts the failure as an error. Dividing by \(h(a)\) proves (2). [Leen, original success-masked Transport proposition](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23269488)

There is a complementary **small-maximum** estimate for \(w=Z^{9/10}\), \(\log Z=C_1 k\), with \(C_1\) fixed. On the event that both gates are present before the root, the probability of no gain at least \(A\) between them is at most
\[
         h(a)^{-1}\{ e^{-c_1 k/A}+O(k^{-c_0})\},                        \tag{3}
\]
for large \(A,k\) with \(k/A\) bounded below by a fixed positive number. The positive \(c_1\) depends on \(C_1\) only. Test the first odd landing at \(Z\) by absence of a gain through its first odd entrance at \(w\); take the test as one upon a double overshoot, and as absence of such a gain on the infinite remaining path if the lower gate is missing. At the reference source \(Z^\alpha\), put \(t_1=\lfloor0.03\ln Z\rfloor,\ t_2=\lfloor0.09\ln Z\rfloor\). With error \(O(Z^{-c'})\) its first odd entrance at \(Z\) occurs by \(t_1\): at \(\lfloor t_1/2\rfloor\) the negative log drift dominates \((\alpha-1)\ln Z+\ln2\), and an all-even run to \(t_1\) is exponentially unlikely. This uses the exact fair native dictionary with first bit one; the log correction through \(t_2\) is \(O(t_2 2^{t_2}/Z^\alpha)\). Deterministically all those states exceed \(Z^{9/10}\), since \(Z^\alpha2^{-t_2}>Z^{9/10}\). Fresh fair bits strictly after date \(t_1\) therefore supply a deterministic native window of length comparable to \(k\) between the gates, apart from the stated probability. The independent completed excursion trials in [Critical log-square masks](CRITICAL_LOG_SQUARE_MASKS.md) yield a homogeneous odd-to-odd gain at least \(A\) within this window with failure at most \(\exp(-c_1 k/A)+O(e^{-c'k})\). Real additive corrections only increase that gain. The reference first gate and the trial event need not be independent; the two failure bounds are added. A gain in the displayed window forces test zero when the upper gate precedes it, including on a missing lower gate. Transport and division by \(h(a)\) give (3).

## A pathwise bound at every depth

Take dyadic \(k=2^n\). Almost surely, for all sufficiently large dyadic \(k\), the segment of depths \([k,4k]\) is bracketed by the gates \(Z=e^{8(\lambda+1)k}\), \(w=e^{\lambda k/2}\). Indeed an odd vertex in \([4k,5k]\) exists by the almost-sure frequency and is below \(Z\) by the height law. Every depth at least \(k\) is above \(w\) from some point onward, and the fixed first odd vertex nearer the root is below \(w\) eventually. A gain in the indicated depth interval is then a gain between these true gates. Set \(A=k(\ln k)^{1+\epsilon}\) in (2). The upper bound is summable over dyadic \(k\). The first Borel–Cantelli lemma gives the upper bound on every sufficiently late such interval, without needing a summable exception probability for the almost-sure bracketing itself. From the height law, any gain greater than one between \(i<j\) with arbitrarily large \(j\) requires \(i>j/2\) eventually: otherwise \(\log V_i<\log V_j\), using the fixed bounded initial portion separately. Every such pair of indices is in one of these dyadic windows. Finite initial recoveries can be absorbed, proving the upper bound for every large depth.

For the lower estimate choose \(Z=e^{\lambda k/3}\), \(w=Z^{9/10}\). Both gates are present before the root eventually, since the first odd vertex has fixed finite height on each ray. Both have outward index less than \(k\) eventually, since every larger index exceeds \(Z\) by the almost-sure height law. Choose \(A=c_3 k/\ln\ln k\) with \(c_3>0\) small. The failure bound (3) is summable at dyadic \(k\): choose \(c_1/c_3>2\). Borel–Cantelli and monotonicity of \(G_k\) between powers of two prove the lower side of (1). With \(A=b k\) instead, the same argument at single depths gives \(\limsup_k\Pr(G_k<b k)\le h(a)^{-1}e^{-c_1/b}\) for each fixed small \(b>0\), proving tightness away from zero.

For upper tightness take again the upper gate \(Z=e^{8(\lambda+1)k}\), and now grid downward to a fixed large bottom threshold \(B_0\), ending at the last \(z\ge B_0\), so \(z<B_0^\alpha\). With probability tending to one an odd vertex below \(Z\) occurs at a depth at least \(k\). Between this first forward entrance and the bottom gate, the same argument as for (2) sums the errors to \(O((\ln B_0)^{-c_0})\) and the native date budgets to \(O(k)\). If the path ends at \(a\) before reaching the bottom odd gate, gains before the root are still covered by the last extended test, which permits an infinite segment. If the bottom odd gate occurs before the root, the part from that odd vertex to \(a\) belongs to one of a fixed finite collection of true paths; its gain is bounded in terms of \(B_0,a\). A crossing ratio resets at that gate. With \(A=bk\), first letting \(k\to\infty\) and then \(B_0\to\infty\) yields \(\limsup_k\Pr(G_k\ge bk)\le C_a/b\). This proves the remaining tightness assertion without summing pointwise unquantified errors over random depths.
