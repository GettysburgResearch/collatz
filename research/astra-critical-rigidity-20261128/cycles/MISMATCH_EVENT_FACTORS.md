# Low multiplicative rank captures little height at the actual mismatch dates

Let \(p\) be the least odd period of a positive ordinary Collatz cycle and \(x_t\) its odd states. At a proper lag \(\ell\), form the post-mismatch multiset
\[
 {\cal M}_\ell=(x_b:b\in B_\ell)\ \uplus\ (x_{b+\ell}:b\in B_\ell),
 \qquad B_\ell=\{i+1\bmod p:e_i\ne e_{i+\ell}\}.
\]
Put \(E=\sum_{x\in{\cal M}_\ell}\log x\), using natural logarithms. One physical date occurs at most twice. After seeing the entire cycle and this lag, choose any multiplicative subgroup \(G\subset\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}\) of rank \(r\). At each occurrence of \(x\), choose any positive integer divisor \(u_x\mid x\) lying in \(G\). Then
\[
 \begin{split}
 E/p&\longrightarrow\infty,\\
 \frac{\sum_{x\in{\cal M}_\ell}\log u_x}{E}&\longrightarrow0
             \qquad\text{whenever }r=o(\sqrt{E/p}).                     \tag{1}
 \end{split}
\]
Both statements are uniform over proper lags, including growing mismatch counts. The second is uniform over the groups, their generators and the divisor choices. In particular, even the largest parts of these states supported on a postselected pool of \(o(\sqrt{E/p})\) distinct primes account for a vanishing share of their logarithmic height. The multiplicative rank of the post-mismatch states tends to infinity at every lag. The assertion measures the arithmetic of precisely the marked states, which can have vanishing time density.

The first statement follows from the [ordinary mismatch-event theorem](MISMATCH_EVENT_HEIGHT.md). Changing the logarithm base and replacing \(\log(x+1)\) by \(\log x\) loses at most \(2p\log2\). The arithmetic statement follows from the finite sampled inequality below.

## A rank bound for height-weighted samples

Write \(2^{e_i}x_{i+1}=3x_i+1\) and assign to every full odd edge the positive cost
\[
 c_i=\log3+(e_i-1)\log2,\qquad V=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}c_i .
\]
All intervening edges are charged even when marked dates are far apart. Since \(x_i\ge1\), the positive lap product gives \(\sum e_i\le2p\), so \(V\le p\log6\). Let \(S\) be any subset of dates and \(H_t=\log x_t\). For \(0<\eta\le1\) put \(S_\eta=\{t\in S:\log u_t\ge\eta H_t,\ H_t>0\}\). Then
\[
                \sum_{t\in S_\eta} H_t \ \le\ \frac{r(r+2)}{\eta}V.    \tag{2}
\]
The \(r=0\) case follows because \(G=\{1\}\). The estimate is independent of the cardinality and the placement of \(S\), and permits groups generated by composite rationals with arbitrarily large prime support.

For the elementary return estimate, take \(d\ge1\) actual forward edges from date \(i\) to date \(j\), writing \(F_h=\sum_{v=0}^{h-1}e_{i+v}\). The affine numerator
\[
 C=\sum_{h=0}^{d-1}3^{d-1-h}2^{F_h}
      =2^{F_d}x_j-3^d x_i
\]
is a positive multiple of \(\gcd(x_i,x_j)\). Since \(F_h\le h+F_d-d\), the geometric sum gives \(C\le(3^d-2^d)2^{F_d-d}<\exp(\sum_{v=i}^{j-1}c_v)\), on lifted times. Thus
\[
 \log\gcd(x_i,x_j)<\sum_{v=i}^{j-1}c_v,\qquad
 |H_{i+1}-H_i|\le c_i.                                                 \tag{3}
\]
The second estimate follows directly from \(H_{i+1}-H_i=\log(3+1/x_i)-e_i\log2\) and \(x_i\ge1\). Applying (3) on a full lap gives \(H_i<V\). These affine gcd and return-cost estimates are established tools. [Weaver, Paper 137, §§1–3](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/137) · [Weaver, Paper 138, §2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/138) · [Murmuration Collatz and Solomon, Paper 182, equations (9)–(11)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/182)

Place the dates in their actual order on the circle of circumference \(V\), with successively spaced distances \(c_i\). Give each \(t\in S_\eta\) the backwards interval of length \(\eta H_t/(r+2)\) ending at \(t\). It is shorter than a full turn. Suppose \(r+1\) such intervals cover one point and order their endpoints forward from that point. For two endpoints \(i<j\) in this order let \(d_{ij}\) be the intervening forward cost. Then
\[
 d_{ij}\le \frac{\eta H_j}{r+2},
 \qquad H_i\ge H_j-d_{ij},
 \qquad d_{ij}\le\frac{\eta H_i}{r+2-\eta}\le\frac{\eta H_i}{r+1}.
\]
The covered point fixes a common lift of all endpoints. By (3) and \(u_i\mid x_i\), every two selected divisors satisfy
\[
                  \log\gcd(u_i,u_j)<\frac{\eta}{r+1}\min(H_i,H_j).
\]
Thus each of these \(r+1\) divisors satisfies \(\log u_i>\sum_{j\ne i}\log\gcd(u_i,u_j)\).

These inequalities force multiplicative independence. If \(\prod_i u_i^{n_i}=1\) in a nonzero integer relation, choose \(i\) maximizing \(|n_i|>0\). For each prime \(l\), the equality of valuations gives \(v_l(u_i)\le\sum_{j\ne i}v_l(u_j)\). Capping each summand at \(v_l(u_i)\) preserves this inequality, hence \(u_i\mid\prod_{j\ne i}\gcd(u_i,u_j)\), a contradiction. This is the usual affine-gcd independence criterion applied to actual divisors. Consequently the intervals have overlap multiplicity at most \(r\). Integrating their lengths over the cost circle proves (2).

There is also a finite open-path version. For an ordinary path \(x_0,\ldots,x_n\) let \(V\) be its actual edge cost and \(H_*=\max_{0\le i\le n}\log x_i\). The same intervals lie in a real interval of length \(V+\eta H_* /(r+2)\). Their overlap proof holds unchanged, so the right side of (2) becomes \(r(r+2)V/\eta+rH_*\), where \(H_*\le H_0+V\). This endpoint allowance keeps short paths from arbitrarily large sources in scope.

## Divisors, prime pools and postselected groups

For the two lists of marked states in \({\cal M}_\ell\), apply (2) separately and use \(K=2r(r+2)V\). For \(r>0\) and \(E>0\), integration over \(0<\eta<1\) gives the finite result
\[
 \sum_{x\in{\cal M}_\ell}\log u_x
  \ \le\ \int_0^1\min(E,K/\eta)\,d\eta
  =\begin{cases}E,& E\le K,\\ K[1+\log(E/K)],& E>K.\end{cases}         \tag{4}
\]
Indeed each \(x>1\) contributes its height times the length of \(0<\eta\le\log u_x/\log x\). Taking \(r=o(\sqrt{E/p})\) gives \(K/E\to0\), proving (1). If the selected states themselves all lie in \(G\), (2) with \(\eta=1\) gives the sharper finite inequality
\[
                        r\ge \sqrt{1+E/(2V)}-1 .                         \tag{5}
\]
The same proof applies to a bounded union of rank-\(r\) groups with one selected group per occurrence, by multiplying \(K\) by the number of groups. A coset of a rank-\(r\) group is contained in a group of rank at most \(r+1\).

Yang's September 2026 paper and Weaver's Papers 137–138 already bound visits to postselected multiplicative groups, establish strong rank growth in positive-density samples, and analyze prime-to-pool escape. Paper 138, (35c), gives a relative-height estimate for the *number* of exceptional times on a nonperiodic orbit. Paper 182 charges prime powers shared with a recent chronological inventory. The estimate here weights arbitrary sparse samples by their actual logarithmic heights, permits divisors chosen in one moving group of arbitrary prime support, and combines the finite inequality with the divergent height at the post-mismatch dates. It imposes no generation bound on those states in an unrestricted candidate. ## A full-denominator inequality from the ranks of reduced numerators

Let \(x_i=y_i/q\) be any primitive positive rational cycle with \(p\) odd phases, \(A=\sum e_i\), and its full common reduced denominator \(q\). For a subgroup of rank \(r\) containing all actual positive integer numerators \(y_i\), one has
\[
                 p\log_2 q\ \le\ A+r(r+2)(2A-p).                       \tag{6}
\]
In particular, whenever \(\log_2q\ge c A\) with fixed \(c>0\), the multiplicative rank of the reduced numerators is at least \(\sqrt{1+(cp-1)/2}-1\) for \(cp\ge1\). This applies on every growing-odd-period family with bounded mismatch count at some proper lag, by the [complete-denominator theorem at the native period](ALL_FIXED_ROTATION_DENOMINATORS.md). The rank concerns the actual integer numerators in lowest terms.

Indeed the arc identity becomes \(2^{F_d}y_j-3^d y_i=qC\). Since every \(y_i\) is coprime to \(q\), the gcd of \(y_i,y_j\) divides the same \(C\) as before. Set
\[
  c'_i=\log(3+q/y_i)+(e_i-1)\log2,\qquad
  V'=\sum c'_i=(2A-p)\log2 .
\]
Each edge cost is positive and at least \(c_i\); the total identity follows by multiplying \(2^{e_i}y_{i+1}/y_i=3+q/y_i\). Also \(|\log(y_{i+1}/y_i)|\le c'_i\), since \(\log(3+q/y_i)>\log2\). Apply the full-lap gcd bound to see \(\log y_i<V'\). The circle proof of (2) now applies to arbitrary samples of the \(y_i\), their divisors, and \(V'\). On all dates with \(\eta=1,\ u_i=y_i\), it gives \(\sum_i\log y_i\le r(r+2)V'\). Finally \(\prod_i(3+q/y_i)=2^A\) gives \(\prod_i y_i\ge q^p 2^{-A}\). Combining proves (6). The argument also includes \(r=0\), when all numerators equal one.
