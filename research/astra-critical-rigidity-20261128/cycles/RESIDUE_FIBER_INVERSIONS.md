# The reduced clock, its order modulo the complete denominator, and true odd-phase inversions

**Proposed elementary written theorem.** Let a primitive positive rational Syracuse cycle have odd phases \(C\), minimum \(m\), maximum \(X\), odd period \(p\), total native division exponent \(A\), and complete reduced denominator \(q\). Write
\[
 g=\gcd(A,p),\qquad B=A/g,\quad P=p/g,\qquad
 h=\operatorname{ord}_q(3^P2^{-B}).
\]
Use \(h=1\) at \(q=1\). The order always divides \(g\), because the positive cycle determinant \(2^A-3^p\) is divisible by \(q\), which is prime to six. Define the finite integer
\[
 I_q(C)=\sum_{d\ge1}\#\{(x,y)\in C^2:\ y=2^d x+u,\
                 u\text{ a positive odd integer},\ 3u<2^d-1\}.            \tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
                I_q(C)\ \ge\ g/h-1,\qquad
                I_q(C)\ \equiv\ g/h-1\pmod2.                              \tag{2}
\]
The offset \(u\) in (1) is a genuine integer on the original rational scale. In particular, if \(X\le16m\) then every inversion in (1) has \(d=3,\ u=1\), and its upper phase goes through the exact native chain
\[
                         8x+1\ \longmapsto\ 6x+1\ \longmapsto\ 9x+2.
\]
If also \(X<9m+2\), there are no inversions and
\[
                 \gcd(A,p)=\operatorname{ord}_q(3^{p/g}2^{-A/g}).        \tag{3}
\]
Thus (3) holds at **every physical width \(X/m\le9\)**, with no large-period or large-minimum qualification. In particular \(g\mid\varphi(q)\) there (take \(\varphi(1)=1\)). More generally (3) holds at all widths with \(I_q(C)=0\). Equation (2) also identifies the parity of the number of genuine affine pairs at widths between nine and sixteen.

The ordinary predecessor counts inversions on the **full unaccelerated circuit** in sixfold order. It gives a \(g-1\) lower bound, parity, lag symmetry and the ordinary coprime-clock consequence when \(X<9m+2\). Here the raw circuit uses just odd phases and dyadic scaling. Sorting inside complete-denominator residue classes identifies \(g/h\) sorted circuits and yields the rational order in (3). The previously recorded rational cycle \(5/7,11/7\), with \(g=2\), has \(3/4\equiv-1\bmod 7\) and order two as predicted. ## Which pairs in the dyadic lift reverse order?

Let \(\mathcal Z=\{2^n x:x\in C,\ n\in\mathbb Z\}\). The representation is unique: the \(q x\) are distinct odd integers. Let \(D(z)=2z\) and
\[
             N(2^n x)=2^n(3x+1)=2^{n+e_x}x'.
\]
These commute and are bijections on \(\mathcal Z\) because the genuine native cycle permutes its distinct odd phases. Each \(qz\) lies in \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\); reduce it modulo the odd integer \(q\), using the inverse of two. Its residue is a unit if \(q>1\). For \(q=1\) use the single trivial residue. Write \(\mathcal Z_\ell\) for the ordered subset having residue \(\ell\). The residue maps under \(D,N\) are multiplication by two and three. All occurring residues form one coset of the subgroup generated by two and three, because the original \(D,N\) action is transitive on \(\mathcal Z\).

Take a strictly ordered pair in the same fiber whose images under \(N\) reverse. Divide by the common dyadic scale so the upper member is \(y\in C\); the lower is \(2^d x\). The lower correction must exceed the upper correction, so \(d\ge1\). Equality of residues gives \(y-2^d x=u\in\mathbb Z\): the difference has denominator dividing \(q\) and its numerator is divisible by \(q\). It is positive and odd since \(q\) and \(qy\) are odd. The inversion condition is exactly \(3u<2^d-1\). Conversely every triple in (1) gives exactly one inversion modulo simultaneous dyadic scaling. Ties cannot occur since \(N\) is injective. Finiteness follows from \(2^d<X/m\). If \(X\le16m\) then \(d\le3\), and only \(d=3,u=1\) satisfies the displayed test. For \(y=8x+1\), \(3y+1=4(6x+1)\) and \(3(6x+1)+1=2(9x+2)\); both successors have odd reduced numerator, giving the stated exact native exponents two and one.

## Sorting preserves the mean logarithmic winding

Each nonempty fiber, in increasing real order, is order-isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z\). It is a finite nonempty union of geometric progressions with common ratio \(2^s>1\), \(s=\operatorname{ord}_q(2)\) (take \(s=1\) at \(q=1\)). A \(D\)-commuting bijection \(F\) with the original residue update \(\ell\mapsto3\ell\) has a well-defined mean
\[
             w(F)=\frac1p\sum_{x\in C}\log_2(F(x)/x).
\]
The sum may use any one representative on each of the \(p\) rays, by homogeneity. For any two homogeneous bijections of these rays and their lifts, the composition mean is the sum of the two means, by reindexing. Thus \(w(D)=1,\ w(N)=A/p\), the last equality by telescoping the true odd successor heights.

If some order on a fiber is inverted, adjacent members of that fiber have inverted images. Exchange their images and those of all simultaneously doubled copies. The inputs belong to distinct physical rays, since homogeneity preserves order on a single ray. Consequently all these paired swaps are disjoint. Each swapped pair is adjacent on its own input fiber; \(D\) bijects the respective ordered fibers increasingly. Swapping these adjacent entries removes exactly one class of within-fiber inversions modulo \(D\). In detail, comparisons with a third member to one side of both entries are permuted in pairs; the same conclusion holds for pairs of two disjoint swapped blocks since the total order between the input blocks is unchanged. Only the internal comparison of the chosen pair and its translates is lost. This pairing is \(D\)-equivariant, so it counts inversion classes. The new homogeneous bijection retains the same residue action and \(w(F)\), since the product of its two selected images is unchanged using representatives at the chosen adjacent pair. It composes the ray permutation with one transposition.

After precisely \(I_q(C)\) steps there is a homogeneous bijection \(N_0\), increasing from each fiber onto its successor fiber, with the same mean \(\rho=A/p\). Both \(D\) and \(N_0\), as well as their inverses, are increasing between the appropriate fibers.

## Each sorted ray cycle has length \(hP\)

For integers \(u,v\) write \(M=D^vN_0^u\). It commutes with \(D,N_0\) and has mean \(v+\rho u\). If \(2^v3^u=1\bmod q\), it is a strictly increasing bijection of each fiber to itself. Such a bijection is a translation on its discrete rank set \(\mathbb Z\). The shift is the same on every occurring fiber: \(D,N_0\) are increasing bijections between their respective fibers and commute with \(M\), while the residue multipliers two and three act transitively on the occurring residues. Thus \(M(z)>z\) everywhere, \(M(z)<z\) everywhere, or \(M(z)=z\) everywhere. The sign is exactly the sign of \(v+\rho u\), by the mean formula.

If a physical ray returns to itself after \(u>0\) applications of \(N_0\), then \(D^vN_0^u\) fixes one lift on it for some integer \(v\). The unit residue of that lift forces \(2^v3^u=1\bmod q\); hence the preceding sign identity forces \(v+\rho u=0\). Since \(\rho=B/P\) is reduced, \(u=kP,\ v=-kB\), and the residue identity now forces \(h\mid k\). Conversely \(D^{-hB}N_0^{hP}\) preserves all residues and has mean zero, so it fixes every lift. Every sorted ray cycle has least period exactly \(hP\); there are \(p/(hP)=g/h\) of them. The original ray permutation had one cycle. Every transposition changes the number of cycles by plus or minus one. The number of transpositions is \(I_q(C)\), giving both claims in (2).

In the zero-inversion case the same proof gives an order test on every native arc. If \(2^v3^u=1\bmod q\), then for all phases \(x_t\),
\[
 \operatorname{sign}\big(2^{v+E_{t+u}-E_t}x_{t+u}-x_t\big)
                  =\operatorname{sign}(v+uA/p),                         \tag{4}
\]
where \(E_t\) is the extended cumulative native division clock. This is a simultaneous statement on all actual cuts, for positive and negative \(u\).
