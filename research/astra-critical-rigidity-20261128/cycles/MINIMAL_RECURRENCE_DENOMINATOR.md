# The complete divisor carried by the minimal chronological recurrence

**Proposed written theorem.** On any primitive positive rational odd cycle take its primitive odd period \(p\), total valuation \(A\), real minimum \(m\), common reduced denominator \(q\), reduced clock \(P=p/\gcd(A,p)\) and \(B=A/\gcd(A,p)\). Let \(S\) be the shift on the unfolded sequence \(V_i=2^{E_i}x_i\) with \(E_0=0\). Write \(f\) for its monic minimal rational annihilating polynomial and \(r=\deg f\). Then
\[
              q\mid |f(3)|,\qquad
 q=\frac{|f(3)|}{\gcd\bigl(|f(3)|,\ |G(S)U_0|\bigr)},\quad
          G(T)=\frac{f(T)-f(3)}{T-3},\quad U_i=2^{E_i}.                 \tag{1}
\]
The conventional meaning of \(G(S)U_0\) here is \((G(S)U)_0\). Moreover, for an integer \(e\) dividing \(P\), with \(P/2\le e\le P\) and \(e\mid r\), and \(\xi=2^{A/p}>3\), one has
\[
                (\xi^e-3^e)^{r/e}\le |f(3)|
                           \le (\xi^e+3^e)^{r/e}.                       \tag{2}
\]
Consequently for **every** sequence with \(m\to\infty\),
\[
                       \frac{\log_3 |f(3)|}{r}\to1,\qquad
                          \limsup\frac{\log_3 q}{r}\le1.                \tag{3}
\]
There is no real-width or sheet-count condition. When the [sharp near-octave lower bound](POOLED_CYCLIC_DENOMINATOR_RATE.md) applies and \(\liminf m/r\ge1\), it follows that \(\log_3 q/r\to1\) for arbitrary \(\gcd(A,p)\), including sequences with \(r<p\). In that regime the entire remaining cancellation \(|f(3)|/q\) is subexponential in chronological degree. [Chronological dimension and radical degree](NEAR_OCTAVE_CYCLE_DENOMINATORS.md)

## Proof

The native update reads \((S-3)V=U\). Since \(S^pV=2^A V\), minimality gives \(f\mid T^p-2^A\) over \(\mathbb Q\), by polynomial division. Monicity and Gauss's lemma give \(f\in\mathbb Z[T]\). Positivity of the cycle implies \(2^A>3^p\), so \(f(3)\ne0\). Evaluating the elementary polynomial identity \(f(T)=f(3)+(T-3)G(T)\) on \(V\) gives \(f(3)V=-G(S)U\), with \(G\in\mathbb Z[T]\). At date zero every forward \(U_i\) is an integer and \(V_0=x_0\) is reduced over \(q\). This proves (1), including prime powers at nontrivial canceled factors. Translating the initial origin gives the same formula at every true phase. The special full polynomial \(T^p-2^A\) recovers the ordinary closure determinant by this same identity.

Over \(K=\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}\), the automorphisms of \(K(2^{1/P})/K\) act on \(2^{1/P}\) by a subgroup of the \(P\)-th roots of unity. All those roots of unity are in \(K\); hence this is a Galois extension, the subgroup has order \(e=[K(2^{1/P}):K]\mid P\), and consists of all \(e\)-th roots. Also \(2^{e/P}\in K\). With \(d=P/e\), inclusion \(2^{1/d}\in K\) forces \(d\le2\): the real field generated by the positive radical \(2^{1/d}\) is not Galois when \(d>2\), by Eisenstein and the nonreal conjugates, whereas every finite subfield of an abelian Galois extension of \(\mathbb Q\) is Galois. Thus \(e\ge P/2\), as in the chronological rank theorem.

The full roots of the unfolded clock polynomial are \(\xi\zeta\), \(\zeta^p=1\), with \(\xi=(2^{1/P})^B\). Since \((B,P)=1\), the conjugates over \(K\) of each root form the full \(e\)-gon of its rotations by \(e\)-th roots. The roots are simple, and the rational polynomial \(f\) contains a union of complete such orbits. At the real argument three, the contribution of the orbit of \(\alpha=\xi\zeta\) has magnitude \(|3^e-\alpha^e|\), between \(\xi^e-3^e\) and \(\xi^e+3^e\). Multiplication over its \(r/e\) orbits proves (2).

By the positive product identity \(B/P=\log_2 3+O(1/m)>\log_2 3\), so \(P\to\infty\) as \(m\to\infty\) and \(\xi\to3\). The published finite irrationality measure at the reduced fraction \(B/P\) gives \(B/P-\log_2 3\gg P^{-C}\) for a fixed \(C\). In the lower estimate of (2),
\[
          \xi^e-3^e=3^e\left(2^{e(B/P-\log_2 3)}-1\right)
                                  \gg 3^e P^{-C} .
\]
The upper estimate is at most \(2^{r/e}\xi^r\); the losses in its normalized logarithm and in that of the lower estimate tend to zero as \(e\ge P/2\to\infty\). This proves (3). [Classical BLS logarithm bound, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.22405/2226-8383-2018-19-1-15-25)
