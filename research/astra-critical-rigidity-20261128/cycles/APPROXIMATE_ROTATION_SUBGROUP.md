# Simultaneous near periods form a bounded-order subgroup under positive occupation

On a primitive positive integer cycle of odd length \(p\) write \(s(h)=\#\{i:e_{i+h}\ne e_i\}\), including \(s(0)=0\). Consider any of the following hypotheses along a growing family of cycles, with fixed \(\delta>0\).

1. At least \(\delta p\) odd dates have \(\log(1+x_i)\le W\), for a positive scale \(W\) that may vary with the cycle. Use \(v=W\).
2. Choose a multiplicative subgroup \(G\) of rank \(r\) after seeing the cycle, and positive divisors \(u_i\mid x_i\) in that group. At least \(\delta p\) odd dates have \(\log u_i\ge\eta\log x_i\), for a fixed \(\eta>0\). Use \(v=(r+1)^2\).

For each fixed \(C>0\), all shifts with \(s(h)v\le Cp\) belong, on all sufficiently large cycles, to **one subgroup** of \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) of order at most \(\lfloor1/\delta\rfloor\). Equivalently, this bound holds for the order of the subgroup generated by all the indicated shifts. For example, if the relevant set of dates has density strictly greater than one third, only the zero shift and, on an even period, possibly its unique order-two shift can qualify. The generating-set statement constrains several simultaneous near periods even when their orders differ.

Let \(D\) be the set of dates in the selected hypothesis. For every fixed \(C'\) one has
\[
   \sup_{\substack{h\ne0\\s(h)v\le C' p}}
               |D\cap(D+h)|/p\longrightarrow0,                            \tag{1}
\]
with the supremum of an empty set interpreted as zero. In the first case both dates have paired log height at most \(2W\); (1) follows from the [uniform ordinary paired-height theorem](UNIFORM_ROTATION_HEIGHT.md) after a constant change of logarithm base. In the second case this is exactly the [moving-rank divisibility theorem, (3)](ROTATION_RANK_TRADEOFF.md). The group and its selected divisors are fixed within each cycle when comparing multiple lags.

Here is the finite packing step. Put \(A_B=\{h:s(h)v\le Bp\}\). The Hamming metric gives \(s(h-h')\le s(h)+s(h')\). Thus any fixed number \(k\) of distinct translates \(D+h_i\), with \(h_i\in A_B\), have pairwise intersections of size \(o(p)\), uniformly in the choices, by (1) at \(C'=2B\). Inclusion-exclusion through pairs gives
\(p\ge|\bigcup_i(D+h_i)|\ge k\delta p-o(p)\).
Writing \(N=\lfloor1/\delta\rfloor\), taking \(k=N+1\) shows \(|A_B|\le N\) eventually for **each fixed** \(B\). For \(h\in A_C\), the \(N+1\) elements \(0,h,\ldots,Nh\) lie in \(A_{NC}\); hence every such \(h\) has order at most \(N\). All these elements belong to the subgroup killed by \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,N)\), of cardinality at most \(L\) in a cyclic group. Every element in their generated subgroup is a sum of at most \(L\) signed generators: take a shortest path from zero in the undirected Cayley graph on that subgroup. Consequently this whole subgroup lies in \(A_{LC}\), whose cardinality is at most \(N\). All scale factors here are fixed before taking the period limit.

There is a balanced-minimum version with a sharper harmonic threshold. Fix \(\lambda>0\) and suppose \(m/m_{\rm bal}\ge\lambda\). Let \(c_0=1-2\log(2)u<0.96355\) be the specific pressure constant in the [growing-budget balanced-minimum theorem](BALANCED_MINIMUM_GROWING_BUDGET.md). For fixed \(C\), the subgroup generated by \(\{h:s(h)\log p\le Cp\}\) has order at most \(\lfloor c_0/\lambda\rfloor\) eventually whenever \(c_0/\lambda\ge1\). If \(c_0/\lambda<2\), only zero can qualify. Indeed orders of the individual qualifying nonzero elements are bounded by the growing-order conclusion, and their fixed-order bounds give \(M\lambda\le c_0\). Their generated cyclic group has bounded order by the same common-annihilator argument. Its generator is a bounded signed sum of qualifying shifts, so also has \(s(h)\log p=O(p)\); applying the fixed-order estimate to that generator gives the bound on the generated group. When no nonzero shift qualifies the subgroup is trivial.
