# The full reduced clock strengthens the denominator bound near width three

**Proposed written theorem.** Use the positive rational cycle notation and the fixed width \(2<R<3\) of [the rational six-sum theorem](RATIONAL_SIX_SUM_DENOMINATORS.md). In particular \(p\) is the primitive odd period, \(A\) its total division exponent, \(q\) the complete reduced denominator, \(m\) the real odd minimum, \(g=\gcd(A,p)\), and \(P=p/g\). Put \(\gamma=\log_2 3,\ \eta=\log_2(R/2)\) and
\[
 \nu=5.116201,\quad
 \theta=\frac{\eta}{6\gamma}\log_2(4096/924),\quad
 \chi=\frac{\eta\log6}{3\log3},\quad b=1/(\nu\theta)-1 .
\]
Then along all such cycles with \(p\to\infty\),
\[
 \liminf\frac{\log q}{\log p}\ \ge\
 \max\left\{ \frac{b}{b+\chi},\
       \min\left(1-\chi,\frac{1-\nu\theta}{1-(\nu-1)\theta}\right)\right\}.
                                                                         \tag{1}
\]
In particular, the lower bound tends to \(0.68198908\ldots\) as \(R\uparrow3\). The previous rational bound tends to \(0.60106615\ldots\). On any subsequence with \(m/p\to\infty\) one has the separate stronger rate
\[
 \liminf\frac{\log q}{\log p}\ \ge\
                 \frac{1-\nu\theta}{1-(\nu-1)\theta}.                    \tag{2}
\]
The additional inputs are the [exact reduced-clock order modulo the full denominator](RESIDUE_FIBER_INVERSIONS.md) and a spatial grid generated by the **actual reduced** slope on the large-minimum branch. Equal folded ranks of this slope may contain different rational physical phases.

## One native representative at each reduced rank

Suppose \(m/p\to\infty\). Write \(\rho=A/p=B/P\) in reduced terms. The exact product yields \(\rho-\gamma\le C/m\). The published two-logarithm theorem gives \(\rho-\gamma\gg P^{-\nu}\) here, since \(B\asymp P\), and hence \(m\ll P^\nu,\ P\to\infty\). Use the raw lift of the rational six-sum proof, \(\widetilde X_t=2^{E_t}x_t\). The correction
\[
 \delta_t=\log_2(\widetilde X_t/\widetilde X_0)-t\rho
\]
is \(p\)-periodic and has \(\sup_{t\in\mathbb Z}|\delta_t|\le Cp/m=o(1)\), by summing the true positive angular corrections and subtracting their full-lap mean.

Choose \(1\le\tau\le P\) with \(\tau B\equiv1\bmod P\), and write \(\tau\rho=k+1/P\). For all integers \(t,j\) we have
\[
 \log_2(\widetilde X_{t+j\tau}/\widetilde X_t)
           =kj+j/P+\delta_{t+j\tau}-\delta_t.                            \tag{3}
\]
The error in (3) is \(o(1)\) uniformly even when \(j\tau\) crosses many native periods. Use \(N=P,\ W=\lfloor P/(\log P)^5\rfloor\) in equations (4)–(7) of the rational six-sum proof. Equation (3) replaces the continued-fraction sampling estimate there. Explicitly, with \(L=2^{1-v}m,\ F_t=\lfloor\log_2(\widetilde X_t/L)\rfloor\), the columns \(Z_{t,j}=2^{-F_t-kj}\widetilde X_{t+j\tau}\) have the same two tags and the same exact native edge. At an anchor with fractional base angle tending to zero, the fundamental array \(Y_r=2^{-F_*-kr}\widetilde X_{*+r\tau}\), \(0\le r<P\), satisfies \(Y_r/L=2^{r/P}+o(1)\) uniformly. At interior ranks, (3) gives \(F_{*+r\tau}=F_*+kr\), so the compared columns are literally \(Y_{r+j}\). Completion of the \(P\) chosen dates as one physical cycle is unnecessary: the scalar recurrence uses the full actual \(p\) dates, while the required zero interval is tested on these chosen dates by all-rank (3).

Consequently the zero-to-integer propagation, the six-mass calendar argument, the sparse recurrence contradiction applied to \(qY_r\), and the binary \(B_6\) extraction carry through without change. As in the rational theorem, if \(\log(qm)/\log P\) is bounded use the polynomial-height argument; if the ratio tends to infinity the resulting upper bound on \(\log P/\log(qm)\) is automatic. Hence
\[
                   \log P\le(\theta+o(1))\log(qm).                       \tag{4}
\]
All calendars in this proof still originate from the given rational cycle.

## Combining the two complete-denominator restrictions

The exact-order theorem gives \(g\le\varphi(q)\le q\) in this width band, including \(q=1\). In particular \(p\le qP\). To prove an exponent lower bound, restrict to any subsequence of bounded \(\log q/\log p\), pass to limits
\[
 \beta=\lim\log q/\log p,\quad \pi=\lim\log P/\log p,\quad
 \sigma=\lim\log(1+m)/\log p .
\]
These real exponents are bounded since \(P\le p\) and \(m\ll P^\nu\) when \(m\ge1\). On \(m/p\to\infty\) we get \(1-\beta\le\pi\), \(\sigma\le\nu\pi\), and \(\pi\le\theta(\beta+\sigma)\) by (4). Since \(\nu\theta<1\) already at \(R=3\), elimination gives (2).

For (1), if \(\sigma\le1\), the [uniform rational four-copy bound](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/5136b7fe70176172405a0de168a2de2ed26429b8/research/astra-global-rigidity-20261128/cycles/FOUR_COPY_WIDTH.md) gives \(1\le\beta+\chi\sigma\) after taking its arbitrarily small exponent loss to zero. Thus \(\beta\ge1-\chi\). When \(\sigma>1\), \(m/p\to\infty\) and (2) applies. The existing minimum-to-period estimate \(b/(b+\chi)\) applies in both cases, proving (1). All three rates concern the complete denominator; no assumption on the order of a proper factor of \(q\) is involved.
