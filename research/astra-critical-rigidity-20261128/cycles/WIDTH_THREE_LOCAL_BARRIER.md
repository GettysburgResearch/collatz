# The width-three boundary for a local sixth-difference argument

Let \(P(T)=(T-1)^2(T^2-1)(T^3-1)(T^4-1)(T^5-1)\), the order-six, degree-16 spatial stencil of PR213. For **every \(R>3\)**, every finite past and future depth, and every finite number \(J\ge17\) of neighboring spatial positions, there are \(J\) parallel trajectories of positive odd **ordinary integers** with the following properties.

- All their odd states are distinct, prime to three, and belong to one band with maximum/minimum strictly less than \(R\). Their minimum can exceed any specified threshold.
- Their folded labels \(Z_{t,j}=c_{t,j}x_{t,j}\), with \(c_{t,j}\in\{1,2\}\), follow one mechanical calendar \(b_t\in\{1,2\}\) and satisfy \(2^{b_t}Z_{t+1,j}=3Z_{t,j}+c_{t,j}\). At every date the labels are in strictly increasing spatial order and every consecutive spatial difference of orders one through five is strictly positive.
- On one chosen 17-position stencil, \(H_t=P(E)Z_{t,j}\) takes the values \(0,2,2,0\) at one three-edge window and \(0,-2,-2,0\) at another. It is zero before, between and after both excursions. The past, gap and future with zero height can have arbitrarily prescribed lower lengths.

Here \(E\) advances the spatial position by one. Each trajectory has every prescribed predecessor and successor in the supplied window. The construction does not identify different paths as phases of one closed ordinary orbit. PR213's global width theorem proves \(\liminf X/m\ge3\) for a growing ordinary cycle. It uses a local integer-run classification for this exact \(P\) and a subsequent argument around the closed circuit. The control above gives genuine sign changes already at widths arbitrarily close to three from above, including when a proposed local test uses the true grade transitions and arbitrarily many adjacent ordered integer labels. A new global constraint could still improve the width bound. ## The three-edge signed return

Write \(\gamma=\log_23,\ \alpha=\gamma-1\), \(\phi_t=\{\tau+t\gamma\}\), \(b_t=\lfloor\tau+(t+1)\gamma\rfloor-\lfloor\tau+t\gamma\rfloor\). Choose a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\) with \(3\cdot2^\delta<R\). Take a date with \(0<\phi_t<\delta\). At this date and its next two dates the phases are \(\phi_t,\phi_t+\alpha,\phi_t+2\alpha-1\), and the three successive windings are \(1,2,1\). The preceding winding is two. These strict inequalities persist on an open phase interval. Irrational rotation supplies arbitrarily late visits to that interval, so the two gadgets and any required finite padding can be separated.

For one positive gadget write \(u=\log_2 c\). The following rows specify three positive and three negative coordinates, each with unit coefficient of the indicated sign. All exterior coordinates in time have \(u=1\).

| Relative date | Positive triple \(u\) | Negative triple \(u\) | Signed sum \(d_t\) | Winding |
|---:|:---:|:---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 110 | 000 | 2 | 1 |
| 1 | 110 | 100 | 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 000 | 111 | −3 | 1 |
| 3 | 111 | 111 | 0 | — |

The only forbidden grade transition would have \(b_t=1,u_t=1,u_{t+1}=0\), giving native division zero. Every displayed transition and both exterior joins avoid it. Since \(c=1+u\) and the coefficients sum to zero, their signed tag sum is \(d_t\). The three-step carry vanishes exactly by \(9\cdot2+6\cdot1+8(-3)=0\), and \(2^{b_t}H_{t+1}=3H_t+d_t\) gives \(0,1,1,0\). Swapping the two triples gives the negative of this path with the same legal transitions.

The polynomial \(P\) has coefficients \(+1\) at \(0,3,5,11,13,16\) and \(-2\) at \(1,8,15\). Partition the six positive slots into three pairs, assigning one positive coordinate of the table to both slots in each pair. Each negative slot receives one negative coordinate. Put \(u=1\) at coefficient-zero positions and anywhere else in the spatial strip. The actual \(P\)-weighted tag sums and trajectory are now double the ones displayed. Use the positive gadget at a first selected visit of the phase rotation and the swapped gadget at a later visit. Choose constant tags two outside these six departure rows. This yields exactly the signed recurrence in the statement.

The small six-copy cancellation also locates a threshold for the confined-grade product in the entropy method. In the usual half-open two-grade model, normalize folded sizes to \(2^{\phi}\) in one octave. The variable sites, where either tag is available, have \(0\le\phi<\log_2(R/2)\). For \(R\le3\), two distinct variable sites have elapsed winding at least two. Indeed adjacent dates separated by a one have next phase \(\phi+\alpha\ge\alpha\). If two triple sums of physical affine carries at fixed endpoint tags agree, their earliest differing digit has signed sum in \(\{\pm1,\pm2,\pm3\}\), times a ternary power. Every later differing term is divisible by four after dividing out their common power of two. This is impossible modulo four. Thus equality forces equal triple sums at every date in that ideal model. For every \(R>3\), the displayed three-edge event has all its variable phases below \(\log_2(R/2)\) and realizes a noncoordinatewise cancellation. This identifies the threshold for those three-against-three local carries. The preceding four-segment work already gave the exclusion at \(R\le3\) and an example above \(27/8\). ## Realization on ordinary integers with all lower differences positive

Let \(n\) denote the total number of chronological edges, starting and ending at constant tag two, and \(B=\sum_{t<n} b_t\). The prescribed native exponents are the positive integers \(a_{t,j}=b_t+u_{t+1,j}-u_{t,j}\). Their total is \(B\) on each spatial column. Let \(K_j\) denote the ordinary affine carry of this full native word, so that \(2^B x_{n,j}=3^n x_{0,j}+K_j\). Applying \(P\) to the scaled form and using its zero initial and final signed heights gives \(P(E)K_j=0\) at the chosen spatial start. This identity involves only the specified tags and their three-edge cancellations; it holds before choosing any integer starting values.

Set \(M=3\cdot2^{B+1}\). For every column choose an integer \(v_j\) with \(v_j\equiv1\bmod3\) and \(3^nv_j+K_j\equiv2^B\bmod 2^{B+1}\). The latter congruence is the exact native cylinder: reducing to each prefix gives the specified divisions with odd outputs, or equivalently use the terminal odd quotient and read the prescribed word backwards in the two-adic integers. The empty prefix input is odd. The two congruences give \(P(E)v_j\equiv0\bmod M\) at the chosen start by the preceding carry identity and the vanishing coefficient sum. Subtract this integer signed sum from the final stencil slot, whose coefficient is \(+1\). Now \(P(E)v_j=0\) exactly and all source residue classes persist.

Choose positive integers \(C,A_0\) and replace the sources by
\[
                   x_{0,j}=v_j+M\bigl(A_0+C(1+j)^5\bigr).
\]
This preserves the native cylinders and the prescribed zero \(P\)-height because \(P\) annihilates polynomials of degree at most five. At every later date the addition to each folded column equals \(2M3^t2^{-\sum_{k<t} b_k}(A_0+C(1+j)^5)\). All differences of orders one through five of \((1+j)^5\) are strictly positive at the nonnegative spatial indices. Choosing \(C\) large first enforces all the required lower signs, simultaneously on the whole finite rectangle. Choosing \(A_0\) large then gives positive actual odd states and arbitrarily large minimum. All phases are units modulo three because the sources are units and every actual incoming odd edge gives \(2^a x'\equiv1\bmod3\).

As \(A_0\to\infty\) with everything else fixed, the physical phases divided by a single common scale approach \(2^{\phi_t}/c_{t,j}\). The limiting quantities with \(c=2\) belong to \([1/2,1)\); those with \(c=1\) occur only at the three phases of a gadget, with upper bound \(2^{\alpha+\delta}\). Their common limiting width is strictly less than \(R\) by \(2\cdot2^{\alpha+\delta}=3\cdot2^\delta<R\). At different dates the limiting phases are distinct modulo dyadic scaling by irrationality. At the same date different tags separate the limits, and equal tags give distinct integer states by the strict first differences. This establishes all the simultaneous ordinary claims.
