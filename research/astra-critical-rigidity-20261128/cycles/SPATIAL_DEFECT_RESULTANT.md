# A short resultant for spatial defects and a necessary native order reversal

**Proposed written theorem.** For a positive primitive rational odd cycle, let \(p,A,m,X,q\) be its odd period, total valuation, minimum, maximum, and complete reduced common denominator. Write \(\Delta=2^A-3^p>0\). Suppose \(m\to\infty\), \(X/m\le 2^{1+\eta}\), \(\eta\ge0\) and
\[
                             \eta\,\log(2+\eta m)\longrightarrow0.       \tag{1}
\]
Along any such sequence with **no physical pair**
\[
                        0<y-2x<1/3,\qquad x,y\text{ odd phases},        \tag{2}
\]
one has \(\gcd(A,p)=1\) eventually and
\[
                             \log(\Delta/q)=o(p),\qquad
                             \log_3 q/p\longrightarrow1.                \tag{3}
\]
Thus for any fixed \(\epsilon>0\), cycles with \(q\le3^{(1-\epsilon)p}\) must eventually contain (2). The gap is on the original rational scale: its integer numerator over the whole common denominator is positive, odd, and strictly smaller than \(q/3\). There is no restriction on \(p/m\), nor any width exponent involving the irrationality measure in (1). The proof applies the ordinary sort on all real dyadic lifts. Same-residue sorting alone gives a different order test. [Complete-denominator residue-fiber order](RESIDUE_FIBER_INVERSIONS.md) · [General near-octave denominator without assuming order](POOLED_CYCLIC_DENOMINATOR_RATE.md)

The arithmetic statement behind (3) works without a width or minimum assumption. Suppose \(\gcd(A,p)=1\), \(p\ge2,\ \log_2 3<A/p\le B\) for fixed \(B\), and at some chronological cut the true cumulative exponent satisfies
\[
        E_i=\lfloor iA/p\rfloor-1_{[iA]_p\in S}\qquad(0\le i<p)       \tag{4}
\]
for a set \(S\subseteq\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\), \(1\le K\le p\). Let \(J\) count its runs as a subset of this *linear* interval. Then
\[
                  \log(\Delta/q)\le C_B\sqrt{pK\log(2+J)}.             \tag{5}
\]
For \(S=\varnothing\) the common cancellation factor is exactly one. In particular (5) allows arbitrarily many substitutions in (4); the resultant costs their spatial reach and the logarithm of their run count. The exact empty-defect Christoffel coprimality theorem predates this result. Paper 4 proves it using parent determinants and credits Knight's earlier proof. Paper 216 treats binary shortcut rotation codings with at most two components by a repetition and discrepancy argument. Equation (4) instead prescribes the odd *cumulative deviation* on a physical-clock rank prefix, allowing arbitrary components there. ## Arithmetic proof retaining the canceled factor

The source numerator is \(M=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}3^{p-1-i}2^{E_i}\), and \(G=\gcd(M,\Delta)=\Delta/q\). If \(G=1\) there is nothing to prove. Work modulo the full \(G\), prime to six. Choose integers \(u,v\) with \(pu+Av=1\). The unit \(w=2^u3^v\bmod G\) satisfies \(w^p=2,\ w^A=3\), using \(2^A=3^p\bmod G\). Put \(t=w^{-1}\). By (4), the \(i\)-th summand of \(M/3^{p-1}\) is \(t^{[iA]_p}\) times \(1\) or \(1/2\). These ranks are all residues exactly once. Multiplying the sum by \(2(t-1)\) and using \(t^p=1/2\) gives
\[
            F(t)=0\bmod G,\qquad
                      F(T)=1+(T-1)\sum_{j\in S}T^j .                    \tag{6}
\]
This operation needs no inverse of \(t-1\). The empty case has \(F=1\). Otherwise \(F\) is monic of degree at most \(K\), with coefficient Euclidean norm at most \(1+2J\) by telescoping each run. In particular \(F(1)=1\).

For an integer \(1\le H<p\), Dirichlet's pigeonhole argument gives \(1\le b\le H\) and an integer \(a\) with \(0<\ell=|bA-ap|\le p/H\). It suffices to use the \(H+1\) fractional parts \(jA/p\) in \(H\) equal intervals, or their minimum circular separation; coprimality and \(H<p\) rule out zero. Taking a nearest integer gives \(1\le a\le (B+1)H\). The same \(t\bmod G\) satisfies one of the two binomials
\[
                        3^bT^\ell-2^a,\qquad 2^aT^\ell-3^b .
\]
Each possible complex common root with \(F\) would be an algebraic integer (because \(F\) is monic) with a positive power equal to a nonintegral rational. This is impossible. The integral resultant is therefore nonzero. It lies in the ideal over \(\mathbb Z[T]\) generated by the two polynomials, by the adjugate identity for the Sylvester matrix. Evaluating at \(t\bmod G\) shows that the *whole* \(G\) divides this nonzero integer, including every prime multiplicity. The Sylvester matrix has \(\ell\) shifted coefficient rows of \(F\) and \(\deg F\) shifted binomial rows. Hadamard's inequality yields
\[
                 \log G\le (p/H)\log(1+2J)+C_B K H.                    \tag{7}
\]
Take \(H\) within a fixed factor of \(\sqrt{p\log(2+J)/K}\), truncated to \(1,\ldots,p-1\). Since \(1\le K\le p,\ J\le K\), this changes the balance by at most an absolute factor, including the finite smallest periods. This proves (5).

## From native order to the defect support

For the true phase set \(C\) take all dyadic lifts \(\mathcal Z=\{2^n x:x\in C,n\in\mathbb Z\}\) and \(N(2^n x)=2^n(3x+1)\). Distinct true phases have distinct dyadic rays: their numerators over \(q\) are odd. Thus \(N\) is a bijection on \(\mathcal Z\) commuting with doubling. Under (1) one has \(X<4m\) eventually. If two lifts in increasing order have reversed images, normalize the upper input to an actual odd \(y\); the lower must be \(2^d x\), \(d\ge1\), because its positive correction must be larger than the upper correction \(1\). The real bound \(X<4m\) forces \(d=1\), and reversal is exactly (2). Ties are impossible by bijectivity. In the absence of (2), \(N\) preserves the full real order.

Write its strictly increasing lift enumeration as \(z_j,\ j\in\mathbb Z,\ z_0=m,\ z_{j+p}=2z_j\); there are exactly \(p\) representatives in \([m,2m)\). An increasing bijection of this discrete ordered set is a single rank shift. Since \(N^p(m)=2^Am\), the shift is \(A\). Its action on rays is transitive by primitivity, so \(\gcd(A,p)=1\). The actual point on the ray of \(z_j,\ 0\le j<p\), equals \(z_j\) or \(2z_j\), since \(m\le x\le X<4m\). Mark \(j\in S\) in the second case. At physical source \(m\) this gives \(E_i=\lfloor iA/p\rfloor-1_{[iA]_p\in S}\): compare \(N^i(m)=z_{Ai}\) to \(2^{E_i}x_i\). Every marked \(z_j\) is at most \(2^\eta m\). Let \(K\) count all \(z_j\le2^\eta m\) in this octave; the marked indices belong to the prefix of length \(K\), and \(0\notin S\).

The number of runs in this prefix satisfies
\[
                           J\le 6(2^\eta-1)m.                           \tag{8}
\]
At the first index \(j\ge1\) of each run, \(N(z_{j-1})=3z_{j-1}+1\) and \(N(z_j)=3z_j+1/2\). Their strict ordering gives \(z_j-z_{j-1}>1/6\). These disjoint gaps occur inside \([m,2^\eta m]\), proving (8).

It remains to count the rank prefix, without assuming \(m\) is comparable to \(p\). Its fraction equals the proportion of a full true lap for which \(\theta_i=\{\log_2(x_i/m)\}\in[0,\eta]\). For some absolute \(\delta>0\) one has
\[
                              K/p=O(\eta+m^{-\delta}).                  \tag{9}
\]
Indeed take arbitrary consecutive blocks of \(H=\lfloor\sqrt m\rfloor\) native dates, allowing any number of laps. The true angles differ from a rotate of the slope \(\log_2 3\) by \(O(H/m)\) on each block. Consecutive continued-fraction denominators \(b\le\sqrt H<b'\) obey \(b\gg H^{c}\) for a fixed \(c>0\) by the classical finite irrationality measure. Splitting \(H\) into \(b\)-point blocks and comparing each to a uniform grid gives interval discrepancy \(O(1/b+b/H+H/m)=O(m^{-\delta})\), uniformly in the true start. Average the block counts over all \(p\) starting positions to get exactly \(H K/p\) as their mean occupancy, including when \(H>p\). This proves (9). [Published BLS bound, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.22405/2226-8383-2018-19-1-15-25)

By (1), (8) and (9), \((K/p)\log(2+J)\to0\). Since \(A/p=\log_2 3+O(1/m)\) by the true product identity, (5) gives \(\log G=o(p)\). Finally \(p\to\infty\) since its rational clock tends to the irrational \(\log_2 3\). The same classical two-logarithm estimate gives \(A-p\log_2 3\gg p^{-C}\) for some fixed \(C\), so \(\Delta=3^p(2^{A-p\log_2 3}-1)\ge3^p p^{-O(1)}\). The upper bound \(\log\Delta\le A\log2=p\log3+o(p)\) proves (3). This proof uses only the classical finite irrationality measure.
