# Sparse primitive periodic pairs at every large exact grade and on every persistent dyadic cylinder

The two results below use the [universal thin-center entrance](UNIVERSAL_RATIONAL_MERGERS.md) and the [forward return to translation one](ADJACENT_CYCLE_CLOSURE.md). The shortcut map on an odd-denominator rational uses numerator parity. For two periodic tracks, *joint primitive period* means their least common native return time. Odd density on each track is its grade divided by that period.

## A primitive adjacent pair for every sufficiently large exact odd grade

For every sufficiently large integer $p$ there are positive periodic phases $z,z-1$ with joint primitive period $N$, exactly $p$ odd departures on **each** arm, and
$$
                    N\ge (1-o(1))p\log_2 p .                              \tag{1}
$$
In their paired length-$N$ native words, only $O(p/(\log p)^2)$ dates have unequal letters. In particular the two increasing odd calendars solve the [static ordered carry equation](ADJACENT_CYCLE_CLOSURE.md) at exact grade $p$.

To prove this, set $l=\lfloor\log_2 p-2\log_2\log_2p\rfloor$, $q=2^l-3$, $j=\operatorname{ord}_q(3)$ and $a=(3^j-1)/q$. Starting with original translation $A-B=1$, use one U to attain $(j',a')=(1,1)$. Follow the universal entrance to $(j,a)$, taking the part of its native calendar after its mandatory original first step. The entrance costs $O(j)$ odd departures and native steps. At this relation insert $R$ copies of the length-$l$ one-odd word centered at $1/q$ on **both** arms. Each complete copy preserves the affine relation exactly: if $F$ is its affine map then $F(3^j A-a)=3^j F(A)-a$ since $F(1/q)=1/q$ and $a=(3^j-1)/q$. The word is physically admissible on both tracks by refining $A$ in the periodic center cylinder.

Use the forward return from $(j,a)$ to original translation one. Its combined odd grade is $O(j)$, by its level descent and $O(\log(2+|a|+3^j))$ final translation steps, and its calendar can be fixed independently of $R$. This closes a pair of equal-grade words. With no repetitions write their common grade as $p_0=O(j)=O(2^l)$, uniformly in $l$. Take $R=p-p_0$. It is positive and $R/p\to1$. The native loop has length $N\ge lR$, and at most $2p_0$ mismatch dates, since all mismatches occur outside the copies and use an odd departure. Its rational centers are adjacent and positive for large $p$ by the carry equation and $N/p\to\infty$.

Delete every common-even date from the cyclic word of parity **pairs**. The remaining cyclic sequence contains $R$ consecutive common-odd events. Outside this run there are at most $2p_0<R$ events, and at least one mismatched event, namely the initial U. If the native pair word were a nontrivial power then this shortened cyclic word would be the same power of a nonempty event word. Each copy would contain a mismatch, so no mismatch-free interval could cover at least half the whole event period. This contradiction proves the asserted joint primitivity. A shorter common rational return would repeat its paired native parity tape, and therefore cannot occur.

## An exact dyadic closure description for synchronized source pairs

Let $X$ be the set of odd $2$-adic sources $s$ such that at **no** finite date do the two affine branch maps for $(s,3s)$ agree identically on their current source cylinder. Both maps have a positive integer power of three as their numerator slope before division by the common $2^t$. Thus exclusion from $X$ means a finite synchronized collision with equal slopes and intercepts. The excluded cylinder contains infinitely many positive ordinary integer source representatives having the same synchronized collision. A collision at a single rational intersection of unequal slopes is a different condition.

For any fixed $\eta>0$, let $D_\eta$ consist of odd rational sources $s_*$ of odd denominator such that $T(s_*),T(3s_*)$ are distinct positive periodic rational phases of the same odd density strictly below $\eta$. Then in the odd $2$-adic integers,
$$
                        \overline{D_\eta}=X.                              \tag{2}
$$
The set $X$ is perfect and nowhere dense. More strongly, every source cylinder whose two terminal affine maps are distinct contains members of $D_\eta$ of arbitrarily small positive density and arbitrarily large joint primitive period. Each such cylinder also contains a subcylinder disjoint from $X$.

Indeed once two branch maps agree identically on a cylinder, all future refinements stay merged there. This makes its complement $X$ closed. Distinct periodic phases at the first step can never subsequently coincide on the given source, since a common period would propagate such equality back to date one. Thus $D_\eta\subseteq X$.

Fix an odd source cylinder through $k\ge1$ dates whose terminal branch maps are distinct. On the cylinder choose a positive integer representative avoiding the roots of every earlier same-date difference. This is possible: any earlier identically zero difference would propagate to the terminal maps, and each other nonzero affine difference has at most one rational root. Its actual $k$-step pair is therefore unmerged. Start a prospective periodic loop at the relation $(1,1)$, equivalently $w=3z-1$, of the forced first departure from an odd source and its triple. Append the given calendars from date 1 to date $k$. The return procedure then reaches translation one with a finite fixed extra calendar. Now U leads to $(1,1)$; follow the thin-center entrance at any prescribed $l\ge3$, insert $R$ one-odd common laps, return to translation one, and take one final U to close the loop on the original relation $(1,1)$. All transitions are valid dyadic refinements. All words outside the $R$ copies may be fixed after $l$ and the prescribed cylinder are fixed.

Because the initial and final slope ratio is three, the two loop words have equal grade. Their rational periodic centers satisfy $w=3z-1$: the affine identity $F_V(3A-1)=3F_U(A)-1$ sends the fixed point $z$ of $F_U$ to the fixed point of $F_V$. These phases cannot coincide, since their unique possible intersection $1/2$ has even denominator whereas a periodic native word has odd-denominator center. For large $R$ both are positive, and their common odd density tends to $1/l$. The same event argument with a U outside the run proves joint primitivity when $R$ is large. The odd rational $s_*=(2z-1)/3$ has first-step pair $(z,w)$ and its next physical calendars are the prescribed ones, so it belongs to the original source cylinder. Choose $1/l<\eta$ and then $R$ sufficiently large. Taking arbitrarily large $l$ supplies positive densities tending to zero. Taking $R$ arbitrarily large at fixed $l$ supplies unbounded joint primitive periods.

Finally, from the first return to translation one, use U to reach $(1,1)$ and append the last four steps of the actual five-step generic merger from $s=23$. Its endpoints both equal 20 with original grades $3,2$, so its affine maps coincide on the entire refined cylinder. This gives the promised subcylinder and proves that $X$ has empty interior. The trivial cylinder of odd sources has distinct terminal maps after the mandatory first step, so $X$ is nonempty. Infinitely many members of $D_\eta$ occur in every neighborhood of any $x\in X$ (their densities or joint periods distinguish a subsequence). This excludes isolated points and completes (2).

For example $s=1$ belongs to $X$: the native orbits from $1$ and $3$ settle into opposite phases of the same ordinary cycle and are never equal at the same date. Thus (2) also applies around a fixed ordinary source whose convergence is known. These constructive arguments give a topological classification and make no measure claim. Almost-everywhere coalescence is a separate prior probabilistic result, not a novelty claim of this note.
