# Arithmetic and leading-digit laws on a true inverse ray to a fixed ordinary target

**Proposed, source-qualified written theorem.** Let \(T(x)=x/2\) on evens and \((3x+1)/2\) on odds. Fix any positive nonperiodic integer \(a\) prime to three. Put \(h(v)=d\{n:T^t n=v\text{ for some }t\ge0\}\), using natural density among all positive integers. Beginning at \(V_0=a\), choose each actual parent \(Tb=V_j\) with probability \(h(b)/h(V_j)\). This is the ordinary source-counted inverse law. Almost surely, for \(\mu=2/\log(4/3)\),
\[
                   \log V_k=k/\mu+O(\sqrt{k\log(k+2)}).                 \tag{1}
\]
Every binary word \(w\) of length \(e\) occurs in the first \(k\) outward parities \(V_1\bmod2,\ldots,V_k\bmod2\) with count \(k2^{-e}+O_w(\sqrt{k\log(k+2)})\). In particular on the embedded odd inverse path the limit frequency of **each** valuation word \((a_1,\ldots,a_s)\) is exactly \(2^{-\sum a_i}\), with count error \(O_{a_1,\ldots,a_s}(\sqrt{j\log(j+2)})\) through odd depth \(j\). The odd heights there satisfy \(\log Y_j=j\log(4/3)+O(\sqrt{j\log(j+2)})\).

There is also joint equidistribution of actual integer heights, residue classes and leading digits. Write \(M=2^e3^d u\), \((u,6)=1\), and
\[
 \pi_M(c)=2^{-e}u^{-1}\Pr(R\equiv c\bmod3^d),\qquad
 R=\sum_{i\ge1}3^{i-1}2^{-(A_1+\cdots+A_i)}\ \in\mathbb Z_3 ,
 \quad\Pr(A_i=v)=2^{-v}\quad(v=1,2,\ldots)
\]
for independent \(A_i\). The modulus-one factors equal one. For every fixed integer base \(B\ge2\), class \(c\bmod M\) and frequency \(\ell\in\mathbb Z\), simultaneously,
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^k 1_{V_i\equiv c\bmod M}\exp(2\pi i\ell\log_B V_i)
 = k\,1_{\ell=0}\pi_M(c)+O_{M,B,\ell}(\sqrt{k\log(k+2)}).             \tag{2}
\]
Constants in these almost-sure estimates can also depend on the ray. Thus conditional on each positive-mass residue class, leading digits obey Benford's law in every integer base. Statement (2) gives fixed Fourier-mode bounds. It gives qualitative equidistribution for intervals of significands by trigonometric approximation.

The source scope matters. Valbuena already proves an almost-sure odd inverse height law at speed \(\log(4/3)\) under the canonical measure. Weaver's Paper 12, Theorem 9, proves a positive upper occurrence frequency for every valuation word, and §10.1 proves equivalence of the natural and canonical actual odd inverse laws at nonperiodic odd unit roots. Its Theorem 9 explicitly leaves frequency limits untreated. Consequently the almost-sure odd-clock results here hold under both laws. Weaver's later finite-cap inverse chains use different kernels. Lagarias and Soundararajan establish finite forward Benford approximations over typical starting integers. Here the entire infinite ray ends at one prescribed integer, uses the unbounded natural parent mechanism, and obeys the joint residue and significand law. The forward fate of the prescribed integer remains unrestricted. ## From counted sources to one infinite ray

Leen's *Every target* corollary supplies the natural densities \(h(v)\); Mazur's positive predecessor theorem implies \(h(a)>0\) for \(3\nmid a\). For a transient \(v\), the predecessor basins of its one-step parents partition its basin apart from \(v\) itself. Indeed the first arrival has a unique penultimate state; a different parent on the same orbit would force a return to \(v\). Thus the displayed transitions are normalized. After \(D\) steps each legal path with endpoint \(b\) has mass \(h(b)/h(a)\). Among ordinary integers through \(X\) which reach \(a\), the last \(D\) steps before arrival have this limiting distribution: the fewer-than-\(D\)-step ancestors form a finite set, and each endpoint labels its entire basin. Distinctness of all ray states implies \(V_k\to\infty\) on every ray. For an odd root, the natural density among all starts reaching any odd ancestor equals its relative odd predecessor density, by stripping the initial powers of two and summing their geometric weights. Reading the odd nodes on our ray thus gives precisely Weaver's natural odd law: each specified odd prefix endpoint has its basin ratio, and infinitely many odd nodes occur almost surely by the result below. [Leen, every target](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23269488) · [Mazur, positive predecessor density](https://www.proofatlas.ai/papers/positive-lower-density-collatz-predecessors/paper-v1.pdf)

For any specified \(F\subseteq\mathbb N\) with
\[
               \sum_{j\ge0}\sqrt{2^{-j}|F\cap[2^j,2^{j+1})|}<\infty,   \tag{3}
\]
almost every such ray has only finitely many vertices in \(F\). To see this, condition an ordinary source on reaching \(a\). A vertex visited between the first global minimum and the first arrival at \(a\) belongs to one of the finitely many path segments from \(r\le a\) reaching \(a\). Their maximal height is bounded in terms of \(a\). All sufficiently high vertices on the incoming segment are therefore seen through the minimum date. Apply the [uniform all-height estimate](../literature/ALL_HIGH_TARGETS.md) to \(F\cap[2^J,\infty)\). For each fixed inverse depth \(D\), taking the source-density limit bounds its probability of a hit there by \((C\sum_{j\ge J}\sqrt{2^{-j}|F_j|}+\varepsilon_J)/h(a)\), with \(\varepsilon_J\to0\) from the joint uniform error and \(F_j=F\cap[2^j,2^{j+1})\). The bound is independent of \(D\), so monotone convergence removes the depth cutoff. Then let \(J\to\infty\). A countable list of specified masks obeys (3) simultaneously on almost every ray.

## Finite arithmetic and one block at a high source

Write \(L=\log y,\ n(y)=\lfloor L/4\rfloor\). Uniformly on the octave \([Y,2Y)\), each of the at most two possible block lengths \(n=n(y)\) is \(O(\log Y)\). For the uniform sample of the **whole** octave, the odd residue modulo \(q=3^d u\) and its first \(n+e+1\) shortcut parity bits are within \(O(q2^{n+e+1}/Y)\) in total variation of an independent uniform residue and fair tape. Indeed each prescribed parity word gives one class modulo the corresponding power of two, and CRT gives one class for each joint prescription. We may take the union over the at most two lengths without conditioning on their selection.

In the independent tape model the evolving odd-modulus residue \(r\) follows \(F_0(r)=r/2,\ F_1(r)=(3r+1)/2\). On \(u\) both are permutations. Their group contains \(3r+1\) and its conjugate \(3r+2\) by multiplication by two, hence translation by one. This makes the finite chain irreducible, and the even self-loop at zero makes it aperiodic; its limiting law is uniform. Convergence is geometric from every initial state, by finite irreducibility and aperiodicity (one fixed power of the transition matrix has all entries positive).

Modulo \(3^d\), any common word with at least \(d\) ones erases the starting state. Under fair bits its stationary law is the displayed reduction of \(R\): the affine update divided by \(2\) contributes an additive unit numerator at each past odd date, and the backward distances to successive ones have independent positive geometric gaps. Equivalently the one-step stationary equation is the average of the \(F_0,F_1\) pushforwards, which iterates through the preceding even run to give the same geometric series. To see uniform geometric convergence on the full odd modulus, use two halves of a \(t\)-step word. By its midpoint the \(u\)-coordinate has become close to uniform with exponential error. With exponentially high probability the second half contains \(d\) ones and makes the final ternary residue a function of its bits alone. Those bits act by a permutation on the midpoint \(u\)-residue; this proves geometric convergence to the product of the two marginal laws, uniformly in the starting pair.

Append a buffer of \(\max(1,e)\) future bits to this finite state. Each step consumes its first bit, updates \(r\), and appends a fresh fair bit. After the prescribed initial buffer, the same argument shows uniform geometric convergence to the preceding product times independent fair buffer bits. A residue mod \(2^e\) is exactly one \(e\)-bit departure word. Thus the bounded state function \(f=1_{\text{specified residue mod }M}\) has stationary mean \(\pi_M(c)\); a specified initial pattern can likewise be tested by enlarging the buffer.

Here are explicit concentration details that also handle a real significand. Let \(P\) be the transition matrix of this finite state, let \(\xi\) be the consumed bit and put \(z_\xi=\exp(2\pi i\ell\log_B(3^\xi/2))\). The operator \(P_\ell\) averages \(z_\xi g(\text{next state})\) over the allowed transitions. When \(\ell=0\), the centered \(f-\pi_M(c)\) equals \((I-P)g\) for a bounded \(g\), by the summable geometric convergence. When \(\ell\ne0\), \(I-P_\ell\) is invertible. Otherwise a nonzero fixed vector of \(P_\ell\) on the unique recurrent class would, at a state of maximal modulus, give equality in the triangle inequality at every accessible edge. Irreducibility propagates this equality to the entire class. Each closed directed cycle would then have phase product one. The class is closed under both bit extensions and is finite, so a sequence of constant-zero appends eventually gives an all-zero consumed-bit cycle, and constant-one appends give an all-one cycle. Those two cycles require both \(\ell\log_B2\) and \(\ell\log_B(3/2)\) to be rational, impossible by multiplicative independence of \(2,3\). The transient block has spectral radius below one even after twisting, since its matrix powers are dominated entrywise in modulus by those of the untwisted transient block. This proves invertibility. Use \(f=(I-P_\ell)g\) in this case.

With \(W_i=\prod_{t<i}z_{\xi_t}\), the sum of the weighted centered tests (uncentered for \(\ell\ne0\)) is a bounded endpoint term plus a martingale whose increments have modulus at most \(2\|g\|_\infty\): telescope the differences \(W_i g(Z_i)-W_{i+1}g(Z_{i+1})\) and subtract their conditional means. The bounded-difference martingale inequality applied to real and imaginary parts gives exceptional probability \(O(L^{-6})\) for discrepancy exceeding \(C_{M,B,\ell}\sqrt{L\log L}\), with a fixed sufficiently large constant. The initial real phase cancels in absolute value; it may depend arbitrarily on the observed bits. For the true map, every state through \(n\) steps is at least \(y/2^n\). Hence actual log heights differ from the multiplicative evolution with factors \(3^\xi/2\) by at most \(O(n2^n/y)\) through the whole block, using the additional factor \(1+1/(3x)\) on odd departures. This changes the Fourier sum by \(O_{B,\ell}(n^2 2^n/y)=o(1)\). The same finite-state argument with \(\xi\) as its test counts the odd departures with mean \(1/2\).

For each fixed test, the fraction of exceptional starts in octave \([2^j,2^{j+1})\) is therefore \(O(j^{-6})\), including the exponentially small complete-grid error. All these bad sets satisfy (3). We conclude that on the ray every sufficiently high \(V_k\) has the stated discrepancy on its genuine chronological block of \(n(V_k)\) steps. The threshold may depend on the test and on the ray.

## Concatenating true blocks and identifying the odd clock

Along a reverse ray \(V_k\le 2^ka\); hence for large \(y=V_k\), \(n(y)\le k\). Write \(L'=\log V_{k-n(y)}\). The odd-count estimate and the preceding additive correction give
\[
             L'=L-n(y)/\mu+O(\sqrt{L\log L}).
\]
In particular, at sufficiently large \(L\) one has \(L'\le\rho L\) for a fixed \(\rho<1\), since \(n(y)=L/4+O(1)\). Starting at any large depth \(k\), take complete consecutive blocks until the remaining initial height is bounded by the ray-dependent threshold. Its depth is uniformly bounded on that ray because the states are distinct. The sum of all block errors is \(O(\sqrt{L\log L})\) by the geometric height decrease. Summation gives \(k=\mu L+O(\sqrt{L\log L})\), which inverts to (1). For every residue and Fourier test the same block construction yields (2), since it partitions the actual vertices \(V_k,\ldots,V_1\) apart from a bounded remainder. For a word count the at most \(e-1\) crossings at each block boundary contribute only \(O_e(\log L)\). Reversing the tested word converts the chronological parity observations to outward order.

Odd indices in the first \(k\) vertices consequently number \(k/2+O(\sqrt{k\log(k+2)})\). The \(j\)-th odd vertex has index \(2j+O(\sqrt{j\log(j+2)})\); its real height follows from (1). A given outward sequence of \(s\) odd gaps is the binary pattern beginning and ending in one with the specified intervening zero runs, of total length \(1+\sum a_i\). Its native frequency is \(2^{-1-\sum a_i}\). Dividing by the odd frequency \(1/2\) gives the asserted exact odd-clock rate, with the same discrepancy order. For the canonical odd law at a periodic root, the outward chain exits its periodic cycle almost surely: the probability of traversing one more full lap around that cycle is \(3^p/2^A<1\), by the true positive cycle's product formula and the cancellation of profile ratios. After exit the root is transient, so the same almost-sure assertions apply after a finite initial segment. No sampling statement for an individual exceptional forward trajectory is required.
