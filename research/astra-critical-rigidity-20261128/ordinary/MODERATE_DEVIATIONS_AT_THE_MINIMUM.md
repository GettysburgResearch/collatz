# What survives at the actual global minimum

**Proposed, source-qualified written theorem.** Use the positive shortcut, each source's first global minimum, and the normalization \(Z_X\) of [the capped functional moderate-deviation theorem](CAPPED_FUNCTIONAL_MODERATE_DEVIATIONS.md), now on \([0,1]\). Thus \(Z_X(1)\) uses the genuine first minimum date. Write \(I\) for the half Dirichlet energy on \([0,1]\) with initial value zero, infinite off absolutely continuous paths. The speed is \(\ln\ln X\) and all path spaces carry the uniform norm.

1. The actual paths satisfy the open-set lower bound and the compact-set upper bound with rate \(I\). These are the two bounds of a **weak large-deviation principle**.
2. For each fixed \(0<e<1/2\), replace the passage threshold \(n^{1-s}\) by \(\max(n^{1-s},\exp((\ln n)^e))\), retaining the same centering and minimum cap. These paths satisfy the full large-deviation principle on \([0,1]\) with rate \(I\).
3. The full large-deviation principle for the actual paths on \([0,1]\), their exponential tightness at the stated speed, and Leen's odd-octave bottom condition (BS′) are equivalent. The latter asks that for every \(B>0\) some \(\kappa_B,C_B\) give, for all large \(Y\) and uniform odd \(W_Y\in[Y,2Y)\),
\[
 \Pr(J_*(W_Y)>\ln Y/d+\kappa_B\sqrt{\ln Y\,\ln\ln Y})
       \le C_B(\ln Y)^{-B},\qquad d=\ln(4/3).
\]
Here \(J_*\) counts odd departures before the first minimum. This condition remains unproved for the genuine map. Leen proved its equivalence with the sharp *scalar* odd-clock upper bound and used it for scalar minimum-time bounds. The conclusions here concern entire paths, including an unconditional endpoint weak principle. ## Keeping a genuine terminal path in the lower bound

First use odd \(W_Y\), deterministic gates \(x_s=Y^{1-s}\), \(L=\ln Y\) and \(b_L=\sqrt{L\ln L}\); fixed positive normalizing constants can be absorbed in tolerances. Fix \(0<\eta<1\), \(x=Y^\eta\) and \(\rho>0\). With \(P\) the odd landing at \(x\), let \(g(P)\) require
\[
 |\ln(x/P)|\le\rho b_L,\qquad
 \sup_{1\le z\le x}\left|t_P(z)-\mu\ln(x/z)\right|\le\rho b_L ,
\]
where \(t_P(z)\) is the first native passage at or below \(z\), capped at the first global minimum of \(P\). For \(P\le z\) it is zero. Then \(H_x(g)\to1\). Compare with a fresh odd \(W_{x^{1.001}}\) by the relative landing law. Its full native clock process, centered by the log-distance to the varying height and capped at its true minimum, has sup norm \(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt L)\) by Leen's full functional Brownian theorem on odd octaves. Its first native crossing at \(x\) differs from its odd arrival by a short final halving run and \(\ln(x/P)=O_{\Pr}(\ln L)\), by the short genuine parity segment in the capped proof. Every earlier value exceeds \(P\). At gates \(z<P\) the fresh full clock minus its arrival date at \(P\) is precisely \(t_P(z)\), including a missed gate. At \(P\le z\le x\) use the undershoot. Subtracting the fresh clock at \(x\) proves the assertion for each fixed tolerance.

In the capped proof's finite mesh rectangle lower bound retain this \(g\) at the last odd gate in place of \(1\). Its factor is \(H_x(g)=1+o(1)\); the bounded-test estimates at previous gates are unchanged. The polygonal interpolation failure on \([0,1-\eta]\) costs more than any prescribed fixed energy by choosing a sufficiently fine mesh. The last physical native/odd gate difference and undershoot are \(O_A(\ln L)\) except with probability \(O_A(L^{-A})\) for arbitrary \(A\), by that proof. On the retained event the centered path on the remaining interval stays within \(C\rho\) of its last centered value, including gate comparisons inside the final halving run. For any finite-energy \(f\) and any open ball about it choose \(\eta\) small enough that \(f\) oscillates there by less than a quarter of the ball radius and then take \(\rho\) smaller still. The lower bound costs at most \(I_{[0,1-\eta]}(f)\le I(f)\). A top odd octave has positive proportion among all integers. The change from \(Y^{1-s}\) to \(n^{1-s}\) moves \(s\) by \(O(1/L)\), with the first endpoint handled as in the capped proof. An event uniformly close to the continuous \(f\) stays within the larger open ball under this time change. This proves the genuine full open lower bound.

Every actual sample path is continuous from the left at \(1\). Thresholds sufficiently near \(1\) from above are less than \(2\), hence their native dates all coincide with the minimum date. The functions continuous from the left there form a closed subspace \(D\). For a compact set \(K\) restrict \(K\cap D\) to \([0,1-\eta]\) and use the capped upper bound. As \(\eta\downarrow0\), the infima of the restricted energies tend to \(\inf_{K\cap D} I\) by compactness and lower semicontinuity on each shorter interval. Indeed bounded restricted energies give an absolutely continuous limit up to \(1\) with the same energy bound, by Cauchy–Schwarz and weak \(L^2\) compactness; membership in \(D\) fixes its endpoint. This proves the compact upper bound.

## Reaching a moving terminal scale

From \(x_0=Y^\eta\) set \(\ln x_i=l_i=\eta L a^i\) for fixed \(0<\eta<1/4\) and \(a=0.999\). Stop at the first edge crossing \(L^e\), using its full segment. Allocate allowances proportional to \(\rho b_L a^{i/4}\), with total at most \(\rho b_L\). The smallest is \(\gg_{\eta,\rho}L^{(1+e)/4}\sqrt{\ln L}\), much larger than \(\ln L\). The exponential maximal inequality bounds each failure by

## The remaining failures and the scalar bottom condition

Assume (BS′). Put \(z=\exp(L^e)\) on an odd octave. At \(Y'=z^{1.001}\), its reference native minimum date exceeds any fixed \(\rho b_L\) with probability \(O_B(L^{-B})\) for every \(B\): outside an arbitrarily small inverse power of \(\ln Y'\), (BS′) bounds its odd minimum clock by \(O_B(\ln Y')=O_B(L^e)=o(b_L)\). Leen's native dictionary bounds the native clock by \((\log_2 3)J_*+\log_2(2Y')+O(\log(J_*+1))\). Whenever the first odd landing \(P\) at \(z\) exists, its remaining minimum time is no larger than the reference minimum time because all prelanding states exceed \(P\). The relative unmarked landing theorem therefore bounds the outer probability of a remaining time exceeding \(\rho b_L\) by \(O_B(L^{-B})\), with arbitrary \(B\). The last halving run to \(P\) is \(O_A(\ln L)\) except with an arbitrary-power error: the last segment tests its whole odd gap by its upper-landing test and a fresh finite parity segment, with the same earlier-segment accounting for repeated landings. Missing passage to \(z\) costs smaller than every inverse power of \(L\). Every subsequent capped first-passage date is between that last native entrance and the actual minimum date. This proves exponential equivalence between the processes of parts 1 and 2. For varying \((\ln n)^e\) inside an octave it suffices to do this at the larger deterministic \(z=\exp(2L^e)\); the remaining path bounds apply to every later gate.

Conversely the full principle gives arbitrarily large polynomial decay of the slow native endpoint tail by taking its normalized level large. The same upper bound up to a fixed factor holds on the odd octave \([Y,2Y)\) by counting it in \([2,2Y]\). The true product gives \(\tau_*\ge(\log_2 3)J_*+\log_2(W_Y/m(W_Y))\). Leen's minimum-tail theorem makes \(\ln m(W_Y)>L^e\) smaller than every inverse power of \(L\). Using \(\mu=(\log_2 3)/d+1/\ln2\) proves (BS′). Exponential tightness and the weak bounds imply the full bounds by discarding the complement of a compact set at arbitrary exponential cost. In the other direction, part 2 and the preceding exponential equivalence give exponential tightness in the full norm topology. Specifically the polygon approximation gives compact containment by bounding the mesh values and a sequence of sup-norm approximation errors tending to zero. Every cutoff law has finite support at each integer \(X\), so finite initial exceptions to each approximation may be included in its finite covers. This proves the equivalences.
