# A functional moderate-deviation principle for the genuine descent above a fixed power

**Proposed, source-qualified written resolution of a stated preprint conjecture.** Let \(T\) be the positive shortcut, \(n\) uniform on \(2,\ldots,X\), and \(\tau_*(n)\) its first global minimum date. For \(0\le s\le1-\eta\), with fixed \(0<\eta<1\), let \(\tau_n(s)\) be the first native date at which \(T^i n\le n^{1-s}\), capped at \(\tau_*(n)\) if the level is missed. Put \(d=\ln(4/3)\), \(\mu=2/d\), \(\sigma^2=2(\ln3)^2/d^3\). Define, with any bounded convention when \(n\le e\),
\[
    Z_X(s)=\frac{\tau_n(s)-\mu s\ln n}{\sigma\sqrt{\ln n}\sqrt{\ln\ln X}} .
\]
In the uniform topology of \(\ell^\infty[0,1-\eta]\) these actual integer path laws obey a large-deviation principle with speed \(\ln\ln X\) and good rate
\[
 I(f)=\begin{cases}
 \tfrac12\int_0^{1-\eta}|f'(s)|^2\,ds,&f(0)=0,\ f\text{ absolutely continuous},\\
 +\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                                           \tag{1}
\]
An infinite integral also means infinite rate. Thus the lower bound on each open set \(G\) is \(-\inf_G I\) and the upper bound on each closed set \(F\) is \(-\inf_F I\), for logarithmic probabilities divided by \(\ln\ln X\).

Leen explicitly conjectures this statement in the original *A functional moderate-deviation principle* remark. The proof uses his relative passage and kernel estimates. The two additional operations are factoring successive threshold events through a reference source at an arbitrarily small **fixed** log-height distance above the last landing, and controlling all thresholds between grid points with one short genuine parity segment per grid cell. The assertion stops at \(1-\eta\). Leen's different question about the slow-side probability **at the minimum itself** is outside its scope. ## The local joint estimate at arbitrarily short fixed ratios

For an odd source \(W_y\) uniform on \([y,2y)\), write \(J_x\) for its first odd-step passage count at or below \(x\), and \(P_x\) for that odd landing. Failed passages have zero mass for landing tests. Write \(\sigma_J^2=2(\ln2)^2/d^3\). For any fixed
\[
        1<e_-\le \ln y/\ln x\le e_+<1+0.02d,\qquad L_x=\ln x ,
\]
for \(0\le g\le1,\ |t|\le K\sqrt{\ln L_x}\), either sign, and any fixed \(A,K>0\), one has
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[g(P_x);
     \ \pm(J_x-\ln(y/x)/d)\ge t\,\sigma_J\sqrt{\ln(y/x)},\ J_x<\infty\right]
  =(1+O(L_x^{-c}))\,H_x(g)\overline\Phi(t)+O_A(L_x^{-A}) .             \tag{2}
\]
Here \(c>0\) may be decreased; constants and onset may depend on the fixed ratio interval. \(H_x\) is the identical shell functional in the cited original main text and supplement. In particular the unmarked landing satisfies \(\mathbb E[g(P_x);J_x<\infty]=(1+O(L_x^{-c}))H_x(g)+O_A(L_x^{-A})\). At arbitrarily larger sources \(y\ge x^{1.001}\) this last unmarked comparison holds by the published *Relative landing law*. A missing local crossing has probability \(O_A(L_x^{-A})\), by the actual short-word bound below or Appendix A's good-prefix event.

We spell out the range extension in (2), since the supplement's global moderate-deviation theorem states the lower source ratio \(1.001\). Use its **local** joint calculation together with main Appendix A, rather than applying that global theorem outside its range.

* In main Appendix A, proof of *Timed relative passage on a general source interval*, Steps 0–6 check the complete-word reduction and its four individual error bounds for exactly the displayed \([e_-,e_+]\). In its notation \(k=n-\ell\asymp L_x,\ \ell=\lfloor L_x^{4/5}\rfloor\), and \(k\le0.02 L_x\). The good event, disjoint crossing cells \((n,M)\), positive possible sources, centered-total estimates, and removal of the fiber errors all hold on this interval. In the supplement's proof of *Joint relative passage*, a bounded function \(\Psi(n,P)\) attaches separately to these disjoint cells. The good-event, noncentral-total and kernel-remainder errors are bounded by sums of absolute errors \(O_A(L_x^{-A})\), using those same four Appendix A estimates. The exception within \(O(x^{-1/2})\) of the integer-cut boundary contributes \(O_A(L_x^{-A})\) by *Shell sums* (a): its proof uses only the range of \(M\) and a translated periodic interval at each \(n\). At every remaining central cell the supplementary relative error is \(O(\sqrt{\ell/k}\log k+1/y)=O_{e_\pm}(L_x^{-1/10}\log L_x)\). Thus the displayed *Joint relative passage* formula with weights \(\Omega_y(n,M)\) and arbitrary \(\Psi\) holds also on this ratio interval. This uses the reduction and error bounds, not an inference from a bounded-frequency characteristic function.
* For the one-sided time test times \(g(P)\), the supplement's *Kernel tails* lemma sums these weights for each \(M\). Its proof needs \(\bar n_M=\ln(2y/M)/d\asymp L_x\), \(\mathcal V_M=\sigma_J^2\ln(2y/M)\asymp L_x\), and that the centered \(O(\sqrt{\bar n_M\ln\bar n_M})\) band after adding \(\ell\) lies in the stated \(n\)-window. Main Appendix A Steps 4 and 7 give these bounds; the remaining irrational fractional-part sum in the lemma is uniform in its translate and in \(\bar n_M\) in this range. Its errors are relative \(O((L_x/\ln L_x)^{-\gamma_0/2}+L_x^{-1/2}(\ln L_x)^{3/2})\), with Leen's \(\gamma_0=10/143\), plus arbitrary \(O_A(L_x^{-A})\). No smoothing in the landing test is required.
* In the resulting Gaussian mixture the excess center is \(\delta(M)+\ln2/d\), and \(\mathcal V_M-\sigma_J^2\ln(y/x)=O(L_x^{4/5})\). By the source-independent *Shell sums* (b), excess centers larger in modulus than \(O(L_x^{2/5}\ln L_x)\) have total shell mass \(O_A(L_x^{-A})\). On the rest, changing the Gaussian center and variance moves its tail argument by \(\Delta\) with \((1+|t|)|\Delta|=O_K(L_x^{-1/10}(\ln L_x)^{3/2}+L_x^{-1/5}\ln L_x)\). The Gaussian Mills ratio gives a relative \(1+O(L_x^{-c})\) error after decreasing \(c\). Summing the bounded test gives \(H_x(g)\). This is the tilt-removal estimate from the proof of the supplement's *Moderate deviations with clock–landing independence*. All constants take \(e_->1\) fixed first.

For an interval with distinct fixed endpoints in units \(\sigma_J\sqrt{L\ln L}\), \(L\asymp L_x\), (2) also factors its probability times any \(g\) into \(H_x(g)\) times the corresponding Gaussian interval probability, with the same relative \(1+o(1)\) and an arbitrary additive \(O_A(L^{-A})\). On one side of zero subtract tails from the nearer endpoint; the farther tail is negligible in relative size. Across zero subtract the two small outer tails from total mass. Endpoints equal to zero give a limiting half-tail. Clock rounding by one does not change these limits or their logarithmic rates, by the same bounds with shifted thresholds.

## Finite-dimensional probabilities under the odd octave law

Put \(L=\ln Y,\ b_L=\sigma_J\sqrt{L\ln L}\), and \(x_s=e^{(1-s)L}\) for \(W_Y\). Work first on equally spaced fine meshes \(0=s_0<\dots<s_m=1-\eta\), with \(h=s_j-s_{j-1}\) fixed before \(Y\to\infty\). Use the initial date zero at \(s_0\); effects of the true octave source exceeding \(Y\) by a factor at most two are included below. Choose the mesh sufficiently fine that even after adding a small buffer \(\delta L\), the ratio of successive log heights is below \(1+0.02d\), uniformly since \(1-s_j\ge\eta\). On failure to reach the final gate assign a dummy process. Such failure has smaller probability than every power \(L^{-1}\) by Leen's minimum-tail estimate at \(Y^\eta\), or the finite stage comparisons below.

For fixed nondegenerate bounded intervals \(I_j\), consider the event that each centered odd-count increment, divided by \(b_L\), belongs to \(I_j\). Its limiting log probability divided by \(\ln L\) is
\[
               -\sum_{j=1}^m\ \inf_{v\in I_j} v^2/(2h).                \tag{3}
\]
At the first threshold, (2) applies with the indicator of every remaining increment as a function of the first landing. Subsequent odd entrances are obtained along the future of this landing, including a zero additional time on overshoot. This factors out the first Gaussian interval probability of variance \(\sigma_J^2 hL\), leaving \(H_{x_{s_1}}(g)\) for a genuine remainder test \(g\).

At a general consumed gate \(x=x_{s_j}\), compare \(H_x(g)\) with \(\mathbb E[g(P_x);J_x<\infty]\) at the fresh reference source \(y'=x e^{\delta L}\), using the relative unmarked estimate, in either direction. Choose \(0<\delta\ll h,\eta\), fixed for the limit in \(L\). On that source the centered extra clock \(J_x-\delta L/d\) exceeds \(\epsilon b_L\) in absolute value with probability
\[
                    O\big(L^{-c_0\epsilon^2/\delta}\big)+O_A(L^{-A}),  \tag{4}
\]
where \(c_0>0\) can be fixed independently of \(\delta\). This follows directly from (2) with \(g=1\), the Gaussian variance \(\sigma_J^2\delta L\), both signs, and the arbitrary additive floor; \(K\) in (2) can depend on \(\delta,\epsilon,A\). The constant in the big \(O\) need not be uniform in \(\delta\).

Off this small event, the next clock from the landing to \(x_{s_{j+1}}\), centered at \(hL/d\), differs by at most \(\epsilon b_L\) from the entire reference-source clock to \(x_{s_{j+1}}\) centered at \((h+\delta)L/d\). Their later landing and all subsequent increments are identical. Thus expanding the next interval by \(\epsilon\) bounds from above, and contracting it bounds from below. The fresh whole crossing has the factorization (2) at log-height distance \((h+\delta)L\). In particular the new Gaussian factor has variance \(\sigma_J^2(h+\delta)L\); no assumption of independence for a conditioned first landing has been made.

Iterate this procedure a fixed \(m\) times. Given \(\epsilon\) below half the smallest interval width, first take \(A\) larger than the total Gaussian cost of the contracted intervals plus two, uniformly at all sufficiently small \(\delta\). Choose \(\delta\) small enough that (4) costs \(O(L^{-A})\). Intermediate relative factors and the at most \(O_m(1)\) additive remainders do not affect the log of the product at speed \(\ln L\); \(H_x(1)=1+o(1)\). The enlarged and contracted Gaussian boxes have costs with \(h+\delta\) in every denominator after the first. Let \(\epsilon\downarrow0,\delta\downarrow0\) in these bounds, keeping each choice fixed before taking \(L\to\infty\), to obtain (3). The same upper argument with one-sided unbounded intervals (and other increments unconstrained) gives finite-dimensional exponential tightness: at any fixed mesh the cost of some increment exceeding \(M b_L\) in absolute value tends to infinity with \(M\). Rectangles and this tightness yield the ordinary finite-dimensional large-deviation bounds with quadratic increment rate.

## A uniform estimate between two mesh points

For the centered odd-clock process \(F_L(s)=(J_{x_s}-sL/d)/b_L\) on success there is an absolute \(c_1>0\), such that at all sufficiently fine fixed meshes and every fixed \(\rho>0\),
\[
 \limsup_{Y\to\infty}(\ln L)^{-1}
 \log\Pr\left(\max_j\sup_{s_j\le s\le s_{j+1}}|F_L(s)-F_L(s_j)|>\rho\right)
                                  \le-c_1\rho^2/h.                      \tag{5}
\]
At the initial grid point one may assign \(F_L(0)=0\); the actual odd passage through \(Y\) is also covered by this estimate. The constant may be decreased independently of \(h,\rho\).

Inside one cell starting at threshold \(x\), the event is a bounded test of \(P_x\): inspect the centered passage counts from this landing through each intermediate threshold; if the source already overshot one, its additional count is zero. Compare with the reference \(y'=xe^{hL}\) at cost of a bounded multiplicative constant and \(O_A(L^{-A})\), for arbitrary fixed \(A\). For the outer source use either the relative unmarked large-source comparison or (2)'s local unmarked version, whichever range contains its fixed ratio. At the first cell start directly from \(W_Y\) with zero initial count. A failure to reach the lower edge of the cell can be counted as bad.

At that reference the logarithmic distance from the source to the bottom of the cell is at most \(2hL+O(1)\). Use \(K=\lceil 40 hL\rceil\) native dates, choosing the mesh sufficiently fine in terms of \(\eta\) that \(2^{2K+2}/y'\) is exponentially small in \(L\). The true finite parity tape through \(2K+2\) differs by this amount in total variation from fair bits with the first one fixed. Every true state in the prefix is exponentially large in \(L\). The accumulated nonlinear logarithmic correction is \(o(1)\). With error \(O(e^{-c hL})\) the state at date \(K\) is below the lower gate by the elementary binomial bound, since \(40/\mu>2\). With an additional exponentially small error there is an odd date by \(2K\) after that state, so all odd entrances occurred by \(2K\). With error \(O_A(L^{-A})\) the longest zero run in this tape is at most \(C_A\ln L\). Consequently at every odd entrance its log undershoot is \(O_A(\ln L)\): the first native crossing of any threshold loses at most \(\ln2\), followed only by halvings up to the odd landing. This holds simultaneously for the entire continuum of thresholds.

Write \(Q_t\) for the number of odd departures through native dates \(0,\ldots,t-1\) on this tape. Uniformly over all its subintervals, the deviation of \(Q_v-Q_u\) from \((v-u)/2\) is bounded by \(2\max_{t\le 2K}|Q_t-t/2|\). The probability that this maximum exceeds \(c_2\rho b_L\), for any fixed absolute \(c_2>0\), is \(O(L^{-c_3\rho^2/h})\) by the exponential martingale maximal inequality. For two successive or intermediate odd gates, the exact log product and the preceding overshoot estimate give
\[
 \left|Q_v-Q_u-\ln(x_{\rm upper}/x_{\rm lower})/d\right|
       \le C\max_{t\le 2K}|Q_t-t/2|+O_A(\ln L).
\]
This includes repeated landings across overshot gates and the first-source comparison, where \(\ln(W_Y/Y)=O(1)\). Choose \(c_2\) small enough, then take \(A\) large compared to \(\rho^2/h\). Summing over fixed finitely many cells gives (5). In particular the polygonal interpolation of the mesh values is exponentially good as \(h\to0\): its sup error is at most twice the left-hand oscillation.

## Native dates, integer cutoffs and the uniform-topology rate

On a fixed fine mesh the same local reference tests bound the largest odd gap through \(Y^\eta\) by \(C_A\ln L\), and the native duration through that gate by \(C_\eta L\), outside probability \(O_A(L^{-A})\) for arbitrary \(A\). In detail, a large gap wholly within any odd-to-odd gate segment or an overly long segment is a bounded test of the upper landing, with the exceptional reference estimates just proved. The first segment starts at the true source. Odd gates split whole gaps, so the union covers the original trajectory. For the undershoot at the upper edge of a segment, its entering gap was already controlled in the preceding segment. Upon success, all native states prior to the first native crossing of \(Y^\eta\) exceed that threshold; through the final odd entrance the current height is still exponentially large, by the gap bound. Hence the entire nonlinear correction is \(o(1)\). If needed, apply these same bounds down to the smaller level \(Y^{\eta/2}\), which still lies on a fixed-power range. The first native crossing of any relevant level is within \(O_A(\ln L)\) native dates of its odd entrance. Its odd arrival's log height is the threshold log plus \(O_A(\ln L)\). The exact shortcut product therefore gives simultaneously
\[
  \tau^T_{\le x_s}(W_Y)= (\log_2 3)\,J_{x_s}(W_Y)+sL/\ln2+O_A(\ln L)   \tag{6}
\]
outside that exceptional probability. Since the landing occurs, the cap at the true minimum does not alter its first native passage. The deterministic mean coefficient in (6) is \(\mu=(\log_2 3)/d+1/\ln2\), and \(\sigma=(\log_2 3)\sigma_J\).

For a uniform integer \(n\le X\), disintegrate into \(n=2^e v\) and odd octaves \(v\in[y,2y)\) with \(y=X/2^{e+j+1}\) (harmless interval-endpoint rounding). Removing \(e+j>C_A\ln\ln X\) costs \(O_A((\ln X)^{-A})\), by the geometric source masses. On a retained cell \(\ln n,\ln y,\ln X\) differ by \(O_A(\ln\ln X)\), and the initial halving run has length \(O_A(\ln\ln X)\). The physical threshold \(n^{1-s}\) corresponds on the odd octave to \(s'=1-(1-s)\ln n/\ln y\), with \(|s'-s|=O_A(\ln\ln X/\ln X)\). When \(s'<0\) the initial odd source and threshold lie near the zero-time endpoint; (5) on the first cell bounds the error there, or the preceding initial halving run supplies the crossing. At other dates (5), applied down to \(y^{\eta/2}\), shows that the uniform time change is exponentially negligible at the deviation speed: for each tolerance first bound the error using adjacent cells of length \(h\), then send \(h\downarrow0\) and \(A\to\infty\). The deterministic centered drifts change by \(O_A(\ln\ln X)\). Normalizations differ by a factor \(1+o(1)\), harmless by exponential tightness of the norm from (3) and (5). All upper bounds hold uniformly over these octaves by the usual sequential characterization as \(y\to\infty\), and there are only \(O_A((\ln\ln X)^2)\) retained cells. For the lower bound one can retain a single fixed-proportion top odd octave. This transfers the finite-dimensional and interpolation bounds to the stated \(Z_X\).

For completeness take nested uniform dyadic meshes on \([0,1-\eta]\). The energy of a grid polygon is half the sum of squared increments divided by their interval lengths, as in (3). These energies increase upon refinement for a fixed underlying function. Functions starting at zero with bounded energy are uniformly bounded and equicontinuous by Cauchy–Schwarz; their continuous uniform limits have energy at most the liminf, by taking every fixed grid and using lower semicontinuity of its finite sum. The supremum of the grid energies is exactly (1) on continuous functions: their polygonal slopes are bounded in \(L^2\) if that supremum is finite, so a weakly convergent subsequence integrates to the given function; for an absolutely continuous function the grid slopes are cell means of its derivative and converge in \(L^2\) whenever the derivative is in \(L^2\). For a closed set, apply the grid upper bound to polygons within uniform distance \(\rho\) of it and (5) to the approximation error. On bounded energy sublevels compactness gives the claimed closed-set bound by first refining the grid and then reducing \(\rho\). For an open set around a finite-energy \(f\), choose a tolerance ball inside it and a fine grid on which its polygon lies inside half that ball. The grid lower probability for a still smaller polygon neighborhood has exponential cost at most \(I(f)\); subtract the approximation failure, whose cost by (5) can be made strictly greater. This yields the open-set bound and proves (1).
