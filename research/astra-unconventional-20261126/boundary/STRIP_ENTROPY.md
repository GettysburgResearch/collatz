# The width of an ordinary shortcut sojourn

Let \(T(x)=x/2\) for \(x\) even and \(T(x)=(3x+1)/2\) for \(x\) odd. Suppose an injective orbit segment stays in \([X,3^{1+\eta}X]\) for \(L\) successive states. For fixed \(0\le\eta\le1\), uniformly as \(X\to\infty\),
\[
                      L\le X^{\eta+o(1)} .
\]
The two endpoints use the full shortcut orbit. A confinement assumption on the odd subsequence alone gives a different width.

Choose \(k=\lceil\log_2(3^{1+\eta}X+1)\rceil\). Each length-\(k\) parity word specifies its starting residue modulo \(2^k\), by induction on the binary shortcut map, so distinct admissible starts in this interval give distinct words. Remove the last \(k\) starts. With \(u=\log_3(x/X)\), \(\rho=\log_3 2\), and \(p_i\) the number of odd steps among the first \(i\), the heights satisfy
\[
 \log_3(T^i(x)/X)=u+p_i-\rho i+E_i,\qquad 0\le E_i\le k/(3X\log3)=:\varepsilon.
\]
Consequently each \(p_i\) belongs to \([\rho i-u-\varepsilon,\rho i-u+1+\eta]\). For \(\eta<1\) and sufficiently large \(X\), this gives two possible integers at most. Their simultaneous admissibility requires the phase \(\{\rho i-u-\varepsilon\}\) to lie in a fixed circle arc of length \(\eta+\varepsilon\), up to a choice of endpoints. Uniform equidistribution of irrational rotation in intervals, uniformly over their translates, bounds the number of two-choice instants by \((\eta+o(1))k\). For example, approximate intervals by unions of finitely many equal cells and apply equidistribution to these cells. As \(u\) varies over its bounded range the possible integer sets can change at just \(O(k)\) values; count the open cells and their endpoints separately. Thus there are at most \(O(k)2^{(\eta+o(1))k}\) words, giving the result. For \(\eta=1\), injectivity gives the result directly.

There is a dynamical obstruction to closing the argument by entropy alone. In the limiting logarithmic skew system allow a real height \(u_i\in[0,1+\eta]\) and binary \(\epsilon_i\) with \(u_{i+1}=u_i-\rho+\epsilon_i\). If \(0<\eta<\min(\rho,1-\rho)\), take \(u_i=\{\theta-i\rho\}+b_i\), where \(b_i\in\{0,1\}\) can be freely chosen whenever \(\{\theta-i\rho\}<\eta\), and is zero otherwise. Such instants are nonadjacent, each surrounded by the two base increments \((0,1)\). A toggle replaces them by \((1,0)\). The resulting binary dictionary has exponential rate at least \(\eta\log2\), since the toggle positions have frequency \(\eta\) and distinct choices of interior toggles yield distinct words. The construction concerns symbolic lifts.

There is additional rigidity if polynomially long ordinary segments exist. If along \(X\to\infty\) their lengths are \(L\ge X^\delta\) for some \(\delta>0\) in a fixed multiplicative strip, take any subsequential weak limit of the distributions of parity itineraries and log heights viewed from a uniform interior time. Compactness gives a stationary two-sided process \((\epsilon_i,u_i)\) with bounded heights satisfying the exact limiting cocycle above. The binary process has entropy at least \(\delta\log2\): the \(k\)-word at a random start among the first \(L-k\) has entropy \(\log(L-k)\) by the ordinary residue injection. For fixed block length \(m\), subadditivity over the \(\lfloor k/m\rfloor\) consecutive blocks and a final remainder gives an upper bound \((k/m)H_m+o(k)+O(m)\), where \(H_m\) is the entropy of the limiting stationary \(m\)-word law. The finite translated block laws have total variation \(O(k/L)\) from that at the initial uniform start, and entropy is continuous on each fixed finite alphabet. Now divide by \(k\), then let \(m\to\infty\).

Disintegrate the joint law given the entire binary sequence. Translation of the height by the measurable increment \(\epsilon_0-\rho\) translates each conditional height distribution into the conditional distribution for the shifted sequence. Its lower median \(f\) is therefore a bounded measurable function of the parity itinerary with \(f\circ\sigma-f=\epsilon_0-\rho\) almost everywhere. Hence \(\exp(2\pi i f)\) is an eigenfunction of the binary process with eigenvalue \(\exp(-2\pi i\rho)\). This excludes weak mixing for such a limit. The argument does not force an arithmetic counterexample to have such polynomially long narrow segments. In particular, the previously studied bounded cycle branch has odd endpoints in \([m,16m/3]\) and shortcut states can extend up to about \(8m\); this width is too large for a subpower conclusion.
