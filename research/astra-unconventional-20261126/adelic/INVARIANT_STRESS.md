# Full Galois symmetrization with two-place clearance

**PROPOSED written result.** Let $x,y$ be an actual ordinary odd edge, $dy=3x+1$, $d=2^a$, $q=3/d$, $X=E_x^{-1}$ and $Y=E_y^{-1}$. Assume $\min(x,y)\ge10^6$. This section constructs a fully Galois-invariant expression with positive real Rogers value of order at most $\max(X,Y)^2$ per edge after rational centering. It can be adjusted to clear both dyadic and ternary contractions while retaining full invariance, strict positivity and a uniform denominator. The construction leaves finite places outside two and three.

## I1. Four clock powers cancel the first variation

Write $c=\pi^2/6$ and $F(t)=L(e^t)-c$ on the standard real branch. This is odd by the positive reciprocal identity. Choose
$$u_1=-24,\quad u_2=-16,\quad v_1=8,\quad v_2=16.$$
For each pair $(u,v)$ let $d'=v-u$, $k=(u+v)/2$, $j=(v-u)/2$. Give the four pairs coefficients $w_{uv}=\epsilon_{uv}/d'$, with $\epsilon_{11}=\epsilon_{22}=1$ and $\epsilon_{12}=\epsilon_{21}=-1$. Define the local symbol
$$D_i=-\operatorname{sgn}(\log q)\sum_{u,v}w_{uv}
\left(\sum_{\eta,\epsilon=\pm1}\{q^k X^{\eta j}Y^{\epsilon j}\}
-\{q^u\}-\{q^v\}\right).$$
All values belong to the field generated by the two Pell fields. Each four-term sum is invariant under the entire multiquadratic Galois group, including when the field labels coincide. The coefficient denominator divides 480. Evaluate each symbol in this paragraph as $L(t)-c$, so the raw and centered values differ by an explicitly bounded-denominator multiple of $c$. The extreme arguments below stay on the claimed sides of one, and the mixed arguments are separated from one, so the displayed symbols are defined.

Put $b=\log q$, $h=\log\theta<0$, $T=\max(X,Y)$ and $P=\log(E_x E_y)$. One has $q=\theta^2 X/Y$ and $\log(E_x/E_y)=2h-b$. The four log arguments are $kb\pm jP$ and $ub+d'h,vb-d'h$. For the mixed terms with the baseline removed put
$$M(s)=\sum_{u,v} w_{uv}\left(F(ub+d's)-F(ub)+F(vb-d's)-F(vb)\right).$$
Their value is $M(h)$. Since $w_{uv}d'=\epsilon_{uv}$ and the signed row and column sums vanish, $M(0)=M'(0)=0$. For nonzero $t$ direct differentiation gives
$$F''(t)=-\frac{t}{8\sinh^2(t/2)},\qquad
M''(0)=8\left(F''(8b)-2F''(16b)+F''(24b)\right).$$
Write $\phi(t)=|F''(t)|$ for $t>0$. It decreases there and $\phi(16|b|)/\phi(8|b|)=2r/(1+r)^2$, $r=e^{-8|b|}\le(3/4)^8<0.101$. Therefore $M''(0)$ has sign $-\operatorname{sgn} b$ and magnitude greater than $4\phi(8|b|)$.

Uniformity is necessary since the dyadic clock is unrestricted. The identities $1-\theta=Y+v$ and $v=\theta X$ give $T/2\le|h|\le4T$, as $T<1/(12\cdot10^6)$. Also $|b|>1/4$, and the unperturbed arguments have magnitude greater than two. On $|t|\ge1$ the absolute logarithmic derivative of $|F''(t)|$ is at most four, by the formula $1/t-\coth(t/2)$. Since $d'\le40$, perturbing the arguments by at most $40|h|$ changes each second derivative by less than $0.001\phi(8|b|)$. The sum of the absolute derivative coefficients, including both endpoints of each pair, is 256. Thus throughout the segment from zero to $h$, the signed second derivative is greater than $3\phi(8|b|)$. Double integration gives $-\operatorname{sgn}(b)M(h)>1.5 h^2\phi(8|b|)$.

It remains to bound the two extreme terms uniformly. After using the reciprocal identity their contribution for a single pair is $L(t_1)-L(t_2)$, where
$$t_1=\theta^{2k}X^vY^{-u},\qquad t_2=\theta^{-2k}X^{-u}Y^v.$$
Both are positive and at most $512T^{24}e^{-8|b|}$: the minimum of $X/Y,Y/X$ is at most $2e^{-|b|}$, each monomial has total degree at least 24 and at least eight powers of the smaller variable, and $\theta^{\pm2k}<2$. Using $L(t)\le t(2+|\log t|)$ for $0<t\le1/2$, and $2\sum |w_{uv}|<1$, bounds the absolute contribution of all such terms by
$$512 T^{24}e^{-8|b|}(2+24\log(1/T)+8|b|).$$
In contrast $h^2\phi(8|b|)\ge T^2 |b|e^{-8|b|}$. The ratio is at most $512(16T^{22}+96T^{21})<1/2$, using $\log(1/T)\le1/T$. This proves strict positivity.

For the upper bound, the same derivatives give $|M''(s)|\le257\phi(8|b|)<52$ on the segment ($8|b|>2$), so the resulting centered value lies in $(0,500T^2)$. On a simple cycle with minimum $N\ge10^6$, summing these bounds gives less than $14/N$, using $T_i^2\le X_i^2+Y_i^2$ and $\sum_{n\ge N}1/n^2\le2/N$. All signs and constants here are properties of the real edges; ordinary integrality is used for the uniform period-free estimate through distinct integer states.

## I2. Rational local contractions and small rational corrections

The following assertion applies to a single edge or a finite simple cycle. Put $D_0=480\sum_i D_i$, so the coefficients of $D_0$ are integral. At every place above two, respectively three, the contraction of its boundary is the class of a *positive rational* $r_2$, respectively $r_3$, independent of which place above that rational prime is used. Indeed, in each nonzero-$k$ four-term group the four arguments have the common valuation $kv(q)\ne0$, since $X,Y$ are global units. The four complements have the common valuation $\min(0,kv(q))$. Their product is rational by sign invariance; the product of the arguments is $q^{4k}$. Hence only rational classes occur. At $k=0$ the four arguments pair with their reciprocals and the four boundaries cancel. The clock terms are rational. Contraction of any wedge with its own valuation twice is zero, so each $r_p$ is a $p$-unit.

Here is a way to repair a rational contraction without a real lower bound on $|\log r_p|$. For a positive ternary-unit rational $t$, define
$$A_3(t)=\tfrac12\{t^2\}-\tfrac16\{t^6\}.$$
Its boundary is $-[t]\wedge[1+t^2+t^4]$ and its ternary contraction is $[t]$, since $t^2\equiv1\pmod3$ and $v_3(1+t^2+t^4)=1$. For $w=5^{-n}$, both $r_3w$ and $w$ can be made arbitrarily small at the real place, while remaining ternary units. Subtract $A_3(r_3w)-A_3(w)$ from $D_0$. This clears **all** ternary contractions and changes the raw real value by arbitrarily little. Its centered value changes by the same amount because the two copies carry identical coefficient sums.

Multiply the adjusted expression by six. Its dyadic contraction is the class of a positive rational $r$ with $v_2(r)=v_3(r)=0$. The ternary condition follows by applying the two valuations in reversed orders to its boundary, whose ternary contraction was cleared. For a rational $2,3$-unit $t$ set
$$A_2(t)=\tfrac12\{t^2\}-\tfrac14\{t^4\}.$$
The boundary is $-[t]\wedge[1+t^2]$; its dyadic contraction is $[t]$ since odd rational squares are $1\pmod8$, and its ternary contraction is zero. Subtract $A_2(rw')-A_2(w')$ using a possibly different $w'=5^{-n'}$ and divide by $6\cdot480$. Both changes can be made smaller than any prescribed positive real tolerance. All added symbols are rational, hence full Galois invariance is preserved, and the common coefficient denominator is at most **11520** in the divisibility sense. The adjustment never requires $w$ or $w'$ to approximate an unknown orbit parameter; any sufficiently large exponents work.

For a simple integral cycle with $N\ge10^8$ choose a tolerance smaller than half the initial value. The resulting centered value lies strictly between zero and $21/N<\pi^2/(120\cdot11520)$. Thus the U7 grid forces a nonzero rationalized boundary. It cannot lie wholly in the exterior square of the global unit group. Indeed, the rational unit representation of a totally real Galois field is the regular representation minus the trivial character by Dirichlet's unit theorem and the logarithmic embedding. For an elementary abelian two-group all characters are rational and distinct. Thus the invariant exterior square of the unit representation is zero. If every finite valuation contraction vanished, a decomposition of the rationalized multiplicative group into units and a complement mapping injectively to fractional ideal divisors would put the boundary in precisely that exterior square. Invariance gives a contradiction. **Some finite place above a rational prime at least five must detect the adjusted boundary.** The same reasoning applies to a single sufficiently high edge using the stronger local $500T^2$ estimate.

The last prime is a necessary boundary defect, not a contradiction to the Collatz cycle. Its occurrence can depend on the explicitly added rational factors. The construction does not prove that the original Collatz relation rules out every such prime.

## I3. Primes of the uncorrected single-edge factors

Fix one edge satisfying I1. Set
$$\alpha_{uv,\eta,\epsilon}=q^k X^{\eta j}Y^{\epsilon j},\qquad
\Gamma(n)=\prod_{u,v,\eta,\epsilon}(1-\alpha_{uv,\eta,\epsilon}^{\,n})^{\epsilon_{uv}\eta},\qquad
\gamma_n=\Gamma(3n)/\Gamma(n).$$
For every finite set of rational primes $S$ and every $L\ge0$, some prime $p\notin S$ occurs in the fractional ideal of $\gamma_n$ for arbitrarily large $n$ divisible by $3^L$. It splits in the actual source Pell field $F_x$ and satisfies $p\equiv \pm1\pmod{3^{L+1}}$. These are primes of the original edge's cyclotomic factors at varying index. They need not divide an orbit state or the fixed-index boundary of I2.

Flipping $Y$ permutes the factors with the same sign. Flipping $X$ reverses the exponents. If the fields coincide, the simultaneous flip also reverses the exponents. In all cases $\Gamma(n)$ belongs to $F_x$ and has norm one. It has zero valuation at every place above two or three. For $k\ne0$, all four arguments have the same nonzero valuation, and all four complements have the same valuation. For $k=0$ the two reciprocal pair ratios give exactly $X^{2jn}$, a global unit. No argument equals one by the separation proved in I1.

Write $t=\log\alpha$ in the physical embedding. In the sum giving $\log|\Gamma(n)|$ use
$$\log|1-e^{nt}|=n\max(t,0)+\log(1-e^{-n|t|}).$$
The terms linear in $n$ cancel. For each pair, including the factor $\eta$ but before multiplying by $\epsilon_{uv}$, the extreme contribution is $-(kb+jP)$. The mixed contribution is $vb-d'h$ for $b>0$, and $-(ub+d'h)$ for $b<0$. Each expression is an affine function of $u,v$ and the signed row and column sums vanish. Among the remaining terms there is a unique smallest $|t|=c_0>0$. It is the smaller in magnitude of $8b-32h,8b-24h$, which differ since $h<0$ and have the same sign by the I1 bounds. All other mixed magnitudes are at least $16|b|-40|h|$. The extremes satisfy $e^{-|t|}\le512T^{24}e^{-8|b|}$, smaller than either candidate's $e^{-|t|}$ since $T<10^{-7}$ and $|h|\le4T$. The unique term has coefficient $+1$ or $-1$. Thus $\log|\Gamma(n)|=\pm e^{-nc_0}(1+o(1))$ with fixed sign. In particular $|\gamma_n|\to1$ and $|\gamma_n|\ne1$ for all sufficiently large $n$.

Enlarge $S$ to contain two and three. The $\alpha$ are units outside those primes. Take $n$ divisible by $3^L$ and by all the finite reduction orders of the sixteen $\alpha$ at all places over $S\setminus\{2,3\}$. The factors of $\gamma_n$ there are powers of $\Phi_3(\alpha^n)=1+\alpha^n+\alpha^{2n}\equiv3$, all units. If $\gamma_n$ were a unit everywhere, its norm-one quadratic trace would be an integer. But $2<|\gamma_n+\gamma_n^{-1}|<3$ for sufficiently large such $n$. Hence some $p\notin S$ has a nonzero valuation on $\gamma_n$. It must split in $F_x$, since conjugate valuations have opposite signs. At a place over $p$, some $\Phi_3(\alpha^n)$ vanishes in the residue field, so $\alpha^n$ has order three. The reduction order of $\alpha$ is divisible by $3^{L+1}$. The compositum is multiquadratic, so its residue degree is at most two (the residue Galois group is cyclic of exponent at most two). Thus $3^{L+1}$ divides $p^2-1$, giving the claimed congruence. This proof handles cancellation in the signed product.
