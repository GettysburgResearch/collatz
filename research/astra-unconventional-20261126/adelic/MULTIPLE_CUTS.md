# Integral moments at arbitrarily many internal orbit cuts

**PROPOSED written theorem.** Choose any positive odd ternary-unit endpoint \(y\), any number of segments \(J\ge1\), and a degree \(d\ge1\). Give every segment its own exact odd and shortcut clocks \((k_j,H_j)\), with
\[
               k_j\ge A(d+1),\qquad H_j\ge B(d+1)^2 k_j.
\]
The constants are absolute and independent of \(J,y\). Choose a source residue \(c_j\in\{1,5\}\) for each cut, including the initial source. Consider all positive ordinary paths
\[
        x_0\longrightarrow x_1\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow x_J=y
\]
whose \(j\)th segment uses precisely \((k_j,H_j)\) and whose left endpoint satisfies \(x_{j-1}\equiv c_j\bmod6\), for \(1\le j\le J\). Write \(t_j=(x_{j-1}-c_j)/6\) and \(v_d(t)=(\binom t0,\ldots,\binom td)\). The integer lattice generated by the actual cut-moment vectors is exactly
\[
             \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}\left\{
                  v_d(t_1)\otimes\cdots\otimes v_d(t_J)
                   \right\}=\mathbb Z^{(d+1)^J}.                    \tag{1}
\]
Thus even the mixed products at all marked internal states have their full generic integral lattice, with specified ternary-unit classes. Every one of the \(J\) segment carries is a genuine exact integer. The witnesses have initial height at most \(y2^{\sum H_j}/3^{\sum k_j}\); similarly every internal height is bounded using just the clocks to its right. In particular rank grows exponentially in \(J\) for a fixed degree and fixed clocks per segment. The theorem imposes no condition on the subsequent orbit of \(y\).

## A full one-segment lattice in either unit chart

Fix \(c=1\) or \(5\) and write \(t=(x-c)/6\) for positive odd sources congruent to \(c\bmod6\). We first prove, at the same one-segment thresholds, that the integral span of \(v_d(t)\) over this restricted fiber is \(\mathbb Z^{d+1}\). We give the changes to the [one-segment proof and its routing lemma](MOMENT_LATTICE.md).

1. Beyond the initial \(k-2\) unit edges let \(w\) be their current site and \(T\) the remaining total. Choose \(2\le h\le19\) satisfying \(2^h\equiv 2^T w-3-9c\pmod{27}\). The right hand side is a ternary unit and 2 generates the units modulo 27, since its powers have order 18. For example \(2^9\equiv-1\) and \(2^6\not\equiv1\pmod{27}\), which eliminate the proper maximal divisors of 18. Take the two inverse exponents \(T-h-18j,h+18j\) for \(0\le j\le d\). The resulting positive odd sources \((2^T w-3-2^{h+18j})/9\) all have the desired chart. Their coordinates are distinct modulo all primes \(p\ge5\), \(p>d\), with \(\operatorname{ord}_p(2^{18})>d\). At these primes the binomial determinant is a unit. The remaining primes of size \(p>d\), \(p\ge5\), satisfy \(p\le2^{18d}\) and \(\operatorname{ord}_p(2)\le18d\). Thus the fixed-clock routing lemma with \(b=p,r=1\) prescribes \(t\bmod p\) and the ternary chart simultaneously. For the smaller \(5\le p\le d\), use the smallest \(b=p^u>d\), \(r=1\). In both cases the congruences specify \(x\bmod 3b\) because \(2,3\) are units modulo \(b\) and parity is automatic.
2. At 3, for the smallest \(3^u>d\), specify \(x\equiv c+6a\bmod3^{u+1}\). Parity then gives \(t\equiv a\bmod3^u\). This costs one extra ternary digit and still meets the same bounds.
3. At 2, put \(2^u>d\) minimal and \(s=u+1\). For each \(a\bmod2^u\) choose a positive odd representative \(n\equiv c+6a\bmod 3\cdot2^s\) with \(n<3\cdot2^s\). The first \(s\) true odd edges from \(n\) reach a ternary unit \(z\) at cost \(B_n\le3s+2\), by \(2^{B_n}z\le4^s n\). Route from the target at remaining grades \((k-s,H-B_n)\) to \(w\equiv z\bmod3^{s+1}\). Attaching the chosen suffix exactly as in the one-segment proof yields a positive odd input differing from \(n\) by \(2^{B_n}(w-z)/3^s\), hence a multiple of \(3\cdot2^s\). It has the desired unit chart and coordinate modulo \(2^u\). Here the period bound is \(O(3^{s+1})=O((d+1)^{\log_2 3})\).

All constants can be enlarged absolutely to allow the displayed thresholds at both stages of concatenation. These local witnesses give full reduction at every prime, using the same interpolation and Smith-index arguments as for unrestricted odd sources.

## Composing saturated integer images

We prove (1) by induction on the number of marked cuts. The one-segment unit-chart result is uniform in its endpoint. Fix any admissible outer path from \(x_1\) to \(y\), and let \(V\) be its moment tensor. The chart on \(x_1\) makes it a positive ternary unit. Therefore the integer combinations of first-segment rows ending at this particular \(x_1\) contain every standard basis vector \(e_a\in\mathbb Z^{d+1}\). Concatenating each such first-segment path with the same outer path supplies \(e_a\otimes V\) in the full-path lattice. By induction the outer \(V\)'s span their own entire standard lattice, so all \(e_a\otimes e_{a_2}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{a_J}\) occur as integer combinations over actual full paths. This proves (1).

A finite vector of integer-valued rational polynomials in the cut coordinates, of degree at most \(d\) separately in each variable, has an integral coefficient matrix in the product binomial basis by repeated finite differences. Its evaluation lattice on these paths therefore equals its generic one on \(\mathbb Z^J\). Rational vectors follow by clearing denominators. This includes the complete carry of each segment,
\[
                     2^{H_j}x_j-3^{k_j}x_{j-1},
\]
and their polynomial products within the degree bound. The calculation preserves interior ordinary states and complete weighted carries of the specified segments. The individual unit increments at unmarked steps inside each segment can still impose further restrictions on finer observables.

For arbitrarily many cuts the source supports are genuine finite subsets of positive integers, and the terminal remains fixed. The exponential algebraic rank makes no assertion of exponential *smallness* of the archimedean images. Even the quadratic polynomial \((t_1-a)^2\) recovers the one-coordinate obstruction to extracting a new point with nonnegative coefficients.
