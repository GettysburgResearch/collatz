# Squareclasses on an ordinary Collatz trajectory

**PROPOSED written mathematics.** The shortcut map is `T(x)=x/2` at an even positive integer and `T(x)=(3x+1)/2` at an odd one. This packet gives an explicit height bound in terms of the multiplicative squareclasses of the odd states on **one actual path**. It applies to a full cycle of arbitrary period and to finite prefixes of an escaping orbit. A second argument obtains private ramifying primes in short windows. The universal convergence and nontrivial-cycle questions remain open.

Write `[a]` for the image of a positive rational number in the vector space `V=Q_{>0}^*/(Q_{>0}^*)²` over `F₂`. For a positive integer, the vector has one coordinate for each prime appearing to an odd exponent. The rank of a set of integers means the dimension of the span of these vectors.

## A1. An elementary obstruction to even square products

Suppose `a₁,…,a_{2r}` are distinct nonnegative integers, `r≥1`, and `0≤a_i≤B`. Put `b=B+1`. For every positive integer

$$X>(64b)^{2r+2},\qquad \prod_{i=1}^{2r}(X+a_i)\text{ is not a square.}$$

Here is an explicit polynomial approximation proof. Write `P(X)=∏(X+a_i)` and expand the square root at positive infinity as `X^r∑_{j≥0}c_j/X^j`. The power series is the product of the binomial series `(1+a_i t)^{1/2}`, with `t=1/X`. Each coefficient of `(1+t)^{1/2}` has absolute value at most one, and its degree-`k` coefficient times `4^k` is integral. For `k≥1` this follows from `binom(1/2,k)=(-1)^{k-1}Catalan_{k-1}/2^{2k-1}`. Counting the weak compositions of `j` among `2r` factors gives

$$4^j c_j\in\mathbb Z,\qquad |c_j|\le {j+2r-1\choose j}B^j\le 4^r(2b)^j.$$

Define `Q(X)=∑_{j=0}^r c_j X^{r-j}` and `D=4^r`. Thus `DQ(X)` is integral at every integer `X`. When `X>4b`, the tail satisfies

$$|\sqrt{P(X)}-Q(X)|\le 2\,4^r(2b)^{r+1}/X.$$

At the claimed threshold this is less than `1/D`. If `P(X)` were a square, the integer grid would force `√P(X)=Q(X)`. The polynomial `R=D²(P-Q²)` is a nonzero integer polynomial because each root of `P` is simple. Its coefficient absolute values are at most

$$16^r\bigl(b^{2r}+(r+1)(8b)^{2r}\bigr)\le (64b)^{2r+1}.$$

Indeed the sum of the coefficients of `P` is at most `b^{2r}`, and every coefficient of `Q` has modulus at most `(8b)^r`. The elementary Cauchy root bound for a nonzero integer polynomial with coefficients of modulus at most `K` puts every root in `|X|≤1+K`. A nonzero constant has no root. This contradicts `R(X)=0` at the indicated `X`. This is the elementary square-root-at-infinity argument often called Runge's method in this setting.

## A2. Near-full squareclass rank at growing ordinary windows

Fix an actual `H`-step segment `x_i=T^i(n)`, `0≤i≤H`. A row means a departure time `0≤i<H` with odd `x_i`. Let `s_i` count the odd departures before time `i`. Write the exact shortcut identity

$$2^i x_i=3^{s_i}n+C_i,\qquad t_i=C_i/3^{s_i}.$$

Here `C₀=0`. An even step keeps `C_i` unchanged. An odd step sends it to `3C_i+2^i`. Thus `t_i` stays constant at an even step and gains `2^i/3^{s_i+1}>0` at an odd departure. The values of `t_i` **at odd departures** are pairwise distinct, even if physical states repeat. Moreover `0≤t_i≤2^i−1`. With `D=3^{s_H}` all `a_i=D t_i` are distinct nonnegative integers at odd rows, and `a_i≤6^H`. Put `z_i=2^i x_i/3^{s_i}=n+t_i`.

If

$$n>2^{(3H+7)(H+2)},$$

no nonempty even-cardinality set of odd rows has square product of its `z_i`. In fact a set of size `2r` would yield the integer square `∏(Dn+a_i)=D^{2r}∏z_i`. A rational number whose square is integral is itself integral. A1 applies because `2r≤H`, `64(6^H+1)≤2^{3H+7}`, and `Dn≥n`.

The vectors

$$\bigl([x_i],\ i\bmod 2,\ s_i\bmod 2,\ 1\bigr)\in V\oplus\mathbb F_2^3$$

are linearly independent under that height condition. A nonempty vanishing sum would select evenly many rows with square physical product and with both clock sums even, making their normalized product a square. In particular the physical odd-state rank is at least `m−3` when the segment has `m` odd departures. This elementary argument allows `H` to grow on the order of `√log n`.

## A3. All-period height bound for the field generated by an orbit

Let `r` be the rank of the odd departure values on a finite shortcut trajectory from `n₀` to its recorded maximum `M`, including every intermediate departure. Choose `H=2r+8`. Put

$$B(r)=2^{H+(3H+7)(H+2)}.$$

Then

$$M\le\max(2^H n_0, B(r)).$$

If the maximum occurs before step `H`, use `T(x)≤2x`. Otherwise take the `H` steps immediately before that maximum. For starting state `n`, the same inequality gives `n≥M/2^H`. If the window has `m` odd departures, its endpoint also satisfies `M≤2^{2m-H}n`, since its even steps multiply by `1/2`. By maximality `M≥n`, hence `2m≥H`. Were `M>B(r)`, A2 would give physical rank at least `m−3≥r+1`. Every one of those states was counted in the original span.

* On **any** positive ordinary cycle with odd-state rank `r`, the maximum is at most `B(r)`. Start `H` steps before a maximum, wrapping around as often as necessary. This bound is uniform in the sizes of the prime generators and the cycle period. In particular only finitely many ordinary cycles can have rank at most any fixed `r`.
* On **any** fixed positive ordinary trajectory, finite total odd-state rank is equivalent to eventual periodicity. If the rank is finite, apply the bound at each new maximum, and use determinism on a finite set of integers. Eventual periodicity gives a finite set of state values in the other direction. This leaves the identity of any eventual cycle open.
* On an injective ordinary shortcut prefix of length `N`, the maximum is at least `N+1`. For one fixed source the accumulated rank is therefore `Ω(√log N)` as `N→∞`. Equivalently the compositum of the rational quadratic fields generated by the odd values has degree at least `2^{c√log N}` eventually, by the usual squareclass degree formula. The set of generators here grows with the prefix.

The first missing inference in a universal escape proof would be an upper bound on the squareclass rank for **each fixed seed**, or some incompatible bound at its height records. Integer membership in a single ordinary orbit gives no such bound in this argument. The cycle bound also requires rank information before it can exclude an individual cycle.

## A4. Private ramified primes at all but two rows

Fix `H` and a finite set `S` of rational primes, allowed to be empty. For every sufficiently large ordinary starting value, all except at most two of its odd departure rows admit a prime `p_i` outside `S` with odd valuation at that row and zero valuation at every other odd departure row of the window. The primes so obtained are distinct. The assertion is uniform over all actual `H`-step words and makes no growth assumption about their future.

For `i<j` odd rows, the intervening word has at least one odd departure. It gives `2^{j-i}x_j=3^{s_j-s_i}x_i+C_{ij}` with strictly positive integer `C_{ij}≤3^{j-i}−2^{j-i}`. The bound follows by induction on the word, since the all-odd recurrence maximizes its affine constant. Thus `gcd(x_i,x_j)` divides `C_{ij}`. At a fixed word, enlarge `S` by the divisors that are primes of the finitely many positive `C_{ij}`. If row `i` has no private prime of odd valuation outside the original `S`, its squarefree part `d_i` is supported on that enlarged finite set. This permits only finitely many values of `d_i`.

Three such failures `i,j,k` would give

$$F(n)=\prod_{h\in\{i,j,k\}}(3^{s_h}n+C_h)=2^{i+j+k}d_i d_j d_k\, y^2$$

for an integer `y`. The roots `−t_i,−t_j,−t_k` of `F` are distinct. For each fixed polynomial and multiplier this nonsingular cubic has finitely many integer solutions by the hyperelliptic bound of Bérczes, Evertse and Győry, Theorem 2.2, applied over `Q` with just the archimedean place. All choices of words, rows and twists form a finite set for fixed `H,S`, so a single onset suffices. Along a fixed injective orbit, every bounded range of values occurs only finitely often. Consequently the odd rows supported modulo squares on any fixed `S` have zero upper Banach density in shortcut time: for a fixed window length its tail blocks contain at most two, and then the window length can tend to infinity.

Two exceptions occur on arbitrarily large actual starts, already with `S` empty and the two consecutive odd states separated by a single even shortcut departure. The Pell solutions `u,v` of `4v²−3u²=1` include `(1,1)` and are preserved by `(u,v)↦(7u+8v,6u+7v)`. Starting from `(1,1)` gives unbounded odd `u,v`, and the two odd states are `u²` and `v²`, with the intermediate even state `2v²`. In particular `225→338→169`. The window of three steps records both odd departures. This is sharpness across changing sources.

## A5. Known convergent controls attain maximum local ramification

For **every** fixed finite shortcut word and **every** prescribed finite prime set `S`, there are unbounded ordinary positive sources that realize the word, reach 1, and have a private outside-`S` prime of odd valuation at **every** odd departure in the word.

If the word has an odd departure, `C_H` is a unit modulo 3: its last odd update contributes a power of 2. Write `s=s_H`. Since `2` generates the units modulo `3^s`, choose arbitrarily large integers `e` in one progression such that `2^{e+H}≡ C_H (mod 3^s)`. The resulting integers `n=(2^{e+H}−C_H)/3^s` are eventually positive. They realize exactly the supplied word and end at `2^e`. To see legality directly, fix one ordinary realization of the word (or obtain it inductively using the parity congruence). The congruence `3^s n+C_H≡0 (mod 2^H)` uniquely fixes its source class because `3^s` is odd. A parity word has precisely that class, by induction along the affine steps. If there are no odd departures, take `n=2^{H+e}`.

Fix an odd row `i`. If its squarefree part `d` were in a fixed finite list for infinitely many `e`, the suffix ending at `2^e` would give

$$2^{e+H-i}=3^{s-s_i}\,d\,y^2+C_{iH},\qquad C_{iH}>0.$$

In one residue `e+H−i=3q+h`, `h∈{0,1,2}`, this is the integral curve `2^h X³−C_{iH}=3^{s-s_i}d y²`, `X=2^q`. Its cubic roots are simple because `C_{iH}` is nonzero. Theorem 2.2 again gives finitely many integer solutions, contradicting unbounded `q`. Enlarge `S` by the same pair carries as in A4. Every odd row eventually has an odd valuation outside that enlarged set, and such a prime is private by the pair divisibility. Take the maximum of the finitely many onsets. Hence the actual convergent controls have physical squareclass rank **equal** to their number of odd rows. Taking the all-odd word of increasing length exhibits unbounded local ranks in the basin of 1. This supplies no uniform bound in A3 across changing seeds, and leaves open the existence of a separate bound for each seed.
