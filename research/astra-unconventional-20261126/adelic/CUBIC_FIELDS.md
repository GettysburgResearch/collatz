# A cubic trace field for each occurring Pell field

**PROPOSED written theorem.** Let $x$ be an ordinary positive odd state with $3\nmid x$. This includes every state following the first odd departure. Put $d=2^a$, $dy=3x+1$, and let $E_x$ and $F_x$ be the norm-one unit and its real quadratic field from P1. The physical real cube-root trace
$$t_x=E_x^{1/3}+E_x^{-1/3}$$
generates a cubic number field $B_x$ with one real embedding. Its discriminant is exactly
$$\operatorname{disc} B_x=81\operatorname{disc} D_x,\qquad
D_x=\mathbb Q\left(\sqrt{-x(3x+1)}\right)=\mathbb Q\left(\sqrt{-dxy}\right).$$
Its normal closure is an $S_3$ extension with quadratic resolvent $D_x$. If two such sources have the same Pell field, they generate the same cubic field inside the physical real line. Across $f$ distinct Pell fields the corresponding cubic unit radicals generate an extension of degree $3^f$ over the multiquadratic base containing the cube roots of unity. In particular the field-count lower bounds of P2/P3 hold with this cubic lift.

## C1. The local discriminants

The minimal polynomial is
$$P_x(T)=T^3-3T-(12x+2).$$
Indeed $t_x^3-3t_x=E_x+E_x^{-1}=12x+2$, and $P_x(Z+2)=Z(Z+3)^2-12x$ is Eisenstein at three. Its polynomial discriminant $-36^2 x(3x+1)<0$ gives the signature and quadratic resolvent. At three the shifted root is a uniformizer of the totally ramified cubic completion. In its basis $1,Z,Z^2$ the valuations of the three nonzero terms of a linear combination with $\mathbb Q_3$ coefficients are distinct modulo the ramification index three. Consequently an integral combination has all coefficients in $\mathbb Z_3$, so the polynomial discriminant gives the field discriminant exponent four there. The quadratic resolvent is unramified at three, since $3\nmid x(3x+1)$.

At any prime $p\ne3$ dividing the polynomial discriminant the polynomial has a simple root modulo $p$. Use
$$P_x(T)=(T-2)(T+1)^2-12x=(T+2)(T-1)^2-4(3x+1).$$
At $p\mid x$ the root is $2$, at $p\mid3x+1$ it is $-2$, and at two it is $0$; in each case the derivative is nonzero. Hence the local cubic algebra splits as $\mathbb Q_p$ times a quadratic étale algebra by Hensel's lemma. The latter has the same discriminant squareclass as the cubic, so it is precisely $D_x\otimes\mathbb Q_p$. Products of local integer rings give equality of the two maximal-order discriminant exponents, including at two. At all remaining primes both discriminants are units. Their signs are negative, giving the asserted exact identity. This argument uses neither a global conductor formula nor an estimate for the index.

For states in the same Pell field $F$, let $\lambda>1$ generate the totally positive norm-one units of $F$, an infinite cyclic group. Write $E_x=\lambda^{e_x}$, $e_x>0$. The Dickson recurrence $P_0=2$, $P_1=T$, $P_{j+1}=TP_j-P_{j-1}$ gives $t_x=P_{e_x}(\lambda^{1/3}+\lambda^{-1/3})$. The latter trace has degree at most three since $\lambda+\lambda^{-1}\in\mathbb Z$. Thus all the irreducible cubic traces for this $F$ generate that same cubic field. Conversely distinct $F$ give distinct quadratic resolvents: their squareclasses are obtained from those of $F$ by multiplication by $-3$.

## C2. Independent cubic radicals and the whole-cycle relation

For a finite collection of post-initial states take $K$ to be the compositum of their Pell fields and $\mathbb Q(\mu_3)$. Each $E_x$ is a noncube in $F_x$: if $E_x=u^3$ there, the physical real $u$ is a norm-one unit, so $t_x=u+u^{-1}$ would be an integer. It remains a noncube in $K$, since $[K:F_x]$ is a power of two and taking a norm sends a hypothetical cube root to a cube root of $E_x^{[K:F_x]}$. In the $\mathbb F_3$ space $K^\times/K^{\times3}$ its line is in the eigenspace of the quadratic character of $F_x$. Galois automorphisms either fix or invert $E_x$. Distinct characters give independent nonzero lines: applying the signed average for one character kills the others, and the group order is invertible modulo three. Thus adjoining the $f$ cube roots gives degree $3^f$ by Kummer theory and Galois group $(\mathbb Z/3)^f$ over $K$. This extension is unramified at finite places away from three, since the radicals are roots of $Z^3-E_x$, whose discriminant $-27E_x^2$ is a unit there. For the general Kummer correspondence see J. S. Milne, *Fields and Galois Theory*, chapter 5, [author's edition](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ft.html).

For an ordinary cycle of $m$ odd states and total dyadic clock $A$, the product of its quadratic resolvent characters is the character of $(-1)^m 2^A$. Indeed their defining squareclasses multiply to $(-1)^m2^A(\prod_i x_i)^2$. This restricts the parity of the transposition Frobenius classes of the $m$ cubic polynomials at every common unramified prime. The cubic unit lines above remain independent across distinct labels even when these quadratic characters satisfy relations. Neither the quadratic parity relation nor the large cubic extension degree rules out a positive cycle or an escaping orbit; the base fields can grow along the trajectory.

The strictly checked Lean kernel in `CubicLift.lean` covers the trace formula, the physical local factorizations, the discriminant polynomial and the exclusion of every primitive rational projective root at three. The local maximal-order and Kummer arguments above are written mathematics.

## C3. Maximal cubic splitting groups on a density-one set of windows

Fix $h\ge1$. Along every infinite injective ordinary positive odd orbit, the normal closure of the $h$ cubic fields beginning at odd time $i$ has Galois group exactly $S_3^h$ for a set of $i$ of natural density one. Time is measured in odd departures. The fields and their ramifying primes vary with the window.

First show that the $h+1$ vertex squareclasses of a density-one set of these windows are independent modulo $W=\langle[2],[-1]\rangle$. Split the odd times into blocks of $w>h$ rows and fix an individual clock cutoff $k$. If every clock in a block is at most $k$, the total shortcut length of the block is at most $wk$. The injective integer orbit tends to infinity. For all sufficiently late such blocks, A2 gives vertex rank at least $w-3$, hence deficiency at most five modulo $W$. Consider windows of $h+1$ vertices wholly inside the block. In each one of the $h+1$ start residue classes modulo $h+1$, the windows are disjoint. Their nontrivial dependency vectors would be linearly independent in the one common kernel, so at most five in that class are dependent. At most $5(h+1)+h$ starts in a good block are lost, including boundary crossings. For $n$ odd steps the total clock is at most $2n+\log_2 x_0$ by multiplying $2^{a_i}x_{i+1}=3x_i+1\le4x_i$. The fraction of starts in blocks containing a clock greater than $k$ has upper limit at most $2w/k$. First take $w$ large and then $k$ large. This proves the density claim without uniformity of the A2 onset in either parameter. The window-rank counting argument was supplied during the coordinating review.

The $h$ squareclasses of $D_{x_i}$ are independent in such a window. Modulo $W$, each is $[x_i]+[x_{i+1}]$ and the edge incidence vectors of a path are independent. Their quadratic compositum $D$ has degree $2^h$. The associated $F_{x_i}$ are distinct. Put $K=D(\mu_3)$, which contains all $F_{x_i}$ since $F_{x_i}$ has the product squareclass of $D_{x_i}$ and $-3$. If $M$ is the compositum of the normal closures of the cubic polynomials, then $D\subseteq M$ and
$$MK=K(E_{x_1}^{1/3},\ldots,E_{x_h}^{1/3}).$$
For a physical cube root $z$ the three roots are $\zeta^j z+\zeta^{-j}z^{-1}$, $j=0,1,2$. Conversely $z$ is a $K$-linear combination of two of them by the invertible two-by-two system. C2 gives $[MK:K]=3^h$, so $[M:D]\ge3^h$. Since $\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)$ embeds in $S_3^h$, the lower and upper orders agree. This proves the full direct-product assertion.

## C4. A uniform bound at the ramifying prime

Over the local multiquadratic base at three, adjoining all these cube roots at once has degree at most $27$, independent of the number of states. Indeed $F_x$ has local squareclass $3$ when $x\equiv1\pmod3$ and $-3$ when $x\equiv2\pmod3$. At a fixed place over three take the local base $K_0=\mathbb Q_3(\sqrt3,\sqrt{-3})$, which contains every completion in question and $\mu_3$. For either quadratic subfield $F=\mathbb Q_3(\sqrt{\pm3})$, the class of $E_x$ belongs to the anti-invariant part of $\mathcal O_F^\times/\mathcal O_F^{\times3}$ under its quadratic involution. The full unit quotient has dimension two for $\sqrt3$ and three for $\sqrt{-3}$: the principal units have two free $\mathbb Z_3$ directions by the local logarithm and one additional torsion direction precisely when $F$ contains $\mu_3$. Indeed the logarithm is a lattice isomorphism on a sufficiently deep congruence subgroup and its finite kernel is the local roots of unity. The prime-to-three residue units contribute nothing. The invariant subspace has dimension exactly one, from $\mathbb Z_3^\times/\mathbb Z_3^{\times3}$. The norm and restriction give this equality since the extension degree two is invertible modulo three. Thus the anti-invariant dimensions are respectively one and two. Injection into $K_0^\times/K_0^{\times3}$ preserves these dimensions, again by the prime-to-three degree. Their sum has dimension at most three, proving the bound by Kummer theory. The same degree bound holds if the actual completion of $K$ is smaller than $K_0$, since the base-change degree is a power of two. At every finite place away from three the Kummer extension over $K$ is already unramified by C2.

In particular arbitrarily large global groups in C3 coexist with a bounded local cubic rank at three. A small local inertia subgroup can have arbitrarily many conjugates in the global group; its size alone gives no bound on $h$.

## C5. Two coherent dyadic embeddings of one entire real orbit field

For a finite or infinite ordinary positive odd orbit, omit any initial state divisible by three. Let $B$ be the subfield of the physical real line generated by all $t_x$ on that orbit. **There is a single embedding of $B$ into $\mathbb Q_2$ taking every $t_x$ to an even $2$-adic integer.** There is also a single embedding into the compositum of the quadratic extensions of $\mathbb Q_2$ taking every $t_x$ to an odd local integer (valuation zero). Both embeddings are fixed for the entire trajectory.

For distinct Pell labels the corresponding *nonnormal cubic fields* inside the physical real line are linearly disjoint. For any $f$ such representatives, their compositum $B'$ has degree at most $3^f$. Over the multiquadratic base $K$ of C2, the three conjugates of $z=E_x^{1/3}$ are cyclically permuted, and $t_x=z+z^{-1}$ has three distinct conjugates there. Hence $K(t_x)=K(z)$ and $[KB':K]=3^f$, forcing equality over $\mathbb Q$ too. Consequently compatible embeddings can be chosen independently once per Pell label. Taking tensor products of the chosen embeddings for each finite set of fields and then their direct limit works also for infinitely many labels.

To choose them, use the common generator $t_F=\lambda^{1/3}+\lambda^{-1/3}$ of C1 for each label. For every $x$ in this field $3\nmid e_x$, because $E_x$ is a noncube. At any prime of $F$ over two, $E_x\equiv1$ since its monic norm-one quadratic has even trace. The residue multiplicative group has order one or three, so $\lambda^{e_x}\equiv1$ implies $\lambda\equiv1$. Hence $\lambda+\lambda^{-1}\in2\mathbb Z$. The cubic for $t_F$ reduces to $T(T+1)^2$ modulo two. Its unique even root is in $\mathbb Q_2$ by Hensel's lemma; its other two roots are odd in an extension of degree at most two. The Dickson recurrence of C1 gives $P_e(0)=0$ modulo two and $P_e(1)$ successively $0,1,1$ according to $e=0,1,2\pmod3$. Therefore all the $t_x=P_{e_x}(t_F)$ have the selected parity simultaneously. This establishes both embeddings.

Define the physical positive algebraic number
$$U_x=\left(\frac{t_x+1}{t_x-1}\right)^3\frac{3x+1}{3x}.$$
It satisfies $U_x>1$ since $t_x>2$, and $U_x\to1$ as $x\to\infty$. The absolute field norm from $B_x$ is one, as the norms of $t_x+1$ and $t_x-1$ are $12x$ and $4(3x+1)$. With $v_2(2)=v_3(3)=1$ its local valuations are
$$v_{2,\mathrm{even}}(U_x)=a,\qquad v_{2,\mathrm{odd}}(U_x)=-a/2,
\qquad v_{3,\mathfrak p}(U_x)=0\ \text{at every ternary place}.$$
For the even root both $t_x\pm1$ are units. For either odd root the factorizations of C1 give $v_2(t_x+1)=1$, $v_2(t_x-1)=1+a/2$, since $t_x\pm2$ are units. At three $v_3(t_x-2)=1/3$ by the shifted Eisenstein polynomial, so $v_3(t_x+1)=1/3$ and $t_x-1$ is a unit. Multiplication along the first $n$ odd departures gives a strictly increasing physical product, and valuations $A_n,-A_n/2,0$ at the indicated fixed places, where $A_n$ is its actual accumulated clock. Thus these properties do not require changing primes or choosing separate embeddings for successively longer windows. The opposing dyadic embeddings are consistent with the product formula. They furnish no upper bound on the heights or total clocks of an integer orbit.

The ordinary height cost is quantitative. For a finite prefix write $A_F=\sum_{i:F_{x_i}=F} a_i$. Under the uniform counting measure on all embeddings of its physical cubic compositum into $\overline{\mathbb Q}_2$, the valuation of $V=\prod_i U_{x_i}$ has exactly the distribution
$$Z=\sum_F A_F Z_F,\qquad \Pr(Z_F=1)=1/3,\qquad \Pr(Z_F=-1/2)=2/3,$$
with independent $Z_F$. This follows from linear disjointness and the coherent parity within each label. It counts embeddings, so a quadratic local factor receives two appearances even when ramified. In particular the dyadic contribution alone gives the absolute logarithmic Weil-height bound
$$h(V)\ \ge\ (\log2)\,\mathbb E\max(0,-Z)
=\tfrac12(\log2)\,\mathbb E|Z|
\ \ge\ \frac{\log2}{2\sqrt6}\sqrt{\sum_F A_F^2}.$$
Indeed $\mathbb E Z=0$, $\mathbb E Z^2=\sum_F A_F^2/2$ and $\mathbb E Z^4\le3(\mathbb E Z^2)^2$ by expanding and using $\mathbb E Z_F^4=3/8$. Hölder gives $\mathbb E|Z|\ge(\mathbb E Z^2)^{3/2}/(\mathbb E Z^4)^{1/2}$. Along any escaping orbit the physical $n^{-1}\log V$ tends to zero by Cesàro averaging since $U_{x_i}\to1$. The true global height still grows at least on the order of $\sqrt n$, from this single rational prime. This is the explicit obstruction to inferring a small global height from the real correction on the fixed positive trajectory.

For one state there is an exact height formula:
$$3h(U_x)=\log\big(x(3x+1)\big)+\log U_x.$$
Indeed the two local factorizations give, now as rational functions at the physical cubic trace,
$$x=(t_x-2)(t_x+1)^2/12,\qquad
U_x=\frac{(t_x+1)(t_x+2)}{(t_x-1)(t_x-2)}.$$
Substitution and expansion give the integer cubic
$$x(3x+1)(U_x-1)^3-9(3x+1)U_x^2-27xU_x=0.$$
Its coefficients are primitive: a common prime would divide the leading coefficient $x(3x+1)$, then both $9(3x+1)$ and $27x$. This is impossible since $3\nmid x$ and $\gcd(x,3x+1)=1$. The norm-one $U_x>1$ cannot be rational, so it generates $B_x$ and this is its primitive minimal polynomial with positive leading coefficient. The two other conjugates are nonreal and have common modulus $U_x^{-1/2}<1$. The Mahler-measure formula for the absolute logarithmic height gives the identity. Thus the archimedean correction tends to one through algebraic numbers of height $(2/3)\log x+O(1)$. The last two theorems in `CubicLift.lean` check the rational-function parameter and the cubic elimination over every characteristic-zero field.
