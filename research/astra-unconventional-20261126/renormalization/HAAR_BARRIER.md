# A positive-measure exceptional set at the last Mersenne gap

**Written theorem with an exact finite replay; no Lean certification.** Reviewed 2026-11-26. This extends [the finite-cover barrier](FINITE_COVER_BARRIER.md). Use the e6 row \(N=2^{11+162m}\), \(z=(128N-271)/81\). The fixed-word grammar starts from either \(2^U N-1,\ U=0,1\) and allows finitely many actual forward or legal reverse shortcut edges, with the script independent of the exponent on the supplied ternary cylinder. The previous fixed-parameter calculation at \(N_*=590\) classified every admissible affine endpoint of slope at most \(A=128/81\) at this ternary parameter and the limiting binary coordinate \(N=0\). This note proves that a positive-measure set has the same classification.

**Statement.** In the ball \(N=590\bmod 3^{385}\) a set of relative Haar measure at least
\[
     1-\frac{2360(128/81)^2}{10000}>0.410
\]
has precisely the eight subcap endpoint forms in the 590 calculation. They give neither a guaranteed subfloor ordinary or triple source nor a strictly smaller canonical query. Thus this particular exceptional set has Hausdorff dimension one in \(\mathbb Z_3\). All of the ball lies in the ternary exponent closure \(2^{11}(1+3^5\mathbb Z_3)\). These conclusions concern profinite inputs; the estimate gives no positive ordinary exponent in the exceptional set.

**The denominator depth of a prefix.** Cancel inverse–forward pairs. After \(h\) forward steps on the Mersenne form, the inverse script starts at signed center \(-1\), with actual affine coefficient \(2^U(3/2)^h\). At any subsequent prefix with \(o\) reverse odd letters and \(E\) reverse even letters write \(d=o-h\). Its form is \(\alpha N-b\) with
\[
        \alpha=2^{E+U}(2/3)^d,\qquad b\ge1 .
\]
If it is ternary-integral at a unit \(N\) and \(d>0\), the reduced denominator of \(b\) is exactly \(3^d\). Depth starts at \(-h\), stays constant on reverse even letters and increases by one on reverse odd letters. Every word ending at depth greater than \(D\) therefore has a prefix at depth exactly \(D\).

**Uniform counting at 590.** At any depth \(d>0\) where a prefix is legal at \(590\), the integer \(x=590\alpha-b\) is positive, without a bound on \(\alpha\). For completeness, follow \(-b\) forward until \(d\) odd departures have cleared its denominator. The time \(t\) satisfies \(t\le d+E\). The integer \(x\) shares these \(t\) branches since \(v_2(x+b)\ge E+d\). At the clearing time the resulting integer is \(590\,2^{E'+U}-y\), with \(E'=E-(t-d)\ge0\). Here \(y\) follows the reflected negative-integer dynamics to 1 with exactly \(E'\) even steps. Since every odd reflected step is nondecreasing above 1, \(1\le y\le2^{E'}\) and so the clearing endpoint is at least 589. Nonpositive integers cannot reach that interval. It follows that \(1\le x\le590\alpha\). At fixed \(d,E,U\), distinct affine forms have distinct \(x\), so there are at most \(590\alpha\) legal forms, even when arbitrarily long words realize a form.

Set \(R=10000\) and \(D=385\). Every positive integer through \(590R=5900000\) actually reaches 1 within 384 shortcut clocks. A direct exhaustive replay in [check_haar_bound.cpp](check_haar_bound.cpp) computes this bound with largest first-hit time attained at 5649499 and maximum height 1206246808304. Each input is followed until it reaches a previously certified smaller input or 1, then its exact chain length is propagated backwards. The guard on the maximum nonmemoized path and the overflow guard terminate the replay with failure if triggered. Consequently at 590 every legal depth-\(D\) prefix has \(\alpha>R\). Otherwise its clearing time \(t\ge D\) would find a site \(\ge589\) after the orbit of \(x\le590R\) already reached the two-cycle.

**Random continuation out of the ball.** Select \(N\) with Haar conditional law on \(N=590\bmod3^D\). Every prefix to depth \(D\) that is legal at any point of this ball is legal at 590: all ternary guards through this prefix are equivalent to the integrality of the endpoint, and all previous forms have depth at most \(D\). Indeed a legal forward traversal from the integral inverse endpoint transports all intermediate values back to the original target in \(\mathbb Z_3\); parities are controlled by the unchanged binary coordinate. Conversely every legal prefix at 590 is legal throughout the ball. For any one such prefix, \(\alpha N-b\) has the full Haar distribution on \(\mathbb Z_3\), since \(v_3(\alpha)=-D\).

For genuine reverse shortcut scripts over a ternary Haar target, the supremum \(B\) of their forward slopes satisfies \(\Pr[B\ge t]\le t^{-2}\) for \(t>1\). To verify the bound with strict \(>\), take the prefix-free first-crossing scripts. A legal script with \(o\) odd letters among \(\ell\) has a target cylinder of Haar measure \(3^{-o}\); this follows by multiplying its one affine congruence by \(2^\ell\), or by iterating the inverse guards. Under independent letters of probabilities \(3/4\) on odd and \(1/4\) on even, its word mass equals \(C^2 3^{-o}\) for \(C=3^o/2^\ell\). Prefix-freeness bounds the sum of those word masses by one. The non-strict bound follows from thresholds increasing to \(t\). This is the upper half of OP-B1 in [the pinned PR 201 source](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/3eb251fae9082fef33c047079a511d1c3f4c5969/research/unconventional-20261126/operator/TERNARY_BARRIER.md); the elementary argument is reproduced here.

A continuation from a depth-\(D\) prefix of slope \(\alpha\) ending at any slope \(\le A\) requires a reverse script of forward slope at least \(\alpha/A\). The conditional chance for each distinct prefix form is at most \(A^2/\alpha^2\), irrespective of any dependence between different forms. Summation over prefixes, then over \(E,U\), yields
\[
 \Pr[\text{an endpoint of slope }\le A\text{ descends through depth }D]
 \ \le\ \sum_{U=0}^1\ 
     \sum_{E:\,\alpha_{E,U}>R}\frac{590 A^2}{\alpha_{E,U}}
 \ \le\ \frac{2360A^2}{R}.
\]
For each \(U\) the \(\alpha_{E,U}=2^{E+U}(2/3)^D\) double as \(E\) increases; the geometric tail is less than \(2/R\). The values at depth below \(D\) have exactly the same integrality and ternary residue as at 590, since their variation across the ball is divisible by 3. Their binary data are unchanged. The eight previously certified subcap forms all have short realizations in the ball. The complement of the event in the display therefore has exactly that list. The measure lower bound follows, and a subset of positive one-dimensional Haar measure has Hausdorff dimension one for the standard 3-adic metric.

The parameter map \(m\mapsto2^{11}(2^{162})^m\) is a bijection from \(\mathbb Z_3\) onto this unit coset and scales congruence depth by five, by \(v_3(2^{162u}-1)=5+v_3(u)\). Thus the same positive relative mass statement holds for the ternary exponent parameter. The proposed proof that an unbounded digit selector works because its only nonterminating inputs are finitely many explicitly controlled rational points is incompatible with this fixed-word grammar. An unbounded selector can still terminate on every ordinary integer while failing on a positive-measure closed set of nonordinary ternary inputs. Ordinary integrality, input-dependent peak lengths and extra physical label relations are still available beyond this claim.


The same proof applies in every ball with depth D at least 385: the finite first-hit bound stays 384, while the geometric-tail estimate is independent of D. Thus 590 is an accumulation point of a set of controls of Hausdorff dimension one at every such precision. This statement does not assert an ordinary integer exponent in that set.
