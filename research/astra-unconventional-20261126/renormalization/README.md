## Exact cost, at an unbounded visitor clock

The first-arrival theorem computes the exact minimum number of visitor odds as a function of the original all-odd ascent length $r$. A positive source $x=2^ra-1$ rises to $y=3^ra-1$. Count a smaller visitor only when it enters $y$ on the other incoming edge. Every such visitor spends at least $r+K(r)$ odds. Here $L(r)$ is the minimum clock for $r$ odds into the signed target $-2$, and $K(r)$ is least with $3^k>2^{k+L(r)+1-r}$. Each $r$ attains its bound on infinitely many positive cofactors $a$, including ordinary convergent sources whose first intersection in native-clock order occurs at $y$. At a supplied $a$ one of finitely many explicit residues modulo $3^{K(r)}$ is necessary and sufficient for equality; their exact frequency is below $2^{-K(r)}$. The excess $K(r)-r$ tends to infinity. For instance $r=11$ costs at least 34 visitor odds. At the supplied cofactor $a=1$ the complete finite trajectories of all $1\le w<2047$ only meet the ascent at native clock zero or miss the entire ascent. The two native-clock-zero visitors include 1819, which reaches 2047 after two odds. ## Testing upper-grade priority on every grade-two parent

All canonical parents whose triple hits one with two odd steps are
$$
          w_s=(32\cdot4^s-5)/27=1+32v,\quad
          27v+1=4^s,\quad s=18+27m,\quad m\ge0 .
$$
Indeed the forced first two upper steps give $27w+5=2^{e+2}$. The congruence $w=17\bmod48$ is $2^{e+2}=464\bmod1296$. For $e\ge2$ this is equivalently $2^{e-2}=29\bmod81$, which holds precisely at $e=39\bmod54$ by the order of 2 modulo 81; $e<2$ is impossible by size. Set $e=2s+3$. The upper hit is at clock $2s+5$. Five lower steps reach $4^s+1$, then each pair of steps sends $4d+1$ to $3d+1$, finishing at $3^s+1$ at that same clock. [Previously proved two-odd characterization and descent](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/156652e03fe7a78f9cd4442039779cc622fdf3de/research/ayh-resource-reset-20261124/closing-proposal/RANK_TWO_DESCENT.md)

Two universal alternatives for a grade-first search have concrete countercontrols. The supplied hard source here is $z=3233$, $j=11$. It and all displayed comparison examples converge.

* **Every grade-two upper deadline.** Every point at every clock on the actual lower trajectory of 3233 is $\le638468$: at clock 107 it reaches one and then stays in the two-cycle. Throughout $0\le t\le2s+5$, the lower orbit from $w_s$ is $\ge3^s+1\ge387420490$. During the first five clocks its values are $1+32v,2+48v,1+24v,2+36v,1+18v,2+27v$. The inequality $3\cdot3^s+2\le2\cdot4^s$ gives $3^s\le18v$ for $s\ge2$. During the next $s$ pairs the lower values at the pair boundaries decrease from $4^s+1$ to $3^s+1$, with a larger odd-step image inside each pair. Thus allowing an arbitrarily large new parent while using its supplied full upper clock still misses this one actual orbit.
* **Every synchronized compensated postpeak clock.** At a hit at one at clock $h$ with $p$ odd steps, put $I=2(r+p)-h$ for initial exponent $r$. Two extra cycle clocks preserve $I$. A same-clock common endpoint with the augmented grades matched forces equal $I$ after continuing both trajectories to a common hit at one. For $3^{11}-1$ the hit $(h,p)=(89,45)$ gives $I=23$. If $3^s+1$ matches, its actual hit satisfies $2^h\le4^p(3^s+1)$, by $2T(n)\le4^{n\bmod2}n$. Therefore $4^s\le2^{23}(3^s+1)$. As $4^{60}>2^{23}(3^{60}+1)$ and the strict inequality persists for larger $s$, only $s=18,45$ survive. Their hits $(127,62)$ and $(303,146)$ give charges 33 and 79. Neither can match. Ordering by upper odd count before integer height is a legitimate well-order. The rank-two descent quoted above supplies a smaller unconstrained canonical defect after a *height* contraction. It supplies no bound of two on the replacement upper odd count. A transfer to some larger rank-two parent alone would therefore leave a possible least rank-two defect unresolved. An available sufficient return to the original height floor would instead be a merger of the original bad arm to a rank-two lower arm with $2^{40}(3^s+1)<z$, or some alternate ordinary contraction of the required size. Neither all-clock work lower bounds nor varying-cofactor sharp examples select such an $s$ for a supplied $z$. These controls exclude the two stated comparison rules, including on convergent data; other asynchronous comparisons remain possible.
