# Relative affine transports and the complete lattice of moment eigenvectors

Let $x_i=c_i/d$ be the odd phases of a positive rational cycle in lowest terms, where $d$ is coprime to six and $c_i$ is odd. The physical exponents satisfy $3c_i+d=2^{a_i}c_{i+1}$. Suppose there are $p$ phases and $h=\sum_i a_i$. In particular $2^h>3^p$, since the update has positive additive term. The statements below also identify which conclusions depend on having a cycle.

## The primitive eigenline lattice at every degree

Set $e=(1,0)^T$, $f_i=(c_i,d)^T$ and

$$
E_i=\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\0&2^{a_i}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B_i=\begin{pmatrix}1&c_i\\0&d\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The two columns obey $E_i e=3e$ and $E_i f_i=2^{a_i}f_{i+1}$, giving the exact edge factorization
$$E_i B_i=B_{i+1}\operatorname{diag}(3,2^{a_i}).$$
For the full lap based at $i$, $e$ and $f_i$ are therefore eigenvectors with eigenvalues $3^p$ and $2^h$.

For any $m\ge1$, use the degree-$m$ symmetric power over $\mathbb Z$ with its monomial basis $X^{m-j}Y^j$, $0\le j\le m$. The $m+1$ rational eigenspaces of the lap have distinct eigenvalues $3^{p(m-j)}2^{hj}$. Their respective **primitive integer generators** are the forms
$$X^{m-j}(c_iX+dY)^j.$$
Indeed the two linear factors are primitive and their product is primitive by Gauss's lemma. Their coefficient columns are exactly $\operatorname{Sym}^m B_i$. Its full Smith diagonal is
$$1,d,d^2,\ldots,d^m.$$
To see this directly, $B_i=\operatorname{diag}(1,d)\begin{pmatrix}1&c_i\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. The second factor is unimodular over the integers, as is its action on integral forms (substitute the integral inverse). The first factor acts diagonally by $d^j$. These entries already divide their successors. Consequently, if $\mathcal E_{m,i}$ is the sum of the full primitive integer eigenlines, then
$$
\operatorname{Sym}^m(\mathbb Z^2)/\mathcal E_{m,i}
  \cong \bigoplus_{j=1}^m \mathbb Z/d^j\mathbb Z,\qquad
[\operatorname{Sym}^m(\mathbb Z^2):\mathcal E_{m,i}]
  =d^{m(m+1)/2}.
$$

The eigenline choices are canonical up to sign and order. The primitive bases are simultaneously transported at every edge by the symmetric powers of the diagonal above. For an ordinary cycle ($d=1$), they form a global compatible unimodular frame in **every rank**. The binomial moment equations $(2^{a_i}x_{i+1})^m=(3x_i+1)^m$ occur inside this frame. Treating the distinct differences $2^{jh}-3^{jp}$ appearing among the eigenvalue gaps as independent mandatory divisors of the frame index would give an incorrect index already at $d=1$. This computes the full lattice for this family of moment constructions; other representations or comparisons involving different integer orbits require separate arguments.

The Lean kernel proves the exact homogeneous transport and the full degree-bounded integer coefficient image of $P\mapsto P(c_i+dX)$, namely coordinatewise divisibility by $d^j$. The identification of the primitive eigenspaces with these columns uses the written Gauss-lemma argument above. [MomentTransport.lean](MomentTransport.lean)

At the round-thirteen 41-phase control the same formula gives at degree four the four nontrivial invariant factors $d,d^2,d^3,d^4$ with $d=44835377399$, and index $d^{10}$. At the ordinary fixed odd phase $x=1$, it gives index one in every degree.

## The relative transport group at one terminal

This statement needs no cycle. Fix a positive odd ternary-unit numerator $y$ on the fixed denominator lattice. A true inverse path of odd depth $k$ and total shortcut clock $E$ has forward homogeneous matrix
$$M_C=\begin{pmatrix}3^k&C\\0&2^E\end{pmatrix}.$$
Here $2^E y=3^k z+dC$ for the odd numerator $z$ at its source, and the action on actual rational values in the affine chart is $s\mapsto(3^k s+C)/2^E$.

Consider the subgroup of the rational affine group generated by $M_{C_1}^{-1}M_{C_2}$ over all pairs of physical paths having the same terminal and the same $(k,E)$ within that pair. Each generator is a translation by
$$
(C_2-C_1)/3^k=(z_1-z_2)/d.
$$
Since $3^k(z_1-z_2)=d(C_2-C_1)$ and $3\nmid d$, the quotient is integral. Both numerators and $d$ are odd, so it is even.

This subgroup is **exactly the integer translations by $2\mathbb Z$** at every such terminal. To realize the generator, take $E\ge8$ with $2^Ey>85d$ and $2^Ey\equiv31d\pmod{27}$. Such clocks occur in an infinite progression: 2 has order 18 modulo 27 (its ninth power is $-1$ and its sixth is $10$), and both sides are units. The two physical reversed words
$$(E-4,3,1),\qquad(E-6,4,2)$$
have carries $16+3\cdot2+9=31$ and $64+3\cdot4+9=85$. Their difference is $54=2\cdot3^3$. The whole equations ensure integrality at each division as in Theorem 1. Each resulting source is positive by $2^Ey>85d$. Every intervening site is positive by running the positive affine edge equations forward from that source. Oddness follows at each integral edge. These are genuine paths with exactly the required difference between their sources.

## Adjoining a native lap

On the cycle above, based at any phase ($y=c_i$ is automatically a ternary unit), include also the full native lap and its inverse as abstract affine transformations. Write $R=\mathbb Z[1/6]$ and $\lambda=3^p/2^h$. The generated group in the rational affine chart is exactly
$$\{s\longmapsto \lambda^j s+t:\ j\in\mathbb Z,\ t\in R\}.$$

Indeed the lap itself is $s\mapsto\lambda s+C/2^h$ with integer $C$, so the displayed group contains all generators. Conjugating translation by 2 by any signed lap power gives translation by $2\lambda^j$. Their additive span is $2\mathbb Z[\lambda,\lambda^{-1}]$. Bezout for the coprime integers $3^p,2^h$ shows $1/2^h\in\mathbb Z[\lambda]$ and $1/3^p\in\mathbb Z[\lambda^{-1}]$. Hence this span is $2R=R$. Removing $C/2^h$ yields the pure dilation, proving equality.

The group depends on the period pair but loses the reduced denominator. Its orbit **of the marked phase** is $x_i+R$, since the native lap fixes $x_i$ and its translation part belongs to $R$. The order of this coset in $\mathbb Q/R$ is precisely $d$. Thus retaining the marked phase recovers the original denominator; all unmarked relative translations and all signed lap conjugates are already available on both the ordinary and reduced rational lattices. Composition in the rational affine group omits the stepwise ternary integrality and positivity conditions. For example the group orbit of the ordinary phase 1 contains zero while no positive predecessor path from 1 has source zero.
