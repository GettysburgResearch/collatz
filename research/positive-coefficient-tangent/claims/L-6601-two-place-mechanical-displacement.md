# L-6601 — one mechanical displacement controls both the real remainder and the 2-adic root

**Claim ID:** `L-6601`  
**Status:** **PROPOSED**  
**Authoring agent:** `gpt56-positive-tangent-01`  
**Created:** 2026-07-31  
**Dependencies:** elementary parity-word affine algebra  
**Scope:** pairs of equal-length, equal-weight parity words; specialized below to first crossings and their mechanical extremizer  

## 1. Odd-position formula

Let `u` be a binary parity word of length `j` and weight `q`. Write its odd positions, indexed from zero, as

\[
0\le d_1(u)<d_2(u)<\cdots<d_q(u)<j.
\]

Its exact affine numerator is

\[
\boxed{
A_u=
\sum_{i=1}^{q}3^{q-i}2^{d_i(u)}.}
\tag{1}
\]

Thus

\[
T_u^j(x)={3^q x+A_u\over2^j}.
\]

The least nonnegative parity-cylinder representative `r(u) in [0,2^j)` is

\[
\boxed{
r(u)
\equiv
-\sum_{i=1}^{q}2^{d_i(u)}3^{-i}
\pmod{2^j}.}
\tag{2}
\]

Here every inverse power of three is taken modulo `2^j`.

## 2. Dominated word versus mechanical word

Let `v` be a first-crossing word and let `w` be its upper mechanical extremizer from `T-6605`. Prefix dominance implies

\[
d_i(v)\le d_i(w)
\qquad(1\le i\le q).
\tag{3}
\]

Put

\[
h_i=d_i(w)-d_i(v)\ge0.
\]

The total displacement is

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{q}h_i=\mathcal I(v),
\]

the adjacent-swap distance of `T-6605`.

Define the exact positive displacement integer

\[
\boxed{
\Delta A(v)
=A_w-A_v
=
\sum_{i=1}^{q}
3^{q-i}2^{d_i(v)}(2^{h_i}-1).}
\tag{4}
\]

If `v!=w`, then `Delta A(v)>0`.

## 3. Real and 2-adic faces of the same integer

Because the two words have the same denominator and multiplier,

\[
\boxed{
E(w)-E(v)={\Delta A(v)\over2^j}.}
\tag{5}
\]

On the other hand, subtracting `(2)` gives

\[
\boxed{
r(v)-r(w)
\equiv
3^{-q}\Delta A(v)
\pmod{2^j}.}
\tag{6}
\]

Thus the integer `Delta A(v)` is simultaneously:

```text
the complete Archimedean loss from leaving the mechanical extremizer;
and
the complete finite-place increment of the canonical initial residue.
```

This is an exact two-place coupling, not an analogy.

## 4. Earliest displaced odd position

Assume `v!=w`, and let `i_*` be the unique index for which

\[
d_*=d_{i_*}(v)
\]

is minimal among all `d_i(v)` with `h_i>0`.

In `(4)`, the `i_*` term has 2-adic valuation exactly `d_*`, because `2^(h_i*)-1` and the power of three are odd. Every other nonzero term has strictly larger 2-adic valuation. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\nu_2(\Delta A(v))=d_*.}
\tag{7}
\]

Since multiplication by `3^{-q}` is a 2-adic unit, `(6)` yields

\[
\boxed{
\nu_2\bigl(r(v)-r(w)\bigr)=d_*.}
\tag{8}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{|r(v)-r(w)|\ge2^{d_*}.}
\tag{9}
\]

## 5. Exact wrap law

Let

\[
s(v)=\bigl[3^{-q}\Delta A(v)\bigr]_{2^j}
\qquad(1\le s(v)<2^j)
\tag{10}
\]

be the canonical positive residue of the displacement. Then

\[
\boxed{
r(v)=[r(w)+s(v)]_{2^j}.}
\tag{11}
\]

There are exactly two ordinary cases:

### No wrap

If

\[
r(w)+s(v)<2^j,
\]

then

\[
r(v)=r(w)+s(v).
\tag{12}
\]

### Wrap

If

\[
r(w)+s(v)\ge2^j,
\]

then

\[
r(v)=r(w)+s(v)-2^j.
\tag{13}
\]

A small canonical residue far from the mechanical word can therefore arise only through an explicit modulus wrap.

## 6. Late-edit lower bound

Equation `(9)` gives the immediate fork

\[
\boxed{
\max\{r(v),r(w)\}\ge2^{d_*}.}
\tag{14}
\]

More sharply, if

\[
r(w)<2^{d_*},
\tag{15}
\]

then the negative difference alternative is impossible, and

\[
\boxed{
r(v)\ge r(w)+2^{d_*}>2^{d_*}.}
\tag{16}
\]

At a no-descent first crossing, `T-6604` gives the elementary upper bound

\[
r(v)<{j\over\lambda},
\qquad
\lambda=j\log2-q\log3.
\tag{17}
\]

Consequently, if

\[
2^{d_*}\ge{j\over\lambda}
\tag{18}
\]

and `r(w)<2^{d_*}`, then `v` cannot be a canonical target failure.

Thus every surviving late-edit candidate satisfying `(18)` must use the wrap branch `(13)` from a mechanical residue already at least `2^{d_*}`.

## 7. Combined frontier

`T-6605` controls the total displacement `sum h_i` from the real remainder margin.

`L-6601` controls the earliest displaced position `d_*` and the exact finite-place wrap.

`T-6606` controls the repeated-factor length generated by the total displacement.

Together, a canonical delayed-crossing target failure must negotiate all three constraints:

```text
few shifts        -> long repeated factor -> T-6604 gap obstruction;
late first shift  -> exponentially large root unless a 2^j wrap occurs;
early many shifts -> large mechanical remainder loss.
```

The remaining unresolved family consists of words whose edits are numerous or early enough to avoid `(18)`, while their exact displacement residue wraps so as to keep the canonical root below the no-descent ceiling.

## 8. Gap audit

- The wrap branch can genuinely make `r(v)` small; it is not excluded here.
- Equation `(14)` bounds the maximum of the two canonical roots, not always `r(v)` alone.
- The theorem does not provide a distribution law for `s(v)` modulo `2^j`.
- It does supply the exact two-place integer whose modular behavior must now be controlled.
- No universal canonical descent theorem or proof of Collatz is claimed.
