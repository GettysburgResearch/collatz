## 1. Statement

Consider `N>=1` consecutive corrected stages

\[
m=m_0,m_0+1,\ldots,m_0+N-1,
\qquad m_0\ge12,
\tag{1}
\]

of one ordinary trajectory.  Let

\[
W_{m_0},W_{m_0+1},\ldots,W_{m_0+N}
\in\mathbb Z_{>0}
\tag{2}
\]

be all `N+1` scaled ordinary boundary tails touched by those stages, and put

\[
\mathcal S_N
=\left\{p\text{ prime}:
p\mid\prod_{j=0}^{N}W_{m_0+j}\right\},
\qquad
s_N=|\mathcal S_N|.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus `s_N` counts all distinct ordinary boundary primes, including `2` and
`3` if they actually divide a boundary word.  Define

\[
\boxed{C=1542^{771}=(6\cdot257)^{3\cdot257}.}
\tag{4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
N\le
4^{256}\left\lfloor
\exp\!\left(C(2s_N+2)\right)
\right\rfloor
\le
4^{256}\exp\!\left(C(2s_N+2)\right).
}
\tag{5}
\]

Consequently, with `log` denoting the natural logarithm,

\[
\boxed{
s_N\ge
\max\left\{
0,
\left\lceil
{C^{-1}\log(N/4^{256})-2\over2}
\right\rceil
\right\}.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Equivalently, for every integer `Q>=0`,

\[
N>4^{256}\exp\!\left(C(2Q+2)\right)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
s_N\ge Q+1.
\tag{7}
\]

The constant is enormous, so (6) is structural rather than numerically useful.
It is nevertheless an effective strengthening of the qualitative fresh-prime
necessity in `PR #3/T-0032`.

## 2. The exact imported bound

For an `n`-term equation

\[
a_1x_1+\cdots+a_nx_n=1
\tag{8}
\]

in a subgroup `Gamma` of `(K^*)^n` of rank `r`, the quantitative form of the
Evertse--Schlickewei--Schmidt theorem bounds the number of nondegenerate
solutions by

\[
\boxed{
A(n,r)=\exp\!\left((6n)^{3n}(r+1)\right).
}
\tag{9}
\]

This is Theorem 1.1, equation (1.2), of J.-H. Evertse, H. P. Schlickewei,
and W. M. Schmidt, *Linear Equations in Variables Which Lie in a
Multiplicative Group*, Annals of Mathematics 155 (2002), 807--836.  The
primary source was checked against its TeX source.  At the audited PR-13 head,
`LIT-KTHM-0043` correctly imports finiteness and says that the source gives an
explicit bound, but does not itself display (9).  Thus (9), rather than an
unrecorded guessed constant, is the extra external input in this claim.

## 3. The coarse product rank, recorded correctly

The immediate group used in `PR #3/T-0032` is

\[
G_N=\langle-1,2,3,\mathcal S_N\rangle
\subset\mathbb Q^*.
\tag{10}
\]

If

\[
d_N=|\mathcal S_N\cup\{2,3\}|,
\tag{11}
\]

then unique factorization in `Q^*` gives `rank(G_N)=d_N`; the generator `-1`
is torsion.  The safe tuple group

\[
\Gamma_N=G_N^{257}\subset(\mathbb Q^*)^{257}
\tag{12}
\]

therefore has rank

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}\Gamma_N=257d_N,
}
\tag{13}
\]

not `d_N`.  In particular it is at most `257(s_N+2)`.  This full direct-product
rank is valid but wasteful.  The word-by-word proof below exploits the
correlated exponent vectors and replaces it by a subgroup generated by at
most `2s_N+1` elements.

## 4. The normalized stage solutions

For the source word

\[
w_m=(i_{m,0},\ldots,i_{m,255})\in\{0,1,2,3\}^{256},
\tag{14}
\]

`PR #3/L-0031` gives

\[
2^{\mathcal E_m}W_{m+1}
=3^{\mathcal A_m}W_m
+\sum_{k=0}^{255}
2^{U_{m,k}+\alpha_{i_{m,k}}}
3^{V_{m,k}+\beta_{i_{m,k}}}.
\tag{15}
\]

Divide by the positive left side and set

\[
x_{m,*}
={3^{\mathcal A_m}W_m
\over2^{\mathcal E_m}W_{m+1}},
\tag{16}
\]

\[
x_{m,k}
={2^{U_{m,k}+\alpha_{i_{m,k}}}
3^{V_{m,k}+\beta_{i_{m,k}}}
\over2^{\mathcal E_m}W_{m+1}}
\qquad(0\le k<256).
\tag{17}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{x_{m,*}+\sum_{k=0}^{255}x_{m,k}=1.}
\tag{18}
\]

All coordinates are positive, so no nonempty subsum can vanish.  Every
solution in (18) is nondegenerate in the exact sense required by (9).

Both endpoints of every stage are covered by (2): stage `m` uses `W_m` and
`W_(m+1)`.  In particular, retaining the final endpoint `W_(m_0+N)` is
essential for putting every denominator in one common group.

## 5. Affine scale factorization for one fixed word

Fix one word

\[
w=(i_0,\ldots,i_{255}).
\tag{19}
\]

Put

\[
D_m=2^{m-8},
\qquad
t_{m,j}=D_m(256+j).
\tag{20}
\]

Every exponent in (15) is affine in the same single integer `D_m`.  More
precisely,

\[
\mathcal A_m=aD_m+a_0,
\qquad
(a,a_0)=(687232,1792),
\tag{21}
\]

\[
\mathcal E_m=eD_m+e_0,
\qquad
(e,e_0)=(1082752,2816),
\tag{22}
\]

and for `0<=k<256`,

\[
U_{m,k}=u_kD_m+u_{k,0},
\tag{23}
\]

\[
u_k=11\left(256k+{k(k+1)\over2}\right),
\qquad
u_{k,0}=11k,
\tag{24}
\]

\[
V_{m,k}=v_kD_m+v_{k,0},
\tag{25}
\]

\[
v_k=7\left(
256(255-k)+{255\cdot256-k(k+1)\over2}
\right),
\qquad
v_{k,0}=7(255-k).
\tag{26}
\]

All displayed coefficients are integers.  Define one mixed `2`--`3` tuple

\[
g=(g_*,g_0,\ldots,g_{255})\in(\mathbb Q^*)^{257}
\tag{27}
\]

by

\[
g_*=2^{-e}3^a,
\qquad
g_k=2^{u_k-e}3^{v_k}.
\tag{28}
\]

The same exponent `D_m` drives both the binary and ternary scale parts, so
`g^(D_m)` is one group direction, not two.

For the fixed word, put

\[
c_{w,*}=2^{-e_0}3^{a_0},
\tag{29}
\]

\[
c_{w,k}
=2^{u_{k,0}+\alpha_{i_k}-e_0}
3^{v_{k,0}+\beta_{i_k}}.
\tag{30}
\]

These are fixed nonzero rational coefficients.  For every
`p in mathcal S_N`, define the tuple directions

\[
L_p=(p,1,\ldots,1),
\qquad
R_p=(p^{-1},p^{-1},\ldots,p^{-1}).
\tag{31}
\]

Here `L_p` records the numerator `W_m`, which occurs only in the star
coordinate, while `R_p` records the denominator `W_(m+1)`, which occurs in
all 257 coordinates.  If `nu_p` is the ordinary prime valuation, define

\[
y_m
=g^{D_m}
\prod_{p\in\mathcal S_N}
L_p^{\nu_p(W_m)}R_p^{\nu_p(W_{m+1})}.
\tag{32}
\]

Coordinate by coordinate, equations (16)--(17) and (21)--(30) say

\[
x_{m,j}=c_{w,j}y_{m,j}
\qquad(j=*,0,\ldots,255).
\tag{33}
\]

Thus every occurrence of the fixed word gives a solution of the one fixed
coefficient equation

\[
c_{w,*}y_*+\sum_{k=0}^{255}c_{w,k}y_k=1
\tag{34}
\]

inside the subgroup

\[
\Lambda_N
=\langle g,\ L_p,R_p:p\in\mathcal S_N\rangle
\subset(\mathbb Q^*)^{257}.
\tag{35}
\]

There are `1+2s_N` displayed generators, hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}\Lambda_N\le2s_N+1.
}
\tag{36}
\]

No independence assertion among these generators is needed; an upper bound on
the actual rank is sufficient because `A(n,r)` is increasing in `r`.

## 6. Distinctness and counting

For a fixed word, the first two toll coordinates satisfy

\[
{x_{m,0}\over x_{m,1}}
=2^{\alpha_{i_0}-\alpha_{i_1}-11(t_{m,1}+1)}
3^{\beta_{i_0}-\beta_{i_1}+7(t_{m,1}+1)},
\tag{37}
\]

where

\[
t_{m,1}=2^m+2^{m-8}.
\tag{38}
\]

Multiplication by the fixed coefficients in (33) is coordinatewise
injective.  Multiplicative independence of `2` and `3` and strict increase of
(38) therefore show that different scales carrying the same word produce
different `y_m` solutions of (34).

Let `N_w` be the number of occurrences of `w` among the first `N` stages.
Applying (9) with `n=257` and (36) gives

\[
N_w
\le A(257,\operatorname{rank}\Lambda_N)
\le\exp\!\left(C(2s_N+2)\right),
\qquad
N_w\le\left\lfloor
\exp\!\left(C(2s_N+2)\right)
\right\rfloor.
\tag{39}
\]

There are `4^256` possible words, so summing (39) proves (5).  Taking
logarithms gives

\[
s_N\ge
{C^{-1}\log(N/4^{256})-2\over2}.
\tag{40}
\]

Since `s_N` is a nonnegative integer, applying the ceiling and truncating a
negative small-`N` lower bound at zero proves (6). **QED**

## 7. Coarse all-word comparison

Equation (18) itself has coefficient one for every word.  In fact, stages at
different scales cannot give the same ordered `x` tuple even when their words
differ.  Indeed

\[
\alpha_i\in\{0,1,2,3\},
\qquad
\alpha_{i_0}-\alpha_{i_1}\in[-3,3].
\tag{41}
\]

Equality of two ratios (37) would force

\[
11(t_{m,1}-t_{m',1})\in[-6,6],
\tag{42}
\]

and hence `m=m'`.  Applying ESS directly to all stages in the coarse full
product `Gamma_N` is therefore valid.  It gives, for example,

\[
N\le
\exp\!\left(C\{257(s_N+2)+1\}\right).
\tag{43}
\]

This comparison is much weaker than (5), but it serves two audits: the source
equation really is word-independent, and anyone using `G_N^257` must retain
the rank multiplier `257`.  The sharper result requires freezing a word so
its constant exponent vector can move into the coefficients of (34).

## What this advances

- `PR #3/T-0032` proves only that infinitely many primes must eventually
  appear.  Equations (5)--(7) give an explicit finite-prefix tradeoff.
- The proof first records the safe product rank correctly as `257d_N`, then
  uses the special endpoint and scale geometry to reduce the relevant
  fixed-word rank to at most `2s_N+1`.
- The binary and ternary scale vectors cost only one rank direction because
  both are driven by the same integer `D_m`.
- The final boundary `W_(m_0+N)` is included.  Omitting it would fail to place
  every denominator in the common group.

## Gap and scope audit

- The lower bound is extraordinarily weak because `C=1542^771`; it is not a
  feasible stage-by-stage search estimate.
- The theorem counts distinct primes across a finite segment.  It does not say
  which stage introduces a prime, how large that prime is, or how it is routed
  through a cap stitch.
- This is conditional on an ordinary corrected-stage trajectory already
  existing.  No finite initialization, infinite grammar, or Collatz
  counterexample is constructed.
- The claim uses the external ESS theorem as a black box; it does not reprove
  the Subspace Theorem.

## Remaining uncertainty

Can the two endpoint directions per prime be reduced further by exploiting
the recurrence relation between `W_m` and `W_(m+1)` across repeated-word
occurrences?  Any such rank reduction would improve the coefficient of `s_N`
in the exponent, although the dominant factor `1542^771` from the general ESS
theorem would remain.
