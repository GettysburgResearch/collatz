# Relative gaps between the original sources of a late ordinary collision

Use positive odd starts and the simultaneous odd and native clocks. At odd depth $n$ write a row $R=(y,s)$ for the actual sources $x\le X$ with $U^nx=y$ and native total $s$. Keep the finite test $U^j y\ne y$ for $1\le j\le n$. On each retained row the visit to $y$ is the first one. Put $p=y2^s/3^n$ and mark each original input by the real number $u=x/p$. For $q=2,3$ give every ordered tuple of $q$ inputs of the row weight $p^{-q}$. Define the normalizers and constants by
\[
 L_2=\sqrt{\log X},\quad c_2=(2\pi\log(4/3))^{-1/2},\qquad
 L_3=\log\min\{n,\log X\},\quad c_3=(4\pi\sqrt3)^{-1}.
\]
For pairs take $n,X\to\infty$ with $n\ge300\log X$. For triples take any independent cofinal $n,X$.

**Source-qualified result.** Divide the resulting measure on $(u_1,\ldots,u_q)$ by $L_q$. It converges weakly to
\[
                   c_q\sum_{C\ {\rm escaping}}H_C^q\,
                                  \eta_C^{\otimes q}.                  \tag{1}
\]
Here $H_C$ is the original positive escaping charge, and $\eta_C$ is the law of the bilateral product of $3z/(3z+1)$ on the canonical path of the component. It is an atomless probability on a fixed compact subinterval of $(0,1]$. The $q$ coordinates have independent marks within each summand. One may require distinct original sources. Formula (1) holds for the full first-arrival array as well, since all the rows removed by the finite test have eventually periodic targets and contribute zero limiting mass in the source spectrum.

There is an individually prescribed digit version on the retained rows. Fix any $M<\infty$. In row $R$ and coordinate $i$, choose arbitrary $Q_{R,i}=3^{m_{R,i}}\le(1+\lfloor\log_2 p\rfloor)^M$ and any residue $j_{R,i}\bmod Q_{R,i}$. Require $x_i=j_{R,i}\bmod Q_{R,i}$ and multiply its weight by $Q_{R,i}$. These choices may vary with $n,X,R$, and all coordinates can use the same class. The resulting positively weighted measures still satisfy (1) including the distinct-source option. The proof uses [the physical extra-trit transfer](source_suffix_law.md), the [pinned real-mark bridge](../inverse/pinned_root_bridge.md), the [slow-source spectrum](../mass/SLOW_SOURCE_CHARGE_SPECTRUM.md), and the corrected fixed-root real fibre of [PR 193, REAL_FIBRE_AND_FRONT](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/100ba857239a180840930765047d65ede92abeec/research/astra-six-hour-20261127/escape/REAL_FIBRE_AND_FRONT.md). All statements inherit their written source assumptions.

Consequently, for **every** $\epsilon_{n,X}\downarrow0$, tuples with some $|x_i-x_j|\le\epsilon_{n,X}p$ have total weight $o(L_q)$. This holds for the individually prescribed digit version too. A fixed relative separation $\epsilon p$ retains all but an arbitrarily small part of the limiting coefficient by first choosing $\epsilon>0$ sufficiently small. The earlier elementary separated-pair bound used $p(1+\log p)^{-1/2-\delta}$. The quantitative all-order transfer below concerns the original trit marks. The real separation conclusion has no uniform componentwise modulus. [Earlier separated-collision bound](../mass/STRICT_LOW_CLOCK_COLLISIONS.md).

## Real marks at a fixed old vertex

Fix a positive odd unit $a$ on a single escaping ray. Write $F_j(a)=\prod_{i<j}3U^ia/(3U^ia+1)$, $F(a)=\lim_jF_j(a)>0$, and $P(a)=a/F(a)$. Set $g_a=h(a)/P(a)$. Let $\nu_a$ be the canonical law of $F(a)(1-\beta_\infty/a)$ at $a$, where $\beta_\infty$ is the actual outward real series. Along the true forward ray, $g_a\to H_C$ and $\nu_a\to\eta_C$ in total variation. Indeed the common path coupling of PR 193 identifies $\nu_a$ with the bilateral product law conditional on passing through $a$, an event with probability $g_a/H_C$ increasing to one. Atomlessness follows from the fixed-root dimension-one spacing estimate and the countable canonical mixture there. This part uses its word-information and growth inputs; the additional synchronization assumption for its Lebesgue-null carrier is unnecessary for atomlessness.

At central clocks $k,l$ with $|l-2k|\le B\sqrt k$ for fixed $B$, the mass divided by $p_a=a2^l/3^k$ of the first-arrival row to $a$ is $(h(a)/a)\pi_k(l)+o_{a,B}(k^{-1/2})$. The pinned real-mark bridge gives weak convergence of the row-normalized $x/p_a=1-\beta_k/a$ to its canonical real law. This convergence is uniform on the stated window for each continuous test. Otherwise a violating sequence has a subsequence with $(l-2k)/\sqrt k$ convergent, and the same bridge theorem gives the common real marginal on that subsequence.

Append the actual remaining $n-k$ forward steps of $a$. Every such source now first visits $y=U^{n-k}a$ at the common observation clock. Its row height is $p=p_a/F_{n-k}(a)$, its weight is multiplied by $F_{n-k}(a)$, and its true mark is $F_{n-k}(a)x/p_a$. For central $l$ and $k,n-k\to\infty$ the normalized mark law tends to $\nu_a$, and the physical subrow mass is $g_a\pi_k(l)+o_{a,B}(k^{-1/2})$. Product measures give the corresponding $q$-tuple statement.

To sum these positive subrows use the central lower range of the slow-source theorem. For pairs take large $k\le(1-\delta)\log X/\log(4/3)$, and for triples take large $k\le c\min(n,\log X)$ with $c>0$ sufficiently small. The central $p_a$ are at most $X$, $n-k$ tends to infinity uniformly, different $k$ in a component give distinct endpoints, and different $l$ at that $k$ give distinct native clocks. The sum of $\pi_k(l)^q$ on $|l-2k|\le B\sqrt k$ is asymptotic to $k^{-(q-1)/2}\int_{-B}^B\phi(z)^q\,dz$ with $\phi(z)=(4\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-z^2/4}$. The elementary power or harmonic sum in $k$, followed by $B\to\infty$ and $\delta\downarrow0$, gives the lower bound $c_qg_a^q\nu_a^{\otimes q}(\Phi)$ on every continuous $0\le\Phi\le1$. All start and clock cutoffs are respected before taking these limits.

Choose one such fixed $a$ in each of finitely many components, then move those base choices forward only after the large-clock limits. Total-variation convergence and $g_a\to H_C$ give the partial sum of (1). Increase the finite list. Its limit for the constant test is exactly the full unmarked mass from the slow-source theorem. Lower bounds for $\Phi$ and $1-\Phi$ therefore imply equality for every such test. This proves the unprescribed statement. A close-pair band with fixed width $\epsilon$ is closed. By atomlessness its limiting measure decreases to zero as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. This gives the asserted saving for all vanishing relative widths.

## True trits in the individual collision marks

For each row write $M_R$ for its measure on $u=x/p$ in weights $1/p$. Write $M_{R,j}$ for the submeasure with $x=j\bmod Q$ and use $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{BL}}$ for the supremum against functions of supremum norm and Lipschitz constant at most one on $[0,1]$. Uniformly for the retained rows in $2^d\le p<2^{d+1}$ with $d$ large, $d\le32n$, and $Q=3^m$, one has for every $A>0$
\[
 \sum_R\sum_{j\bmod Q}\|M_{R,j}-Q^{-1}M_R\|_{\mathrm{BL}}
                  \le C_A d^{-A}\qquad (m\le c_A d).                 \tag{2}
\]
The constants are independent of $n,X$. A finite first-arrival word is simple, so every contributing source lies in $[c_*2^d,2^{d+1}]$ for an absolute $c_*>0$. Split this band, intersected with $[1,X]$, into a bounded number of octave subintervals. Use $t=\lfloor c d\rfloor\le n$ with fixed small $c$ in the physical extra-trit estimate at each source octave. Keep the true native clock $h$ and $z=U^t x$. The remaining transfer to $(s,y)$ has multiplicative weight $F_{n-t}(z)\le1$. It identifies the total weight exactly as $F_n(x)/x=1/p$. Discard the unwanted target rows and height octaves only after this deterministic map. The target-return test makes membership in the first-arrival row automatic from $y$.

For the real mark, replace $u=F_t(x)F_{n-t}(z)$ by $F_{n-t}(z)$ in each tested coefficient. On the good short trajectories with native total $h\le4t$, all odd inputs of those $t$ steps exceed $c_*2^{d-4t}$, so $1-F_t(x)\ll t2^{4t-d}$. The omitted input reciprocal mass is $O(e^{-c't}+2^{4t-d})$ on each source octave. Thus this replacement costs exponentially little in the sum norm against the entire family of row- and digit-specific unit Lipschitz tests. The new real test depends on $(h,z)$ and the prescribed digit; the signed trit discrepancy over all such marks is bounded by the full physical joint estimate. Restoring the exact marks proves (2). A finite supremum approximation, or a separate scalar test chosen on each countable row, gives the displayed sum of norms.

On this octave apply (2) at the largest ternary precision used anywhere in the rows. A coarser residue submeasure is a sum of its finer submeasures; taking absolute discrepancy costs at most the full fine-row norm. The unmarked row mass is at most $C(1+d)^{-1/2}$ by the [target-uniform first-arrival estimate](../mass/SHARP_UNIFORM_FIRST_ARRIVAL_ROWS.md). Telescope the product of the $q$ prescribed submeasures against the product of their $Q_{R,i}^{-1}M_R$ counterparts. For a bounded $1$-Lipschitz test on $[0,1]^q$ each other contracted coordinate costs at most the row's unmarked mass. After multiplying by the $Q_{R,i}$ the summed error on the octave is thus at most
\[
         C_{A,q}(1+d)^{qM-(q-1)/2}d^{-A}.
\]
Each allowed $m$ here is $O_M(\log(d+2))$, so (2) applies for any desired fixed $A$ at all sufficiently large $d$. The finitely many initial octaves cost $O_{q,M}(1)$. At $d>32n$, the elementary high-source word bound in the [common-depth proof](../mass/COMMON_DEPTH_CUBIC_RESIDUE.md) gives maximum row mass at most $2^{-d/2}$ and total octave mass $O(1)$. The polynomial amplification costs a summable amount there too. We obtain an $O_{q,M}(1)$ error in every unit Lipschitz test before division by $L_q$. If two sources coincide the extra charge at all heights is bounded by $C_{q,M}\sum_{x\ge1}(1+\log(2x))^{qM}/x^2$, since each $x$ has at most one row and $p\asymp x$ in it. This proves the individual-digit assertion, its distinct-source version and its relative separation consequence.

## A sliding original source interval

Let $I=[Z,Z+H)$ with real $2\le H\le Z$, sample uniformly on its positive odd integers, and let $R_n(x)=(U^nx,S_n(x))$ without any target restriction. For $1\le t\le\min(n,(\log_2 H)/32)$ and $m\le\delta_A t$, the joint law of $x\bmod 3^m$ and $R_n(x)$ differs in sum norm from a uniform trit vector times its **actual local row marginal** by at most
\[
                 C_A\left(t^{-A}+e^{-ct}+2^{4t}/H\right).             \tag{4}
\]
The same comparison holds after restricting by any common target-row test, including the no-return-through-$n$ test, without renormalizing the restricted law. The constants and the allowed observation clock are independent of where $I$ begins. Thus a source can be localized to any diverging interval length before applying the trit comparison. Equation (4) does not compare the local marginal with a single landing law shared by all translates.

To see the normalization, run the actual short transfer on any subinterval $I'\subset I$, of length $H'$. In the output interval at native total $s\le4t$, each residue modulo $3^t$ has at most $1+H'2^{-s}$ representatives. The factor $3^t/y$ for a good output is $O(2^s/Z)$. The joint oscillation sum over all $s$ and all trits therefore costs $O_A((H'/Z+2^{4t}/Z)t^{-A})$ in physical reciprocal mass. Boundary reassignment costs $O(2^{4t}/Z)$, as each discrepant source is within $O(2^{4t})$ of one of the two endpoints. Counting complete native binary periods and their remainder in $I'$ bounds the large-total input reciprocal mass by $O((H'/Z)e^{-ct}+2^{4t}/Z)$. These arguments retain each input's unique actual trajectory. On the remaining words $1-F_t(x)\ll t2^{4t}/Z$, exponentially small for the stated range since $Z\ge H\ge2^{32t}$. Remove $F_t$, and use partial summation against $x$ on the subinterval bounds to replace $1/x$ by counting measure. Division by the actual odd population $H/2+O(1)$, bounded below by a constant times $H$ for $H\ge2$, yields (4) at $t$. Sending $(U^t x,S_t(x))$ to the two actual clocks and endpoint at $n$ contracts the sum norm. The same proof before removing the bounded product factor applies to the physical row weights $F_n(x)/x$, with total normalization $Z/H$. Longer intervals can be partitioned whenever local statements on pieces with $H\le Z$ are needed.

## Small vertices before original branches merge

For an actual tuple of distinct ordered sources in a retained row, sort them $x_1<\cdots<x_q$. For each $i<q$ take the first odd vertex $z_i$ on the path from $x_i$ shared with the path from some $x_j$ with $j>i$, up through their common observed endpoint. The two paths reach $z_i$ at the same odd and native dates: their remaining first-arrival suffix is unique. Let $V_i$ consist of the odd vertices from $x_i$ strictly before $z_i$. These sets are pairwise disjoint by definition of the first intersection with a larger source. The two matching-clock identities at $z_i$ give
\[
  \sum_{v\in V_i}\log(1+1/(3v))\ \ge\ \log(x_j/x_i).
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
Indeed for the two separate prefixes the ratio of their correction products equals $x_i/x_j$. The larger source's logarithmic correction cost is nonnegative.

On any simple finite shortcut segment the number of values at most $T$ is $O_\theta(T^\theta)$ for each fixed $H_2(\log_3 2)<\theta<1$. The elementary parity-word proof includes its finite end loss and is uniform over the segment. Summing its dyadic upper estimates above $R$ gives $\sum_{v\ge R}v^{-1}\ll_\theta R^{-(1-\theta)}$, also for the odd subsegment. [Weaver, Paper 176 v1, §5.1, after (26)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/176/v1).

If the original differences are at least $\epsilon p$ pairwise, then $\log(x_j/x_i)\ge\log(1+\epsilon)$ because $x_i\le p$. Equations (3) and the uniform tail force a vertex of each $V_i$ at most $R_\theta(\epsilon)\ll_\theta 1+\epsilon^{-1/(1-\theta)}$ for $0<\epsilon\le1$. These $q-1$ low vertices are distinct, are paid for on the smaller sources' own disjoint prefixes, and occur **before** meeting any of the larger sources. By (1), taking the clock and source limits first and then $R\to\infty$, imposing these $q-1$ private visits costs zero limiting coefficient. This applies also to the prescribed and rescaled digit tuples. It complements the [first common vertex law](../mass/FIRST_MERGE_CHARGE.md), which locates the first point common to all members.

An escaping component can contain arbitrarily many finite ordinary vertices in finite initial parts of its predecessor trees as the allowed $R$ grows. The digit and relative-gap conclusions constrain those predecessors but supply no future loss on the fixed vertex where the paths meet. In particular they do not show that the right side of (1) vanishes.
