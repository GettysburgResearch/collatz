# A finite post-meeting clock gives decreasing upper approximations

**Source-qualified written theorem.** The original first-meeting inputs, including their actual joint clocks, already have a tight distribution on ordinary meeting vertices *before* testing survival of the common future. This gives a positive upper approximation to the escaping square and cubic coefficients at each fixed additional clock. Its ingredients are the [first-merge charge identities](FIRST_MERGE_CHARGE.md), the [fixed-row bounds](SHARP_UNIFORM_FIRST_ARRIVAL_ROWS.md), the [signed physical crop calculation](SLOW_SOURCE_CHARGE_SPECTRUM.md) and the uniform [ordinary minimum tail](PREDECESSOR_MINIMUM_POWER.md).

On positive odd inputs \(x\le X\), expose the first \(K_X=\lceil100\log(2X)\rceil\) odd departures, recording their native totals. For any actual row first reaching \(y\) at \(k,t\), \(1\le k\le K_X\), let \(n_y\) be its number of these sources and \(n_{y,v}\) their counts by the last incoming parent \(v\). Use \(p_y=y2^t/3^k\), \(F_T(y)=\prod_{i<T}3U^iy/(3U^iy+1)\), and the falling factorial \((n)_q=n(n-1)\cdots(n-q+1)\). For an integer \(T\ge0\), set
\[
 B_q(X,T)=\sum_{k,t,y} F_T(y)^q p_y^{-q}
                    \left((n_y)_q-\sum_v(n_{y,v})_q\right),\qquad q=2,3 .
\]
Every quantity is a finite rational computed by forward iteration. No component, periodicity or convergence test is made. The parent difference counts earliest simultaneous meetings, since once all members have met all their later incoming parents coincide. All members first visit the specified meeting vertex at the marked clocks.

Set \(b_2(X)=\sqrt{\log X}\), \(b_3(X)=\log\log X\), \(c_2=(2\pi\log(4/3))^{-1/2}\), and \(c_3=(4\pi\sqrt3)^{-1}\). Write \(a_y=h(y)/y\), \(w_v=3v/(3v+1)\), \(H_y=a_y\) at nonperiodic \(y\), and \(H_y=(1-\omega_C)a_y\) on a cycle with primitive multiplier \(\omega_C\). Define child weights as
\[
 b_{y,v}=w_v a_v
       -1_{\{v,y\text{ on the same cycle}\}}\,\omega_C a_y,\qquad
 J^{\rm first}_{q,y}=H_y^q-\sum_{Uv=y} b_{y,v}^q ,
\]
where the subtraction is present only for the unique periodic parent on a cycle. Nonunit odd sites have zero coefficient. Then \(b_{y,v}\ge0\), \(\sum_v b_{y,v}=H_y\) and \(\sum_y J^{\rm first}_{q,y}<\infty\). For each fixed \(T\),
\[
 \lim_{X\to\infty} \frac{B_q(X,T)}{b_q(X)}
             = c_q\sum_y J^{\rm first}_{q,y}F_T(y)^q .                 \tag{1}
\]
Already at \(T=0\) the contributions by individual meeting vertex converge in total variation. Thus the contribution of all meeting vertices above \(R\) has normalized limit tending to zero as \(R\to\infty\), independently of their later ordinary behavior. The upper bounds are the limits in (1) with the additional clock fixed. A finite calculation at a given source height has no certified one-sided error, and the proof provides no effective joint choice of source height and additional clock. The right sides of (1) decrease as \(T\to\infty\) to \(c_q\sum_{C\text{ escaping}}H_C^q\) by dominated convergence and the [charge disintegration, (2)](FIRST_MERGE_CHARGE.md). The remaining test of \(F_T(y)\) acts on the same finite positive vertex. No uniform rate for this last limit is supplied.

For any prescribed integer schedule \(T(X)\to\infty\), including \(T(X)=\lceil\log\log(3X)\rceil\), one consequently has
\[
 \frac{B_q(X,T(X))}{b_q(X)}\longrightarrow
                   c_q\sum_{C\text{ escaping}}H_C^q .                    \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, write \(\mu_X\) for the normalized finite measure on meeting vertices before the future test and \(\mu=c_q\sum_y J^{\rm first}_{q,y}\delta_y\). Then
\[
 \sup_{T\ge0}\left|\frac{B_q(X,T)}{b_q(X)}-\int F_T^q\,d\mu\right|
       \le\sum_y|\mu_X(\{y\})-\mu(\{y\})|\longrightarrow0.
\]
The integrals along the chosen schedule tend to the right side of (2) by dominated convergence. This supplies a single finite forward statistic at every height and a two-sided asymptotic along the full diagonal. Neither the total variation argument nor the fixed-vertex limit gives an effective error estimate at a particular height.

## One anchor

At a fixed \(y\), the first-arrival table has a signed geometric-clock source of net \(H_y\). Its full crop \(p_y\le X\) has \(q\)-power sum asymptotic to \(c_q H_y^q b_q(X)\), by the signed-kernel crop proof: the impulse heights have a fixed positive lower bound, and the omitted signed sum norm controls the cropped norm by Minkowski. The same argument applies to a finite linear combination of fixed shifts of such source tables at a single anchor. The main asymptotic comes from \(k\le(1-\epsilon)\log X/\log(4/3)\) with \(|t-2k|\le B\sqrt k\), taking \(\epsilon\downarrow0,B\to\infty\). This range is inside \(K_X\).

At a nonperiodic parent the exact incoming subtable is \(w_v m_{k-1,t-e_v}(v)\), with \(e_v=v_2(3v+1)\). At a periodic parent it is instead the raw unrestricted parent subtable times \(w_v\), minus the raw table at \(y\) shifted by one primitive lap with coefficient \(\omega_C\). Every earlier visit to \(y\) uses that last periodic parent. Raw tables at a fixed periodic vertex are geometric sums of true first-hit tables over complete laps. On central rows this gives coefficient \(a_v\) times the geometric local kernel: truncate the geometric series after \(C\log k\) laps with \(C\) large, using the uniform point bound on true rows; the retained logarithmic shifts have point error \(O(\log k/k)\) for the kernel and all their log-heights are comparable to \(k\). Thus the incoming central coefficients are precisely \(b_{y,v}\), nonnegative by the underlying counts, and they sum to \(H_y\) by stationary transfer. This includes the fixed point, whose periodic incoming coefficient is zero.

Use the central local limits for each finite selection of parents and count all nonconstant parent choices. This gives the lower coefficient \(c_qJ^{\rm first}_{q,y}\) as in the first-merge note. For the upper, any nonempty retained row has \(p_y\le X/c_*\) by the simple-path correction bound \(c_*>0\). In that entire crop subtract the contributions of all-equal choices in any finite list of parents from the total row powers. The fixed-anchor crop for the total and central lower for the subtracted terms give the matching upper on increasing the list. Repeated sources for triples are harmless: per primary input the total partners bound below gives \(O(x^{-2})\) for a prescribed repeated pair after summation, so the global cost is bounded. For a fixed anchor the same conclusion follows just from the uniformly bounded row mass.

## A uniform partner bound

In a coincident earliest-meeting row, every input is between \(c_*p_y\) and \(p_y\). For each individual original \(x\), there are at most \(C x/\sqrt{1+\log x}\) possible partners over *all* qualifying meeting dates and vertices. In an escaping component they all lie in the one full synchronized corrected-height cell of \(x\), to which the universal peak bound applies. In a cyclic component fix any one cycle phase \(a\). If the meeting vertex is nonperiodic, the pair subsequently first hits \(a\) with the same two clocks. If the meeting is on the cycle, the partners' clocks to their first visits to \(a\) can disagree by at most one primitive lap, in both odd and native counts together. Indeed, viewed from their common first visit to \(y\), their first \(a\) is at its first subsequent phase date or precisely one lap earlier; when \(a=y\) it is the marked date itself. Thus at most three of the first-hit rows at \(a\) can contain partners of this fixed \(x\). Each such occupied row has physical height comparable to \(x\), by comparing its partner to \(x\) at the first meeting and then applying simple-path correction to that partner's first visit to \(a\). The uniform first-row peak applies to all three. For triples the other two sources both meet the primary at the triple site, though they may have met the primary earlier. The same necessary corrected-cell or three-row test holds. No bound on the number of actual cycles or their periods is used.

Consequently an original source subpopulation of fraction \(\epsilon\) in an octave \([Y,2Y)\) costs \(O(\epsilon(1+\log Y)^{-(q-1)/2})\) there when one coordinate belongs to this subpopulation. Sum over dyadic octaves and over the \(q\) choices. In particular the inputs whose full ordinary orbit never visits \([1,r]\) together cost \(o_r(1)b_q(X)\), uniformly in the large-\(X\) limit, by the all-octave minimum bound.

## Tightness before extending the common future

Only finitely many components meet the odd integers at most \(r\). In each such escaping component choose one actual anchor \(a\) on the common tail after all its sites at most \(r\); in a cyclic component choose one cycle phase. Once every member of a tuple has some visit at or below \(r\), they all visit that component's selected anchor. A *nonperiodic* earliest common vertex must lie at or before the anchor, and all members first hit the anchor at the same clocks. In the escaping case, if the anchor had come first on all these paths, it would itself have been an earlier common meeting; its relative clocks are fixed by the common nonperiodic tail. The cyclic case follows since a nonperiodic vertex precedes any cycle. The finitely many periodic target phases in these selected components may simply be retained.

At a fixed anchor \(a\) take the level \(L_j=\{v:\operatorname{firstOdd}(v,a)=j\}\), beyond the primitive period when \(a\) is periodic. Its vertices are nonperiodic and incomparable. The normalized flow of all paths passing through \(v\) is \(B_v=F_{j}(v)a_v\). Then \(\sum_{L_j}B_v=H_a\), and \(\max_{L_j}B_v\to0\). For nonperiodic \(a\), the sum follows by ordinary trace transfer through \(j\) levels. For periodic \(a\), first unfold exactly one lap to give \((1-\omega_C)a_a=\sum_u F_{j(u)}(u)a_u\), where \(u\) ranges over all off-cycle immediate parents of cycle phases and \(1\le j(u)\le |C_{\rm cycle}|\) is its first odd distance to \(a\). Then unfold the nonperiodic subtrees for the remaining depths. For the maximum, the least ancestor tends uniformly to infinity across deep first-visit levels: no bounded source's finite path to its first \(a\) can contain arbitrarily deep pre-hit vertices. Use \(a_v\le\kappa d_v\) and the uniform predecessor-minimum estimate. Both arguments use genuine first visits.

Choose \(j\) with small maximum and a finite subset \(V\subset L_j\) capturing all but \(\eta\) of its flow. On the anchor's exact table the part outside these branches is positive, with signed source equal to the original fixed-anchor source minus the finitely many translated fixed-\(v\) sources multiplied by their actual suffix factors \(F_j(v)\). Its net is at most \(\eta\). The fixed-anchor cropped \(q\)-norm asymptotic and Hölder therefore make the normalized cost of at least one outside coordinate \(O_a(\eta)\). Explicitly bound the per-row loss by \(q M^{q-1}M_{\rm out}\) and sum. If all coordinates lie in a single one of the selected branches, their normalized cost is at most \(c_q\sum_{v\in V}B_v^q+o(1)\le c_q H_a(\max B_v)^{q-1}+o(1)\). In both comparisons the original first-meeting weight is at most \(c_*^{-q}\) times the anchor weight, and the anchor crop may be enlarged to \(X/c_*\). If the coordinates instead use two or more distinct selected branches, their first common vertex belongs to the finite union of the actual suffixes from \(V\) to \(a\). Hence all but arbitrarily small normalized mass is on a finite set of meeting vertices. First fix \(r\), then choose levels and branch lists for its finitely many anchors, take \(X\to\infty\), and finally expand \(r\). This proves tightness at \(T=0\) independently of any later survival estimate. Together with the fixed-target limits it proves (1) and summability. Multiplication by any bounded function of the actual meeting vertex preserves this total variation convergence.

For a test that instead constrains the common native clock after first meeting, the exact identity is \(S_N(y)-N\log_2 3=\log_2(y/U^Ny)-\log_2 F_N(y)\). It tends to minus infinity on an escaping tail and to plus infinity on a recurrent one. A condition on the incoming low visits or digits can be evaluated already at the explicit terminating merger at 5. An upper estimate for the later shared tail must address the actual supplied vertex, even after the high-meeting remainder has been removed.
