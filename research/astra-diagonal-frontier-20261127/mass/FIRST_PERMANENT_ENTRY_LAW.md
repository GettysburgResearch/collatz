# Natural law of the first permanently passing ordinary seed

**SOURCE-QUALIFIED written result.** Use the odd map \(U(x)=(3x+1)/2^{v_2(3x+1)}\). Let \(P\) consist of positive odd \(z\) with \(4A_j(z)\le7j\) at every odd clock. For a positive odd source \(x\), define \(F(x)\) as the first member of \(P\) on its actual orbit, including time zero, or \(\dagger\) if none occurs. There is a probability \(\mu\) on the countable set \(P\cup\{\dagger\}\) such that both natural-uniform and reciprocal-weighted positive odd sources through \(X\) have \(F\)-laws converging to \(\mu\) in total variation. For some \(\sigma>0\) the errors are \(O((\log X)^{-\sigma})\). In particular the law is tight in **absolute ordinary landing height**, including the cemetery separately, and
\[
 \mu(\{z\in P:z\ge M\})\ll (\log M)^{-\sigma}\quad(M\ge3).          \tag{1}
\]
The same conclusions hold on all positive integers after first taking the odd part, with the same limit. PR200 gave a subpower upper bound on the actual first-safe height with an exponential-in-octave exceptional set. The present absolute-height tightness is a different limiting statement.

The external inputs are Valbuena's two-scale logarithmic first-passage stabilization, local regularity and Banach two-scale lemma, together with Mazur's comparison of natural and logarithmic local landings as stated there. We use PR201's ordinary seed count. Valbuena's original countable-label theorem assumes **pointwise invariance under every sufficiently high successful passage**. Marking \(P\) gives exceptions. The local bound below controls them at both exact scales; invoking the original theorem verbatim would not do so. [Valbuena at a7275d5a, source and labels in its two-scale and natural-upgrade sections](https://github.com/x1xhlol/collatz-two-coordinate-obstruction/blob/a7275d5a73814d3d9a400c6fe6577de9542b03a3/paper/tao-basin-densities.tex) · [PR200, critical first-safe entry at eb910869](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/eb91086951b66923787f696801f0c3ca52a0077d/research/six-hour-20261127/pointwise/CRITICAL_SAFE_ENTRY.md) · [PR201, global principal envelope at d21bac74](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/d21bac748e705a64b6b57a7996a8ad6282f0bcbf/research/six-hour-20261127/escape/GLOBAL_PREFIX_TRACE.md).

## Local estimate before the landing

Let \(E\) be any deterministic subset of positive odd integers with \(\#(E\cap[1,Y])\le C_E Y^d\), where
\[
 \delta=1-d>\kappa={2((1001/1000)^3-1)\log2\over\log(4/3)}.
\]
Let \(L\) be any countable-valued label satisfying \(L(x)=L(Ux)\) when \(x\notin E\). The constants below are uniform over all such labels for this fixed envelope. PR201 supplies this hypothesis for the union \(D\) of positive least sources over all passing finite words: \(d=H_2(4/7)\). Every member of \(P\) belongs to \(D\) because its growing dyadic modulus eventually exceeds the source. The explicit margins are \(0.0144709<\kappa<0.0144711\) and \(0.0147718<1-H_2(4/7)<0.0147720\). The accompanying [rational check](check_first_entry_law.py) encloses the logarithms by the positive atanh series with a geometric remainder.

For either \(a=1001/1000\) or \(2001/2000\), let \(Q\) be log-uniform on the odd integers in either \([x^a,x^{a^2}]\) or \([x^{a^2},x^{a^3}]\). Put \(\lambda=\log(4/3)\), \(R=\lfloor0.1\log x\rfloor\), \(K=(\log x)^{3/5}\), natural logarithms throughout. Valbuena's local lemma and its proof (labels `ct-regular-prefix`, `ct-local-log-path`, `ct-local-time`) supply, outside probability \(Ce^{-c(\log x)^{1/5}}\),
\[
 |A_j-2j|\le K,\quad
 |\log U^jQ-(\log Q-\lambda j)|\le C K\quad(j\le R),\qquad
 \tau_x(Q)\le{(a^3-1)\log x\over\lambda}+C K<R.                  \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\tau_x\) is first entrance at or below \(x\). The strict last bound uses \((1.001^3-1)/\log(4/3)<0.011<0.1\). The above log-height statements apply to the full unstopped prefix; the actual stopping is taken only on this good event.

For any prelanding visit \(y=U^jQ\in E\) on this event, the word through that visit has total at most \(B=2((1.001)^3-1)\log x/\lambda+O(K)\). The number of all positive-exponent words of arbitrary length with total at most \(\lfloor B\rfloor\), including the empty word, is \(2^{\lfloor B\rfloor}\). If the source lies in an octave \([Y,2Y)\), (2) also gives \(y\le 2Ye^{CK}\). A fixed target and fixed complete word determine at most one ordinary source by affine inversion. Thus the reciprocal mass of the possible good sources in that octave is at most
\[
 {1\over Y}\,2^B C_E(2Ye^{CK})^d
 \ll x^{\kappa}Y^{-\delta}\exp(O(K)).
\]
The source interval is above \(x\); its possibly truncated first octave has \(Y\ge x/2\). There are \(O(\log x)\) octaves, and the log-law normalizer is bounded below by a positive constant times \(\log x\) at both fixed scales. With \(g=\delta-\kappa>0\), the normalized good-source probability is \(O(x^{-g/2})\) for sufficiently large \(x\). Include failure and all irregular paths with the error from (2). Under either logarithmic source law, for every fixed \(B_0>0\),
\[
 \Pr\{L(Q)\ne L(\operatorname{Pass}_xQ)\}\ll_{B_0,E}(\log x)^{-B_0}.       \tag{3}
\]
In this comparison, the landing uses Valbuena's default 1 on failure. On a successful path, mark the departed sites only; reaching a marker on the terminal step preserves the label up to that landing. An arbitrary prior failure probability is never discarded. For the uniform law on the same odd block, each atom has mass at most \(C\log x\) times its corresponding log-law mass: compare the harmonic normalizer to the number of sites, using the largest site in the block. Thus (3) holds at every fixed logarithmic order there also.

## Two-scale transfer and rates

With \(I\) the countable label set, set \(A(t)=\sum_{q\le e^t,\ q\text{ odd}}\delta_{L(q)}/q\) in \(\ell^1(I)\). The norm is \(t/2+O(1)\) and the increment over \(h\ge0\) has norm at most \(h/2+O(1)\), uniformly over the label. For either scale \(u\), the block labels on the two local source intervals have total-variation distance \(O((\log x)^{-c_u})\): apply (3) to both source-to-landing comparisons and push Valbuena's `ct-tao-tv` through \(L\). For \(p_u(t)=(A(ut)-A(t))/((u-1)t)\), the harmonic block normalization, including a possible endpoint site, gives \(\|p_u(ut)-p_u(t)\|=O(t^{-c_u})\) with \(t=u\log x\). The positive exponent constants can be reduced below one. The scales have irrational log ratio by the prime 7. Valbuena's `ct-two-scale` applies: on the first scale the error is summable along each geometric progression, and on the second it goes to zero. It gives convergence of \(A(t)/t\) in norm, with rate \(O(t^{-\eta})\) for \(0<\eta<\min(c_{1001/1000},1)\). Normalizing gives a probability, without loss of mass at ever higher label values. The constants in this argument depend on the envelope and the input estimates, not the individual label; the lemma's first-scale geometric-tail error and common norm/increment bounds give this uniformity.

For the natural upgrade take \(u=1001/1000\), \(y=x^u\) and uniformly sampled odd inputs in \([y,y^u]\). Mazur's local comparison `ct-mazur-natural-transport` bounds the full distance of *landing* laws from the corresponding log-law landing by \(O((\log x)^{-v})\) for any fixed \(0<v<5/143\). Push it through \(L\); both discrepancies between source and landing labels satisfy (3). The log source law on that block is within \(O((\log x)^{-\eta})\) of the limit, by differencing \(A\) at the two block endpoints. For arbitrary large \(X\), use \(x=X^{1/u^2}\), so the block covers all odd integers in \([X^{1/u},X]\) and the omitted initial proportion is \(O(X^{1/u-1})\). This proves the natural assertion with any fixed \(0<\sigma<\min(c_{1001/1000},1,5/143)\). Splitting an arbitrary positive integer by initial two-adic valuation gives the same rate on all positive inputs: use valuations at most \(\lfloor(\log_2 X)/2\rfloor\) so the odd input cutoff is \(\ge\sqrt X\), and bound the remaining source mass by \(O(X^{-1/2})\) in either normalization (or \(O(X^{-1/2}\log X)\), harmless).

## First permanent seeds, finite-depth approximants and component mass

Take \(L=F\); all exceptional departed edges lie in \(P\subset D\). The bad starts, those which do not reach 1, are exactly the starts with a finite label. Indeed on a bad orbit every odd state is at least 3. Its forward debt \(B_j=7j-4A_j\) satisfies \(B_j\ge(7-4\log_2(10/3))j-O_x(1)\to\infty\). Its earliest global minimum is its first safe date. A source reaching 1 eventually repeats gap two, so it has no safe tail. If the first safe date is \(h>0\), its debt is strictly below zero because the earlier date zero has debt zero. At its safe landing \(z\), the physical product gives
\[
 2^{7h/4}<2^{A_h}\le(x/z)(10/3)^h,\qquad x>z[2^{7/4}/(10/3)]^h>z.
\]
Thus every finite label at a positive odd input is no larger than that input. Compare the law through \(M-1\) with \(\mu\) to obtain (1). In particular all positive-density predecessors of a specified bad unit target cannot send their first entries to growing heights: some **single** ordinary permanently safe seed has a first-entry cell of positive natural density in its component. This uses positive natural density of the target's predecessors and countable tightness. It does not require a nonperiodic component.

Let \(M_N\subset D\) be the finite set of all passing length-\(N\) principal sources, and replace \(P\) by \(M_N\) in the first-visit definition, obtaining \(F_N\) and its limiting law \(\mu_N\). For each fixed odd input, \(F_N(x)=F(x)\) eventually. In a bad orbit the finite path up to the first safe site has only finitely many previous sites, each eventually failing a prefix; the safe site itself is eventually principal. In a convergent orbit finitely many sites are visited and all eventually fail. The estimates above are uniform over \(N\) because every exception set lies in the same \(D\). First choose a common large finite source cutoff, then pass \(N\to\infty\) on that finite set. This triangle inequality gives \(\mu_N\to\mu\) in total variation; it supplies no rate in \(N\). Computing the complete basin of a finite target set is still an all-time hitting problem.

## Exact density at a fixed nonperiodic permanent unit

For a positive odd target \(z\) write \(d_z\) for the **relative natural density among odd integers** of inputs reaching \(z\), not a value of the harmonic trace. For a fixed nonperiodic odd unit \(a\in P\), use the natural backward entrance law \(Q_a\) of PR193's fixed-root theorem. A depth-\(j\) unit ancestor \(z\) has cylinder probability \(d_z/d_a\). Under \(Q_a\) the sum of inverse gaps is \(2j+o(j)\) almost surely. Nonunit ancestors have zero natural density. These are source-qualified inputs; the stationary \(h\)-transformed law is not substituted for \(Q_a\). [Canonical inverse paths at aa686899](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/aa6868996f6f30b12d91c367f7c6edcfd93784b2/research/astra-six-hour-20261127/escape/CANONICAL_INVERSE_PATHS.md).

Define reverse cumulative debt \(R_j=7j-4\sum_{i=1}^j e_i\), \(R_0=0\). An ancestor at depth \(j\) is permanently safe exactly when \(R_j\) is a **weak running maximum** of \(R_0,\dots,R_j\). Each forward prefix of length \(h\le j\) has debt \(R_j-R_{j-h}\); the subsequent physical tail starts at the same permanent \(a\). Thus first forward entry at \(a\) is absence of all later reverse weak records, equivalently strict negativity of every reverse partial sum after zero. [FirstEntryRecord.lean](FirstEntryRecord.lean) checks the finite deterministic identity, including ties.

For each nonperiodic permanent unit \(z\), put \(q_z=Q_z(R_i<0\text{ for every }i\ge1)\). Then
\[
   \mu(\{z\})=d_zq_z,\qquad
   d_a=\sum_{\substack{z\in P,\ U^jz=a\text{ for some }j\ge0}}\mu(\{z\}).
                                                                         \tag{4}
\]
For the first equality, at fixed reverse depth \(k\), conditioned natural predecessors of \(z\) converge on the depth-\(k\) partition to \(Q_z\), in total variation, as in the fixed-root construction. The omitted inputs with fewer than \(k\) odd steps have relative natural density zero. Indeed for sources through \(X\) and fixed target and odd length, their total division count is \(O_{k,z}(1+\log X)\) by the affine product and the bound \(3+1/u\le4\); only polynomially many words are possible in that count. The event of no positive-time weak record in these first \(k\) reversed steps has limiting conditional probability \(Q_z(R_i<0,1\le i\le k)\).

Among the physical inputs hitting \(z\), the first safe landing belongs to its predecessor cone, and the event that this landing is an earlier physical ancestor deeper than \(k\) has arbitrarily small limiting probability as \(k\to\infty\). In fact choose finitely many finite labels carrying all but \(\epsilon\) of the global finite first-entry mass using the total-variation law just proved. Every chosen label that reaches this *nonperiodic* target does so at a unique finite depth, so increasing \(k\) beyond their maximum removes them all. Divide the residual by \(d_z>0\). This justifies passing from finite reverse tests to the displayed infinite event in the natural predecessor population. For the second equality observe that \(\{x:x\to a\}\) is exactly the union of the first-entry cells indexed by permanent ancestors of \(a\): an input reaching the already permanent \(a\) has encountered its first permanent seed by then. The tight law gives countable additivity in this identity. Equivalently \(R_j=-j+o(j)\) under \(Q_a\); its last weak record is finite almost surely, and its location at \(z\) has mass \((d_z/d_a)q_z\). All ancestors of a nonperiodic target are nonperiodic themselves. Equation (4) is not asserted for a periodic target where first-hit depths and complete laps need separate handling.

## The escape-ray obligation at a specified scale

On an escaping positive ray, its forward debt from a fixed start is \(B_j\ge\delta_0 j-O(1)\), \(\delta_0=7-4\log_2(10/3)>0\). The envelope \(M_j=\min_{t\ge j}B_t\) is nondecreasing. A rise requires \(B_j=M_j\), hence permanence at \(j\), and its size is at most \(B_{j+1}-B_j\le3\). Consequently the permanently safe phases have lower density at least \(\delta_0/3\). This is the existing Pliss selection specialized to the floor-three bound. [PR201 permanent sieve, §2](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/d21bac748e705a64b6b57a7996a8ad6282f0bcbf/research/six-hour-20261127/escape/PERMANENT_SIEVE.md).

Enumerate the successive permanent unit phases \(z_0,z_1,\dots\) in chronological order, discarding an initial nonunit if needed. Their ordinary predecessor sets are nested and \(d_{z_i}\ge d_{z_0}>0\). For each \(i>0\), the reverse word reaching \(z_{i-1}\) has probability \(d_{z_{i-1}}/d_{z_i}\) and contains a weak record. Therefore
\[
 q_{z_i}\le1-{d_{z_{i-1}}\over d_{z_i}},\qquad
 \sum_{i\ge1}q_{z_i}\le\log{\sup_i d_{z_i}\over d_{z_0}}<\infty.   \tag{5}
\]
This argument is also a direct cylinder proof of the summability one obtains from (4). The chronological time of \(z_i\) is \(O(i+1)\), and \(U^t x+1\le(3/2)^t(x+1)\), so \(\log(z_i+1)=O(i+1)\). An eventual bound \(q_z\ge c/\log(2+z)\) on all nonperiodic permanent units would exclude escape, as would \(c/[\log(2+z)\log\log(3+z)]\). Neither is proved. The fixed-root mean-two law controls a sufficiently distant reverse tail at a specified root; its onset need not be uniform over consecutive roots of one ray. PR193's concentration on the genuine incoming ray explains exactly how such a uniform lower bound can fail under the counterexample hypothesis. Cycle uniqueness remains separate from (5). [Moving real fibres and backward concentration](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/aa6868996f6f30b12d91c367f7c6edcfd93784b2/research/astra-six-hour-20261127/escape/REAL_FIBRE_AND_FRONT.md).

A criterion encompassing both eventual cycles and escape is that every finite atom of \(\mu\) vanish. If any ordinary component misses 1, its positive-density unit predecessor set supplies a positive finite atom by the present law; conversely any finite label is on a bad orbit. This proof gives the density and identifies the recurrent input still required. It does not evaluate that atom or show it is zero.

The absence-of-record probability can also be defined at nonpermanent nonperiodic roots, by the same reverse event. A concrete obstruction to uniformity occurs in the ordinary tree of 1. The slow-climb theorem supplies arbitrarily large nonperiodic units \(x\) taking any prescribed positive number of exponent-one steps to an ordinary \(y\) which reaches 1 and satisfies \(d_y<(1+\epsilon)d_x\). Taking \(x\) large makes \(y>1\), hence nonperiodic. The cylinder from \(y\) back to \(x\) already has first reverse debt \(+3\). It follows that \(q_y\le1-d_x/d_y<\epsilon/(1+\epsilon)\). The witnesses lie outside \(P\), and the theorem gives no upper bound on their sizes in terms of \(\epsilon\). Its sibling and first-doubling criteria use different events from (5). [Weaver and Wren, relative growth, Theorem B](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/200/v2).
