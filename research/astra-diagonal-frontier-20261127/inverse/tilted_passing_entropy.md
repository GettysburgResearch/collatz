# The tilted entropy exponent on a supplied ordinary inverse tree

Let $G_\tau(a)$ be the positive odd ternary-unit ancestors of $a$ which pass every forward $\tau$-gap prefix through their first visit to $a$, as in the [retained-tree theorem](retained_passing_trees.md). Put $h(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)$ using natural logarithms. Let $\mu_\theta$ be the law on ternary units of $\sum_{i\ge1}3^{i-1}2^{-S_i}$ for iid gaps of mass $p(e)=\theta(1-\theta)^{e-1}$.

**Theorem.** Suppose $\log_2 3<t<\tau<2$, $\theta=1/t$, $t h(1/t)>\log3$, and $\mu_\theta$ is absolutely continuous with respect to ternary Haar probability. For every specified positive odd ternary unit $a$,
\[
 \liminf_{X\to\infty}
    \frac{\log|G_\tau(a)\cap[1,X]|}{\log X}
 \ \ge\ \frac{t h(1/t)-\log3}{t\log2-\log3}.                      \tag{1}
\]
If $a$ is $\tau$-permanent, the counted sources in (1) are again permanent ancestors of this same $a$. The statement is pointwise in the supplied integer root; its threshold may depend on that root.

Leen's absolute-continuity interval as stated by Wren includes $1/2\le\theta\le0.550024$. Consequently for $1/0.550024<\tau<2$ one can send $t\uparrow\tau$ in (1). This gives a lower exponent $1-I(\tau)/(\tau\log2-\log3)$, exactly the ceiling on the *single-block all-state normalization* derived in the companion theorem. The latter ceiling is not an independent upper bound on (1). The guaranteed quadratic deficit as $\tau\uparrow2$ has leading coefficient $1/(4\log(4/3))$. At $\tau=7/4$, absolute continuity at any nearby $1/t>4/7$ would give (1), but the cited AC interval does not reach these parameters. The proof only assumes the stated AC premise and supplied-state inverse-kernel theorem. [Leen, Tilted 3-adic Syracuse laws, Theorem 3.7 (Zenodo v1, 2026)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23268832) · [Wren, Paper 154, §4.3, quantitative statement of the AC criterion](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/154) · [Weaver, Paper 127 v1, Theorems 5–6 and equation (36)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/127/v1).

## Many finite words on Haar-most source cylinders

Normalize Haar $m$ on the ternary unit group. The law in the theorem has density $f$ relative to $m$ and is equivalent to $m$. In fact, by the memoryless geometric law its distribution satisfies $\mu_\theta\ge(1-\theta)(x\mapsto x/2)_*\mu_\theta$, as seen by conditioning the first gap to be at least two in the infinite sum. Thus $f(y)\ge(1-\theta)f(2y)$ almost everywhere. Its zero set is invariant modulo null sets under multiplication by two (one-sided inclusion suffices by invariance of Haar). This map is ergodic on the unit group: the powers of two enumerate each full finite unit quotient, so every finite-cylinder conditional expectation of an invariant function is constant. Martingale convergence gives ergodicity. Since $\int f\,dm=1$, we have $0<f<\infty$ almost everywhere.

On a joint sample of the iid gap sequence, let $Y_j$ be the offset of its remaining infinite tail after $j$ gaps. Its law is $\mu_\theta$ at each $j$, and $Y_j=(2^{E_j}Y_{j-1}-1)/3$ when gaps are indexed from one. Conditional on $Y_0=y$, the mass of a length-$b$ word $w$ equals
\[
  3^b p(w)\,1_{\text{all physical ternary edges, terminal unit}}
                 \frac{f(I_w y)}{f(y)}.                              \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, conditioning on $w$ leaves its tail $Y_b$ independent with law $\mu_\theta$. The affine map $Y_b\mapsto Y_0$ multiplies by $3^b2^{-S_b}$, carrying Haar to a density factor $3^b$ on its image. Disintegration establishes (2) on a common full-measure set of source roots. This is only a calculation in the stationary tilted model at this stage.

Write $H=t h(1/t)$, the Shannon entropy of one geometric gap. By the iid law, $S_b/b\to t$ and $-\log p(w)/b\to H$ in probability. Also $\log f(Y_0)/b$ and $\log f(Y_b)/b$ tend to zero in probability because their common distribution is finite almost surely. Given any small $\eta>0$, with joint probability $1-o(1)$ the realized word therefore has $|S_b/b-t|\le\eta$ and conditional mass at most $e^{-b(H-\log3-\eta)}$. Consequently for $\mu_\theta$-probability $1-o(1)$ of roots the conditional probability of this event exceeds $1/2$. Such a root admits at least $e^{b(H-\log3-2\eta)}$ distinct words of those allowed totals ending in units, for all sufficiently large $b$. Counting these words depends only on $y\bmod3^{b+1}$. By $m\ll\mu_\theta$, their good source cylinders have Haar probability $1-o(1)$, also separately in both unit classes modulo three.

## A true stationary edge flow with prescribed cost and entropy

For a large fixed $b$, on every good source cylinder choose uniformly among all its words just counted (the cost condition suffices for this selection). On every other cylinder choose a deterministic length-$b$ legal unit word of total at most $4b$. Such a word exists since every ternary unit has a unit inverse gap at most four; this follows by checking its residue modulo nine. Resolve ties from $b+1$ digits only. Every selected gap is bounded by $r=\max(6,4b)$.

Start with Haar on a single unit class modulo three. Conditional on the initial $b+1$ digit cylinder and the chosen word, the initial state is Haar on this cylinder. After $j$ selected inverse edges, it is precisely Haar on one cylinder of depth $b+1-j$: $I_{w[1:j]}$ acts there by an affine bijection with linear part $2^{S_j}/3^j$. In particular the endpoint after $b$ letters is Haar on its new unit class. The two unit classes therefore define an ordinary two-state stochastic matrix at complete block boundaries. Choose any stationary law for this finite matrix and start with its mixture of the two conditional Haar laws. Then $Y_b$ and $Y_0$ have identical laws as points of the full ternary unit space. Since bad cylinders have probability $o(1)$ in each starting class, the expected block cost divided by $b$ lies in $[t-\eta-o(1),t+\eta+o(1)]$. Its conditional word entropy given the entire initial state is at least $b(1-o(1))(H-\log3-2\eta)$.

For each gap value $e$ define the measure $\Lambda_e=b^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}\Pr(Y_j\in\cdot,E_{j+1}=e)$. The previous exact cylinder statement shows that each such measure has a density constant modulo $3^{b+1}$. With $\nu=\sum_e\Lambda_e$, the complete-block stationarity telescopes to the equality of **full ternary measures**
\[
                         \sum_e(I_e)_*\Lambda_e=\nu.                  \tag{3}
\]
There is no projection on finite digits in (3). In particular $\nu$ has a density constant modulo $3^b$ since each pushforward removes one input digit. The one-edge conditional entropy of this flow is at least the block conditional word entropy divided by $b$. To verify this, choose a uniformly random phase $J$ and condition the next gap first on its current state $Y_J$. Further conditioning on $J,Y_0,E_1,\ldots,E_J$ only reduces entropy. Given these data, $Y_J$ is already determined. Summing the resulting conditional entropies over phases is the chain rule for the entire selected word given $Y_0$. The one-edge mean cost is exactly the expected block cost divided by $b$.

To give every legal branch strictly positive probability, take the background rule choosing uniformly among all legal unit exponents up to $r$. By Paper 127, Theorem 5, this rule has a stationary law of strictly positive locally constant density (its rule reads modulo nine). Its stationary edge measures $\Lambda_e^0$ obey (3). Choose $0<\delta\ll\eta/r$, form $\Lambda'_e=(1-\delta)\Lambda_e+\delta\Lambda^0_e$, and set $\nu'=\sum_e\Lambda'_e$. Dividing their densities gives conditional probabilities $p'_e=d\Lambda'_e/d\nu'$ on every cylinder. These are constant modulo $3^{b+1}$, zero on illegal labels, strictly positive on *every* legal label $1\le e\le r$, and sum to one. The primed version of (3) makes $\nu'$ stationary for the Markov kernel which applies the actual maps $I_e$ with these probabilities. Concavity of conditional entropy under mixing joint distributions, or conditioning further on the mixture component, shows that its stationary edge entropy is at least $1-\delta$ times that of the initial flow. Its mean differs from the initial one by at most $\delta r$.

## Fixed ordinary root and last record

The mixed rule satisfies every hypothesis of Paper 127, Theorem 5 with $M=b$ and fixed cap $r\ge6$. That theorem gives unique stationarity among *all* ternary probabilities and almost-sure empirical convergence from each supplied state. Hence its stationary law is precisely $\nu'$. Theorem 6 applies to the complete ordinary path from any fixed positive odd ternary unit. Write $A=\int\sum_e e p'_e\,d\nu'$ and $B=\int-\sum_e p'_e\log p'_e\,d\nu'$. For this actual positive integer chain, almost surely
\[
       S_n/n\to A,\qquad \log X_n/n\to g=A\log2-\log3>0,
       \qquad -\log\Pr(W_n\mid X_0=a)/n\to B.                       \tag{4}
\]
The last conclusion follows by the proof of Paper 127, equation (36): the negative log of a realized branch probability minus its conditional expectation is a bounded martingale difference, since the finite set of positive probabilities has a positive minimum. The conditional expectation is locally constant, so Theorem 5 applies at this particular supplied integer. Equations (3)–(4) transfer two stationary averages; no high ternary digit has been drawn independently on the arithmetic path.

Choose $\eta$, then $b$, then $\delta$ so that $A<\tau$ and $B\ge H-\log3-O(\eta)$, with $|A-t|=O(\eta)$. Let $j_n$ be the last maximizer of $V_j=\tau j-S_j$ on $0\le j\le n$. Its integer $X_{j_n}$ passes every forward prefix on the path to $a$ because $V_{j_n}\ge V_i$ for each earlier $i$. In (4) we have $V_n=(\tau-A)n+o(n)$ with strictly positive slope. It follows that $j_n=n-o(n)$: the partial error is uniformly $o(n)$ over $j\le n$ by separating a finite initial interval. Also $\max_{j\le n}\log X_j\le(g+o(1))n$.

For each fixed $\xi>0$ all large $n$ give probability at least $1/2$ to full words with $j_n\ge(1-\xi)n$, every height at most $e^{(g+\xi)n}$, and individual word mass at most $e^{-(B-\xi)n}$. There are at least $e^{(B-\xi)n}/2$ such words. A recorded source and its depth $j_n$ determine its entire prefix by deterministic forward iteration even if $a$ is periodic. There are at most $n+1$ possible depths and at most $(n+1)r^{\xi n}$ discarded suffixes. The physical first visit to $a$ may occur earlier in that prefix and still passes. Taking $n=\lfloor\log X/(g+\xi)\rfloor$ and then $\xi\downarrow0$ gives a lower exponent $B/g$. Finally let $\eta\downarrow0$ to obtain (1). The caps and perturbations are each fixed before the long arithmetic path is followed; the outer approximations only optimize the resulting exponents.

For $\tau$ sufficiently close to two, the same lower exponent holds with the counted interval replaced by $[X,2X]$. The extension constructed in the companion [ordinary octave argument](retained_passing_trees.md) sends each old marked $y\le X/C_\tau$ along a true passing path to an odd unit in $[X,2X]$ within $O_\tau(1+\log X)$ steps. Every possible old $y$ for a given new source is then one of those finitely many forward iterates, and the logarithmic multiplicity disappears in the exponent. A variable-$\tau$ onset or the same octave claim for the entire AC range is not asserted. The [exact finite edge-flow control](check_retained_trees.py) tests the high-digit balance in (3) on a two-edge block policy at a deeper output precision than its lookup window.
