# Brownian bridges at an exact native clock and a supplied ordinary root

Fix a positive odd integer $a$ coprime to three. Choose integers $s_n$ with $(s_n-2n)/\sqrt n\to z\in\mathbb R$. Among the genuine positive odd inputs $v$ with $U^n v=a$ and total native clock $s_n$, give each input equal probability. Read its actual odd gaps *outward* from $a$, form $D_j=S_j-2j$, and interpolate linearly. Under the averaged joint mixing assumption (J), the ordinary trace inputs of the [fixed-root Brownian law](fixed_root_brownian.md), and the fixed-target signed source estimate below,
\[
             (D_{nt}/\sqrt n)_{0\le t\le1}
                 \ \Longrightarrow\
     (\sqrt2(W_t-tW_1)+tz)_{0\le t\le1}\qquad\text{in }C[0,1].       \tag{1}
\]
Jointly with this path, every fixed finite initial outward word converges to its canonical distribution $P_{h,a}$, independently of the limiting bridge. The exact real offset $\beta_n=\sum_{i=1}^n3^{i-1}2^{-S_i}$ converges jointly to the canonical real offset $\beta_\infty$ independent of this bridge. The sampled terminal input is allowed to be a multiple of three. Each legal length-$n$ word has all preceding states ternary units. Conditioning on the clocks makes all its raw weights $3^n2^{-s_n}$ equal.

Paper 211 gives uniform bridge minorants with a moving residue. Its ordinary fair-composition bridge limit is used here as an input. Our two-sided limit (1) fixes the positive ordinary residue and retains all its positive compatible words. It includes the terminal mean $z$ and permits any fixed real $z$. ## Normalization at an exact total

Write $\pi_n(s)=2^{-s}\binom{s-1}{n-1}$ and $\rho_{n,s}(a)=3^n\Pr_0(\beta_n=a\bmod3^n,S_n=s)$ for independent fair positive geometric letters. Then uniformly on each fixed central window,
\[
                   \rho_{n,s}(a)/\pi_n(s)\longrightarrow h(a).     \tag{2}
\]
For completeness this follows from the [target-stopped first-arrival convolution](../mass/SHARP_UNIFORM_FIRST_ARRIVAL_ROWS.md), (6)–(7). At root $a$, the mass divided by $a$ on **first** arrivals is a convolution $c^a*g$, where $g_j=\pi_j$, the signed source is summable in both clocks, and its mass at physical heights $p_0\ge X$ is $O_{a,B}((\log X)^{-B})$ for all $B$. At a central final total the final height $p=a2^s/3^n$ satisfies $\log p\asymp_a n$. All source terms with $p_0\ge\sqrt p$ cost $o(n^{-1/2})$. The kernel at all smaller source heights is $O_a(n^{-1/2})$ by the height-ratio estimate following (7) there. Truncate the summable source to finitely many cells. Each fixed translate has $\pi_{n-i}(s-t)/\pi_n(s)\to1$ uniformly on the central window. Thus the first-arrival coefficient is $\sum c^a$. This same number is the large-depth limit of the sum over all native totals of the divided first-arrival mass, by summing its convolution.

For a nonperiodic root all arrivals are first arrivals. At a periodic root with primitive odd length $l$, native total $b$ and multiplier $\lambda=3^l2^{-b}<1$, the complete table is the sum of translates of the first-arrival table at $(jl,jb)$ with multiplier $\lambda^j$, $j\ge0$. Split the sum at a sufficiently small fixed fraction of $n$. Below that index the shifted height $p\lambda^j\ge\sqrt p$ gives the uniform bound $O_a(n^{-1/2})$ from the same target-stopped theorem; above it the geometric tail is exponentially small since all first-arrival masses divided by $a$ are at most one. This permits finite truncation before taking the limit relative to $\pi_n(s)$. The scalar coefficient for all words is therefore $a\sum c^a$ at nonperiodic roots and $a\sum c^a/(1-\lambda)$ in the periodic case. To identify the coefficient it suffices that $h(a)$ is the Cesàro limit of the full ordinary traces $\rho_n(a)=\sum_s\rho_{n,s}(a)$, provided by the canonical trace input. Indeed the sum over $s$ of the divided first-arrival row tends to $\sum c^a$ by absolute summability of its source. At a periodic root this row equals $(\rho_n(a)-\lambda\rho_{n-l}(a))/a$ exactly, with zero at negative depths. At a nonperiodic root it equals $\rho_n(a)/a$. Taking Cesàro limits identifies the respective calculations with $h(a)$ in (2). The stronger individual-depth trace limit in Paper 176 agrees. Positivity of $h(a)$ on ordinary units gives eventual nonemptiness of every central conditioned set. ## Late deletion has arbitrarily small power tails

Use the [upper functional comparison](functional_upper.md) and the progressively restricted densities $r_x^I$ of the [fixed-root Brownian proof](fixed_root_brownian.md). The growing restriction $K_x$ imposes $1.9d\le S_d\le3d$ at every $I\le d\le d(x)=\lfloor0.9x\rfloor$. For each fixed $I$, $r_x^I\to h(a)P_{h,a}(G_I)$ with $G_I=\bigcap_x K_x$. At precision $x$, $\Delta_x$ is the unmarked projected density of $K_{x-1}\setminus K_x$, and $\sum_x\Delta_x<\infty$. In fact for every $A>0$,
\[
                            \Delta_x=O_{a,I,A}(x^{-A}).               \tag{3}
\]
To check this stronger bound, a nonempty deletion forces $|D_p|\ge0.1p$ at $p=d(x)$ and retains all the previous restrictions. In the proof of the upper comparison use calendar scale $x$, *terminal precision $x$*, and the single marked date $p$, with the two signs tested separately. Start the lift at $\lfloor x^{1/10}\rfloor$ and keep only the usual lower and upper exposed prefix constraints from there through the precision $x-1$. They are all imposed by $K_{x-1}$ at large $x$. At the final precision omit the new deletion. The initial monitoring date $p+2\lfloor x^{1/5}\rfloor$ and its switch at $p+\lfloor x^{1/5}\rfloor$ precede $x$ for all sufficiently large $x$. Thus all lift and switch estimates apply to the actual terminal density; no padding or terminal trace argument is required. At the base, the fair probability of $|D_p|\ge0.1p-O(x^{1/3})$ is exponentially small by its geometric exponential moment. The adjustable errors give (3).

## Finite-dimensional bridge laws

Fix dates $0<t_1<\cdots<t_r<1$ and a Lipschitz test $0\le\phi\le1$ of the corresponding scaled partial sums. Put $p_i=\lfloor nt_i\rfloor$, $Q=\lfloor n^{1/10}\rfloor$, $\ell=\lfloor n^{1/5}\rfloor$. Let $L_\phi(z)$ denote its limiting Gaussian-bridge expectation in (1). At the terminal density impose both $K_n$ and the exact total $s=s_n$. Then
\[
  \frac{3^n}{\pi_n(s)}
  \mathbb E_0[1_{\{\beta_n=a\bmod3^n,\ S_n=s\}}1_{K_n}
                 \phi(D_{p_1}/\sqrt n,\ldots,D_{p_r}/\sqrt n)]
             \longrightarrow h(a)P_{h,a}(G_I)L_\phi(z).             \tag{4}
\]
The two-sided chronology of the fixed-root Brownian proof applies with initial dates $p_i+2\ell$ switched back to $p_i$ at $p_i+\ell$. Within each group of prefixes of depth $d$ and total $A$, the residual slice is the single prescribed total $s-A$. Its oscillation bound is still at most $J_b(k-u)^{-b}$ from (A.6), since it is a nonnegative summand in the bound summed over totals. The prefix grouping, fixed ordinary endpoint count and signed lift errors are otherwise unchanged. Each signed lift error summed over all precisions is $O_{a,I,A}(n^{-A})$ for every $A$, hence $o(\pi_n(s))$.

At a switch the bad unconditioned length-$2\ell$ increment costs $O_A(n^{-A})$. On its complement the Lipschitz change is $O_\phi(n^{-1/6})$. This multiplies a density at the switching precision $x$ bounded by $\rho_x(a)\sup_\tau\pi_{n-x}(\tau)=O_{a,t_r}(n^{-1/2})$: the remaining letters, before imposing their total, are independent. For deletion at $x\le n/2$ the same unread-total argument gives the bound $C\Delta_x/\sqrt n$, with the identical unmarked deletion $\Delta_x$ defined before. Thus the sum of these losses after $Q$ is $o_I(n^{-1/2})$. For deletions at $x>n/2$ one may discard the terminal-total constraint and use (3); their sum is also $o_I(n^{-1/2})$.

At base precision $Q$, conditional on any retained prefix with $S_Q\le Q^2$, the remaining total has probability $\pi_{n-Q}(s-S_Q)=\pi_n(s)(1+o(1))$ uniformly. Here $Q^2=o(\sqrt n)$ and both standardized totals tend to $z$, by the elementary local central limit theorem. Given that residual total the fair composition path converges to the variance-two bridge with endpoint $z$, uniformly over these prefixes by the sequential statement in Paper 211 (B.4). Its deterministic initial offset is $o(\sqrt n)$ and all finitely many shifted time fractions tend to their prescribed dates. Prefixes with $S_Q>Q^2$ cost at most $3^Q\Pr_0(S_Q>Q^2)=O_A(n^{-A})$. The base is consequently $r_Q^I\pi_n(s)(L_\phi(z)+o(1))+o(n^{-1/2})$. This proves (4). The endpoint at time one is deterministic and can be adjoined. Setting $\phi=1$ shows that the missing fraction at this exact total tends to $1-P_{h,a}(G_I)$ after (2). Since $P_{h,a}(G_I)\uparrow1$, the full finite-dimensional bridge law follows upon sending $I\to\infty$.

## Tightness at the exact total

Here the normalizer $\pi_n(s)\asymp_z n^{-1/2}$ must also be preserved for running tests. Work on $K_n$ at first. Up through the integer time $\lceil n^{1/3}\rceil$ every $|D_j|=O(n^{1/3})$ on this event, since the total there is at most $3\lceil n^{1/3}\rceil$ and all gaps are positive. For the opposite endpoint choose $\ell'=\lfloor n^{1/12}\rfloor$ and $b'=n^{1/4}$. The maximum absolute increment over the final $3\ell'$ letters from their start exceeds $b'$ with fair probability at most $2\exp[-c\min((b')^2/\ell',b')]$. Its terminal density on $K_n$ and the exact total is $O_a(n)$ times that bound: at $d(n)$ use the ordinary endpoint inequality (2) of the upper comparison for each of the $O(n)$ possible prefix totals, with this bad tail subdensity and all other checks dropped. Hence the final $3\ell'$ dates can be covered by their starting date with error $b'/\sqrt n$ outside $o(\pi_n(s))$ in raw density.

Cover $[0,1]$ by a regular mesh of tiles of length $\Delta=1/r_0$, with fixed positive integer $r_0$. In each tile compare signed increments from its start to all its dates up to $n-3\ell'$, omitting the already bounded initial dates. Time zero is deterministic. Use the upper comparison's one-sided chronology with initial shifts $2\ell'$, per-coordinate slack $b'$ and growing base $\lfloor n^{1/20}\rfloor$. All nonzero dates are at least of order $n^{1/3}$, so $(x-\lfloor0.9x\rfloor)\ge\ell'$ wherever a switch occurs. They all satisfy $p+2\ell'\le n$. No shift or slack is applied to the separately prescribed final total. The proof for a running maximum uses only polynomially many groups; in the middle range beginning at order $n^{1/3}$ the gap in (A.6) is at least $\ell'$, and elsewhere the ordinary gap is at least $x/10$. Keeping the fixed total in the lift as in (4), its summed errors and the rare switch-increment losses are $O_A(n^{-A})$. Dropping each deleted part suffices for the upper estimate. At the base the same conditional composition argument compares with a full fair bridge after an initial deterministic displacement $o(\sqrt n)$. Its time change is uniformly $o(1)$, also covering the deterministic shifts by twice the switch gap. The base local-CLT ratio is bounded uniformly over all prefixes of total at most the square of the base precision; their unconditioned compatible mass is at most the fixed ordinary trace bound. The maximal permitted numerical perturbation in testing any given candidate increment is at most $2b'/\sqrt n$.

Divide the resulting raw bound by $\rho_{n,s_n}(a)\sim h(a)\pi_n(s_n)$, where $\pi_n(s_n)\asymp_z n^{-1/2}$. All exceptional costs above are $o(n^{-1/2})$, and the retained base contributes at most a constant times $\pi_n(s_n)$ times the conditional fair comparison probability. The upper limit of the normalized mass for one tile with oscillation about its start exceeding $\eta>0$ is at most $C_{a,z}$ times the probability that the limiting bridge has the absolute increment maximum at least $\eta/2$ on that tile. This follows by uniform tightness and the closed-set upper bound for the conditional fair composition; using $\eta/2$ absorbs endpoint roundings, omitted intervals and switching slack. For $\Delta$ sufficiently small depending on $\eta,z$, this probability is $\ll\exp[-c\eta^2/\Delta]$: write the bridge as $\sqrt2W_t-\sqrt2tW_1+tz$, and apply reflection to the Brownian increment maximum on that tile plus a Gaussian tail for $\Delta W_1$. Summing over $1/\Delta$ tiles gives a quantity tending to zero. Linear interpolation is covered by adjoining tiles. Finally send $I\to\infty$ using the missing fraction after (4). This proves tightness in $C[0,1]$ and thus (1).

## Initial history and real offset

For any fixed compatible prefix $w$ of length $d$ and total $A$ with positive terminal unit $b$, its conditional probability is exactly
\[
      3^d2^{-A}\,\rho_{n-d,s_n-A}(b)/\rho_{n,s_n}(a)
               \longrightarrow 3^d2^{-A}h(b)/h(a).
\]
This is the canonical mass of that prefix. A prefix ending at a nonunit cannot be continued and its limiting mass is zero. The countable partition at fixed $d$ has limiting mass one, so convergence there is in total variation. Conditional on each fixed unit prefix the remaining path has exactly the same central-slice model at $b$, with standardized endpoint tending to $z$. The fixed initial time and height changes vanish after rescaling. This gives asymptotic independence for each fixed initial history, then for every bounded function of a fixed number of letters by truncating that countable partition.

On $K_n$, for every $k\ge I$ up to $d(n)$ the total omitted real offset between dates $k$ and $d(n)$ is at most $C(3\,2^{-1.9})^k$. Beyond $d(n)$ each additional gap is at least one, so the final segment costs at most $C(3\,2^{-1.9})^{\lfloor0.9n\rfloor}(3/2)^{n-\lfloor0.9n\rfloor}=O(e^{-cn})$. The canonical path itself has $S_d/d\to2$, so its infinite offset exists and the finite prefix offsets tend to it. Approximate by $\beta_k$ in the joint laws, using (4) for the fraction outside $K_n$, then send $k$ and $I$ to infinity in this order. This proves the real-mark statement. These initial and real statistics are explicitly specified; conditioning on a rare terminal sum is not a stable limit against arbitrary full-path events.

If a first visit to a periodic $a$ is required, exclude the single finite initial history equal to its primitive inverse lap. Any later return to $a$ in an outward path must have begun with that unique lap, by the deterministic forward cycle. Its limiting canonical probability is $\lambda<1$. The finite-history independence therefore leaves (1) intact after this exclusion; the initial-history and real-mark laws become their canonical versions under the same exclusion. At a nonperiodic target every visit in the table is already a first visit.
