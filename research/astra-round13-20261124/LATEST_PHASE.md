## PR194. Mahler extension

The new proof extends PR190 beyond primitive or uniform morphisms to actual one-sided fixed words of **every nonidentity nonerasing binary substitution**. The physical maps must have distinct affine fixed points; when their fixed points coincide both blocks are prefixes and periods of the same periodic infinite gap sequence. Thus a genuinely aperiodic physical gap itinerary already excludes this degeneracy. The earlier determinant nonvanishing, physical inverse contraction, finite number-field transfer and height bounds are unchanged. For irreducible imprimitive matrices the first letter switches, preventing a one-sided fixed point. Diagonal matrices yield only eventually periodic fixed words except for the excluded identity.

For triangular M=[a b;0 d] with b>0, the powers in the two coordinate valuations are explicit. When d>a the positive Perron coordinates permit the earlier transverse-ratio proof verbatim. When a≥d≥2 the second coordinate still tends to zero but its height is o(v₂(x_n)). Because there are infinitely many zeros, the coefficient of each fixed X-power in G₁ is a finite polynomial in Y. The first nonzero coefficient polynomial at y_n is nonzero eventually, with valuation O_R(d^n), so the lowest X-term dominates, as required before applying the product formula.

For d=1 strip the finite fixed-one prefix. Nonperiodicity forces a≥2. At zero beginnings the cumulative ones satisfy c(ak+r)=bk+c(k)+e_r. After the displayed positive real algebraic normalization this gives H(z)=P(z)H(z^a), where real normalization serves only to analyze formal coefficients. The orders modulo l of its rational divisor agree under the unramified power map on nonzero finite points. All a^j preimages would have a nonzero order mod l if any did, contradicting finiteness. All orders including infinity are thus multiples of l, so H is rational over the algebraic closure. The eventual exponential-polynomial coefficient representation and f_(ak)=f_k force the finite set of bases to permute under the ath power; it consists of roots of unity, with constant coefficient polynomials. The univariate moving-point height argument now applies. This closes that precise nonprimitive two-letter substitution case. It gives no finite substitution presentation for an arbitrary exceptional ordinary itinerary, and does not encompass codings from larger alphabets.

## Additional deduction from the half-bit cycle clock

Let a primitive positive ordinary cycle have k odd vertices, canonical period L dividing k, total actual gap clock A, α=log₂3, and use x, D, λ, d from RUN_PARTITION. Under the published convergence floor, our 17/50 theorem gives 0<ε=A−kα<1/2. The fixed point satisfies this directly. Sample **cyclic** m-edge windows at a uniform vertex, 1≤m≤k. Let K̄ be the mean carry and β the mean canonical digit. The exact cycle telescope gives 2K̄=A/k−β and β≥1. Conditional entropy bound (2) now has no boundary clipping. Using −log x=2log λ,

    H(G)−H(B) ≤ log D + log λ [2(m+1)K̄ + m(β−1)]
               = log D + log λ [m(α−1)+εm/k+2K̄]
               ≤ log D + log λ [m(α−1)+α].

The terminal mean is retained explicitly. Since cyclic endpoints have exactly the uniform law on the full cycle, the complete affine residue identity and Jensen give the absolute bound

    Col_(3^m)(uniform cycle) ≥ D^(−1) λ^(−α) exp(−H(B)) 3^(−dm).

The constant is independent of source, cycle, and phase. For contracting canonical phases the stronger zero-cost statement holds: all carries vanish, so the physical and canonical block laws coincide. For arbitrary phases H(B)≤log L. Under 3^m>max q the endpoint law has k distinct residues, hence log k≤H(G). Taking m=ceil(log₃(1+max q)) gives 1+max q ≳ (k/L)^(1/d) with an absolute implicit constant. If m>k, exponential growth in k on that branch already gives the polynomial bound after adjusting the constant. A fixed periodic phase permits the same proof with RUN_PARTITION's c(x) in place of d log3 and a phase-dependent constant: mean carry equals (α−β)/2+ε/(2k), so the finite correction costs O_x(1) for m≤k. Thus the 7.36 and 181.74 lower exponents apply to maximum height as a function of k/L for hypothetical cycles in those respective phases as well.

The finite-phase occupation estimate additionally gives sum_(q∈S,q≥M) 1/q ≪ L M^(−8/25) by summing dyadic shells, uniformly for an injective invariant set with at most L phases.
