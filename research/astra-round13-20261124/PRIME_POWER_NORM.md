# Primitive prime-power clocks and structural zero norms

Written result, using PR 191 at 37b3916633cd32a0c173b20bb18ec3a7ec290a63, research/two-sided-cycle-packing-20261124/README.md, TCP-4, equation (9). The polynomial arguments below also stand independently of the determinant interpretation.

Let a cyclic word have p positive gaps summing to A. Write d_i for their prefix sums, with d_0=0, extended by d_(i+p)=d_i+A. Write g=gcd(A,p), a=A/g, b=p/g, and Q(X)=sum_(i=0)^(p-1) 2^(d_i)X^(p-1-i). Define primitive to mean that the cyclic gap word has least period p. The top factor has complex roots z with z^b=2^a ξ, for ξ a primitive g-th root of unity; using ξ inverse gives the same set. TCP-4 identifies a vanishing determinant J_g with a common root of this factor and Q.

**Theorem.** Let g=l^s with l prime, s≥1. Outside the case g=4 with b even, J_g=0 holds exactly when the gaps have period dividing p/l.

**Proof for odd l.** Fix ξ and let K=Q(ξ). At a prime of K above 2 the normalized additive valuation satisfies v(2)=1: 2 does not divide the odd conductor, so is unramified (equivalently, the order generated by ξ has odd discriminant, since X^g−1 has simple roots modulo 2). Extend v to K(z). The value v(z)=a/b has denominator b in lowest terms, so the ramification degree is divisible by b. Consequently the extension has degree at least b, and the defining binomial gives degree at most b. In particular 1,z^{-1},...,z^{-(b−1)} are linearly independent over K.

Factoring out z^(p−1), and collecting i=r+jb with 0≤r<b, gives for each r the independent coefficient
    B_r(ξ^{-1}),  B_r(Y)=sum_(j=0)^(g-1) 2^(d_(r+jb)−aj)Y^j.
Its coefficients are positive rationals, with negative exponents allowed. Thus Q(z)=0 iff each B_r is divisible by Φ_g(Y). For m=g/l the latter polynomial is 1+Y^m+...+Y^((l−1)m). A quotient has degree <m, hence divisibility says exactly that coefficients of B_r are constant along each congruence class of j modulo m. Injectivity of powers of 2 yields d_(i+bm)=d_i+am for every i, using the cyclic extension at the seam. Taking first differences yields the claimed gap period. Conversely that period implies exactly the prefix-sum relation, since l equal consecutive periods sum to A. The coefficient test gives Q(z)=0.

**Proof for l=2.** Set n=b2^(s−1), M=a2^(s−1). The top factor is X^n+2^M. By the rational binomial irreducibility criterion this is irreducible unless g=4 and b even. Indeed no odd prime dividing n divides M, a negative rational is never a square, and the exceptional condition 4|n with −2^M in −4(Q^×)^4 is equivalent to 4|n and M≡2 modulo 4. For s=1 it would require 4|b with a even, impossible. For s=2 it requires b even, then a is odd automatically. For s≥3 one has 4|M. The criterion is Theorem 1 in Koley and Reddy, “Irreducibility of x^n−a” (2020), https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03787 . Outside the exception, common-root existence is divisibility by X^n+2^M. Reducing Q modulo this monic binomial pairs its coefficients at i and i+n. The remainder vanishes exactly if d_(i+n)=d_i+M for every 0≤i<n, and the cyclic extension gives the same relation for every i. Here n=p/2 and M=A/2. Taking differences and the converse follow as before.

**Sharp exception.** For any k≥0 add k to every gap of (1,1,3,2,4,4,3,2). All resulting words have g=4, b=2, a=5+2k and positive D=2^A−3^p. They have primitive period eight: the only proper divisors of eight divide four, and the first and fifth gaps differ. The base prefixes are (0,1,2,5,7,11,15,18). At t=2^k the shifted carry polynomial Q and top factor X^4+1024t^4 have the common monic factor X²+8tX+32t². The factorizations appear in CycleNormComplete.lean. Thus an infinite primitive contracting family has J_g=0. For k=0 the complete cycle numerator and denominator give 389545/1042015=5993/16031; this is a positive rational cycle, and gives no positive integer counterexample.

**Residual denominator at zero norms.** Let U=X^p−2^A and G be the monic rational polynomial gcd of U and Q. It is integral and divides both in Z[X], by Gauss's lemma. Put V=U/G and B=Q/G. Then deg V≥1, gcd(V,B)=1 over Q, and Res(V,B) is a nonzero integer in the integral ideal generated by V and B (including the case of a constant B). For D>0, G(3)≠0. Cancelling that single common factor from Q(3)/(-U(3)) gives exact reduced denominator |V(3)|/gcd(V(3),B(3)); its denominator is therefore divisible by |V(3)|/gcd(V(3),Res(V,B)). This uses the complete carry of the same word and retains overlapping primes under evaluation. It does not imply that this divisor exceeds one. Any uniform strict saving on the residual full denominator remains open.

The explicit polynomial identities, three integral Bézout certificates, base numerical identity, finite residue argument modulo 105 and parametric relative primality of the full residual numerator and denominator are kernel-checked in CycleNormComplete.lean. The prime-power structure theorem, its hypotheses and its residual resultant corollary are written proofs.

**Exact complete denominator of every exceptional family member.** Set t=2^k. Cancelling F=32t²+24t+9 leaves numerator B=243−486t+540t²+576t³−3072t⁴+8192t⁵ and denominator V=V₁V₂V₃ with V₁=32t²−9, V₂=32t²+9, V₃=32t²−24t+9. All three factors are positive. The three integral Bézout identities in CycleNormComplete.lean show that gcd(B,V₁) and gcd(B,V₃) divide 5103=7·3⁶, while gcd(B,V₂) divides 1215=5·3⁵. No factor V_i is divisible by 3, since t is a power of two. V₂ cannot be 0 modulo 5, since that would require the square t² to be 3 modulo 5. For the remaining prime 7, t modulo 7 runs over 1,2,4 and B modulo 7 takes respectively 1,1,5. Thus gcd(B,V)=1 and V itself is the complete reduced denominator for every k≥0. In particular V≥16031 and increases with k. This is a family with provable full residual survival despite its identically vanishing top norm.

**Uniform-shift denominator theorem for any fixed seed word.** Keep the positive gaps and t=2^k, now for an arbitrary seed. Increasing every gap by k replaces d_i by d_i+ki, A by A+kp, and gives U_t(X)=t^pU(X/t), Q_t(X)=t^(p−1)Q(X/t). Form G,V,B once from the seed as above. Both U and Q are monic; if m=deg V, then m≥1 and n=deg B=m−1. Homogenize the three factors separately as G_t(X)=t^(deg G)G(X/t), V_t(X)=t^mV(X/t), B_t(X)=t^nB(X/t). Their coefficients are integral and U_t=G_tV_t, Q_t=G_tB_t.

Let R be the positive part of |Res(V,B)| coprime to 6, obtained by deleting the 2- and 3-powers. The scaling law gives Res(V_t,B_t)=t^(mn)Res(V,B), also when B=1. This follows immediately from the product of the differences of the roots, with monic factors. The positive integer c_k=gcd(V_t(3),B_t(3)) divides that resultant, hence divides R: V_t(3) divides 3^p−2^At^p, which is coprime to 6. Thus c_k=gcd(R,V_t(3),B_t(3)). Both evaluations are integer polynomials in t, so c_k is periodic starting at k=0, with period dividing the multiplicative order of 2 modulo R (use period 1 for R=1). For the parameter values with 2^At^p>3^p the complete denominator is |V_t(3)|/c_k. On each residue class of k modulo this period, its growth as t→∞ is asymptotic to |V(0)|t^m/c_k. The constant is positive since U(0)=−2^A. The family therefore has an explicit exponent and an exactly periodic bounded cancellation factor after removal of structural polynomial factors. This result allows arbitrary positive seed gap words and any gcd of their clocks. It supplies no bound uniform over changing seed words.

**Independent gap increments on an arbitrary affine ray.** Let the individual gaps grow by k times prescribed u_i≥0, with U=sum u_i>0 and e_i=sum_(j<i)u_j. For t=2^k take F(t)=2^A t^U−3^p and H(t)=sum 2^(d_i)3^(p−1−i)t^(e_i). Factor F=GV,H=GB over Z[t], taking G the primitive rational polynomial gcd with positive leading coefficient. This choice gives integer V,B by Gauss's lemma. Since gcd(V,B)=1 over Q, clearing denominators in a rational Bézout identity produces integer polynomials P,S and a positive constant K with PV+SB=K. The argument works for constant residual polynomials as well. Delete the 2- and 3-parts of K to obtain R. Then for every k≥0 the complete reduced denominator is
    |V(2^k)| / gcd(R,V(2^k),B(2^k)).
Indeed evaluation of the identity bounds the common divisor by K, and V(2^k) divides F(2^k), which is coprime to 6. This cancellation factor is periodic from k=0 with period dividing ord_R(2), again taking period one for R=1.

Put h=gcd(U,A,p). The positive real zero t₀=(3^p/2^A)^(1/U) of F cannot cancel because H(t₀)>0. Its minimal polynomial has degree U/h: in Q(t₀), valuations extending the 2-adic and 3-adic valuations give denominators U/gcd(A,U) and U/gcd(p,U), respectively. More directly, avoiding assumptions about individual local degrees dividing the global degree, take the field norm in t₀^U=3^p/2^A. Writing n=[Q(t₀):Q], the rational norm has integral prime valuations, so U divides both An and pn, forcing U/h to divide n. Conversely 2^(A/h)t₀^(U/h)=3^(p/h) gives degree at most U/h. This minimal polynomial divides V, giving deg V≥U/h, and deg V=U whenever h=1. The complete denominator has growth rate Θ(2^(k deg V)). This covers rays on which some unit gaps stay unit.

The integer-cycle finiteness conclusion for a fixed ray alone has an elementary proof: rotate a growing gap to the final position. At that distinguished boundary the numerator's degree in t is smaller than U, so its positive rational source tends to zero. In particular for the sharp example every gap is at least two after k≥1. The contribution here is the exact residual-denominator behavior and the primitive zero-norm boundary, rather than a uniform ordinary-cycle exclusion.

**Exponent bound from primitivity in the uniform-shift theorem.** For a primitive seed with g=l^s outside the g=4, b even exception, the residual degree m is at least b(1+φ(g)). Indeed X^b−2^a is the minimal polynomial of the positive real root and cannot divide Q, whose coefficients are positive. Its degree b follows by the norm argument at 2. The top cyclotomic factor is irreducible of degree bφ(g): for odd l adjoining z above adjoins ξ=z^b/2^a first and has relative degree b as proved above, and for l=2 the binomial proof gives this directly. The structural theorem excludes this second, distinct factor from the gcd as well. Thus both factors survive. In particular for prime g the residual degree equals p, so a primitive seed with prime clock gcd has zero structural cancellation anywhere; the full denominator under all common shifts grows like a nonzero periodic multiple of 2^(kp). For the exceptional seed above the residual degree is 6 out of 8, agreeing with the explicit formula.
