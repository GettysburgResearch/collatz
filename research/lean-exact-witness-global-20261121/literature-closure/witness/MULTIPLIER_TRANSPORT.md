# Trying to lift the semigroup proof to actual witnesses

**Status:** two necessary clock theorems are kernel-checked. The proposed
global multiplier-to-merger construction remains OPEN. This is a direct
test of one way to import the semigroup proof, not a proof of Collatz and
not an obstruction to source-dependent, arbitrarily long certificates.

The map throughout is the ordinary shortcut map

\[
T(n)=\begin{cases}n/2&2\mid n,\\(3n+1)/2&2\nmid n.\end{cases}
\]

## 1. The global literature theorem and the proposed bridge

Applegate and Lagarias, *The 3x+1 Semigroup*, arXiv:math/0411140v2,
Theorem 1.1, classify the multiplicative semigroup generated by 2 and
\((2j+1)/(3j+2)\). Section 2 uses multiplier-assisted descent, and Lemma
3.1 supplies the multipliers

\[
H=\{5,7,11,13,23,29,43\}.
\]

This suggested the following actual-orbit bridge: replace multiplication
by an element of H with a proof that the input and its multiple meet under
ordinary Collatz iteration. With such a replacement, a decreasing modified
path could transfer convergence back to the original source.

The bridge must retain physical clocks on both sides. Membership of a
rational number in the multiplicative semigroup supplies a product of
generators, but does not supply those clocks. The paper itself explicitly
distinguishes its product representations from ordinary iteration.

Primary text read directly:
<https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0411140v2>, Theorem 1.1, Section 2, and Lemma 3.1.
The author-hosted copy has a broken text layer; the arXiv copy was used for
the readable statement and proof. No result from the paper is a Lean axiom.

## 2. Exact fixed-word rigidity

For any actual finite parity history of length k and odd count p,

\[
2^kT^k(z)=3^p z+C,\qquad 0\le C\le3^k-2^k. \tag{MT1}
\]

The correction satisfies C_0=0 and

\[
C_{j+1}=\begin{cases}C_j&e_j=0,\\3C_j+2^j&e_j=1.\end{cases}
\]

Induction gives the bound in (MT1), including at k=0. Both the coefficient
and correction are those of the actual integer orbit.

Suppose fixed physical words, with data (k,p,C) and (l,q,D), give

\[
T^k(mn+b)=T^l(n).
\]

Cross multiplication gives

\[
(m3^p2^l-3^q2^k)n
=2^kD-2^l(3^p b+C). \tag{MT2}
\]

If this same word pair works at two distinct inputs n, subtracting the
two equations forces

\[
m3^p2^l=3^q2^k. \tag{MT3}
\]

Consequently the slope m is of the form \(2^u3^v\), with integer u,v.
For a positive integral m, all its prime factors are 2 or 3. This applies
to affine lifts as well as pure multipliers; allowing a fixed translation
b does not repair an inadmissible slope. It is a written deduction from
(MT2), not one of the two separately counted Lean declarations below.

The observation applies to a fixed ordinary merger identity on an
infinite congruence class. It does not prohibit a different word pair
for every integer, which is precisely the freedom allowed by the original
question.

## 3. A uniform quantitative theorem for all actual words

The formal result is stronger than a fixed-word test. Let m have a divisor
d>1 with gcd(d,6)=1. If actual independent clocks k,l satisfy

\[
T^k(mn)=T^l(n),\qquad k\le N,\quad l\le N,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{n<6^N.}\tag{MT4}
\]

No parity words are fixed in advance. They may depend on m,n,k,l. Every
multiplier in H satisfies the divisor hypothesis by taking d=m.

**Proof.** Set

\[
a=m3^p2^l,\quad c=3^q2^k,\quad B=2^lC,\quad D'=2^kD.
\]

Equation (MT2) is \(an+B=cn+D'\). The correction bound gives
\(0\le B,D'<6^N\). The coefficients a and c differ: if they were equal,
d would divide \(3^q2^k\), contradicting gcd(d,6)=1 and d>1.

If n were at least \(6^N\), then both B and D' would be strictly less
than n. Uniqueness of quotient and remainder in division by n would force
a=c, a contradiction. This proves (MT4).

The theorem is `CollatzMultiplierTransport.merger_clock_budget` in
[`MultiplierTransport.lean`](../../lean/Collatz/MultiplierTransport.lean).
It imports the existing exact actual-orbit affine identity and proves the
correction bound, coefficient separation and remainder argument in Lean.

The second formal theorem, `no_uniform_merger_clock`, takes n=\(6^N\)
and proves

\[
\neg\exists N\;\forall n>0\;\exists k,l\le N:
T^k(mn)=T^l(n). \tag{MT5}
\]

A finite menu of fixed physical merger paths has a maximum clock, so it
cannot implement any of the wild prime multipliers on all positive
sources. The result does not assert that a merger fails when clocks are
unbounded, nor does it impose a uniform bound on the allowed output
certificate in the original problem.

## 4. A positive control: a legal multiplier really can merge

The restriction to slopes generated by 2 and 3 is necessary. It is not a
claim that all nontrivial multiplier mergers are impossible. For every
integer t>=0, put n=32t+23. The two actual five-clock paths are

\[
\begin{aligned}
n&\to48t+35\to72t+53\to108t+80\to54t+40\to27t+20,\\
3n&\to144t+104\to72t+52\to36t+26\to18t+13\to27t+20.
\end{aligned}
\]

Their words are respectively 11100 and 10001. Their odd counts are 3 and
2; both full corrections are 19. Thus the common slope identity in
(MT3) is satisfied, and

\[
T^5(n)=T^5(3n)=27t+20<n.
\]

This all-parameter identity is independently displayed arithmetic, not
part of the two-theorem Lean count. It is also an early-descent family,
so it supplies no least-counterexample obstruction by itself.

## 5. The attempted closing step

The semigroup argument remains useful as a design for a global descent
cover, but its multiplier steps require a new source-dependent compiler.
The exact new requirement is to construct independent ordinary meeting
clocks, with unbounded dependence on the input and complete supplied
witness, while preserving a well-founded proof dependency for the whole
modified path. No such compiler is constructed here.

This branch therefore does not claim that the semigroup theorem proves
the original implication. It records the exact ordinary arithmetic that a
successful import has to supply, and proves a quantitative constraint that
prevents a finite table of merger paths from being mistaken for that import.
