## Why the new basin argument is not the forbidden basin substitution

The earlier catalog warns that a sparse orbit can have a large predecessor
basin. That warning remains valid. The new argument does not count the
sources in a basin by counting the orbit. It counts **different disjoint
basins** that each have a small source.

Each such basin has a distinct least source. If that least source descends
by a chosen short clock, its path must already have passed through the
basin's own root; otherwise the smaller endpoint would be a smaller
ancestor of that root. The growth bound controls the height of the root,
where the sparse-orbit theorem applies. The remaining least sources are
counted by actual parity cylinders. The two injections have separate
targets and do not lose a multiplicity factor.

This ordinary first-hit implication is the new ingredient. It is not
supplied by an averaged random law, a measure-zero exception argument,
or a congruence satisfied by changing finite sources.

## A direct full-route attempt: repair a bounded rational leaf

The stronger pressure consumer suggests a concrete arithmetic target.
On one hypothetical escaping odd ray, write its actual length-j gap data as

\[
2^{A_j}q_j=3^j q_0+C_j.
\]

Apply the **same complete inverse word** formally to the sibling
\(Sq_j=64q_j+21\). Its rational source is

\[
y_j=64q_0+63\frac{C_j}{3^j}+21\frac{2^{A_j}}{3^j}.
\tag{RS1}
\]

These rational sources are bounded in the real metric, uniformly in j.
Indeed the exact product along the original orbit gives

\[
\frac{2^{A_i}}{3^i}
=\frac{q_0}{q_i}\prod_{k<i}\left(1+\frac1{3q_k}\right).
\]

The inherited distinct-orbit reciprocal budget bounds the product and
makes the sum over i finite. Since
\(C_j/3^j=(1/3)\sum_{i<j}2^{A_i}/3^i\), it converges; the last term
in (RS1) tends to zero. This is a statement about one fixed actual source,
not an independent random series.

The missing operation is to repair this rational inverse history to an
**ordinary integer ancestor of the same fixed sibling** at controlled
height. The new basin-minimum theorem proves the following precise
consumer: if such a repair always supplied ancestors
\(n_j=O((j+1)^d)\) for some \(d<800/799\), the hypothetical escape
would be impossible. More generally the reciprocal source budget is
\(\sum_j1/n_j\le9600\), so the bound
\(O((j+1)\log(j+2))\) would also contradict it.

Keeping the original inverse word cannot do this repair by itself. Its
ordinary source condition is that the new root agree with \(q_j\)
modulo \(3^j\). For the more distant sibling \(S^bq_j\),

\[
S^bq_j-q_j=\frac{(64^b-1)(3q_j+1)}3,
\qquad
v_3(S^bq_j-q_j)=1+v_3(b).
\tag{RS2}
\]

The last equality follows directly from
\(v_3(64^b-1)=2+v_3(b)\). Thus the unedited word is integral exactly
when \(3^{j-1}\mid b\), for j>=1. At the fixed sibling b=1 it already
fails at every j>=2. Choosing a distant sibling with this divisibility
changes the root and incurs an exponentially large index; it does not
provide the demanded repair for b=1. This sharpens the interpretation of
the older large-congruence-shift attempt, rather than treating that old
construction as new.

The balanced inverse compiler from the previous checkpoint supplies actual
integer repairs, but only a per-edge exponent-cost bound. The new pressure
note proves a necessary accumulated cost on that unchanged compiler:
\(\sum_j2^{-t\Delta_j}<\infty\) for every t>799/800. A bounded real
unrepaired leaf therefore cannot be used as evidence that the integer
repair has low cost. No sublinear or near-linear repair bound was obtained
in this pass.
