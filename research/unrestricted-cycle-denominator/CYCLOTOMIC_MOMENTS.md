# Finite cyclotomic tests for an ordinary cycle have exact countermodels

For a finite set S of positive integers, use the ordinary shortcut
map T and define the polynomial

    D_S(z) = Σ_(n∈S) (z^(2n) − z^(2T(n))).

Identical vanishing says precisely that T permutes S. In particular
a single ordinary cycle satisfies this polynomial identity.

**Theorem.** Fix Q≥1 and k≥0. There is C=C(Q,k) such that for arbitrarily
large X one can choose a nonempty finite set S of distinct ordinary
positive integers, all prime to 3 and all in [X,CX], with

    (z^Q−1)^(k+1) divides D_S(z),       D_S(z) ≠ 0.                 (1)

In fact the sets are evaluations of a fixed finite list of positive-slope
integer affine functions at every sufficiently large integer parameter.
The flow equality therefore holds through derivative order k at every
Q-th root of unity, including the real polynomial moments at 1. It
holds with unit mass per state and with the actual next integer at each
state. The full pointwise flow equality and a single ordered cyclic
itinerary are stronger requirements.

## Reachability of the finite label graph

Replace Q by H=lcm(Q,6) as a bookkeeping modulus. Its vertices are
the residue classes modulo H prime to 3, and its edges are r→s whenever
arbitrarily large n≡r (mod H) satisfy T(n)≡s (mod H). Every vertex
has definite parity and hence a definite slope h_r=1/2 or 3/2.

This graph is strongly connected. Write H=2^a 3^b m with gcd(m,6)=1.
From a given starting residue choose its first a shortcut letters:
the parity vector through these a steps is determined by its binary
residue. Conversely any prescribed parity word of length l is realized
on one class modulo 2^l, by solving the odd-slope affine recursion
one new binary digit at a time. After the fixed prefix one may prescribe
any finite continuation. At the end, adding 2^l 3^b m to the original
source, where l is the entire word length, shifts the terminal state
by 3^w 3^b m with w the word weight. This realizes any requested
terminal binary class without changing either odd-part calculation.

For the terminal ternary residue choose a suffix containing b odd steps
that sends every source to this residue modulo 3^b. Start from any
positive odd representative of the desired ternary unit and take b
inverse odd steps. At each step two admissible parities of exponent
in the following sense suffice: choose the two numbers among 1,...,4
with the required parity. If the smaller exponent gives an inverse
multiple of three, increasing it by two gives four times that inverse
plus one. Thus one can keep all inverse states positive odd units.
Reading the shortcut letters forwards supplies the desired suffix:
its numerator slope is divisible by 3^b and its denominator is a
power of two.

Modulo m both E(x)=x/2 and O(x)=(3x+1)/2 are permutations. Their
finite permutation semigroup is a group. The two composites EO and OE
differ by translation by 1/4, so the group contains this translation,
which is transitive on residues modulo m. Between the binary prefix
and ternary suffix insert a word that routes the prefix's m-residue to
the inverse image of the desired m-residue under the suffix. Ordinary
source lifts exist by the Chinese remainder theorem and can be taken
arbitrarily large. This proves the graph claim.

## A finite positive balance of mixed polynomial moments

Take pairs (u,v), where u is any positive multiple of 2H and v any
positive ternary unit. For p,h≥0 with p+h≤k, and each vertex r, form
the integer coordinate

    d_(p,h,r)(u,v) =
       u^p v^h 1_(v≡r) − (h_v u)^p T(v)^h 1_(T(v)≡r).             (2)

Here h_v denotes the slope of v's parity. Real linear functionals on
these vectors are differences of polynomials V_r(u,v) on residue
classes, of total degree at most k. We show that every functional
nonnegative on all the vectors vanishes on them. Reversing signs of
V if necessary, its hypothesis reads

    V_s(h_r u,T(v)) − V_r(u,v) ≥ 0,  r=v mod H, s=T(v) mod H.

Take the greatest degree p in u and let A_r(v) be its coefficient
on class r, zero if absent. Sending u→∞ gives
h_r^p A_s(T(v))−A_r(v)≥0. Let h be the greatest degree across these
coefficient polynomials and a_r the coefficient of v^h. Along any
edge send v→∞ within that edge. This gives

    h_r^(p+h) a_s ≥ a_r.                                           (3)

If p+h>0, the all-odd loop at the residue −1 gives a_(-1)≥0.
The ordinary 1→2→1 loop with slope product 3/4 gives a_1≤0.
Strong connectivity supplies paths −1→r→1, forcing a_r=0
for every r. This contradicts the choice of the largest nonzero
coefficient. If p=h=0, the potential is constant on each class
and its monotonicity on the strongly connected graph forces one
common constant. The zero potential also vanishes on all differences.

Consequently the closed positive cone generated by the integer
vectors (2) is their real span, by finite-dimensional separation.
The positive cone itself equals that span: a convex set and its
closure have the same relative interior in their affine hull.
Thus the negative of any chosen vector is a finite nonnegative
combination of such vectors. This combination may be taken rational
by feasibility of a finite rational linear system (or by reducing to
independent columns). There exist finitely many distinct pairs
(u_i,v_i) and positive integers m_i, not all absent, satisfying

    Σ_i m_i d(u_i,v_i)=0.                                          (4)

The univariate special case also shows directly that an eventually
weakly monotone quasipolynomial under T on all ordinary ternary units
must be constant there.

## Removing multiplicities

For each pair write n_i(t)=v_i+u_i t. Its input residue modulo H
is constant, as is the residue of its output T(v_i)+h_(v_i)u_i t.
The binomial theorem and (4) give, identically in t, equality of
the weighted sums of every tested terminal and initial power j≤k
within each residue class.

Let B=max m_i. For B≥2, partition the integers 0,...,B^(k+1)−1
into B classes according to the sum of their base-B digits modulo B.
The sums of j-th powers, including j=0, coincide across classes for
every j≤k. Indeed for any nontrivial B-th root ξ the generating
polynomial weighted by ξ to the digit sum factors as

    ∏_(l=0)^k (Σ_(a=0)^(B−1) ξ^a z^(a B^l)).

Each factor vanishes at 1, giving all the asserted moment equalities
by finite Fourier inversion. For B=1 take just the singleton {0}.

For pair i choose any m_i of the classes and use each member t of
those classes once. Evaluate n_i(N+Jt) at these parameters, where
J is an integer greater than every |v_i−v_j|. For any polynomial
of degree ≤k, its sums at N+Jt over the classes agree. Linearity
and (4) therefore preserve every residue-wise moment balance.
For equal slopes u_i=u_j, two different parameter indices cannot
give the same exponent, by the choice of J; equal parameter indices
could coincide only for identical pairs. For distinct slopes there
are only finitely many exceptional N. Thus the resulting values
form a set S_N, with no repetitions, for all sufficiently large N.
Its least and largest members are between fixed positive multiples
of N.

Residue-wise balances at all degrees through k give vanishing of
(z d/dz)^j D_(S_N)(z) at each Q-th root for 0≤j≤k. This implies (1)'s
divisibility. To see that the entire polynomial is nonzero for large N,
suppose infinitely many parameters gave a permutation of S_N by T.
One permutation of the fixed finite index set would recur infinitely
often. Comparing affine slopes in N around any cycle of that permutation
would give 2^l=3^w for a positive length l and an integer w≥0, an
impossibility. Thus at most finitely many parameters give invariant sets.

## Exact and approximate Laplace evaluations

For fixed 0<|z|<1 these same noninvariant sets satisfy
|D_(S_N)(z)|≤2|S_N| |z|^(min S_N), exponentially small in N, since
2T(n)≥n. Exact evaluation at a sufficiently small positive real
point is much stronger. An integer has at most two shortcut preimages,
so the coefficients in Σ_(n∈S)(w^n−w^T(n)) lie between −2 and 1.
At the smallest exponent with nonzero coefficient the remaining
finite tail for 0<w≤1/3 has absolute value strictly less than w
to that smallest exponent. Consequently already D_S(1/2)=0 is
equivalent to full flow equality. This equivalence supplies no
inequality deciding whether a nontrivial ordinary cycle exists.
