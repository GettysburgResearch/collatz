# Nonzero bounded-lag mergers before the typical descent clock are harmonically summable

**Proposed, source-qualified.** Use the ordinary shortcut map T, and fix an integer D≥1. A nonzero bounded-lag merger for the two starts 2n and 2n+1 is an equality T^i(2n)=T^j(2n+1) with 1≤|i−j|≤D. Write μ=2/log(4/3), with natural logarithms. For every 0<η<μ, the set of n admitting such a merger with max(i,j)≤(μ−η)log n has finite reciprocal sum. Thus a harmonic divergence among pairs that never meet at equal clocks persists among pairs that fail to merge at any lag of size at most D through this entire subtypical clock. The infinite-time bounded-lag problem requires information beyond this horizon.

Choose a fixed s<1 with 0<s and μs>μ−η. Let τ_s(x) be the first shortcut passage to at most x^(1−s), capped at first attainment of the global minimum if that passage never occurs. Leen's fixed-s moderate deviation theorem controls its early Gaussian tail through K√(log x log log x) for every fixed K. In particular for every fixed A>0 the early event τ_s(x)≤(μ−η)log x has proportion O_A((log X)^(−A)) on [X,2X]: it is contained, for large X, in the lower tail at −K√log log x in standardized units. Use the full initial-interval estimate at 2X to bound the shell, or use the paper's harmonic integral test. The same upper bound holds for starts 2n and 2n+1 by positivity after counting the full comparable shell. Since log n≤log(2n), it bounds the tested early clocks as well. Choosing A>1 and summing over dyadic shells gives finite reciprocal mass. [Leen, *Brownian motion in Collatz orbits*, fixed-interior moderate deviations and harmonic integral test](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23252395)

Off those early events both segments have all their states larger than n^(1−s). For the path of length i from a start x, let q count its odd inputs. Its logarithmic displacement is

    log(T^i(x)/x) = q log 3 − i log 2 + Σ_(odd inputs u) log(1+1/(3u)).

The correction in each tested segment is O(log n / n^(1−s)). The two starts have log-ratio O(1/n). If they meet, subtracting the identities shows that (q−q′)log 3−(i−j)log 2 tends uniformly to zero. For 1≤|i−j|≤D its absolute value has a strictly positive lower bound over all integer q−q′. For each of the finitely many nonzero lags choose the nearest integral multiple of log 3 and use unique factorization. This contradiction excludes every tested nonzero lag for all sufficiently large remaining n, even if a segment repeats a vertex.

There is also an elementary obstruction to a finite dyadic-cylinder argument. At fixed unequal clocks the two affine maps in n have different slopes; equality would require a nonzero power of 2 to equal a power of 3. Each common parity cylinder therefore gives at most one ordinary n and zero dyadic Haar mass. Testing all nonzero lags of absolute value at most D up to clock L gives O_D(2^L) possible n in total: at a fixed larger clock h the number of joint cylinders is at most 2^h and there are at most 2D shorter-clock choices. Hence the extra nonzero-lag mergers before log₂ n−(1+ε)log₂ log₂ n form a harmonically summable set already by this elementary count.
