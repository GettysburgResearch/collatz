# What the exact two-head lines imply about multiplicative rank

**PROPOSED, conditional on the pinned PR197 return and reverse-incidence estimates.** Write $d=\log_2 R$ and use the two narrower slabs in its joint-midpoint theorem. For each surviving returned pair of genuine smaller heads, the exact branch has $2^v z_2-3^w z_1=c$ with $|c|\ll_A 2^v$, $.009d-O(1)\le v\le .015d+O(1)$ and $4\le w\le v$. The shifts there preserve $c$ exactly. [PR197 at 2c1397d, joint target midpoints](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/2c1397d2adcadc620381bca20c465f3bf31a6f8e/research/ayh-resource-reset-20261124/crossing-step/JOINT_TARGET_MIDPOINTS.md)

All original sources having even one $c=0$ pair can be added to the stated $O_A(R d^{-A})$ exceptional set. For fixed labels $(v,w)$ and first dyadic row $z_1\asymp Y$, exact proportionality forces $2^v\mid z_1$, and determines $z_2$. The number of such $z_1$ in the row is $O(Y2^{-v}+1)$. At each target pair PR197 bounds the number of original sources by $O_A(R\sqrt{d\log d}/Y)$ even with the weaker raw-strip version. There are polynomially many rows and labels and $Y\ge 2^{.37d}$ for large $d$ in these slabs. Their total preimage count is $R 2^{-\Omega(d)}$. No assumption of independently sampled arms is made. [PR197, reverse count](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/2c1397d2adcadc620381bca20c465f3bf31a6f8e/research/ayh-resource-reset-20261124/crossing-step/WEIGHTED_TWO_HEAD_GRAPH.md)

On each remaining returned branch PR197 gives $M\ge 2^{.01d}\exp(-O_A((\log(d+2))^{12}))$ distinct joint neighbors, up to an absolute factor for the floor. Use just the positive shifts and fix one returned pair as base $(b_1,b_2)$. Let $r$ be the rank of the group generated in $(\mathbb Q_{>0}^\times)^2$ by coordinatewise ratios $(z_1/b_1,z_2/b_2)$ over the selected points. The vectors $(-3^w z_1/c,2^v z_2/c)$ satisfy $X+Y=1$ in a group of rank at most $r+1$ (adjoin one coefficient vector). Each pair is distinct; hence Beukers–Schlickewei gives $M\le2^{8r+16}$. In particular $r\ge .01d/8-o(d)$ for the neighbors on **each exact branch** that survives. The same inequality with the actual sample size holds for any subcollection. [Beukers and Schlickewei, Theorem 1.1](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa78/aa7826.pdf)

More generally restrict the graph to two specified cosets of respective ranks $r_1,r_2$. At fixed row, labels and nonzero $c$, the point count is at most $2^{8(r_1+r_2)+16}$. There are $O_A(2^v)$ possible $c$. Dividing the count by $Y$ bounds the harmonic weight; adding $c=0$ as above and summing polynomially many labels shows exponentially small weight when, for example, $r_1+r_2\le d/25$. This bound is uniform over the two cosets and cannot be summed over arbitrarily many different choices. It bounds only the edge sum from those specified cosets in the stated narrow slabs, with the genuine PR197 rounding constant.

The published parallel-neighbor property therefore requires high joint rank on its individual populated lines. The fact that $3^w/2^v$ belongs to a rank-two group controls the slope only. Even the simplest physical relation $2y-3x=1$ supports arbitrarily long injective all-odd segments: $x_i=2^{N+1-i}3^i-1$ for $0\le i\le N$. Their joint ratios already have unbounded rank by the same two-term result. These are changing ordinary starts. For coupled inverse fibers with fixed endpoints at grade $p$, the affine carry has $p$ positive summands. The unit-equation bound in [the window theorem](WINDOWS.md) is not uniform in that growing dimension. In particular fixing the eventual values of two arms does not place the interior of either actual trajectory in a specified low-rank coset. The color restriction or the physical history would have to supply an opposing rank bound; none follows from the affine slope or the endpoint alone.
