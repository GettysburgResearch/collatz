# Cross-check of the paired fixed-endpoint argument

**VERIFIED AS A DEDUCTION FROM THE STATED ANALYTIC INPUTS.** For the [fixed-clock theorem](../pressure/README.md), Weaver's Lemma 13 equation (39) retains the exact total and the maximum of all prefix multipliers. It bounds the final offset, which bounds earlier offsets since they increase. Thus the chosen bridge ensures that every positive-depth inverse state lies above the original target. A fixed-depth word has weight $2^{-d}$ and a unique integer decoding, so the count uses the actual source pool. The central shell and first-landing disjointness used in the adjacent-depth refinement agree with the proof of Weaver's Theorem 14. Switching a source divisible by three to $4n+1$ adds exactly two shortcut clocks before the same next odd state. Its multiplier is divided by four and its offset decreases by the new multiplier. The two repaired groups lie in disjoint intervals when needed for the shell count. [Weaver, Lemma 13 and Theorem 14](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/12)

The step intersecting the first-arm pool with Shaik's estimate uses the injective map $x\mapsto5x$. The exceptional fraction $O((\log X)^{-D})$ absorbs the first pool's $p^{-1/2}$ cost. In the proof at (6.32)–(6.33), Shaik's witness is a first passage at the final threshold, and the grade control is obtained at that same clock. Since its threshold tends to infinity, the subsequent first visit to the fixed floor cannot precede that clock. The upper deadline therefore transfers both the clock and the grade estimates, with at most one extra odd input per elapsed shortcut step. Choosing a single bounded target on infinitely many scales is a finite pigeonhole even if the best target changes at other scales. Only a joint basin with positive *upper* density for the selected target follows. [Shaik, proof of Proposition 6.4](https://shaikidris.github.io/polylog/paper/)

For the [inverse-ray theorem](../pressure/INVERSE_RAYS.md), a nonperiodic root gives disjoint basins at each fixed-depth antichain. The canonical weights of low-profile vertices are at most a constant divided by their integer height. Finite antichains with definite canonical mass therefore give a source pool whose total proportion is bounded below independently of depth. The common companion floor is chosen from this total before choosing a root-path vertex. On each selected finite antichain the count thresholds may be taken jointly without exchanging a limit over infinitely many vertices. The ratio $d_y/d_a$ is the entire natural-density probability of a depth cylinder at a nonperiodic root. Thus a common lower bound on probabilities at arbitrarily late depths supplies the same lower bound on the limsup event, without assuming independence. Weaver §10.1 gives equivalence on the whole path space. A positive set of nested rays is a stronger quantifier than independent vertices at successive depths. It still gives no shared second-arm witnesses along the ray and does not prescribe the target $5a$. [Weaver, equations (26)–(31) and §10.1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/12)

This checks the deductions and their quantifiers. It does not independently establish the underlying selected-block mixing, relative passage, density-existence, or polylogarithmic first-passage theorems. The [Pell calculation for the two first odd successors](README.md#the-stronger-observable-and-the-two-fixed-endpoint-arms) explains why fixing the final pair cannot alone bound the multiplicative rank of earlier paired products.
