# Which targets do the integers on one head ray visit?

**PROPOSED, with named analytic inputs.** Use the ordinary positive shortcut map $T(2n)=n, T(2n+1)=3n+2$. On one fixed real mantissa $7\le t<8$, put $N_j(t)=\lfloor81^j t\rfloor$. Each is an ordinary positive integer. For almost every $t$, the targets visited by the forward orbits of infinitely many **distinct** $N_j(t)$ are exactly the positive integers $a$ prime to three. The individual orbits witnessing the different targets may be different. The result neither presupposes convergence of those orbits nor produces a single orbit visiting all the targets.

More precisely, fix **any** deterministic strictly increasing infinite sequence of rows $j_i$. For almost every $t$, simultaneously for every positive $a$ prime to three,
$$
   \limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac1m
   \#\{i\le m:\exists k\ge0, T^k N_{j_i}(t)=a\}>0.
$$
The full-measure set can depend on the row sequence. For every $a$ divisible by three, almost surely only finitely many heads from *all* the rows visit $a$. Positive constants may depend on the target. Even ternary units are included.

There is also one target-independent exponential row schedule $j_{i+1}=\lceil\exp(Dj_i)\rceil$, with a sufficiently large absolute $D$, on which **every prescribed finite word of unit targets occurs with positive lower frequency** almost surely. A word $a_1,\ldots,a_l$ means that the first sampled head visits $a_1$, the next visits $a_2$, and so on, with a separately chosen finite hitting time for each source. Even disjoint successive blocks of length $l$ suffice. In particular 7 and 19 give positive sampling entropy although both reach 11 and no single orbit visits them both.

## Transporting the actual target label

Let $f_a(n)=1$ when an iterate of $n$ is $a$. Then $f_a(n)=f_a(Tn)$ for $n\ne a$. If $m>2^h a$, the first $h$ moves from $m$ preserve this mark, since $T^i m\ge m/2^i>a$ up to that time. The same argument applies to a finite Boolean combination of marks by using the largest target as the floor. For a directly invariant component label use floor zero.

Write $R_j=81^j, G_j(u)=f_a(\lfloor R_j81^u\rfloor)$ on the logarithmic circle $0\le u<1$, and $p_j=\int G_j$. The angular discrepancy argument in [PR197, macroscopic pair closure](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/2c1397d2adcadc620381bca20c465f3bf31a6f8e/research/ayh-resource-reset-20261124/literature-step/MACROSCOPIC_PAIR_CLOSURE.md), display (1), gives for every interval $W$
$$
   \left|\int_W G_j-|W|p_j\right|\le C(q/\sqrt j+1/q)                 \tag{1}
$$
for each continued-fraction denominator $q\le j^{1/4}$ of $\theta=\log_{81}2$, once $j\ge j_0(a)$. Here is the floor check required to apply its proof to a predecessor, which need not be an invariant component. The binary lift of $2^km+r$, $0\le r<2^k$, reaches its affine endpoint in $k$ steps, throughout which its input is at least $m\ge R_j$. In the comparison of $m$ with that endpoint $3^p m+c\ge m$, the common finite binary-grid clock is at most $b=\lfloor\log_2(R_j)/2\rfloor$. Both arms remain above $a$, since $R_j/2^b\ge\sqrt{R_j}>a$. The row-to-row comparison of $m,81m+r$ uses the same bound. Thus all successful physical synchronizations in the proof preserve $f_a$; the finite-grid counting and irrational rotation averaging are unchanged. They use the source-qualified complete-grid fusion estimate of [PR146, growing additive gaps](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437/research/upper-bit-mergers/GROWING_ADDITIVE_GAPS.md). A comparison for an unbounded future target chosen from the sampled orbit would require new uniformity.

The fixed positive predecessor lower-density theorem gives $\liminf p_j>0$ for each unit target. In detail, for odd $a$ a hit in the ordinary $3n+1$ convention is also a shortcut hit: shortcut compression skips only even intermediate values. For even $a$ prime to three, choose $e\ge2$ with $2^e a=4\bmod9$, using the order six of 2. The odd unit $b=(2^e a-1)/3$ reaches $a$ after $e$ shortcut steps. Every predecessor of $b$ contributes. Hence at least $c_a X$ integers below all sufficiently large $X$ have the wanted mark. As in PR197, consecutive full-annulus log shares differ by $O(j^{-1/2})$ by finite-grid fusion. Choose a fixed $L$ with $81^{-L}<c_a/2$. Summing the integer counts below $81R_j$ over its last $L+1$ annuli gives $p_j\gg_a1$, since the earlier starts occupy at most $81^{-L-1}$ of that cutoff, each marked integer in annulus $k$ has log measure at least $1/(81R_k\log 81)$ there, and $p_{j-i}=p_j+o(1)$ for every fixed $i\le L$. The stated density input is [Mazur, fixed-target predecessor theorem, 2026 manuscript and formalization](https://www.proofatlas.ai/formalizations/positive-lower-density-collatz-predecessors/). We have not rebuilt that formalization. The published [Krasikov–Lagarias lower power bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0205002) alone does not give the required positive proportion.

For the qualitative frequency statement one only needs the right side of (1) to tend to zero, by taking the largest convergent denominator up to $j^{1/4}$. Interval-step approximation then gives
$$
   \int_E G_j = |E|p_j+o_E(1)                                        \tag{2}
$$
for every *fixed measurable* $E$. On the head arc $I=[\log_{81}7,\log_{81}8)$, Lebesgue measures in $u$ and $t=81^u$ are equivalent. Average (2) over any fixed subsequence. If $E\subset I$ has positive measure and the upper visit frequency everywhere on $E$ is below $c<\liminf p_j$, reverse Fatou for the bounded averages gives a contradiction to (2). The countable union over rational $c<\liminf p_j$ proves the stronger almost-sure lower bound $\limsup\text{frequency}\ge\liminf p_j$. Alternatively, natural density existence and positivity of each fixed-target predecessor set imply (2) qualitatively by subtracting cumulative counts over fixed intervals. This alternative uses the additional natural-density existence input.

When $3\mid a$, an odd shortcut output is prime to three. Induction backwards shows $T^h n=a\Rightarrow n=2^h a$. At most one such integer belongs to the row $[7R_j,8R_j)$, whose endpoint ratio is below two. The union of its marked mantissa cells has ordinary length at most $R_j^{-1}$. The sum converges, so the first Borel–Cantelli lemma gives finitely many visits for each such $a$. Countable intersection over all positive targets proves the exact classification.

## Conditional estimates on a single earlier cell

A fixed two-logarithm bound gives constants $c,V>0$ with $\|q\theta\|\ge c q^{-V}$ for every positive $q$. Indeed $q\log2-4p\log3\ne0$ for nonzero $q$ and a nearest integer $p$, with coefficient sizes $O(q+1)$. Apply [Matveev, Theorem 2.1 and the rational specialization](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/im314). Consecutive continued-fraction denominators obey $q'\ll q^V$. Thus the right side of (1), using the largest denominator $q\le j^{1/4}$, is $O(j^{-\gamma})$ for some fixed $\gamma>0$.

Choose the log-uniform law on $I$. At row $s$ each entire head cell $C_x=[\log_{81}(x/R_s),\log_{81}((x+1)/R_s))$ has length comparable to $R_s^{-1}$. All previous rows are measurable on this partition because integer prefix deletion recovers the earlier heads exactly. From (1), uniformly in every earlier cell and $j>s$ with $j\ge j_0(a)$,
$$
  |\mathbb P(G_j=1\mid C_x)-p_j| \ll R_s j^{-\gamma}.                 \tag{3}
$$
The same bound holds on nonempty unions of these cells including conditioning on prescribed *earlier* target hits or misses. Choose $D\gamma>2\log81$. At consecutive selected rows the right side is summable in the sequence index. The implied constant for any fixed target is harmless. Each finite product of conditional successes now has a uniform lower bound: after a sufficiently late start, each chosen unit target $a_r$ has conditional hit probability at least $\eta_r>0$, hence their word has conditional probability at least $\prod_r\eta_r$, even given the entire head just before the block. On disjoint length-$l$ blocks the indicators minus their conditional means are bounded martingale differences in the block-end filtration. Azuma's inequality followed by Borel–Cantelli gives a lower block frequency at least $\prod_r\eta_r$. Intersect the full-measure sets for countably many words.

For any one binary target or component mark there is a more precise sampling interpretation. The law of the sampled bits can be coupled to independent Bernoulli bits of parameters $p_{j_i}$ with only finitely many discrepancies almost surely. Generate the sampled bits successively from independent uniform random numbers using their conditional probabilities given past bits, and threshold those same numbers at $p_{j_i}$ for the comparison bits. Equation (3) bounds the mismatch probabilities by a summable sequence. These independent bits describe the sampling law on an auxiliary probability space. No independent physical Collatz transitions are inferred. For the mark of hitting 7, both its one and zero probabilities are bounded below: 19 reaches 11 in four steps, 7 reaches 11 in one, and neither reaches the other. Thus every predecessor of 19 misses 7. The conditional binary entropy of that sampled mark given all sampled prior bits is eventually bounded below by a positive constant, with both targets in the *same* component.

## If a different component exists

Let $f$ indicate the basin of one. Under failure, both marks have positive lower density: in any nonconverging trajectory an odd shortcut input yields a ternary-unit output, and that output's predecessors are nonconvergent. Thus $\eta\le p_j\le1-\eta$ for all sufficiently high $j$. On **any preassigned** infinite row sequence, for almost every mantissa both colors have positive upper frequency by (2). Consequently a set of mantissas of positive measure on which the frequency of one color tends to zero on such a sequence suffices for convergence of all positive integers. On the specified exponential schedule each finite binary word occurs with positive lower frequency almost surely.

There is a quantitative consequence for the *actual* scheduled test of PR197, stronger than divergence of its nonnegative series. Let $a_j$ be its full normalized head-disagreement counts; choose any of its fixed admissible four-suffix, row-residue, fixed-arc and fixed-divisibility schedules, and let $A_j^*$ be its restricted counts. If Collatz fails, some $\eta_*>0,D_*>0$ satisfy
$$
    \sum_{s\le j\le\lceil e^{D_*s}\rceil} A_j^*\ \ge\eta_*
                 \quad\text{for every sufficiently large }s.           \tag{4}
$$
In particular the partial sums grow at least on the order of the iterated logarithm. A subsequence of these moving block sums tending to zero would suffice for convergence, with an exponent $D_*$ sufficiently large for the comparisons.

To see the full-row estimate, use (3) with $j=\lceil\exp(Ds)\rceil$. Conditional on any earlier ordinary head, the probability its basin mark differs from the final mark is at least $\eta/2$ for large $s$. Every such ray changes across at least one adjacent row between those indices; no partner is resampled. The fixed log-head law has density bounded above and below relative to uniform $t\in[7,8)$, so $\sum_{s<k\le j}a_k\gg\eta$. Apply the displayed convolution estimate in [PR197, four-suffix equivalence](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/2c1397d2adcadc620381bca20c465f3bf31a6f8e/research/ayh-resource-reset-20261124/crossing-step/FOUR_SUFFIX_EQUIVALENCE.md) with the replacement permitted by its [scheduled version](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/2c1397d2adcadc620381bca20c465f3bf31a6f8e/research/ayh-resource-reset-20261124/crossing-step/PERIODIC_FOUR_SUFFIX_SCHEDULE.md). Specifically, sum the full-row lower bound from coarse row $s+L+K$, where $K$ is its fixed bounded shift and $L$ is a fixed large constant. In the resulting upper bound, restricted rows below $s$ contribute at most $O(\sum_{d\ge L}(d+1)^2 81^{-d})$: for each lag $d$ there are at most $d+O(1)$ admissible outer rows near the left endpoint and a bounded number of shifts, and $A^*\le1$. The independent boundary term is $o(1)$. Choose $L$ to absorb the left leakage. All other restricted terms have bounded convolution mass per row and indices between $s$ and $\lceil\exp(D(s+L+K))\rceil+K\le\lceil\exp(D_*s)\rceil$ for any fixed $D_*>D$ and large $s$. This proves (4). Take successive disjoint such blocks to get the partial-sum bound; for large $s$ one application of $s\mapsto\lceil e^{D_*s}\rceil+1$ increases the iterated logarithm by at most two. This transfers counts, not the identity of a particular ray through the four-suffix routing.

## Attempt at positive-entropy measure rigidity

The sampled entropy above does not supply a non-Haar circle measure to which Rudolph's theorem could be applied. This can be checked on an *actual* simultaneous circle extension. For irrational $\alpha$, put the empirical measure on $(\{n\alpha\},f(n))$, $1\le n\le X$, where $f$ is the genuine basin mark. The complete-grid synchronization of adjacent sources gives $\sum_{n<X}|f(n+1)-f(n)|=o(X)$. For each nonzero integer $k$, the partial sums of $e^{2\pi i k\alpha n}$ are bounded; summation by parts gives
$$
     X^{-1}\sum_{n\le X} f(n)e^{2\pi i k\alpha n}=o(1).
$$
The same holds with $1-f$. Along any subsequence on which the color share tends to $p$, Fourier uniqueness identifies the entire limit as
$$
       \mathrm{Haar}_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}\otimes
                         ((1-p)\delta_0+p\delta_1).
$$
This is invariant under $(z,c)\mapsto(2z,c)$ and $(z,c)\mapsto(3z,c)$. The model keeps the actual dilation marks asymptotically: doubling preserves them exactly and tripling preserves them off $o(X)$ inputs by the $m,3m$ full-grid merger estimate. Every color slice already has Haar base and maximal base entropy, even for $0<p<1$. Relative entropy in the constant bit is zero. [Rudolph, *×2 and ×3 invariant measures and entropy*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0143385700005629) classifies the positive-entropy ergodic base measure and cannot identify these two slices.

The row clock gives the same diagnostic directly: for every *fixed* $h$, the probability of any change in marks along rows $j,\ldots,j+h$ is $O(h/\sqrt j)$ on the specified head arc by the full-grid bound. Every limit of these contiguous finite marked windows has a constant color coordinate. Large jumps in the positive-entropy sampling theorem have zero sampling density in the original row clock. A proof via this entropy would need an ordinary constraint coupling the color slices, or a demonstrated estimate contradicting (4). Neither the fixed-target mass nor the lower multiplicative rank of translated heads supplies an upper bound on that rank or on those block masses. No such estimate or universal ordinary basin implication is established here.
