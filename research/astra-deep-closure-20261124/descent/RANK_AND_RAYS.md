# Multiplicative rank along the original head progression and fixed-endpoint pools

**PROPOSED written theorem.** Conditional only on the cardinalities and size bounds for PR197's genuine centered neighbors, the **first coordinate alone** of every such family has multiplicative ratio rank at least
$$
   \exp\!\left(c_A\,d/(\log(d+2))^{12}\right).
$$
Here rank is the rational dimension of the span of the prime valuation vectors of the positive rational ratios, and the estimate is for all sufficiently large $d$. This is stronger than the linear-in-$d$ consequence of the two-variable unit equation on each nonzero-intercept branch in the [arithmetic packet](../arithmetic/HEADS.md). That packet's bound for arbitrary subcollections and its bound on the whole incidence graph in two fixed cosets are separate statements. The stronger rank here uses the precise common step and density of indices, and applies even if the real-linear intercept between the two coordinates is zero.

## Dense marked indices in the physical progression

Fix a returned pair in the narrower slabs of [PR197, joint target midpoints](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/2c1397d2adcadc620381bca20c465f3bf31a6f8e/research/ayh-resource-reset-20261124/crossing-step/JOINT_TARGET_MIDPOINTS.md). Put $K=\lfloor2^{.01d}\rfloor$, $T_d=(\log(d+2))^{12}$, and use its positive shifts $1\le l\le K$. At least $M\ge K e^{-C_A T_d}$ distinct indices supply actual labeled neighbors. Write their first coordinates as
$$
          Z_l=z_1+81\,2^v l .
$$
Here $z_1$ denotes the original unshifted first head. The slab yields $z_1\ge 2^{.37d}$ for large $d$, $z_1\le 2^{O(d)}$, and $v\le .015d+O(1)$. Divide all terms by $g=\gcd(z_1,81\,2^v)$, writing $m(l)=B+Hl$ with $\gcd(B,H)=1$. All values satisfy $K^{1/3}<m(l)\le e^{C d}$, since $g\le 81\,2^v$. All prime divisors of the values are coprime to the new step $H$.

Take $Y=\exp(\kappa d/T_d)$ for a fixed sufficiently small $\kappa>0$ depending on $C_A$. For large $d$, $e^2<Y<K^{1/3}$. A $Y$-smooth value $m(l)$ must have a $Y$-smooth divisor $b$ between $K^{1/3}$ and $K^{1/3}Y<K^{2/3}$: multiply its prime factors one at a time until first crossing $K^{1/3}$. Each such divisor coprime to $H$ costs at most $K/b+1\le2K/b$ of the full set of indices, uniformly in $H,B$. Thus their smooth fraction is at most
$$
 2\sum_{b\ge K^{1/3},\;P^+(b)\le Y}\frac1b
 \le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\log K}{3\log Y}\right)
           \prod_{p\le Y}(1-p^{-1+1/\log Y})^{-1}.
$$
The product is at most $(\log Y)^{O(1)}$. Indeed $p^{-1+1/\log Y}\le e/p$ for $p\le Y$, and $1/\log Y\le1/2$ makes the logarithm of each factor at most a fixed multiple of $p^{-1+1/\log Y}$. Partial summation of Chebyshev's bound $\pi(t)\ll t/\log t$ gives $\sum_{p\le Y}1/p\ll\log\log Y$. As $\log K=(.01\log2+o(1))d$ and $\log\log Y=O(\log d)=o(T_d)$, choose $\kappa$ so that the smooth fraction is below $e^{-C_A T_d}/2$. This is a pointwise estimate for the entire short progression, prior to selecting genuinely discordant points. It retains at least $M/2$ non-smooth actual neighbors.

A prime $p>Y$ can divide at most $K/p+1\le2K/Y$ of the $m(l)$, by primitivity. Thus at least $M Y/(4K)$ distinct large primes occur among the selected values. Each value has at most $C d/\log Y=O_A(T_d)$ such prime factors. In the valuation matrix restrict to these large-prime columns and take a basis of rows. Every nonzero column is nonzero on at least one basis row, so the row rank is at least $MY/(O_A(K T_d))$. This exceeds $\exp(c_A d/T_d)$ after reducing $c_A$, since $T_d^2=o(d)$. Passing from the $m(l)$ to $m(l)/m(0)=Z_l/z_1$ decreases multiplicative rank by at most one. The same bound, with adjusted constants, applies after discarding any fixed proportion less than all of the supplied neighbors. The joint coordinatewise ratio group maps onto the first-coordinate ratio group, so its rank is at least as large.

## Rank inside a fixed pair of ordinary target basins

More generally, fix $C>0$ and $c>0$. If $E_X\subset[1,CX]$ consists of at least $cX$ distinct ordinary positive integers, its multiplicative ratio rank is at least $X^{\epsilon}$ for all sufficiently large such $X$, with $\epsilon>0$ depending only on $c,C$. To see this, set $Y=X^u$ for a fixed small $u>0$. The total log of the $Y$-smooth parts of all $n\le CX$ is bounded by
$$
  CX\sum_{p\le Y}\frac{\log p}{p-1}\ll CX\log Y.
$$
This follows by expanding the valuations into prime-power divisibility and using Chebyshev's bound by partial summation. Every wholly smooth $n>\sqrt X$ contributes at least $(\log X)/2$. Choose $u$ sufficiently small so that after also excluding $n\le\sqrt X$, at least $cX/2$ members of $E_X$ have a prime larger than $Y$. Each such prime divides at most $CX/Y$ members. At least $cY/(2C)$ nonzero prime columns are needed, and each row supports $O_{u,C}(1)$ such columns. The same basis argument, losing at most one for normalization by a fixed element, proves the assertion with any fixed $0<\epsilon<u$ after increasing the onset.

Apply this to the [physical two-endpoint pools](../pressure/README.md), section 2. For each fixed first target $y$ at least one selected fixed companion $u$ has a positive upper normalized count of sources hitting both at the required near-critical clocks, *before* pigeonholing the exact clocks and grades. Thus the sources for those fixed two endpoints have ratio rank at least $X^{\epsilon_y}$ on unbounded scales. On a successful vertex of the [inverse-ray theorem](../pressure/INVERSE_RAYS.md), take $c$ proportional to $\eta_a d_y$; the exponent can depend on $y$. In fact the polynomial rank and near-linear multiplicity can be achieved **simultaneously at one exact pair of clocks and grades** at each of these large scales. If at least $cX$ qualified sources at the fixed endpoints partition into $J=O_y((\log X\log\log X)^2)$ exact quadruples, remove all classes of size below $cX/(2J)$. At least $cX/2$ marked integers remain and their absolute valuation rank is at least $X^{\epsilon}$ by the preceding lemma. The group generated by their union lies in the group generated by the class ratio groups and one representative per nonempty class. Thus some retained class has ratio rank at least $X^{\epsilon}/J-1\ge X^{\epsilon/2}$ eventually. This very class also has at least $cX/(2J)$ sources. The exact quadruple can depend on the scale, and the positive exponent on the specified endpoint. Near-linear cardinality of an arbitrarily chosen class alone would not justify the fixed-power rank. The finite cardinality-and-rank selection after the valuation bounds is checked as `HeadSampling.thick_fiber_rank` in [HeadSampling.lean](HeadSampling.lean).

The scalar bridge has bounded length uniformly in $y$: choose a sufficiently large fixed starting exponent, then the next representative of the required unit residue modulo $3^{m+1}$, incurring less than its fixed multiplicative order. This justifies the final change of law without a new two-arm hypothesis.
