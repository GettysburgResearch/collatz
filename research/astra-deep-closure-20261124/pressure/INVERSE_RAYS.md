# One fixed companion target along a positive set of ordinary inverse rays

**PROPOSED, source-qualified written theorem.** This strengthens the fixed-companion assertion of [the clock theorem](README.md) in its dependence on the prescribed first-arm root. Throughout, $U$ denotes the scalar accelerated odd Collatz map, $T$ the shortcut map, and $d_y$ the natural density of the ordinary predecessor basin of a nonperiodic odd unit $y$. The inputs on inverse laws are Weaver, paper 12 v1, §§8–10, including the cited trace, uniform atom floor, basin density partition and equivalence of the two scalar inverse laws. No joint inverse law of $(x,5x)$ is assumed. [Weaver, (26)–(31) and §10.1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/12)

Fix any nonperiodic ordinary odd unit $a$. There exist one ordinary odd unit $u$ and a constant $\eta_a>0$ with the following property. A **positive-probability set of actual scalar inverse rays rooted at $a$**, under both the canonical harmonic law $P_{h,a}$ and the natural-density entrance law $Q_a$, has infinitely many distinct vertices $y$ satisfying both of the following:

1. The canonical scalar profile satisfies $h(y)\le4$.
2. At arbitrarily large original source scales $X$, at least $\eta_a d_y X$ distinct ordinary units $x\in(X/4,4X+1]$ have a first-arm first odd landing at or below $y$ equal to $y$, first-arm affine offset at most $1/2$, and a companion landing $T^t(5x)=u$. Both clocks differ from $c_*\log X$ and both grades from $c_*\log X/2$ by at most $C_a\sqrt{\log X\log\log X}$, with $c_*=2/\log(4/3)$. The target $u$, the fraction relative to $d_y$, and $C_a$ are uniform over these infinitely many vertices. The scales can depend on the vertex. At each successful scale, freezing the four integer clocks and grades costs at most $O_a((\log X\log\log X)^2)$ in multiplicity.

In particular the successive $y$'s lie on the **same ordinary scalar inverse ray**. Every selected $x$ follows the path from its $y$ back to $a$. Either law samples infinite sequences of ordinary integer vertices, with each asserted landing realized by a finite positive trajectory. The second-arm target need not equal $5a$, and the two clocks need not coincide.

### Low-profile antichains retain uniform total reciprocal height

Write $X_j$ for the $j$th odd inverse vertex. By Weaver (28), the Cesàro mean of $E_a[\rho_1(X_j)/h(X_j)]$ tends to one. Here $h\ge\alpha>0$, $\rho_1\le2$ and $h(a)>0$. Consequently the Cesàro average of $P_{h,a}(h(X_j)\le4)$ has positive lower limit: outside this set the integrand is at most $1/2$, and everywhere it is at most $2/\alpha$. In particular infinitely many $j$ have low-profile probability at least a fixed $\theta>0$. This uses the expectation identity directly, rather than interchanging expectation and a pathwise limsup.

A depth-$j$ vertex $y$ uniquely determines its scalar forward word to $a$. Write $D$ for its total shortcut time. The cylinder probability telescopes to $3^j2^{-D}h(y)/h(a)$. Positivity of the forward carry gives $3^j2^{-D}y\le a$. Thus on a low-profile vertex
$$P_{h,a}(X_j=y)\le\frac{4a}{h(a)y}.$$
At each of the selected depths choose a **finite** collection $F_j$ of the low-profile unit vertices with probability at least $\theta/2$. This gives $\sum_{y\in F_j}1/y\ge \theta h(a)/(8a)$ independent of $j$. Distinct vertices at a common depth have disjoint ordinary predecessor basins: otherwise two such odd vertices occur on one forward odd trajectory, and advancing $j$ further odd steps from each would repeat $a$. Nonperiodicity excludes that case. Also $Q_a(X_j=y)=d_y/d_a$ by the entrance-law partition, and hence $\sum_{y\in F_j}d_y\le d_a$.

### A uniform companion menu and selection on the same path space

In the [scalar pool construction](README.md), the bridge exponent can be chosen in a single fixed interval $s_0\le s<s_0+2\cdot3^m$. Choose $s_0$ large enough for the contraction, offset, positivity and first-passage inequalities simultaneously for all $y\ge1$, then take the allowed congruence within the interval. In particular $r\asymp y$ with absolute constants. The proof by adjacent depths therefore gives at least $cX/y$ actual scalar pool members on the **same shell** $(X/4,4X+1]$ for each fixed $y$ and all sufficiently large $X$ depending on $y$, with $c>0$ absolute. The first-clock center is $c_*\log(X/r)+O(1)$, and the first grade has the corresponding half center. For each fixed finite $F_j$, the additive $O(1+\max_{y\in F_j}\log y)$ displacement eventually fits inside the same absolute central-window coefficient. No uniform onset over all $j$ is required.

The disjoint basins make the union of these pools at least $c_a X$ at every sufficiently large $X$ for that finite $F_j$, with $c_a>0$ independent of $j$. Choose **one** floor $K=K(a)$ by the source-qualified fixed-floor estimate in the clock theorem, with ambient loss below $c_a X/2$ for all sufficiently large $X$. As there, the quantitative polylogarithmic first passage supplies the lower clock and grade bounds. Finishing each companion landing at an ordinary odd unit costs $O_K(1)$ and gives one finite set $V_a$. All large-source bounds hold with a common $C_a$ after raising the onset for each finite $F_j$. For every one of the selected depths, a positive multiple $c'_aX$ of sources, with $c'_a$ independent of $j$, satisfies both-arm requirements for some $y\in F_j$ and $u\in V_a$.

For each $y,u$ write $g(y,u)$ for the limsup of its qualified count divided by $X$ on this shell and with this window coefficient. Since the first arm hits $y$, $g(y,u)\le 4d_y$ by natural density. Taking the limsup of a *finite* sum gives $\sum_{y\in F_j,u\in V_a}g(y,u)\ge c'_a$. Discard terms smaller than $\eta d_y$, choosing $\eta>0$ with $|V_a|\eta d_a<c'_a/2$. At infinitely many of the selected depths the **same** $u$ accounts for at least $c'_a/(2|V_a|)$ of the surviving sum. Its supporting vertices have total $d_y$ at least $c'_a/(8|V_a|)$, by the upper bound on $g$. Under $Q_a$ the probability of being at one of these vertices at any such depth is at least $c'_a/(8|V_a|d_a)$. The probability of the limsup of these events is at least that number: the union over every future tail already has this probability. By the equivalence in Weaver §10.1 the limsup also has positive $P_{h,a}$ probability. Every ray from this nonperiodic root has distinct vertices. Reducing $\eta$ by a factor of two converts the limsup bound into the asserted infinitely many finite source scales for each vertex.

For an arbitrary periodic target, one may enter it by a fixed nonperiodic unit odd predecessor and apply the theorem there. At most one direct odd predecessor of a periodic vertex can itself be periodic, while infinitely many direct unit predecessors exist. The resulting rays have the fixed extra edge back to the given target. No scalar entrance-law partition at a periodic root is needed.

### Limit of the uniformity

A small value of $h(y)$ alone does not give a lower bound uniform in $y$ for the scalar basin density. The proof obtains the common floor from the *sum* over a depth antichain, then selects a positive set of nested rays. It fixes $u$ before those rays are selected. It does not force a chosen $u$, put $5y$ on the second route, or furnish compatible second-arm witnesses across different vertices on the ray. In particular, if $a$ belongs to a hypothetical component separated from another root, a ray reaching $a$ obeys the proof unchanged. The data do not determine which component contains its selected $u$.
