# A fixed ordinary target and the companion at its critical clock

**PROPOSED, source-qualified written theorem.** The input is an analytic fixed-total lower bound for ordinary *one-arm* predecessors and a quantitative *one-arm* forward passage theorem. The estimates below impose both on the same positive integer. The ratio-five pointwise pressure (PT) and Collatz remain open. The [inverse-ray theorem](INVERSE_RAYS.md) selects one companion target along infinitely many vertices of a positive-probability set of nested scalar inverse rays. The finite common-clock comparison, record-counting pigeonhole and an obstruction to local word forcing are checked in [ClockTransfer.lean](ClockTransfer.lean).

Use the shortcut map $T(2n)=n$, $T(2n+1)=3n+2$. Write $p_j(n)$ for the number of odd inputs through $j$ shortcuts and $\beta_j(n)=T^j(n)-3^{p_j(n)}2^{-j}n$ for the nonnegative real offset. An ordinary unit here is positive and coprime to 6. All logs are natural unless subscripted. Put $c_*=2/\log(4/3)$ and $L_p=\sqrt{p\log(p+2)}$.

### 1. An exact companion state at every prescribed first-arm endpoint

Fix **any** ordinary unit $y$. For every sufficiently large integer $p$, there is an $e=2p+O(1)$, with the offset $e-2p$ chosen from a fixed two-element set, for which the following statements hold. Put $G=2^e/3^p$. All implied constants may depend on $y$.

* At one exact common clock $e$ and one exact companion grade $p+k$, there are at least $G\exp(-C L_p)$ different ordinary units $x$ such that
  $$T^e(x)=y,\qquad T^e(5x)=Y,\qquad p_e(x)=p,\qquad p_e(5x)=p+k,\qquad \beta_e(x)\le 1/2.$$
  The single value $Y$ satisfies $1\le Y\le\exp(C L_p)$ and $|k|\le C L_p$. Also $G(y-1/2)\le x\le Gy$. Thus one actual PR200 relative state has near-optimal exponent at **every fixed ordinary first-arm target and every sufficiently large odd depth**, with a quantified growing menu. In the notation of PR200 that state is $(k,D)$ with $D=3^{k_-}Y-5\,3^{k_+}y$, so $|D|\le\exp(C' L_p)$.
* Allowing just the companion clock to move, there is one triple $(h,b,u)$ with $|h-e|+|b-p|\le C L_p$ and $1\le u\le C p^A$ such that at least $G p^{-C'}$ of the same type of sources satisfy $T^h(5x)=u$ and $p_h(5x)=b$. Here $A$ is a fixed absolute exponent. Both grades and both clocks are frozen before counting this fiber. One may choose this triple first and then choose the synchronous $(k,Y)$ inside it. The resulting synchronous lower bound remains $G\exp(-C L_p)$.

**Scalar construction with a small real offset.** Use the retained joint atom estimate in Weaver, paper 12 v1, Lemma 13, equation (39). It assumes El Jaouhari's selected-block estimates and averaged joint oscillation with the total valuation retained. There is one unit cylinder $r_0\bmod3^m$ and absolute $R_0,B_0,c>0$ such that for every sufficiently large odd depth $b$ and every $|d-2b|\le2\sqrt b$, the geometric mass of legal offset residues at a supplied $r$ above that cylinder, fixed total $d$, internal multipliers $A_i\le R_0$, and real offsets $0\le B_i\le B_b\le B_0$, is at least $c3^{-b}\pi_b(d)$. Here $A_i=3^i2^{-D_i}$, $D_i$ is the partial total, and $\pi_b(d)=2^{-d}\binom{d-1}{b-1}$. Each word has mass $2^{-d}$ and its outward states are $(r-B_i)/A_i$. Full-depth compatibility implies integrality at every intermediate depth by reduction modulo 3 followed by division. On a positive odd root, integral inverse letters $(2^a z-1)/3$ give positive odd states with the exact valuation $a$. Distinct words at the same depth give distinct ordinary starts by deterministic accelerated forward decoding. [Weaver, §13 and Appendix A](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/12)

Choose $s$ so that $2^sy\equiv3r_0+1\pmod {3^{m+1}}$. Arbitrarily large such $s$ exist since two generates the unit group. Take $s$ large enough that $r=(2^s y-1)/3>\max(2B_0,B_0+R_0 y,y)$ and $(1+3B_0)/2^s\le1/2$. For each inverse word at $r$, all positive-depth states exceed $y$ by the internal bound. Appending the fixed forward odd letter from $r$ to $y$ gives **first odd landing at or below $y$ at $y$**, even if $y$ is periodic. The final forward offset is $(1+3B_b)/2^s\le1/2$, the grade is $p=b+1$, and $e_0=d+s$.

Initially $n$ may be divisible by three. Keep $x=n$ for units and take $x=4n+1$ otherwise. The first odd jump is identical in value since $3(4n+1)+1=4(3n+1)$. Its shortcut duration increases by exactly two and its grade is unchanged. The new source is an odd unit. In the moved group its full multiplier is $1/(4G_0)$ where $G_0=2^{e_0}/3^p$ and its offset is smaller by $1/(4G_0)$. The legal parity path itself proves nonnegativity. First odd landing is unchanged at sufficiently large scales. Each source has at most two preimages under this repair; within either group it is injective.

At $d=2b$, the elementary central binomial bound gives $\pi_b(d)\gg 1/\sqrt b$. Counting the equal-weight words and keeping the larger of the two repair groups gives $\gg_y G/\sqrt p$ sources at one $e\in\{2p+s-2,2p+s\}$. Every such $x$ lies in $[G(y-1/2),Gy]$. In particular $c_*\log(5x)=e+O_y(1)$ uniformly, since $e=2p+O_y(1)$ and $\log G=p\log(4/3)+O_y(1)$.

**Intersecting the actual source pool.** Shaik's quantitative polylogarithmic descent, Corollary 1.2, for any desired fixed $D$ and a suitable $A$, discards $O(Z/(\log Z)^D)$ positive integers up to $Z$. For each remaining $v$ it supplies a physical clock $h$ and its own grade $b'$ with
$$T^h v\le(\log v)^A,\qquad |h-c_*\log v|+|b'-h/2|\ll_A\sqrt{\log v\log\log v}.$$
The grade assertion is explicitly established in the proof following (6.33) for the same clock, which matters on periodic paths. Apply this to **$v=5x$** and $Z=5Gy$. Injectivity bounds the loss in the scalar pool by $O_y(G p^{-D})$. Choose $D>1$. A positive multiple of $G/\sqrt p$ literal sources remain. This step needs the quantitative exceptional count: mere density one does not justify the intersection. [Shaik, Corollary 1.2 and proof of Proposition 6.4](https://shaikidris.github.io/polylog/paper/)

For a remaining source, $|h-e|+|b'-p|\ll_y L_p$. Choose one valid $h$ per source, e.g. the least within the prescribed window. There are $O_y((1+L_p)^2 p^A)$ triples $(h,b',T^h(5x))$. Pigeonholing proves the separate-clock assertion. For every positive integer $v$, $v/2\le Tv\le2v$ and adding one shortcut changes the grade by at most one. Therefore at the **same prescribed** $e$,
$$ T^e(5x)\le 2^{|h-e|}T^h(5x)\le\exp(C_yL_p),\qquad |p_e(5x)-p|\le C_yL_p.$$
There are at most $\exp(C'_yL_p)$ exact synchronous pairs of grades and endpoints, which absorbs the preceding polynomial selection loss. Pigeonholing again proves the first assertion. Fixed scalar depth makes distinct positive sources distinct legal parity words: the target and the word determine the source by the affine formula. The source-height interval has length $G/2$, so exponent one is the sharp exponential order. [Lean clock and grade comparison](ClockTransfer.lean)

### 2. Two fixed ordinary endpoints at separate near-critical clocks

With the additional fixed-floor passage input below, for each prescribed $y$ there is a finite set of ordinary units $U_y$ such that at **every** sufficiently large $X$ at least $c_y X$ distinct odd units $x\in(X/4,4X+1]$ hit $y$ on the first arm and some $u\in U_y$ on the second arm. They obey $\beta_e(x)\le1/2$ and, writing $L=\log X$, their hitting clocks and grades satisfy
$$ |e-c_*L|+|h-c_*L|+|p_e(x)-c_*L/2|+|p_h(5x)-c_*L/2|\ll_y \sqrt{L\log(L+2)}. $$
Consequently there is **one ordinary unit $u=u(y)$ fixed independently of scale** and infinitely many $X\to\infty$ at which the pair $(y,u)$ has at least $c'_yX/(L\log(L+2))^2$ actual sources at one exact pair of clocks and grades. The joint two-target basin itself has positive upper natural density. This conclusion does not identify $u$ with $5y$ or place $u$ in the component of $5y$.

To prove this, use the same scalar construction at $\asymp\sqrt{\log X}$ adjacent depths as in Weaver's proof of Theorem 14. Explicitly put $L_0=\log_2(X/r)$, $\theta=\log_2 3$, choose $|b-L_0/(2-\theta)|\le c_0\sqrt{L_0}$, and $L_0-1<d-b\theta\le L_0$. For small fixed $c_0$ and large $X$, $|d-2b|\le2\sqrt b$ and $\pi_b(d)\gg 1/\sqrt{L_0}$ uniformly. The original starts lie in $(X/4,X]$ since $r/2<r-B_b\le r$. Each row has $\gg_y X/\sqrt{L_0}$ starts. They are disjoint across depths by the first-landing rule. After the unit repair, moved starts exceed $X$ while unmoved starts are at most $X$, hence the full pool contains $\gg_y X$ distinct $x$. Its first clock and grade differ from $c_*\log X$ and $c_*\log X/2$, respectively, by $O_y(\sqrt{\log X})$. [Weaver, (39)–(41)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/12)

Use PR193's derived fixed-floor bound: in a fixed-ratio ordinary interval of scale $X$, for each fixed large $K$, the fraction missing $[1,K]$ by $c_*\log X+C\sqrt{L\log(L+2)}$ is $O((\log K)^{-2})+o_X(1)$. This uses the published-as-manuscript relative first-landing comparison in Weaver §6, (15), in addition to polylogarithmic descent. Choose $K$ once so that applying the estimate in the ambient interval for $5x$, and using injectivity, removes less than half of the linear scalar pool. The constant in the finite-source loss accounts for the interval length. Discard also the $o(X)$ sources where Shaik's quantitative polylogarithmic first passage for $5x$ fails. In the proof of Proposition 6.4, (6.32)–(6.33), its witness is a **first passage** to a dyadic threshold with rank $A\log_2\log(5x)+O_A(1)$, which eventually exceeds fixed $K$. Hence its clock is no later than the first hit on $[1,K]$. It differs from $c_*L$ by $O(\sqrt{L\log(L+2)})$ and its grade differs from $c_*L/2$ by that same order. The upper deadline on the $K$ hit now bounds both the elapsed clocks and the extra grade. From an integer $v\le K$ strip all powers of two. If its odd part is divisible by three, apply one accelerated odd step; the resulting odd output is a unit since $3v'+1\equiv1\pmod3$. This costs $O_K(1)$ physical clocks and gives one from finitely many units. Thus all four estimates hold together. The two clocks and grades take $O_y((L\log(L+2))^2)$ possible quadruples. A finite pigeonhole over $U_y$ at each scale, then along an unbounded subsequence, gives the fixed endpoint and the claimed multiplicity. ### 3. Where the ratio-five proof breaks

PR200's common-suffix theorem chooses the first-arm endpoint $y(q)$ **after** specifying a finite menu of affine relative states. Section 1 chooses a large relative state only **after** fixing $y$ and selecting its actual sources. At a fixed attained pair $(y,Y)$ the subsequent orbits are deterministic. Changing the binary class of the common suffix generally changes $y$. Even a suffix with $\log(2^H/3^a)=o(p)$ would preserve source-count exponent only after proving it routes those same ordinary endpoints. Low real expansion does not establish that reachability, and $H$ of order $p$ with $a/H$ near $\log_3 2$ also changes the required limiting odd fraction. The two fixed endpoints in Section 2 are ordered but the second comes from the coupled source distribution. Nothing ensures a preassigned second endpoint, component, equal grade, or exact common clock. [PR200, finite-menu suffix at abf36c6](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/abf36c63b4d0181541d7e029d390d6f636940750/research/regularity-rigidity-20261124/pointwise-fibers/COMMON_SUFFIX.md)

Weaver's scalar canonical inverse law has positive upper occurrence frequency for each fixed valuation word from every ordinary root. Its precise low-profile statement is $\liminf h(X_j)\le2$, and gives repeated $h\le B$ for $B>2$, without guaranteeing infinitely many $h\le2$. A local paired word at a later depth of a scalar inverse path need not restore the anchored condition at the original root. For example $T^4(5)=1$ but $T^4(25)=44$. The legal local 17-clock paired block from $(72817,364085)$ ends at $(5,25)$ with grade two on each arm; the full 21-clock prefix ends at $(1,44)$. More legal local paired blocks can be prepended indefinitely: at each supplied unit $z$ choose the elementary $A$-branch $(2^t z-7)/9$ using $t\ge6$ with exact ternary valuation two of $2^t z-7$. At every ensuing outer boundary the old four-clock ending still produces $(1,44)$. This finite-history example refutes concatenating unanchored local occurrences as a deterministic implication. It gives no canonical-measure estimate for future recovery. [Weaver, Theorem 9 and (31)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/12) · [Lean finite example](ClockTransfer.lean)

The recent PR195 marked router reaches every ternary class from the prescribed *pair* at cost $\exp(O(k^{2/3}))$ clocks and pays the explicit loss in its equation (6) at full precision. The moving companion menu here has subexponential cardinality, which bounds a counting loss; it does not bound the ternary precision or clock needed for a legal correcting suffix. PR197 supplies many simultaneous translated edges for most discordant sources, with distinct smaller target parameters. It does not select a translating edge in this exact orbit fiber. These inputs leave the first unsupported step at the mass assigned to the anchored relative state $(0,0)$, or to a *preassigned routable menu* at its prescribed $y(q)$. A global upper bound opposing near-linear failed-five fibers would still be needed on that line of attack.
