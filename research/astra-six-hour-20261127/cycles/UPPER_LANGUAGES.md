# Finite-state upper itineraries at a high restricted-cycle phase

**PROPOSED written theorem.** For each prescribed odd-hit budget, the positive integral phases on restricted cycles whose tripled source reaches one within that budget form an effectively computable finite set. The same conclusion holds for an arbitrary fixed nonconstant rational affine upper mark whenever it is an ordinary positive integer. A fixed zero-entropy finite-state family of upper itineraries also gives an effective phase-height bound. These assertions use the actual common ordinary start of the two itineraries. They apply to every on-cycle canonical witness on the bounded alternative, including its explicit witness immediately upstream of the global minimum. The least canonical witness can be off-cycle.

## 1. A compact set of lower itineraries with no rational source

Work with the shortcut map $T$ and its parity sequence. An odd-to-odd transition has form $2^{a_i}x_{i+1}=3x_i+1$, where $a_i\ge1$. Here the *restricted cycle* assumption is $a_i\in\{1,2\}$ and $s\le x_i\le(16s+7)/3$, where $s$ is the cycle minimum. Its phase is $x=x_0$. These are implications of the [pinned bounded-branch theorem](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/32d722c5d5597846da5dad842c8ea15742abc176/research/astra-fixed-target-closure-20261124/ordinary/HEIGHT_STRIP.md). For $A_n=\sum_{i<n}a_i$ put $c_n=3^n/2^{A_n}$. At any pair of odd times $i<j$,
\[
  \frac{x_j}{x_i}=\frac{c_j}{c_i}\prod_{i\le t<j}\left(1+\frac1{3x_t}\right).
\]
Consequently at any fixed finite depth, all the $c_j/c_i$ and their inverses are at most $6$ for every sufficiently large $s$. The onset is effective and uniform at the given depth. The upper bound includes the real-height distortion; unrestricted repetition of a fixed ordinary cycle eventually violates it. Call a lower parity prefix admissible if it starts at an odd, has no consecutive even departures and all the $c_j$ at its observed odd departures have largest-to-smallest ratio at most $6$. This is a decidable prefix-closed tree.

**Lemma.** No infinite path in this tree comes from any rational $z\in\mathbb Z_2$. In particular it has no ultimately periodic lower parity path.

Write $z=v_0/d$ with fixed positive odd $d$, allowing a signed numerator. Following the actual odd departures gives odd integer numerators $v_n$ over the same (possibly unreduced) denominator. Their equations and exact solution are
\[
 2^{a_i}v_{i+1}=3v_i+d,\qquad
 v_n=c_n\left(v_0+\frac d3\sum_{i<n} c_i^{-1}\right).
\]
The constraint $1/6\le c_i\le6$ thus makes $v_n$ eventually positive and tending to infinity, and $|v_n|\le 6|v_0|+12dn$. A repeated numerator would give an eventually periodic future, since each next odd state is unique. Hence the numerators are distinct.

For $m\ge1$ each of the at most $2^m$ possible preceding $m$ odd gaps determines the last numerator modulo $3^m$. In fact iteration gives $2^{A}v_j=3^m v_{j-m}+dC_w$ and $2^A$ is invertible at three. Therefore the number of distinct positive numerators up to $X$ on this path is at most $m+2^m(1+X/3^m)$. Fix $m$ with $12d(2/3)^m<1/2$ and apply this bound at $X=6|v_0|+12dN$. The first $N$ numerators contribute $N-O_{v_0,d}(1)$ positive distinct points there, which is impossible as $N\to\infty$. This proof works also when $3\mid d$ and for a negative initial rational. It uses no termination theorem for positive or negative integers. Related orbit-density and coefficient results are due to [García and Tal](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-90-3-245-250) and occur in the [project's orbitwise coefficient packet](../../integrated/orbitwise-boundaries/README.md).

## 2. Simultaneous tree and effective separation

Let $M(X)=(uX+b)/v$ with $u,b,v$ fixed integers, $uv\ne0$. Only upper values $M(x)$ that are positive ordinary integers are at issue. Consider a fixed finite directed graph whose edges are labeled by shortcut parity and a specified finite set of initial vertices. Its admitted prefixes are the labels of finite walks from those vertices. Suppose every infinite label sequence in the graph is ultimately periodic. Then one can compute a finite depth $H$ such that **no lower admissible prefix of depth $H$ has an upper label prefix in the graph at the same ordinary source**. It suffices here to test existence modulo a power of two, hence this stronger prefix-exclusion does not need an upper height bound.

For the proof one may use pairs of parity prefixes, lower and upper, at common depth $h$. Each determines exactly one dyadic source residue modulo $2^h$ by the affine shortcut lifting formula (or by choosing the bits one at a time). Retain a pair if its residues $l,r$ satisfy $vr\equiv ul+b\pmod{2^h}$, and the two language tests hold. Retaining a finite graph path as part of the upper state is also permitted. An infinite branch gives two $2$-adic sources $\xi,\eta$ with $v\eta=u\xi+b$. An eventually periodic upper parity sequence has a rational source. Specifically a period word of length $L>0$ and odd count $p$ has unique fixed point $C/(2^L-3^p)$ in $\mathbb Z_2$; the inverse affine map of its finite preperiod is rational. This follows directly from parity-prefix uniqueness and the geometric series, including an all-even cycle with fixed point zero. Hence $\xi$ is rational, contradicting the lemma. The finitely branching tree has finite height by König's lemma. Enumerating its levels until the first empty one computes a valid $H$. The argument remains valid if the upper graph is replaced by a decidable prefix-closed tree with only rational $2$-adic sources on its infinite branches. [Lagarias, *The set of rational cycles for the 3x+1 problem*](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-56-1-33-53)

The hypothesis holds for any finite graph language of subexponential word growth. Determinize its prefix language, discard states unreachable from the start, and view missing transitions as rejection. In a strongly connected component containing a state with two different internal outgoing labels, the two distinct labeled loops obtained by returning to that state give two distinct words of a common length (repeat the two loops to their common length; their first labels differ). Concatenation then gives exponential growth. Thus each recurrent strongly connected component of a subexponential deterministic graph is a single directed cycle. Every infinite path ultimately stays in one component and is ultimately periodic. This permits unbounded odd counts, long mixtures of periodic blocks and arbitrary repetitions inside the prescribed graph.

For all restricted cycles at sufficiently large $s$ the actual lower prefix to the computed depth $H$ is admissible, so the actual upper parity prefix at a phase must leave that graph by $H$. In particular there are only finitely many integral phases for which the **whole** upper parity itinerary stays in a fixed graph as above. A finite effective height bound follows from §1 at depth $H$ and $x\le(16s+7)/3$. Below that bound, following the lower odd map inside the finite height strip decides the restricted-cycle membership without assuming general Collatz convergence.

## 3. Odd count before a prescribed terminal

For a nonnegative integer $K$, take the graph of upper prefixes with at most $K$ ones; every infinite admitted path is eventually all zero. The computed empty depth $H_K$ gives the following consequence for actual restricted-cycle phases with $s$ beyond the effective onset. If $M(x)$ reaches $1$ with at most $K$ odd departures, that hit occurs **before $H_K$**, because otherwise the prefixes at depth $H_K$ would survive both tests. Every preimage of $1$ at clock $t$ is at most $2^t$, giving an additional effective source bound. Equivalently use a graph that processes at most $K$ ones and then permits the repeating $10$ tail of the ordinary $1,2$ cycle; this graph has the same eventual-periodicity property. One can therefore decide the finite exception set for each $K$, including true first hits, by bounded physical arithmetic. The proof requires no restriction on the sizes of its $K$ dyadic gaps.

At a bounded bad cycle the good marks include $x-1$, $x+1$, $2x-1$ at every phase, and $3x$ at every native exponent-one phase or phase followed by two native exponent-two edges. In particular the descending-run start $x=1+(4/3)^r(s-1)$ is on-cycle and $3x$ reaches one. The statement for its upper hit retains $r\in\{2,3,4,5\}$. The same statement applies to the least canonical $z$ when its earlier divisibility squeeze puts it on-cycle, notably at $r=2$. See the [ordinary neighbor and canonical proofs](../../astra-unconventional-20261126/cycle/CANONICAL_FLOOR.md).

There is no common $K$ or common finite graph known to contain the full good itineraries at all possible phases. A target-dependent graph built from one actual hitting itinerary makes the computed bound depend on that same itinerary. This does not eliminate the bounded branch.

## 4. All positive cycles of prescribed eccentricity

The finite-language theorem extends to cycles with a fixed bound $C$ on the ratio of largest to smallest odd phase, allowing arbitrary native exponents. Replace $C$ by an integer upper bound at least two. The edges themselves give $2^{a_i}=(3x_i+1)/x_{i+1}\le 3C+1$, so there is a known maximum gap. For each fixed physical depth, as the minimum tends to infinity the observed odd coefficient ratios and their reciprocals are at most $R=2C$, by the edge product in §1. Enforce both the maximum gap and the coefficient test on the lower prefix tree.

For any infinite branch the last $m$ positive gaps at any odd site have total $A\le m\log_2 3+\log_2 R$. The number of ordered positive compositions of length $m$ with sum at most $N$ is $\binom{N}{m}$. The binomial theorem at 2 bounds this by $3^N/2^m$. As $\log_2 3<8/5$ and $3^8<6^5$, there is a constant $\rho=3^{8/5}/2<3$ such that at most $3^{\log_2 R}\rho^m$ preceding words can occur. Each still fixes one terminal residue modulo $3^m$. For a rational source of fixed odd denominator the signed numerator bounds from §1 carry over with constants depending on $R$ and the denominator. A first repetition would force a periodic future, whereas the affine formula gives convergence to $+\infty$. The proportion of permitted terminal residue classes tends to zero, which contradicts the resulting sequence of distinct positive numerators of at most linear height. The proof of §2 now applies verbatim. In particular, for any fixed affine mark and any fixed upper odd-hit budget, the candidate phases over *all positive integer cycles of eccentricity at most $C$* form an effectively decidable finite set.
