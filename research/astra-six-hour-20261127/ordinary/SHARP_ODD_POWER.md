# The exact uniform clock on odd-base perfect powers

For nonnegative \(d\) and positive odd \(b\), let \(j=11+162b^{2^d}\). A budget \(H\) permits the following two independently chosen prefixes. Start the native trajectory at \(M_j=2^j-1\), allow its full initial ascent to \(P_j=3^j-1\), then allow at most \(H\) additional shortcut steps. On the other arm choose **any** earlier exponent \(1\le t<j\), allow the full ascent from \(M_t\) to \(P_t\), and at most \(H\) additional steps. Ask whether the two prefixes have a common site. Clocks need not agree. A visitor that spends an unbounded time after its own peak is outside this definition.

**Theorem.** If \(d\ge494\), the least budget that works for every odd \(b\) is exactly 499. If \(0\le d\le493\), no finite budget works for every odd \(b\). More precisely, at each \(H\) there is an ordinary odd-base residue class containing arbitrarily large positive \(b\) for which every earlier exponent and every pair of allowed clocks misses. These are statements about actual positive integers. A missing bounded-window merger is compatible with both convergence and a later ordinary merger.

The classification extends to **every positive ordinary degree** \(n\). For the original parameters \(m=b^n\) on all positive odd bases, a finite uniform budget exists exactly when \(2^{494}\mid n\), and the optimum is again 499. For sufficiency apply the positive identity at base \(b^{n/2^{494}}\). For necessity write \(n=2^d w\) with \(w\) odd. Raising to the \(w\)-th power is a bijection on every finite binary cyclic subgroup generated by 5, so the lifting argument below produces odd-base \(n\)-th powers on the same arbitrarily deep controls. The positive half at \(d\ge494\) uses \(t=j-9\). It is the unconditional [499-clock positive peak identity](OrdinaryPower.lean). Under the bounded-cycle floor it transfers goodness to the original canonical parent. No floor or Collatz hypothesis enters the lower bounds.

## A carry that cannot acquire more odd steps after a given position

For a chronological parity word, start its integer carry at \(c_0=-1\). At position \(i\) an even bit leaves the carry fixed and an odd bit gives \(c_{i+1}=3c_i+2^i\). With \(p\) odds, after \(h\) positions the physical endpoint from a peak of leading coefficient \(X\) equals
\[
                   (3^pX+c_h)/2^h,\qquad\text{source }X-1 .
\]
This also defines the shortcut operations on rational numbers with odd denominator, with parity read in the numerator. For every leading coefficient used here the initial bit is even. Hence \(c_1=-1\). From this position onward the carry is nondecreasing, since an odd bit increases it by \(2c_i+2^i\ge0\). At an odd position \(i\ge1\) the new carry is at least \(2^i-3\). In particular any such word ending with a prescribed carry \(C\) has **no odd bit at any position \(i\) with \(2^i>C+3\)**. This bound concerns arbitrary word length.

Take the actual positive anchor \(P_{173}\) through \(h_0=498\). It has \(p=240\) odd departures and its signed carry \(C\) satisfies
\[
                 2^{498}T^{498}P_{173}=3^{173+240}+C,\qquad
                 2^{496}\le C+3<2^{497}.
\]
The receipt gives the full integer \(C\). Put \(X_*=-C/3^{240}\), an odd rational with odd denominator. The displayed identity gives \(X_*\equiv3^{173}\bmod2^{498}\). The trajectory from \(X_*-1\) consequently has the same first 498 bits as \(P_{173}\), with endpoint zero. It stays at zero at every later clock and keeps the fixed grades and carry \(240,C\).

Consider any integer saving \(D\ge1\). The abstract earlier source is \(3^{-D}X_*-1\). A same-clock merger with compensated grade means equality of endpoints and earlier odd count equal to native odd count plus \(D\). It persists at every later clock. Advance to at least clock 498. Its earlier carry must now equal \(C\), and no earlier bit at a position \(i\ge497\) can be odd, by the preceding carry bound. Thus the earlier odd count is at most 497, giving \(D\le257\), and its count at 498 must be \(240+D\).

This is a complete finite test. For \(D=1,\ldots,257\) take the least positive residue \(R_D=3^{173-D}\bmod2^{498}\), using the inverse of three when \(D>173\). The positive ordinary starting residue \(R_D-1\) has the earlier word through 498. None of its 257 odd counts equals \(240+D\). This excludes a compensated merger with the rational control at **every** clock and **every** positive saving, including savings corresponding to a negative signed-anchor exponent. In fact no earlier rational source here reaches zero under even unrestricted *unequal* signed clocks. The last native odd bit makes \(C\) a unit modulo three. The reduced denominator of \(3^{-D}X_*-1\) is therefore exactly \(3^{240+D}\). Every odd step removes precisely one factor of three while this denominator is nontrivial, and an even step preserves it. After clearing the denominator an ordinary signed nonzero integer cannot enter zero. Thus a first zero would use exactly \(240+D\) odds and extend to the compensated common zero already excluded. The transfer to actual positive integers below still imposes a bound on both clocks.

## Ordinary odd bases realize every finite prefix of the control

The exact valuation \(v_2((3^{162})^u-1)=3+v_2(u)\) for positive \(u\) follows by separating the odd part and successively squaring a number \(1\bmod8\). At binary precision \(K\), the powers of \(3^{162}\) thus run through every unit \(1\bmod8\) with period \(2^{K-3}\). For \(K\ge499\), choose the exponent residue \(m\bmod2^{K-3}\) solving
\[
                    3^{11}(3^{162})^m\equiv X_*\pmod {2^K}.
\]
Since \(X_*\equiv3^{173}\bmod2^{498}\), this residue has \(m\equiv1\bmod2^{495}\). For each \(0\le d\le493\) it is the \(2^d\)-th power of an odd ordinary residue. Indeed \(5^u\) runs through all \(1\bmod4\) units, with \(v_2(5^u-1)=2+v_2(u)\). The exponent of \(5\) representing \(m\) is divisible by \(2^{493}\). Taking a positive \(2^d\)-th root and adding multiples of \(2^{K-3}\) supplies arbitrarily large odd \(b\) with \(3^j\equiv X_*\bmod2^K\). The replay computes explicit examples by recovering these two logarithms one bit at a time with integer multiplication.

Now fix any window \(H\) and take \(K\ge\max(H,499)\). For all these bases the native actual word and, for *every* \(D<j\), the actual word from \(P_{j-D}\) agree through \(H\) with their respective rational-control words. If the two actual words meet with compensating grades, the exact affine identities give equal integer carries. Their two rational lifts consequently have an equal endpoint at the same clock and compensating grades, already excluded. For sufficiently large \(b\) the [ordinary window separation theorem](CLOCK_SEPARATION.md) makes every possible physical meeting in the two ascent-plus-\(H\) windows a compensated same-clock postpeak meeting. This excludes all earlier choices at that budget. The elementary part of the separation proof suffices if both clocks start postpeak.

For \(d\ge494\), every odd \(b\) has \(m=b^{2^d}\equiv1\bmod2^{496}\), so its native and all candidate earlier words agree with the rational control at least through 498. The same argument excludes the 498-budget window for all sufficiently large such bases. The positive 499-clock identity works for each of them, finishing the exact threshold and exact optimum.
