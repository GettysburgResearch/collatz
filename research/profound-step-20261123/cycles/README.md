# The unit-equation attack on unrestricted cycles

**Full positive-cycle exclusion remains OPEN.** Read
[UNIT_EQUATION_HEIGHT_ATTACK.md](UNIT_EQUATION_HEIGHT_ATTACK.md).

This pass attempts to remove the growing number of terms in the complete
cycle carry by encoding actual odd edges as the two-variable unit equation

\[
(-3n_i)+(2^{a_i}n_{i+1})=1.
\]

The encoding is valid for every primitive cycle. A source-qualified
Beukers--Schlickewei application gives
\(p\le2^{16t+24}\), where \(t\) is the multiplicative rank generated
by its actual odd states. The same argument forces unbounded
multiplicative rank along any infinite non-eventually-periodic ordinary
orbit. It does not give an upper bound on that rank or exclude an
individual integer cycle.

The attempted stronger application fixes the group generated by two and
three, allowing its full algebraic divisible closure, and invokes the
small-height-neighborhood theorem of Evertse--Schlickewei--Schmidt.
The note proves that every ordinary \(n\ge2\) prime to six instead obeys

\[
\frac{h(\eta)}{1+h(\gamma)}
\ge\frac{\log n}{1+\log(3n+1)+2\log n}>\frac18
\quad\text{whenever }(-3n,3n+1)=\gamma\eta.
\]

Here \(\gamma\) lies in the fixed divisible two/three group. The
external theorem requires a much smaller ratio. The full proof accounts
for all finite places and the factor two in affine-vector height under
coordinate inversion. This is an exact obstruction to that proposed
application, not a proof against integer cycles or all unit-equation
methods.

The work is written mathematics, with primary theorem statements and
their precise inspection boundaries recorded. No new Lean theorem,
large cycle census or unrestricted cycle conclusion is claimed.
