# Compressing the whole cycle into a unit equation

Date: 23 November 2026. Base: PR178 at
`4e204f54136c79cf8c9393b34e3330594f99ae0a`.

**Status: attempted unrestricted cycle exclusion; the closing step remains
OPEN.** The direct two-variable unit-equation encoding below is valid at
every period. It trades the growing number of carry terms for the
multiplicative rank of the actual states. A second attempt to keep the
group fixed and use a small-height perturbation fails quantitatively:
every nontrivial positive integer cycle state lies outside the required
neighborhood, even when algebraic roots of the two/three units are allowed.

These are written deductions. The quoted unit-equation bounds are
SOURCE-QUALIFIED by their primary statements. There is no new Lean claim,
no new cycle census, and no asserted novelty for the general use of unit
equations to bound finite-rank orbits.

## 1. The exact unrestricted input

Use the accelerated map on positive odd integers:

\[
U(n)=\frac{3n+1}{2^{a(n)}},\qquad a(n)=v_2(3n+1)\ge1.
\tag{1}
\]

A primitive positive integer cycle has distinct odd states
\(n_0,\ldots,n_{p-1}\), with indices taken modulo \(p\), satisfying

\[
2^{a_i}n_{i+1}=3n_i+1.
\tag{2}
\]

Every state is prime to three: the right side of (2) is prime to three,
so each successor, hence each cyclic state, is prime to three. Adjacent
odd states are coprime, because a common divisor of \(n_i,n_{i+1}\)
would divide one. If the cycle is nontrivial it does not contain 1,
so every odd state is at least 5 and is prime to six.

This retains the same complete integer-cycle problem as the full carry
condition

\[
D=2^A-3^p>0,\qquad
C=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}2^{d_i}3^{p-1-i},\qquad D\mid C,
\tag{3}
\]

where \(A=\sum_i a_i\) and the \(d_i\) are the actual odd positions.
No component of \(D\) has been discarded. Conversely, the complete
positive fixed word in (3) gives the actual integer trajectory, as
explained in the preceding PR178 packet. The present argument starts
after that full integrality hypothesis, rather than replacing it with
proper-factor congruences.

The new PR182 note
[`CYCLE_CANCELLATION.md`](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/b78819e95ee7d39f02022de991b42217821e7590/research/closure-round2-20261123/CYCLE_CANCELLATION.md)

## 2. A valid fixed-arity compression

Let \(G\subset\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}\) be the multiplicative group
generated by the actual odd cycle states, and let \(t=\operatorname{rank}G\).
The group is finitely generated and torsion-free. Choose generators
\(g_1,\ldots,g_t\). In \((\mathbb C^\times)^2\), let \(H\) be generated by

\[
(g_j,1),\ (1,g_j)\quad(1\le j\le t),
\qquad(-3,1),\qquad(1,2).
\tag{4}
\]

Its rank is at most \(2t+2\). Each actual edge supplies the point

\[
Y_i=(-3n_i,\ 2^{a_i}n_{i+1})=(-3n_i,\ 3n_i+1)\in H,
\qquad Y_{i,1}+Y_{i,2}=1.
\tag{5}
\]

The points are distinct because their first coordinates recover the
distinct \(n_i\). Thus a whole primitive cycle supplies \(p\) distinct
solutions of one equation in two variables, in one unchanged group.

Beukers--Schlickewei, Theorem 1.1, bounds the number of solutions of
\(x+y=1\) in the divisible closure of a rank-\(r\) group by
\(2^{8(r+1)}\). Applying that statement to (4) gives

\[
\boxed{p\le 2^{16t+24}.}
\tag{6}
\]

This application does not incur a growing number of variables. The
growth has moved into the group rank. If \(R\) is the number of distinct
prime factors of \(\prod_i n_i\), then \(t\le R\), so the weaker bound
\(p\le2^{16R+24}\) follows as well. No small bound on either \(t\) or
\(R\) was obtained from the cycle equations.

The same argument applies to \(m\) distinct odd source states on one
finite actual path: include its endpoint in \(G\) and obtain
\(m\le2^{16t+24}\). In particular, an infinite non-eventually-periodic
ordinary orbit cannot have all its odd states in one finite-rank
multiplicative group. Otherwise this bound would apply to arbitrarily
long distinct prefixes with one fixed rank bound. This is a deduction
from the external unit-equation theorem, not a new proof that ordinary
orbits have bounded multiplicative rank.

**First attempted closing step.** Prove that the special cyclic relation
(2) keeps \(t\) below the threshold in (6), uniformly in the unknown
period. Adjacent coprimality and the total product identity do not give
that upper bound. The available unit-equation theorem allows a finite
cycle whose number of states lies below its rank-dependent bound. It
does not assert that there are no positive cyclic configurations.

## 3. Trying to keep the group fixed

The natural fixed group is generated by two and three. Its divisible
closure in each coordinate consists of algebraic numbers whose powers
are signed two/three units. Let

\[
\Gamma_{2,3}=\overline{\langle2,3\rangle}^{\,\mathrm{div}}
\times\overline{\langle2,3\rangle}^{\,\mathrm{div}}.
\tag{7}
\]

This group has rank four; its possible roots of unity do not increase
that rank. At every finite place above a prime other than two or three,
every coordinate of every \(\gamma\in\Gamma_{2,3}\) is a unit.

The deeper near-group theorem of Evertse--Schlickewei--Schmidt is
particularly relevant to this proposed repair. Their Theorem 2.1 studies
solutions \(Y_1+\cdots+Y_d=1\) admitting a factorization

\[
Y=\gamma\eta,\qquad \gamma\in\Gamma,
\qquad h(\eta)\le\varepsilon_d(1+h(\gamma)),
\quad
\varepsilon_d=d^{-1}\exp(-(4d)^{3d}).
\tag{8}
\]

The height is the absolute logarithmic affine-vector height, with a 1
included in each local maximum. For a fixed finite-rank group the
solutions lie in finitely many proper linear subspaces. In dimension
two each such subspace meets \(Y_1+Y_2=1\) in at most one point.
This would be a powerful closing input if actual edges could be placed
in this fixed group's neighborhood.

They cannot. The following proposition excludes the proposed hypothesis
for every relevant ordinary edge, independently of its cycle length.

### Proposition: an absolute height gap from the fixed two/three group

Let \(n\ge2\) be an ordinary integer prime to six, and put
\(Y=(-3n,3n+1)\). For every componentwise factorization

\[
Y=\gamma\eta,\qquad \gamma\in\Gamma_{2,3},
\qquad\eta\in(\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\times})^2,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\frac{h(\eta)}{1+h(\gamma)}
\ge
\frac{\log n}{1+\log(3n+1)+2\log n}
>\frac18.
}
\tag{9}
\]

**Proof.** Choose a number field containing all coordinates, and use
product-formula normalized absolute values. For every prime
\(\ell\mid n\), the first coordinate \(\gamma_1\) is an \(\ell\)-adic
unit. Consequently the local contributions above \(\ell\) to
\(h(\eta_1^{-1})\) sum to \(v_\ell(n)\log\ell\). Because
\(n\) is prime to six, summing over these primes gives

\[
h(\eta)\ge h(\eta_1)=h(\eta_1^{-1})\ge\log n.
\tag{10}
\]

This argument permits algebraic roots and arbitrary degree; it does not
depend on choosing a particular rational unit approximation.

The vector height is subadditive under componentwise multiplication.
Scalar heights are invariant under inversion, while in dimension two

\[
h(\eta^{-1})
\le h(\eta_1^{-1})+h(\eta_2^{-1})
=h(\eta_1)+h(\eta_2)
\le2h(\eta).
\]

The factor two is necessary for this argument: affine-vector height is
not in general invariant under simultaneous coordinate inversion.
Since \(Y\) has integer coordinates,
\(h(Y)=\log(3n+1)\). Therefore

\[
h(\gamma)=h(Y\eta^{-1})
\le\log(3n+1)+2h(\eta).
\tag{11}
\]

Set \(a=1+\log(3n+1)>0\). The function \(u/(a+2u)\) is increasing
for \(u\ge0\). Combining (10)--(11) proves the first inequality in
(9). The second is equivalent to \(n^6>e(3n+1)\). For \(n\ge2\),
\(n^6\ge32n\), whereas \(e(3n+1)<3(3n+1)\le(21/2)n\), using
\(e<3\). This proves the strict inequality. \(\square\)

For dimension two, (8) requires
\(\varepsilon_2=\tfrac12\exp(-8^6)<1/8\). Hence **no nontrivial
positive integer cycle edge satisfies the proposed small-height
factorization over (7)**. This is a failure of the proposed application,
not a failure of the external theorem and not an exclusion of the edge.

The known core correctly escapes the statement: at \(n=1\),
\(Y=(-3,4)\) belongs to the fixed group, so one may take
\(\eta=(1,1)\) with height zero. Ordinary convergent states such as
\(n=5\) satisfy the same obstruction (9) as hypothetical cycle states.
Thus (9) is not an integer-cycle discriminator by itself.

## 4. Why the real error does not repair the height hypothesis

The relative one-step correction is

\[
\lambda(n)=\frac{3n}{3n+1}.
\]

It approaches one in the real absolute value, but coprimality gives

\[
1-\lambda(n)=\frac1{3n+1},\qquad
h(\lambda(n))=\log(3n+1).
\tag{12}
\]

The real error decreases while its arithmetic height increases. The
finite-place contributions cannot be omitted in (8). Proposition (9)
shows that changing the two/three unit factor does not remove this
problem, even in the divisible algebraic group.

For a larger fixed finite set of rational primes \(S\), the same proof
gives (10) with \(\log n\) replaced by the logarithm of the part of
\(n\) supported outside \(S\). Thus enlarging the fixed group asks for
an additional bound on that outside-prime height. Alternatively,
adjoining all actual states makes (5) an exact unit equation, but returns
to the uncontrolled rank in (6). Neither operation supplies a uniform
arithmetic restriction on the unknown integer cycle.

## 5. What a successful continuation would need

The finite-arity representation (5) avoids the growing polynomial
degree in the earlier complete-denominator gcd encodings. It does not
avoid all growing arithmetic data. A successful continuation would need
one of the following genuinely additional statements:

- A rank restriction, or a special cyclic refinement of the unit-equation
  theorem, strong enough to exclude the actual ordered points (5).
- A different fixed-rank encoding whose small-height condition is
  established for the actual integer states, while retaining the entire
  closed cycle and its denominator.
- A direct contradiction for the complete condition (3).

None is proved here. In particular, the exponentially large bound (6)
is not presented as a numerical improvement over existing cycle bounds,
and no improved finite search is proposed as a substitute for the missing
unrestricted argument.
