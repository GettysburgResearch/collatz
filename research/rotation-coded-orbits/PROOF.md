# Irrational rotations cannot prescribe ordinary shortcut parities or accelerated valuation floors

**PROPOSED.** Let $0<\alpha<1$ be irrational, $A\ge1$ an integer, $B_h\ge1$ integers, and $\beta_h$ arbitrary real numbers for a nonempty finite list of indices $h$. For a fixed integer offset, let
$$
d_j=A j+\sum_h B_h\lfloor j\alpha+\beta_h\rfloor+\mathrm{offset},\qquad j\ge0.
$$
The 2-adic number $\Xi=\sum_{j\ge0}2^{d_j}/3^j$ is transcendental over $\mathbb Q$. Hence no ordinary shortcut trajectory has Sturmian parity even eventually, and no ordinary accelerated odd trajectory has an eventually two-valued Sturmian valuation directive. More generally the latter cannot have valuation sums of the displayed multiple-floor form. The elementary [single-floor argument](ELEMENTARY.md) suffices to exclude rational sources in both named Sturmian cases.

Separately, consider an affine chart with $Q=2^b>1$, positive odd $P>1$, and finitely many integer digits distinct modulo $Q$. If a digit itinerary is coded by an irrational circle rotation with a finite interval partition and is not eventually periodic, its series $\sum a_j(Q/P)^j$ is transcendental in $\mathbb Q_2$. There is no bound on the number of intervals. This excludes such ordinary itineraries in both the binary $64\to81$ chart and the strict six-branch chart $Q=2^{19},P=3^{12}$. It applies to the actual sequence of affine branch choices in the latter chart; its cap–root horizon has a different meaning.

## A sparse-return argument

Normalize absolute values in a number field by local degree over global degree. We use the algebraic-coefficient S-adic Subspace Theorem: if at each place in a finite set $S$ containing the infinite places there are $d$ independent fixed linear forms, then nonzero $S$-integer vectors with the product of those absolute values less than $H(\mathbf x)^{-\varepsilon}$ lie in finitely many proper subspaces. Use the maximum homogeneous local norm in $H$. The standard projective formulation implies this application since the coordinates are $S$-integral. [Number-field S-integer formulation, Theorem 2](https://people.mpi-sws.org/~joel/publications/hecke-mahler25.pdf)

Here are the precise ingredients used in both applications. Assume a fixed 2-adic sum is algebraic and choose its number field $K$ with the given 2-adic embedding, defining place $v$. For unbounded integers $q$ we obtain a number $G_q\in K$ that equals a 2-adic infinite sum of sparse terms $w_{q,j}M_j$. In the applications:

* The nonzero coefficients are from a fixed finite set, and $M_j$ are positive rational $S$-units with logarithmic height $O(j+1)$. For bounded integer differences $j-j'$ the ratios $M_j/M_{j'}$ range over a finite set.
* At $v$, every tail starting beyond $N$ has absolute value at most $\exp(-cN+O(1))$ for fixed $c>0$ and has nonzero sum if it has any hits. There are always infinitely many hits. At one fixed place $v'$ over an odd prime, $|M_j|_{v'}=\exp(c'j)$ with $c'>0$.
* At $v'$, $G_q$ is bounded independently of $q$. At all places outside a fixed finite $S$ it is integral. At places of $S$ other than $v$, the product of $\max(1,|G_q|)$ is at most $\exp(Cq+O(1))$ for a constant independent of the sample length below. At $v$ it is bounded as well.
* For every fixed $L>0$ there are boundedly many hits in $0\le j\le Lq$ as $q$ varies. Any finite string of hits at bounded relative positions has nonzero weighted sum.

Choose $L$ with $cL>C+1$. Pass to a subsequence with a fixed number of hits through $\lfloor Lq\rfloor$. Group consecutive hits if their intervening gap is bounded on a further subsequence, making each such gap fixed and every gap between groups tend to infinity. Make the coefficients and within-group ratios fixed by the finite-set conditions. The contribution of any group has the form $C_i M_{j_i}$ with fixed nonzero rational $C_i$. If the first group begins at a bounded index, it contributes a fixed rational constant $C_0$ on a further subsequence. Otherwise put $C_0=0$. All remaining first indices $j_i$ tend to infinity and successive differences tend to infinity. Moreover $G_q-C_0\ne0$: once the bounded first group has been removed it is the tail from the first remaining hit onward, with infinitely many hits.

Use the vector $(G_q-C_0,M_{j_1},\ldots,M_{j_k})$, enlarging $S$ by the finitely many denominator primes of $C_0,C_i$ if necessary. At $v$ replace the first coordinate form by $X_0-\sum C_iX_i$, retaining the coordinate forms everywhere else. The replaced form has size at most $\exp(-cLq+O(1))$. Each other coordinate is an $S$-unit. By the product formula the product of all forms is at most $\exp((C-cL)q+O(1))$. The vector height is at most $\exp(O_L(q))$, including when viewed at $v$. If $k=0$ this contradicts the product formula for the nonzero $G_q-C_0$ directly. Otherwise the Subspace Theorem puts infinitely many of these vectors in a fixed proper subspace. At $v'$, the unbounded coordinates have strictly increasing and mutually separated sizes, and $G_q-C_0$ is bounded. Hence in a fixed linear relation the last unbounded coordinate with nonzero coefficient dominates everything earlier. No such coordinate can occur, leaving only $G_q-C_0=0$, which has already been excluded.

This argument uses a finite place as the place of convergence. Its sparse return construction is related to the use of returns in recent work on complex Hecke–Mahler values. The [published complex theorem](https://people.mpi-sws.org/~joel/publications/hecke-mahler25.pdf) is not required here.

## Odd positions and accelerated valuations

An integer translation of every $d_j$ rescales $\Xi$ rationally, so suppose $d_j\ge0$. Let $p/q$ run through convergents to $\alpha$ from below and put $\eta=q\alpha-p>0$, $P_q=Aq+p\sum B_h$. For every $j$,
$$
e_{q,j}=d_{q+j}-d_j-P_q=\sum_h B_h\mathbf1_{[1-\eta,1)}(\{j\alpha+\beta_h\}).
$$
Each interval has positive width and infinitely many visits. Two visits to the same interval have separation at least $q$: its width is $\|q\alpha\|$, and the best approximation property of convergents gives $\|i\alpha\|>\|q\alpha\|$ for $0<i<q$. Thus there are at most $H(\lfloor L\rfloor+2)$ hits through $Lq$, where $H$ is the number of floors.

If $\Xi$ were algebraic, take
$$
G_q=\frac{3^q}{2^{P_q}}\left(\Xi-\sum_{j<q}2^{d_j}3^{-j}\right)-\Xi.
$$
It equals $\sum_{j\ge0}(2^{e_{q,j}}-1)M_j$ at the selected 2-adic place, with $M_j=2^{d_j}3^{-j}$. Each nonzero coefficient is an odd positive integer from a fixed finite set. Since $d_j\ge j-O(1)$ is strictly increasing, the first term of every nonempty tail uniquely minimizes its 2-adic valuation. Between indices at bounded distance the exponents differ by bounded integers. At any place over 3 the $M_j$ grow exponentially with $j$ and $G_q$ is bounded: multiplying the finite prefix by $3^q$ clears its 3-denominators and $2^{-P_q}$ is a 3-adic unit. At every other place the finite formula has at most exponential size in $q$, since $d_j,P_q=O(q)$ there for $j<q$; outside primes dividing 6 and the denominators of $\Xi$ all terms are integral. This verifies the sparse-return argument.

For shortcut parities with odd positions $d_j$, the inverse parity formula gives $x=-\Xi/3$ in $\mathbb Z_2$. It follows by iterating $2x_{n+1}=3^{\varepsilon_n}x_n+\varepsilon_n$ and letting the 2-adic remainder tend to zero. A lower mechanical parity word of density $0<\delta<1$ has odd positions $d_j=\lceil(j+c)/\delta\rceil-1$ for fixed $c$, from its cumulative floor. This agrees with $\lfloor (j+c)/\delta\rfloor$ except for at most one index. Upper mechanical words have the same description up to finitely many changes. Writing $1/\delta=A+\alpha$ gives the displayed single-floor theorem. A two-valued Sturmian accelerated valuation word with values $A<A+B$ has partial sums $Aj+B\lfloor j\alpha+\beta\rfloor+\mathrm{constant}$ up to the possible finite boundary edits. These sums are the odd positions starting at an odd source. Finite initial orbit steps and integer translations of all later exponents preserve the rationality question by a nonzero rational affine change.

## Fixed-affine interval labels

Put $T=Q/P$ and write the digit itinerary $a_j=f(\{j\alpha+\beta\})$ for a function constant on each of finitely many half-open intervals. Conventions at finitely many individual circle points only affect finitely many visits, hence make rational finite changes to the series. If $a_j$ is not eventually periodic, $w_{q,j}=a_{q+j}-a_j$ has infinitely many hits for each $q$. For a convergent from below, every mismatch lies within one backward interval of width $\eta$ at an endpoint of the partition. The same separation bound per endpoint applies. Put $M_j=T^j$ and, under the algebraicity hypothesis, set $G_q=T^{-q}(\Xi-\sum_{j<q}a_jT^j)-\Xi$ where $\Xi=\sum_{j\ge0}a_jT^j$. Its expansion is exactly $\sum_j w_{q,j}T^j$. At 2 every nonzero $w_{q,j}$ has valuation between 0 and $b-1$, so the first hit in a sum uniquely minimizes valuation. At any place over an odd prime dividing $P$, $M_j$ grow exponentially and $G_q$ is bounded since $T^{-q}T^j=(P/Q)^{q-j}$ in the finite prefix. All other bounds follow as above.

For the six-branch chart $x_{j+1}=(Px_j+a_j)/Q$, iteration gives $x_0=-\sum_{j\ge0}a_jT^j/P$ for any ordinary legal trajectory: $T^n x_n\to0$ at 2 since $x_n$ are integers. Its digits satisfy $a_i\equiv-Pr_i\pmod Q$ for distinct input residues $r_i$. The binary survivor chart has digits zero and one and rational normalization $17/81$. Both applications therefore meet the hypotheses. ## Signed floor coefficients

The result about $\Xi$ extends to arbitrary integer $A,B_h$ provided that $d_j$ is strictly increasing and its gap sequence is not eventually periodic. These assumptions imply $d_j\ge j-O(1)$. The exponents $e_{q,j}$ may now be negative, but range over a fixed finite set and can be nonzero only in the same boundedly many return intervals. Infinitely many nonzero indices occur for each $q$: if $d_{j+q}-d_j=P_q$ eventually, then the gaps are eventually $q$-periodic. In any finite group or infinite tail with a hit, compare the two strictly increasing exponent lists $d_{j+q}-P_q$ and $d_j$. At their first differing index the smaller of the two exponents occurs exactly once and is smaller than either exponent at any later index. Since the denominators $3^j$ are odd, this term uniquely minimizes the 2-adic valuation. The nonvanishing and tail bounds in the sparse-return argument therefore remain valid. The 3-adic and height bounds are unchanged.
