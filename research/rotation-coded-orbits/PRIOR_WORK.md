# Scope relative to earlier parity and dictionary results

This comparison uses the public versions available on 18 November 2026. The novelty boundary is narrowest for the rational and Sturmian specializations. The general moving-window statement, the signed multifloor statement and the modular stack results have different hypotheses.

| Earlier work | Overlap and remaining distinctions |
|---|---|
| Lyapunov Weaver, paper 15 v2, Theorems 1, 2, 5 and 9. A finite dictionary of periodic accelerated-exponent words is charged by its total division budget $B$. Strings of exponent one are free, and the other pieces may use arbitrary phases and cuts. Subject to the paper's stopped-cycle convention, the lower count on a fixed positive nonperiodic rational orbit is effective and $\gg\log t\log\log t$ even when $B\le t^\theta$, $\theta<1$. Its fixed-piece excursion exponent is sharply $\log_2 3$. [Paper 15](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/15/v2) | The earlier [fixed expanding template](../sparse-periodic-deviations/PROOF.md) lower bound $\omega(\log N)$ is an alternate and weaker order on their common fixed-dictionary scope. A bounded number of bit substitutions against a fixed expanding template cuts a prefix into a bounded number of periodic pieces and exceptional valuation letters. The least-source exponent one is a direct shortcut-Hamming formulation closely related to paper 15's fixed-budget proof: its good-word inequality for arbitrary small error controls total odd count in terms of endpoint height and total divisions, before the author takes a coarser worst-case excursion exponent. We claim no priority for that arithmetic mechanism. The [bounded-error self-return theorem](ORDINARY_PREFIX_RETURNS.md) permits an unbounded changing block length $q$ with only $O(q)$ observed time. Charging the dictionary consisting of that block uses a linear budget, so the $t^\theta$ conclusion does not supply this self-return statement. |
| Lyapunov Weaver, paper 16 v3, Theorems 1 and 1d, excludes nonperiodic parity on positive rational orbits for every automatic coding and every primitive-substitution coding. Theorems 1e and 1f add specified semisimple and dominant-Jordan nonprimitive classes. The paper labels the earlier automatic and primitive results accepted and the two nonprimitive additions under review. [Paper 16](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/16/v3) | The positive-ordinary nonrealization of Fibonacci, Tribonacci and other overlapping automatic or primitive examples is prior. Our [self-return theorem](ORDINARY_PREFIX_RETURNS.md) has no substitution-presentation hypothesis and gives divergence of Hamming distance on every nonperiodic positive ordinary orbit, including the moving-source extension. Its irrational-rotation consequence allows arbitrary finite interval partitions and endpoints. Paper 16 applies also to positive rational sources and covers many sequences for which bounded Hamming returns have not been established. |
| Lyapunov Weaver, paper 84 v1, Theorems 1, 5 and 8 and the finite-error result in Section 7. Every irrational Sturmian slope has a uniform finite positive-integer approximation exponent; at $\log_3 2$ the explicit bound is $2^{-14}y^{-2}$. It also treats accelerated two-valued mechanical schedules, finite substitutions about a critical mechanical factor, and restricted and unrestricted rational approximants. It credits Pham, Cassidy and De Jesús for qualitative irrationality. [Paper 84](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/84/v1) | Our elementary two-hit argument gives an independent qualitative proof; paper 84 supplies effective finite estimates absent from our proof. Its finite-error center is mechanical. Our moving-source center can be any nonperiodic positive ordinary trajectory, and our varying comparison source can be an arbitrary positive integer under the logarithmic height separation. These are different approximation problems. |
| Jaan Allikvere, 25 July 2026, Theorems 5.3 and 5.4. The Subspace shadow theorem treats exact agreement with $UV^\infty$ past a density-dependent threshold and implies transcendence of **every** Sturmian parity value at every irrational slope and intercept. Without density data an exact initial fractional power of exponent $>\log_2 3$ suffices. [Primary paper and TeX](https://zenodo.org/records/21543841) | Full Sturmian transcendence in our [series proof](PROOF.md) is prior. So too is its consequence for two-valued Sturmian accelerated schedules: every Sturmian word begins with arbitrarily long squares, and the nonerasing substitution of binary return blocks for its valuation letters preserves squares. The older exact-shadow criterion has a lower threshold and an algebraic-input conclusion. Our unrestricted Hamming self-return bound permits a fixed number of substitutions in changing long blocks. Signed multiple-floor exponents need no exhibited exact square or exact periodic shadow. More specialized members can satisfy both hypotheses. |

For a concrete separation of the *named coding classes*, label the arc $[0,\pi/4)$ by one under rotation by $(\sqrt2-1)/4$ and the complement by zero. Its one-frequency $\pi/4$ exceeds $\log_3 2$ and is transcendental. Primitive and automatic binary codings with a frequency have algebraic frequency. The rotation has both $00$ and $11$, hence is not Sturmian. Its zero runs have lengths two and three since the complement length is strictly between two and three rotation increments, and both entry phases have positive measure. Its gaps between ones thus take at least the three values 1, 3 and 4. The arbitrary finite-partition corollary directly excludes this itinerary on an ordinary positive orbit. This comparison does not determine whether an alternative exact-shadow proof works for this particular word.

The cited results do not establish the modular transcendence of the differences of our positive periodic **stack-height** directives, their rational-evaluation orbit bound, or the exact transcendence degree of their difference field. Periodic increment words in that construction produce sparse, increasing-gap parity programs; they are not periodic shortcut parities.
