# Quantitative parity returns on every signed rational orbit

**Proposed corollary.** Take any non-eventually-periodic shortcut-Collatz orbit starting at $x\in\mathbb Q\cap\mathbb Z_2$, of either sign, and put $e_j=T^j(x)\bmod2$. For every $L>\log(3/2)/\log(4/3)$ and fixed $k$, along $q\to\infty$ the disagreements between the first $\lfloor Lq\rfloor$ parities at starts $t$ and $t+q$ cannot be covered by $k$ intervals of total length $o(q)$ whenever $\log(2+|x_t|)=o(q)$. In particular their Hamming distance tends to infinity. If the two odd counts are equal on each disagreement interval, one may use every $L>\log_2(3/2)$. Thus any nonperiodic coding by finitely many intervals of an irrational circle rotation has an irrational inverse-parity source in $\mathbb Z_2$, without first imposing sign conditions on that source.

Here are the changes to the [moving-hyperplane proof](UNBALANCED_INTERVAL_RETURNS.md) and the [balanced case](ORDINARY_PREFIX_RETURNS.md). Fix an odd common denominator $d$ of the trajectory. On its sign-stable tail the finite reciprocal bound from the [signed numerator count](../local-mahler-lifting/SIGNED_PARITY.md) gives $M_m|x_{t+m}|\ll_x 1+|x_t|$ uniformly in $t,m$; the finitely many earlier starts are covered by enlarging the constant. All nonzero states have absolute value at least $1/d$. Also $|x_{t+m}|+1\le(3/2)^m(|x_t|+1)$. Equation (1) of the ordinary proof remains an equality in $\mathbb Q_2$; since $2x_{t+j}+1$ is always a 2-adic unit, a trimmed group's exact 2-valuation is unchanged. Adjoin to $S$ any denominator primes of $d$ and use coordinate forms there. Each coordinate contribution at those additional primes costs a fixed factor depending on $d$, apart from the $\exp(o(q))$ group coefficients already allowed in the proof. The archimedean differences and S-unit factors have the same bounds in absolute value.

A clean run of length $b$ requires $2^b$ to divide the nonzero integer $d(x_{t+j+q}-x_{t+j})$. This supplies the same clean-run height argument with an additive $O_d(1)$ in each of the bounded number of runs. Hence a proposed violation forces $\log(2+|x_{t+q}|)\gg q$ on a subsequence, where ordinary dominance and 2-adic group separation apply as before. Finally the convergent-denominator interval argument for irrational rotations gives only boundedly many mismatches, contradicting the signed return theorem.
