## 0. Summary

Conjecture C1 (bounded increments `a_n` of the collision energy) is **still not proved**. This note
proves the corresponding statement for almost every affine map, shows that it fails for a
topologically generic one, and so identifies C1 as a question about the specific points `A^j`.

Let `AA = Aff(Z_3) = {x -> lam x + B : lam a unit, B in Z_3}` with its Haar probability measure, and

    Q_n(lam, B) = 3^n P( X~ = lam X + B  mod 3^n ),        X, X~ independent Syracuse variables.

For `lam = 2^c` this is `Q_n(g) = <f_n, f_n o g>` of Part II, and `a_n = sum_{j != 0} 4^-|j| Q_n(A^j)`.

1. **Theorem 10.** `Q_n` is the level-`n` density of the unique stationary measure of the pair
   iterated function system `g -> phi_b g phi_a^-1` on `AA`; `(Q_n)` is a martingale and `Q_{n+1} = T Q_n`.
2. **Theorem 11.** Exactly, `||Q_n||^2 = sum_{v<n} (2Q_v - a_v)^2/(6 . 3^v) + Q_n^2/3^n` in `L^2(AA)`, where `Q_v = ||f_v||^2`.
   The series converges because `Q_v <= (5/3)^v`, so `Q_n -> Q` in `L^2(AA)` with `||Q||^2 = 1.2778`.
   This uses nothing beyond Part I. In particular, for almost every unit `lam`, `X~ - lam X` has a square-integrable density.
3. **Theorem 12.** `sup_n Q_n` is in `L^2(AA)`, hence finite for almost every `g`. It is infinite on a
   dense `G_delta` set, which contains every resonant map `phi_w~ phi_w^-1`.

Finiteness almost everywhere is by itself immediate, since `(Q_n)` is a positive martingale. What
Theorem 11 adds is that no mass escapes and that the supremum is square integrable; what Theorem 12
adds is that the exceptional set is large in the topological sense.

So "the correlation of `f` with its image under `g` stays bounded" is true for almost every affine `g` and
false for a residual set of `g`. C1 asks that the points `A^j`, `j != 0`, of the one-parameter subgroup
generated by `A y = 4y + 1` lie on the good side. Lemma 7 of Part II (they are not resonant) is
necessary and, by Theorem 12, far from sufficient: C1 is a Diophantine-type statement about how well
`A^j` is approximated by resonant maps relative to their weights.

## 1. The pair system (Theorem 10)

For `a, b >= 1` let `sigma_ab(lam, B) = (2^{a-b} lam, 2^-b (1 - lam + 3B))`. This is the affine map `g` with
`g o phi_a = phi_b o g'` for `g' = (lam, B)`; it multiplies Haar measure by `1/3`, contracts the `B`
coordinate by `1/3` and rotates `lam`. Choose `(a, b)` with probability `2^-(a+b)`.

**Theorem 10.** (a) The law `Pi` of `(lam, X~ - lam X)`, with `lam` Haar on the units and independent of
`X, X~`, is the unique stationary measure. (b) Its density on the sigma-algebra of `(lam, B) mod 3^n`
is `Q_n`; hence `(Q_n)` is a martingale with mean 1. (c) `Q_{n+1} = T Q_n`, where
`(T Phi)(g) = sum 3 . 2^-(a+b) Phi(sigma_ab^-1 g)` over the `(a, b)` with `g` in the image of `sigma_ab`;
`Q_1 = T 1 = W` takes the values `5/3, 2/3, 2/3, 4/3, 4/3, 1/3`. (d) `Q_n > 0` everywhere, so `Pi` has full support.

*Proof.* (a) If `lam'` is Haar and independent of `(a, b, X', X~')`, then `lam = 2^{a-b} lam'` is Haar and
still independent of them, and `g = sigma_ab(lam', X~' - lam' X')` maps `X = phi_a(X')` to `X~ = phi_b(X~')`,
which are independent Syracuse variables. Uniqueness: the fibre contracts and the walk `lam -> 2^{a-b} lam`
on the compact group of units converges to Haar measure, since its steps generate a dense subgroup and
include the identity. (b) is the definition of `Q_n` after integrating `lam` over a ball. (c) is
stationarity written for densities. (d) Every unit residue mod `3^n` has positive Syracuse mass, and
for any `(lam, B)` there is a unit `x` with `lam x + B` a unit. ∎

## 2. Square integrability (Theorem 11)

*Proof of the formula.* `int Q_n^2 = 3^n P(D~ = lam D mod 3^n)` with `D = X_1 - X_2`, `D~ = X~_1 - X~_2`
independent and `lam` Haar. Given `v_3(D) = v < n`, `lam D` is uniform on the `2 . 3^{n-v-1}` residues of
valuation `v`. With `p_v = P(v_3(D) = v) = 3^-v Q_v - 3^-(v+1) Q_{v+1} = 3^-(v+1) (2Q_v - a_v)` this gives
`3^n sum_{v<n} p_v^2/(2 . 3^{n-v-1}) + 3^n (3^-n Q_n)^2`. ∎

Since `0 <= 2Q_v - a_v <= 3Q_v` and `Q_v <= ||L||^{2v} = (5/3)^v`, the terms are at most `(3/2)(25/27)^v`.
So `(Q_n)` is an `L^2`-bounded martingale: `Pi` is absolutely continuous on `AA` with density
`Q = lim Q_n` in `L^2`, and `||Q||^2 = sum_v (2Q_v - a_v)^2/(6 . 3^v) = 1.2778`. This proof is elementary. It
does not give Tao's theorem, the absolute continuity of the Syracuse measure itself.

`f` itself is not square integrable (Part I). The pair density is, because averaging over `lam` decouples
the two differences: resonances are logarithmic singularities, and logarithms are square integrable.

## 3. Almost every map is good; a generic map is bad (Theorem 12)

*Proof.* (i) Doob's inequality gives `|| sup_n Q_n ||_2 <= 2 sup_n ||Q_n||_2`, finite by Theorem 11.
(ii) For words `w, w~` of equal length `l` let `r` be the affine extension of `phi_w~ phi_w^-1`. If `X = phi_w(X')`,
`X~ = phi_w~(X~')` and `X' = X~' mod 3^m`, then `X~ = r(X) mod 3^{l+m}`. Hence
`Q_{l+m}(r) >= 3^l 2^-(|w|+|w~|) Q_m`, which tends to infinity because `Q_m` does (Part I, Theorem 3).
The resonant maps are the images of the identity under the pair system; the law of the `n`-th image
converges weakly to `Pi`, which has full support, so they are dense. Each set `{sup_n Q_n > K}` is open,
since every `Q_n` is locally constant, and contains them. Baire's theorem applies on the compact group. ∎

## 4. The two level-1 modes

Let `d = (-1, 0, 1) sqrt(3/2)` and `u = (1, -2, 1)/sqrt 2` as functions of `x mod 3`: an orthonormal basis of the
mean-zero functions of level 1. For a density `g = 1 + alpha d + beta u`, the vector `L^n g - f_n` is orthogonal to `f_n` and

    || L^n g - f_n ||^2 = alpha^2 ||L^n d||^2 + 2 alpha beta <L^n d, L^n u> + beta^2 ||L^n u||^2,      ||L^n d||^2 = (3/2) a_n.

So C1, together with the same bound for `u`, says exactly that the Syracuse level laws started from a
residue class mod 3 stay at bounded `L^2` distance from the one started from Haar measure, while both
norms grow like `sqrt n`. Tao's theorem is the statement that their `L^1` distance tends to zero.

| n | `||L^n d||^2` | `||L^n u||^2` | `<L^n d, L^n u>` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0.699989 | 0.603148 | 0.423033 |
| 10 | 0.702253 | 0.590677 | 0.450371 |
| 12 | 0.704422 | 0.583731 | 0.465882 |
| 13 | 0.705382 | 0.581547 | 0.470937 |
| 14 | 0.706242 | 0.579928 | 0.474758 |

The entries involving `u` change by factors 0.74 and 0.76 per level at `n = 14`. The value `3/4` is the weight
of the self-loop `(a, b) = (1, 1)` at pair maps fixing the pole `-1`, so no rate better than `3/4` should be expected.
The `d` entry changes more slowly (0.88 to 0.91, Part II). These are observations.

## 5. Limits

- C1 is not proved. Theorem 12 is about almost every `g`; the points `A^j` form a set of measure zero.
- I do not know whether the restriction of `Q` to the subgroup `{A^t}` is square integrable without
  further input; with Tao's fine-scale mixing and a polynomial bound on `a_n` it is, but that is not
  written out here and does not give values at integers `t`.
- The checks are numerical at levels at most 4 for the group identities (they are exact identities
  with short proofs above) and at levels at most 14 for the Gram matrix.
