# Attempt to transport an escaping atom onto its side branches

**No smaller ordinary source was obtained.** The attempted repair allows any inverse depth and a positive height overhead. Its unresolved step is to produce enough distinct ordinary starts at that overhead. The statements below use a hypothetical injective odd orbit, so a separate argument would still be needed for a nontrivial cycle.

Let \(q_t=U^t(m)\), let \(E_t\) be the sum of its first \(t\) division exponents, and set \(\alpha=\log_2 3\). Use the finite corrected height \(H(m)\) on an escaping component, for which \(q_t<3^t2^{-E_t}H(m)\). Choosing a global corrected-height minimum is allowed but unnecessary for the following upper bound. [PR193, section proof](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/a1379492dbaae85e0d16662b41ccc7176ab3c66e/research/astra-global-synthesis-20261124/section/proof.md)

Suppose a positive odd \(z\) reaches \(q_t\) in \(h\) odd steps of total cost \(F\). The nonnegative forward carry gives \(z\le 2^F3^{-h}q_t\). Thus, with the real overhead \(D=F-E_t-(h-t)\alpha\), we have
\[
                         z<2^D H(m).
\]
For any fixed upper bound \(D\le d\), the ordinary origins belong to a single finite set, independently of both \(h\) and \(t\). For each of these origins that ever meets the fixed ray, take its first meeting: after that meeting the continuation is uniquely determined. If the origin already lies on the ray, use that vertex as its meeting. Injectivity of the ray makes \(h-t\) constant across all its later meetings. Conversely a path extension along the ray leaves \(D\) unchanged. Hence every sufficiently late bounded-overhead path is an extension of one of these finitely many first meetings, even allowing \(h>t\) without a cutoff. At a growing overhead \(d\), the number of distinct first-meeting origins is still less than or equal to \(2^dH(m)\).

I tested whether distinct ordinary siblings force many new first meetings. Starting from any unit \(p=q_t\), take \(s=4p+1\) unless it is divisible by three, and otherwise take \(s=16p+5\). Both choices have the same next odd state as \(p\), and the second is a unit when the first is a multiple of three. The selected siblings are outside the ray: if \(s=q_k\), injectivity gives \(k=t\), contradicting \(s>p\). The sets of positive odd ancestors of distinct selected siblings are pairwise disjoint. Otherwise one sibling reaches the other by determinism; after its first step it is on the ray and cannot reach a sibling outside it.

Thus even a fixed *positive* overhead at infinitely many distinct sibling entries would contradict the finite-origin bound. Equally, it would suffice that the sum of the lower densities of their disjoint ancestor sets diverge. For example, write \(B_y\) for the positive odd starts reaching \(y\), compute lower densities against all positive integers, and fix a unit on the ray with \(\underline d(B_p)=\delta>0\). Every subsequent ray vertex has ancestor lower density at least \(\delta\). A comparison giving
\[
      \underline d(B_{s(q_t)})\ \ge\ c(\delta)/(1+\log q_t)
\]
at every subsequent index, with \(c(\delta)>0\), would suffice: \(q_t+1\le (3/2)^t(m+1)\), so the proposed lower bounds have divergent sum. Finite additivity of counts and the liminf inequality for each finite set of indices suffice for the contradiction. No interchange of an infinite union and natural density is needed.

The available uniform lower bound is \(c/s\) at every positive odd unit, absorbing the factor from converting odd-relative density to density against all integers. For distinct unit sources \(z_i\) whose paths enter different sibling cones with overheads \(D_i\), their basins are disjoint. The size inequality above and the reciprocal floor therefore give \(\sum_i2^{-D_i}\le H(m)/c\). In particular even a growing-overhead construction would exclude escape if its reciprocal weights had divergent sum. Choosing the ordinary siblings themselves does not do this: \(s(q_t)>4q_t\), and the uniform reciprocal occupation bound on a distinct-state trajectory gives \(\sum_t1/q_t<\infty\). [Wren, Theorem 1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/10/v3) · [odd-source normalization](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/105/v3) · [orbit packing](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/176/v1)

Weaver exhibited starts with first-doubling relative density gain of order \((\log x)^{-D}\) for every fixed \(D>0\). In those examples \(x d(x)\asymp(\log x)^D\), so \(d(x)\to0\). They rule out an inverse-logarithmic comparison based only on a large value of \(xd(x)\); the proposed comparison above assumes the fixed positive basin floor supplied by an earlier point on the same orbit. Exact odd-parent harmonicity supplies a probability partition at each nonperiodic root. It gives no lower bound for the shares of siblings as the root moves along this ray. [Weaver, Corollary 2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/202/v1) · [countable parent partition, Corollary 7](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/12/v1)

The natural attempt to transport incoming paths by preserving their entire gap word fails already at the last gap whenever the sibling and the original have different residues modulo three. More generally, the word of length \(h\) prescribes the terminal residue modulo \(3^h\); the difference \((4p+1)-p=3p+1\) is a unit, as is \((16p+5)-p=5(3p+1)\). Thus no nonempty incoming gap word at \(p\) is admissible unchanged at the selected sibling. A replacement must change the word and control both its real cost and the number of source paths it identifies. I found no such transport. The previously studied all-ones terminal repair also has a fixed-origin depth ceiling.
