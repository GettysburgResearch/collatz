# Exact administrative cost and the limits of gluing deletion orders

**Status: PROPOSED, with complete proofs below and bounded exact replay.**
This packet establishes structural facts about the Aaronson–Yolcu–Heule (AYH)
system and rules out one concrete family of proposed orders. It does not prove
Collatz, relative termination of a previously open rule, or the failure of all
matrix interpretations. General commuting-diamond arguments are standard
rewriting theory; no external priority claim is made for their specialization
here.

The arithmetic convention is the shortcut map, halted at 1:

\[
T(n)=\begin{cases}n/2&n\equiv0\pmod2,\\(3n+1)/2&n\equiv1\pmod2,\end{cases}
\qquad n>1.
\]

The source is [AYH, arXiv:2105.14697v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.14697v3),
Sections 3.2 and 4.3, and the authors' [ASCII rules](https://github.com/emreyolcu/rewriting-collatz/blob/8a4dfda60f97a6d33ff0a24fdfa7a172d4bec340/rules/collatz-T.srs).
The source code revision is `8a4dfda60f97a6d33ff0a24fdfa7a172d4bec340`.
The project baseline inspected here is `ab7a62cbbb83860ea9436c5d3cf6cf7ce501066a`.
The obstruction below concerns an exact minimum over **all** mixed
representations. The family `3*2^k+1` is elementary, and no originality of
that source family is claimed. No earlier finite-feature obstruction is used
as a mathematical dependency; the proof is self-contained.

## 1. The rules and their exact arithmetic meaning

A well-formed word is `c w d`, with `w` over `a,b,e,f,g`. Start at 1 and read
left to right. The digits act by

| Symbol | Arithmetic action |
|---|---|
| `a` | `x -> 2x` |
| `b` | `x -> 2x+1` |
| `e` | `x -> 3x` |
| `f` | `x -> 3x+1` |
| `g` | `x -> 3x+2` |

Write `Val(s)` for the resulting positive integer. The rules are

```
D: ad -> d       bd -> gd
X: ae -> ea      af -> eb      ag -> fa
   be -> fb      bf -> ga      bg -> gb
   ce -> cb      cf -> caa     cg -> cab
```

Every `X` rule preserves `Val`, including in an arbitrary valid prefix/suffix
context. The two `D` rules implement one shortcut step. The word `cd`
represents 1 and has no outgoing rule. No assertion about arbitrary ill-formed
strings is inferred from the arithmetic statements below; the cited AYH
equivalence supplies that separate global bridge when needed.

## 2. An exact, unbounded positional rank for all administrative rewrites

Let `s=c w d`. For every ternary digit in `w`, compute the positive integer
represented by the prefix immediately before that digit. Define

\[
H(s)=\sum_{\substack{j:\ w_j\in\{e,f,g\}}}
\ell\bigl(\operatorname{Val}(c w_1\cdots w_{j-1}d)\bigr),
\qquad \ell(q)=\lfloor\log_2q\rfloor+1.
\tag{1}
\]

This is a finite natural number, obtained by a single scan of the actual word.
It requires neither an orbit nor a guessed stopping time.

### SRS-01: unit administrative descent

For every well-formed step `s ->_X t`,

\[
H(t)=H(s)-1.\tag{2}
\]

**Proof.** Consider an interior swap, with positive prefix value `q` before
its binary digit. Before the swap its ternary digit contributes
`ell(2q+b)=ell(q)+1`, where `b` is 0 or 1. After the swap the ternary digit
contributes `ell(q)`. Values after the rewritten pair agree, so every later
ternary contribution is unchanged. Earlier contributions also agree.
For a left-boundary rule, the removed ternary digit contributes `ell(1)=1`;
it is replaced by binary digits, and every later prefix value is unchanged.
These are all nine cases. ∎

Consequently `X` terminates. Its only normal form for value `n` is the usual
binary representation `B(n)=c bin(n)[1:] d`, translated to `a,b`: if a ternary
digit remains, its leftmost occurrence is preceded by `c`, `a`, or `b` and
therefore admits an `X` rule. Value preservation identifies the unique binary
normal form. Every normalization uses **exactly `H(s)` steps**.

For a word with `m` interior symbols, prefix values before position `j` are
less than `2*3^(j-1)`, so (1) also gives an explicit `O(m^2)` upper bound on
administrative work. This bound concerns one conversion of a fixed word,
not the lengths of words generated by the full dynamics.

### SRS-02: exact dynamic replenishment

For `n=Val(s)>1`,

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
s\to_{ad\to d}t &: &H(t)=H(s),\\
s\to_{bd\to gd}t &: &H(t)=H(s)+\ell(n)-1.
\end{array}\tag{3}
\]

The even rule removes a final binary digit, so no contribution changes. The
odd rule replaces a final binary digit by one ternary digit. Its prefix value
is `(n-1)/2`, with bit length `ell(n)-1` because `n>=3` is odd.

If the shortcut trajectory of `n` terminates, let `n_0,...,n_{r-1}` be its
values before reaching 1. **Every complete rewrite sequence** from `s` then
has length

\[
H(s)+\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}
\begin{cases}1,&n_j\text{ even},\\\ell(n_j),&n_j\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}\tag{4}
\]

Indeed there are exactly `r` dynamic steps, and telescoping (2)–(3) counts
all administrative steps. This explicitly measures the debt introduced by
each odd step; it is unbounded even at a single step.

## 3. Scheduling cannot manufacture an easier derivation

### SRS-03: labelled random descent

For any fixed string over the seven-letter alphabet, if one complete rewrite
sequence reaches a normal form, then all complete rewrite sequences terminate
at that normal form and use the same number of occurrences of **each of the
eleven rules**.

**Proof.** Every left-hand side has length 2. Its first letter belongs to
`{a,b,c}` and its second to the disjoint set `{d,e,f,g}`. Therefore distinct
redexes cannot overlap, and a given redex matches exactly one rule. Rewriting
two disjoint redexes in either order gives the identical word after two steps,
with the same two labels.

Induct on the length `L` of a known path to a normal form. If `L=1`, a
distinct redex would survive into the alleged normal form, a contradiction.
For `L>=2` and a different first rewrite, the commuting diamond puts both paths at a common word after
one additional rewrite. The known branch has remaining length `L-1`, so the
induction hypothesis applies to the common descendant. It supplies a path of
length `L-1` from the alternative first successor, to which induction applies
again. The two exchanged rule labels cancel in the comparison of count
vectors. The base case is a normal form. ∎

This theorem also applies to any rule-deletion subsystem, because deleting
rules cannot introduce overlaps. A scheduler search can improve execution
memory or reveal patterns, but for a fixed weakly normalizing source it cannot
reduce the number of rewrite events or avoid a rule that another complete
schedule uses. Changing the **initial representation** remains a real degree
of freedom.

## 4. What an exact administrative potential leaves to prove

### SRS-04: collapse of the unit-administration family

Suppose `P` is any natural-valued function on well-formed words and drops
exactly one under every `X` step. Then there is a function `F:N+ -> N` with

\[
P(s)=F(\operatorname{Val}(s))+H(s).\tag{5}
\]

Conversely every function of form (5) has this exact `X` decrement.

**Proof.** Normalize `s` using `X`, in exactly `H(s)` steps, and set
`F(n)=P(B(n))`. The telescoping equality gives (5). The converse follows
from value preservation and (2). ∎

Thus a potential in this family decreases at least one on **every** full rule
exactly when

\[
F(n)-F(T(n))\ge
\begin{cases}1,&n\text{ even},\\\ell(n),&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}
\qquad n>1.\tag{6}
\]

Necessity can be checked on binary source words, which always expose the
appropriate dynamic rule. Sufficiency follows from (2)–(3). This is a precise
costed arithmetic obligation. Equation (1) resolves administrative
termination completely, while (6) remains open. Giving `F` as an eventual
trajectory cost would assume the desired termination and is not a construction
of a proof.

## 5. A genuine gluing criterion and its first failed instance

Let `r_i(s)` be a **finite nonempty collection** of computable natural-valued
interpretations, each proved to decrease
strictly under **every retained rule in every well-formed context** of its
deletion subsystem. A multistage relative-termination certificate does not
automatically supply such a single scalar function. Choose positive integer scales `a_i`
and nonnegative integer offsets `b_i`. For each positive integer define

\[
E(n)=\min_{\substack{i,\ s=cwd\\\operatorname{Val}(s)=n}}
\bigl(a_i r_i(s)+b_i\bigr).\tag{7}
\]

Every fibre is finite: every digit at least doubles a positive prefix, so its
length is at most `floor(log_2 n)`. Thus (7) is total and computable without
running Collatz.

### SRS-05: exposed-winner criterion

If, for every `n>1`, at least one minimizer `(i,s)` in (7) ends in `ad` or
`bd` and the corresponding dynamic rule belongs to its deletion subsystem,
then `E(T(n))<E(n)` and Collatz follows by natural-number descent.

**Proof.** Apply that dynamic rule to the minimizing word. The resulting word
represents `T(n)` and has strictly smaller cost in the same chart, so it bounds
the minimum at `T(n)` from above. ∎

Also, a minimizing word is irreducible under every value-preserving rule in
its chart: such a rewrite would contradict its minimality in the same fibre.
For an auxiliary-rule deletion, the missing rule is therefore the exact
obstruction to making a terminal ternary digit accessible. The published
existence of all eleven deletion proofs supplies neither the exposed-winner
property nor a compatible choice of scales and offsets.

For the single published certificate in Example 4.3, deleting `af -> eb`,
the first small failure is

| n | Complete fibre size | Minimum rank | A minimizing word |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 3 | 2 | 45 | `cbd` |
| 5 | 2 | 38 | `cabd` |
| 7 | 3 | 26 | `cafd` |
| 11 = T(7) | 4 | 228 | `cabbd` |
| 22 | 7 | 72 | `caffd` |

The failure at 22 also shows that minimizing the representation can obstruct
an **even** arithmetic step: `E(22)=72<228=E(11)`. These are complete finite
fibres, not selected representations.

## 6. An infinite obstruction, even after adding administrative credit

For completeness, the affine maps of that published certificate are
`x -> M_sigma x + v_sigma` on `N^2`:

| Symbol | Matrix | Vector |
|---|---|---|
| a | `[[1,1],[1,0]]` | `(0,0)` |
| b | `[[1,3],[3,4]]` | `(1,1)` |
| c | `[[1,5],[0,0]]` | `(0,0)` |
| d | `[[1,0],[1,0]]` | `(1,1)` |
| e | `[[7,2],[2,5]]` | `(2,1)` |
| f | `[[2,1],[1,1]]` | `(1,0)` |
| g | `[[2,2],[2,4]]` | `(0,2)` |

Here string interpretation is **left-to-right composition**, hence matrix
multiplication in word order; this differs from the arithmetic reading above.
Take `r(s)` to be the first coordinate of `[s](0,0)`. The replay checks the
full affine inequalities for all ten retained rules; it does not merely test
their values on a sample of arguments.

Put

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
u=(1,5),\quad v=\binom11,\quad e_1=\binom10,
\quad n_k=3\cdot2^k+1\quad(k\ge1).
\]

The word `s_k=c a^k f d` represents `n_k` and has

\[
r_k=r(s_k)=uA^{k+2}v+uA^ke_1,
\qquad H(s_k)=k+1.\tag{8}
\]

Let `F_k` denote Fibonacci numbers, with `F_0=0,F_1=1`.

### SRS-06: all-representation dyadic barrier

For every `k>=1` and **every** representation `s` of an integer
`m>2^(k+2)`,

\[
r(s)\ge r_k+F_k.\tag{9}
\]

**Proof.** Give binary digits weight 1 and ternary digits weight 2, and let
`S` be the total weight. Every digit matrix dominates the corresponding
power of `A`, entry by entry:

\[
M_a=A,\ M_f=A^2,\quad
M_b-A=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\2&4\end{pmatrix},\quad
M_e-A^2=\begin{pmatrix}5&1\\1&4\end{pmatrix},\quad
M_g-A^2=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{10}
\]

All affine offsets are nonnegative. A word of weight `S` has arithmetic value
less than `2^(S+1)` because its mixed-base product is at most `2^S` and all
digits are legal. Therefore `S>=k+2`.

If `S>=k+3`, then

\[
r(s)-r_k\ge uA^{k+3}v-uA^{k+2}v-uA^ke_1
=uA^kv\ge F_k.\tag{11}
\]

Here `uA^jv` is increasing; the displayed identity uses `A^2=A+I` and
`Av-e_1=v`.

It remains to treat `S=k+2`. A word using only `a,f` has value at most `2^S`:
`a` multiplies by 2, and `f(x)=3x+1<=4x` for positive `x`. Since our value
exceeds `2^S`, some digit is `b,e`, or `g`. Retain one such matrix defect in
the product and bound all other matrices below by powers of `A`.

For an `e` or `g`, let `i,j` be the weights before and after that digit, so
`i+j=k`. The `e` defect dominates the `g` defect `D_g`. The Fibonacci matrix
identity gives

\[
D_g A^jv-A^je_1=\binom{0}{4F_{j+1}}.
\]

Thus the excess over `r_k` is at least
`4F_(j+1)(uA^i)_2`. For `i=0` this is `20F_(k+1)`. For `i>=1`, use
`(uA^i)_2>=F_i` and
`F_(i+j)=F_i F_(j+1)+F_(i-1) F_j<=2F_i F_(j+1)`.
The result is at least `2F_k` and hence at least `F_k`.

For a `b`, now `i+j=k+1`. Writing `D_b=M_b-A`, direct Fibonacci identities
give

\[
D_b A^jv-A^{j-1}e_1\ \ge\ A^{j-1}v
\tag{12}
\]

entrywise for every `j>=0`. At `j=0`, the two sides of (12) are `(2,5)` and
`(1,0)` respectively, since `A^(-1)e_1=(0,1)`. For `j>=1`, substitute
`A^jv=(F_(j+2),F_(j+1))`; each inequality is immediate. Multiplying (12)
by `uA^i` shows an excess of at least `uA^kv>=F_k`. This covers the last
case and proves (9). ∎

### SRS-07: no fixed administrative repair of this envelope

For a fixed nonnegative integer `lambda`, define the complete fibre minimum

\[
E_\lambda(n)=\min_{\operatorname{Val}(s)=n}\{r(s)+\lambda H(s)\}.
\]

For all sufficiently large `k`,

\[
E_\lambda(T(n_k))>E_\lambda(n_k).\tag{13}
\]

**Proof.** `T(n_k)=9*2^(k-1)+2>2^(k+2)`. Equation (9) bounds the left side
below by `r_k+F_k`. The particular source word in (8) bounds the right side
above by `r_k+lambda(k+1)`. Fibonacci growth eventually exceeds every fixed
multiple of `k+1`. ∎

The same argument applies if `lambda H` is replaced by any nonnegative
penalty `p(s)` satisfying `p(s_k)=o(F_k)`, including any fixed polynomial in
`H(s_k)` and the length of `s_k`. This is an obstruction to modifying **this
specific published certificate** by such penalties. It does not rule out
other certificates, genuinely different well-founded carriers, multichart
winner constructions, or contextual ranks with other administrative behavior.

## 7. What remains worth attacking

The useful positive objects are the exact administrative rank (1), its
replenishment law (3), and the exposed-winner criterion (7). They make several
otherwise vague order proposals falsifiable.

The next substantive gluing target is to construct a finite collection of
certified deletion interpretations and common scales for which every positive
integer has an exposed winning representation. One must prove that winner
coverage over complete representation fibres. The first unsupported step is
exactly this universal coverage; none of the results here establish it.
Another option is to depart from unit administrative descent, thereby escaping
the collapse theorem, while retaining a computable, independently well-founded
carrier and explicit dynamic replenishment inequalities.

No additional search over rewrite **schedules** can cure the bad certificate
family: SRS-03 removes that freedom at a fixed source word, while SRS-06 already
quantifies over every representation at the target.
