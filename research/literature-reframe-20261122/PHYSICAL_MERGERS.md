# Physical mergers: literature, exact backtracing, and an unbounded clock requirement

**Full Collatz closure remains OPEN.** This pass sought a universal physical
merger from a marked integer to a smaller marked candidate. It uses the
primary semigroup and inverse-tree literature to distinguish actual ordinary
backtracing from purely algebraic motion. The new result is an all-parameter
obstruction to a fixed continuation budget after rising-prefix erasure.
It allows unequal clocks on the two branches and every positive erasure
depth. It does not exclude an unbounded-clock selector.

The ordinary shortcut map is

\[
T(n)=\begin{cases}n/2,&n\text{ even},\\(3n+1)/2,&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]

A physical merger is an equality `T^a(n)=T^b(m)` at finite ordinary clocks.
All states in the results below are positive natural integers. The marked
section, frozen from PR173, is

\[
\operatorname{Marked}(n)\iff n\text{ odd},\quad
(d+1)(n-2^d)\le70\,2^d,
\qquad d=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor.
\]

## 2. What physical arithmetic-progression sufficiency gives

One can see the positive mechanism explicitly in the binary-modulus case.
Fix a positive ternary-unit target `q`, a residue `0<=r<2^h`, and the
actual finite prefix of `r`. Write

\[
e=T^h(r),\qquad p=\#\{0\le j<h:T^j(r)\text{ odd}\}.
\]

The ordinary lift identity is

\[
T^h(r+2^h v)=e+3^p v\qquad(v\ge0).
\tag{P1}
\]

If `p>0`, the integer `e` is a ternary unit: after the first odd step a
positive trajectory cannot become divisible by three. Since 2 generates
the units modulo `3^p`, choose `k` with

\[
2^kq\equiv e\pmod{3^p},\qquad2^kq>e.
\]

Then

\[
x=r+2^h\frac{2^kq-e}{3^p}>0,
\qquad x\equiv r\pmod{2^h},
\qquad T^{h+k}(x)=q.
\tag{P2}
\]

If `p=0`, necessarily `r=e=0`, and ordinary doubling gives the same
conclusion directly. Increasing `k` by the period `2*3^(p-1)` in the first
case gives arbitrarily large such physical predecessors. A target divisible
by three can first be advanced to a unit in its own ordinary component.

This independently reconstructs the dyadic part of the affirmative
literature result. It does not claim new priority for arithmetic-progression
sufficiency. The real-height cost is visible: the permitted exponent class
can require a full period of length `2*3^(p-1)`, and the produced `x`
can be very large. Congruence does not set the nonnegative lift coefficient
in `(P2)` to a chosen small value.

For a prescribed smaller marked candidate, a cofinal sequence of congruent
predecessors in the original source's component is therefore insufficient.
One still needs an actual representative within the original source's
height budget. Replacing that finite requirement by convergence in a
profinite topology would lose the same ordinary integer being proved.

## 3. A new bound for all bounded post-run physical mergers

The literature's negative fixed point suggests testing the complete
continuation scheme, rather than one failed multiplier or one residue row.
The following estimate is elementary and keeps every ordinary affine carry.

### Shifted affine identity

For any ordinary start `z` and finite clock `t`, there are integers
`0<=p<=t` and `0<=D<3^t` with

\[
\boxed{2^t(T^t(z)+1)=3^p(z+1)+D.}
\tag{P3}
\]

The exponent can be taken to be the actual number of odd steps. If `z`
is even and `t>0`, then `p<=t-1`. At an odd step the shifted coordinate
is multiplied by `3/2`; at an even step it is replaced by `(z+2)/2`.
Induction proves the nonnegative correction and its displayed bound.
The Lean theorem `shifted_affine` exposes precisely the exponent bounds,
correction bound, and actual-orbit equality needed below.

### The simultaneous separation theorem

Let `H>=0`, `r>=1`, and let `A,B` be positive integers satisfying

\[
A=3^rB,\quad B\text{ odd},\quad
2^H\mid A+1,\quad B>6^H.
\tag{P4}
\]

Then, for **every pair of clocks** `0<=a,b<=H`,

\[
\boxed{T^a(A-1)\ne T^b(B-1).}
\tag{P5}
\]

The two clocks are allowed to differ. No restriction on how the smaller
branch subsequently chooses its parities is imposed.

**Proof.** For `a>=1`, the original tail first halves and then performs
`a-1` consecutive odd steps. Indeed `A+1` is divisible by `2^a`, so

\[
2^a(T^a(A-1)+1)=3^{a-1}(A+1).
\]

At `a=0`, the shifted value is just `A`. Thus in all cases

\[
2^a(T^a(A-1)+1)=3^{p_a}A+D_a,
\quad p_a=\max(a-1,0),\quad0\le D_a<3^a.
\]

For the erased branch, `(P3)` gives

\[
2^b(T^b(B-1)+1)=3^{p_b}B+D_b,
\quad p_b\le\max(b-1,0),\quad0\le D_b<3^b,
\]

because `B-1` is even. A hypothetical merger would imply

\[
\bigl(2^b3^{p_a+r}-2^a3^{p_b}\bigr)B
   =2^aD_b-2^bD_a.
\tag{P6}
\]

Both nonnegative correction terms on the right are strictly below `6^H`.
Since `B>6^H`, division with remainder forces the two integer coefficients
on the left to agree. Unique factorization at 2 then gives `a=b`;
factorization at 3 gives `p_b=p_a+r`. This contradicts the displayed
odd-count bound, including when both clocks are zero. This proves `(P5)`.

This proof also gives the useful contrapositive: such a bounded merger
forces `B<6^H`. The hypotheses cannot be discharged merely by increasing
the size of a source on the same exceptional residue class.

### Ordinary prefix-erasure form

Write

\[
n=2^ku-1,\qquad x_r=2^{k-r}u-1,
\qquad A=3^ku,\quad B=3^{k-r}u.
\]

If `u` is odd, `u>6^H`, and `2^H` divides `3^ku+1`, then

\[
\boxed{T^{k+a}(n)\ne T^{k-r+b}(x_r)
\quad(1\le r\le k,\ 0\le a,b\le H).}
\tag{P7}
\]

Here the complete initial rising runs have been executed on both sources.
The theorem then excludes every pair of continuations inside the same
fixed budget. This is `bounded_erasure_no_meeting` in
[PhysicalTailBarrier.lean](lean/PhysicalTailBarrier.lean).

## 4. Cofinal ordinary marked realizations

The obstruction is compatible with the actual shrinking marked section
and with the earlier binary-horizon non-descent condition.

Fix `H>=1`. For any `s>=3H+4`, set `k=3s`, and choose the unique odd
residue `c` with

\[
1\le c<2^H,\qquad 3^{3s}c\equiv-1\pmod{2^H}.
\]

Such a residue exists because three is invertible modulo `2^H`. Put

\[
u=2^s+c,\qquad k=3s,\qquad d=4s,
\qquad n_s=2^k u-1
       =2^{4s}+2^{3s}c-1.
\tag{P8}
\]

These sources are cofinal. They satisfy all of the following simultaneously:

1. **Exact markedness.** Their binary height is `d`, and their offset is
   less than `2^(3s+H)`. The elementary inequalities
   `4s+1<=8*2^floor(s/2)` and `s>=2H` give
   `(d+1)*(n_s-2^d)<70*2^d`.
2. **All proper erasures remain marked.** For `1<=r<k`,
   `x_r=floor(n_s/2^r)` is odd, positive, marked, and strictly smaller.
   This is exactly the preceding packet's odd-prefix inheritance theorem.
3. **The new tail obstruction applies.** One has `u>6^H`, since
   `s>=3H+4`. Also `u=c mod2^H`, so the choice of `c` gives
   `2^H` dividing `3^k u+1`.
4. **No ordinary descent through the entire binary horizon.** The first
   `k` clocks form the rising run. At its endpoint,
   `3^k=27^s>=16^s=2^(k+s)`. Even allowing a halving at each of the next
   `s` clocks leaves the value at least `n_s`. Thus
   `T^t(n_s)>=n_s` for every `0<=t<=d`.

Consequently every proper erased source is a valid smaller marked
candidate, but **none can meet its parent using at most `H` extra clocks
on each branch after their complete rising runs**. The whole construction
works for every finite `H`, not just for a fixed finite search table.

There is also a quantitative statement in terms of the original source.
Take `s=3H+4`. Then the exact binary height is

\[
d=\lfloor\log_2 n_s\rfloor=12H+16.
\]

For every erasure depth `1<=r<=k`, any equality
`T^(k+a)(n_s)=T^(k-r+b)(x_r)` must therefore satisfy

\[
\boxed{\max(a,b)>H=\frac{d-16}{12}.}
\tag{P9}
\]

Thus the obstruction already forces a continuation budget growing at
least linearly with the source's binary height along an explicit family.
It rules out a sublinear uniform continuation budget for this specific
prefix-erasure architecture. This is a necessary lower bound for a
successful merger certificate, not an upper bound or a claim that the
merger never occurs.

The realization, these four elementary estimates, and `(P9)` are written
deductions.
The Lean module checks the all-parameter affine and separation theorem
`(P3)`–`(P7)`; it does not claim to have additionally formalized `(P8)`.

## 5. The global selector still being sought

The affirmative literature provides ordinary representatives in any desired
arithmetic progression. The marked-prefix results provide smaller marked
candidates in either rounding direction. Neither supplies the missing
actual merger while respecting the original source.

The new result shows that one cannot finish this particular architecture
by a fixed finite dictionary of bounded continuations after complete
rising runs. It leaves several genuinely global possibilities open:

- continuation clocks selected without a fixed upper bound;
- a different source split or a partner outside the prefix-erasure family;
- a well-founded rank that pays for those changes and covers every source;
- a quantitative inverse-tree statement retaining the real-height budget.

No infinite divergent orbit, nontrivial cycle, or absence of an eventual
merger is asserted. In particular, `(P7)` does not say that the candidates
in `(P8)` never merge with their parents. It proves that the required
continuation budgets cannot remain uniformly bounded over this cofinal
marked family.

This was the first unsupported step in the full attempt: upgrading a
physical, but potentially large, backtraced representative to a successful
source-bounded selector for every marked input. The literature cited here
does not discharge that step, and this packet does not replace it by an
axiom.
