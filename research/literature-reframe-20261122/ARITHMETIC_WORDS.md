# Arithmetic words: fixed-source precision, multiplicative rank, and the global budget

**Full Collatz closure remains open.** This pass tried to replace the
previous fixed-block budget by a constraint that survives arbitrary changes
of parity word. It pursued two global routes: precision of actual parity
windows, and finiteness of unit equations in a fixed multiplicative group.
Both give exact constraints. Neither supplies the missing bound on the
complexity of one unrestricted ordinary orbit.

The new Lean module
[`lean/SourceCollision.lean`](lean/SourceCollision.lean) proves a finite
fixed-source collision budget using the actual shortcut map and actual parity
windows. It does not take parity conjugacy as an axiom. The asymptotic
complexity argument is credited to Dubickas rather than claimed as a new
theorem. The unit-equation application is a written deduction from classical
theorems, not a Lean formalization of those theorems.

## 2. Keep the ordinary source fixed

Use the shortcut map

\[
T(n)=\begin{cases}n/2,&n\text{ even},\\(3n+1)/2,&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}
\qquad n_j=T^j(n).
\]

Write `SamePrefix(L,x,y)` when the next `L` actual parity symbols from `x`
and `y` agree. Its Lean definition recursively checks the two current
parities and then the two actual next states. The following theorem applies
simultaneously to every choice of words and positions:

### Fixed-source collision budget

If `i,j<=K`, `n_i != n_j`, and their length-`L` parity windows agree, then

\[
\boxed{2^{L+K}<3^K(n+1).} \tag{W1}
\]

Equal parity windows imply `2^L | (n_i−n_j)`, so their distinct ordinary
values differ by at least `2^L`. The one-step inequality
`2(T(x)+1)<=3(x+1)` supplies

\[
2^K(n_j+1)\le3^K(n+1)\qquad(j\le K).
\]

Comparing the nonzero difference with the larger endpoint proves W1.
The common time horizon is used explicitly; monotonicity of the trajectory
is not assumed.

Thus if `3^K(n+1)<=2^(L+K)`, equal windows among those states force an
actual repeated state. In particular, any doubled initial prefix of length
`2L` with

\[
(4/3)^L\ge n+1
\]

forces `T^L(n)=n`. This conclusion is an actual return, not necessarily a
return to one. It preserves the distinction between aperiodic escape and a
possible nontrivial cycle.

For an aperiodic positive orbit, all its states are distinct. Taking
`K` asymptotically below `L log(2)/log(3/2)` makes the first `K+1`
windows distinct. This recovers the complexity mechanism of Dubickas's
Theorem 5. It does not create a new asymptotic lower bound.

## 3. Why the transcendence shortcut does not close the source

The tempting argument was: the source `n` is algebraic; a nonperiodic
parity sequence with sufficiently simple structure is transcendental; hence
that sequence cannot belong to `n`. The two assertions concern different
numbers.

The ordinary binary digits of `n` are eventually zero. The orbit parities
are the digits of the distinct 2-adic number

\[
Q(n)=\sum_{j\ge0}(T^j(n)\bmod2)2^j.
\]

The conjugacy has the expansion

\[
\Phi\!\left(\sum_{i\ge0}2^{d_i}\right)
=-\sum_{i\ge0}\frac{2^{d_i}}{3^{i+1}},
\qquad \Phi(Q(n))=n.
\]

Applying the p-adic digit theorem to `Q(n)` requires algebraicity of
`Q(n)`, not merely algebraicity of `Phi(Q(n))`. A 2-adic isometry does
not by its definition preserve algebraicity. The established rationality
inclusion for `Phi` runs in the direction from eventually periodic codes
to rational sources. Reversing it would insert the periodicity conjecture
at exactly the intended conclusion.

I also tried applying approximation directly to the fixed rational source,
using a periodic continuation of a finite word. If `z=a/b`, with `b` odd,
shares `L` parities with `n`, the isometry gives

\[
2^L\mid bn-a.
\]

Unless `z=n`, the ordinary integer numerator therefore satisfies

\[
2^L\le|bn-a|\le(n+1)\max(|a|,|b|). \tag{W2}
\]

This is a useful source-preserving precision test, but the rational
shadow's numerator and denominator must both be counted. For a periodic
block with length `h`, odd count `p`, and affine correction `B`, its fixed
point has the form

\[
z=\frac{B}{2^h-3^p}.
\]

The height of this rational is not uniformly bounded when the block
changes. The correction `B` encodes the entire word. A longer prefix
before the periodic shadow adds its own height cost. The new dyadic
precision may be paid for by these growing quantities, exactly as W2
allows.

Repeated or otherwise compressible words can improve this balance; W1
is a particularly transparent instance. No source-independent repetition
property has been proved for an unrestricted orbit. A computable sequence
need not be automatic, and a finite program or finite initial integer does
not imply a linear upper bound on the number of its distinct factors.

## 4. A budget that does survive arbitrary valuation switches

The other route treats actual odd values rather than their word codes.
Let

\[
x_{i+1}=\frac{3x_i+1}{2^{a_i}},\qquad a_i\ge1,
\]

be the accelerated odd trajectory. Fix any finite set of primes `S`
containing two and three, and suppose every value in a finite `N`-edge
prefix `x_0,...,x_N` has all its prime factors in `S`. Let `s=|S|`.
There is no restriction on the valuation word `(a_0,...,a_(N−1))`.

For each edge form the pair

\[
(X_i,Y_i)=(-3x_i,\,2^{a_i}x_{i+1}).
\]

Both coordinates are nonzero `S`-units and

\[
X_i+Y_i=1.
\]

The product of two rational `S`-unit groups has multiplicative rank `2s`;
signs contribute only torsion. Distinct `x_i` give distinct pairs. The
Beukers–Schlickewei theorem therefore gives the entirely word-independent
bound

\[
\boxed{N\le2^{16s+8}} \tag{W3}
\]

whenever the first `N` odd sources are distinct. Unlike a one-block
divisibility budget, W3 is unchanged when the trajectory switches to a
different exponent or an entirely different long word.

There are two immediate consequences.

- An ordinary orbit whose odd values all use one finite prime set is
  eventually periodic. Otherwise its distinct prefixes would violate W3.
  The converse is elementary, because an eventually periodic trajectory
  has only finitely many values.
- On any aperiodic trajectory, let `s_N` count the primes in the first
  `N+1` odd values, with two and three included. Then
  `s_N >= (log2 N−8)/16`. The pool may be chosen after observing the
  prefix; its size must still pay for that prefix.

These are applications of a classical theorem, not a claim of a new
prime-generation theorem. Related consequences are already studied in
the recent primary literature cited above. Their useful feature for this
attempt is the exact invariant needed for arbitrary switching: one fixed
finite-rank multiplicative ambient group.

### The attempted global completion

A source-dependent bound on this prime pool would supply a global budget
for all valuation switches. I attempted to derive such a bound from the
initial source and the affine word identity. This did not close.

The obstruction is explicit. Multiplication and division by powers of two
and three preserve a fixed prime group, but the addition of one does not.
Even `3` produces the new odd value `5`. At later times the same operation
acts on the current, potentially much larger value. No bound on all newly
introduced primes in terms of the original source was obtained.

Allowing `S` to grow with the prefix keeps W3 true, but removes the finite
budget conclusion. A logarithmically growing prime pool is compatible with
arbitrarily long distinct prefixes. This is not a contradiction with the
fixed input: deterministic arithmetic can create new prime factors.

Even if prime support were bounded for every source, the conclusion would
first be eventual periodicity. Exclusion of nontrivial positive cycles
would remain necessary for full Collatz. Neither cycle exclusion nor the
prime-support bound is assumed here.

### Attempted composition of the two budgets

The two constraints retain different information, so I tried using them
together. For the accelerated odd orbit, the elementary height envelope
still gives

\[
x_i+1\le(3/2)^i(x_0+1).
\]

Consequently, a sufficiently long actual parity window distinguishes all
distinct odd states in a finite prefix: if
`2^L >= (3/2)^N(x_0+1)`, no two distinct `x_i,x_j`, with `i,j<=N`,
can share their next `L` shortcut parities. This is the same divisibility
mechanism as W1; it does not require the accelerated valuations to be
bounded.

On the other hand, W3 bounds the number of distinct transition sources in
one fixed prime group. Composing these observations only says that the
number of distinct window labels on those sources is at most
`2^(16s_N+8)`. The window argument labels the same states that the unit
equation counts. It supplies no independent upper bound on the labels
when `s_N` grows, so the composition does not strengthen W3 into a
source-only stopping bound.

An exact sufficient arithmetic bridge would be the following bound for
each fixed ordinary source: for some `epsilon>0` and all sufficiently
large `N`,

\[
 s_N\le(1/16-\epsilon)\log_2 N. \tag{W5: unproved}
\]

In particular, `s_N=o(log N)` would suffice. W3 would give
`log2 N <= 16s_N+8 <= (1-16epsilon)log2 N+8`, bounding `N` and hence
forcing an actual repeated state. The same nontrivial-cycle question
would then remain.

Conversely, eventual periodicity makes `s_N` eventually constant, so it
implies W5 with any fixed `0<epsilon<1/16`. In conjunction with W3,
W5 is therefore an equivalent quantitative formulation of the
eventual-periodicity issue for one orbit, not a separately established
weaker assumption.

The direct height information falls far short of W5. The product of the
distinct primes other than two and three seen by time `N` is at most
`product_(i=0)^N x_i`. Each such prime is at least five, so

\[
(s_N-2)\log 5
\le\sum_{i=0}^{N}\log x_i
<(N+1)\log(x_0+1)
 +\frac{N(N+1)}2\log(3/2).
\]

Thus the height envelope is compatible with a prime pool of quadratic
size in `N`, while the necessary lower bound from W3 is logarithmic.
The source-dependent constant in W1 does not close that difference.
Proving W5, or another inequality coupling new prime factors to forced
early parity collisions, is the first unsupported arithmetic step in this
combined route. It has not been assumed in the checked module.

## 5. Why expanding the whole affine word does not evade this issue

For a shortcut prefix with length `h`, odd count `p`, and odd positions
`d_0<...<d_(p−1)`, its actual endpoint satisfies

\[
2^h n_h=3^p n+
\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}2^{d_i}3^{p-1-i}. \tag{W4}
\]

Every summand in the correction is built from only the bases two and
three. This suggests applying a unit-equation theorem directly to the
whole prefix. It does not preserve all of that theorem's fixed parameters:

1. The number of terms in W4 grows with `p`. The general
   Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt bound depends on the number of variables as
   well as the rank. Applying it separately in dimensions `p+2` does not
   yield one finite bound on all prefix lengths.
2. The moving endpoint `n_h` is an additional coefficient or group element.
   Treating it as a fixed coefficient changes the problem each time `h`
   changes. Adding all endpoints to one ambient multiplicative group is
   exactly the unproved finite-rank condition in the preceding section.
3. Replacing the growing correction sum by a single integer `B` keeps the
   displayed dimension small, but `B` is then an unrestricted changing
   integer, not a fixed `S`-unit. Its height cannot be dropped from a
   p-adic approximation estimate.

The periodic case has an additional equation: the endpoint equals the
starting value. It reduces W4 to
`(2^h−3^p)n=B`, which is the kind of boundary condition exploited by
cycle methods and logarithmic forms. An escaping prefix lacks that
equation. Substituting it would already assume the return that the proof
is seeking.

This identifies two distinct parameters that must be controlled to use
these Diophantine tools globally: how many independent pieces of the word
are retained, and the arithmetic height or rank of the moving endpoint.
Neither is bounded by a fixed-block calculation alone.

## 6. Relation to the previous language result

The prior checked pump module spends a fixed ternary defect when one
inverse block is repeated at one fixed tail. Its written consequence is
that over a fixed finite alphabet of positive accelerated exponents, every
context-free sublanguage of canonical first-hit certificates is finite.
That conclusion already requires any universally successful word method
to carry more information than an unconditional context-free language of
certificates.

The present results address two ways to carry additional information.
W1 keeps the original source and the actual time horizon in every parity
comparison. W3 allows arbitrary words, but retains a fixed multiplicative
ambient group. Neither spends a fresh unrecorded budget when a block
changes. Their respective residuals are now precise: an aperiodic word
can avoid early repeated factors, and an aperiodic arithmetic orbit can
introduce new primes.

No theorem read in this pass prevents both behaviors simultaneously for
every ordinary source. There is also no proved conversion from large word
complexity to a small ordinary merger or from new prime divisors to a
smaller marked component representative.

## 7. First unsupported global assertions

A successful continuation along these routes would need to prove one of
the following types of statement, with all quantifiers tied to the same
ordinary source:

- A forced parity collision at indices and length crossing W1's explicit
  source-height threshold, together with an argument eliminating the
  resulting nontrivial-cycle possibility.
- A bound on the multiplicative rank or prime support of the entire odd
  orbit in terms of its starting value, again followed by cycle exclusion.
- A certified source-preserving rational or ordinary shadow whose dyadic
  precision exceeds its full height cost and whose terminal behavior is
  already known.

No such assertion was proved in this pass. The actual positive deliverables
are the seven checked, source-preserving collision theorems; the quantitative
arbitrary-switch unit-equation budget W3; and the explicit hypothesis checks
showing where conjugacy, transcendence, and unit-equation results can and
cannot be combined. No conjectural global assertion was promoted to a Lean
axiom or a completed dispatcher.
