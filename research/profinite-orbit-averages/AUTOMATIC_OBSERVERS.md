# Finite automata cannot predict a nontrivial ordinary Collatz component

**PROPOSED, source-qualified on ordinary pair synchronization and predecessor positivity.** Let A be a union of complete positive Collatz merger components. For any integer base k≥2, bounded k-automatic weight a(n), and fixed 0≤u<v, on I_X=[uX,vX]∩N one has

    mean_(I_X) 1_A a − (mean_(I_X) 1_A)(mean_(I_X) a) → 0.     (1)

Here k-automatic means that a fixed finite automaton with output reads the base-k digits of n. No assumption on the existence of the ordinary density of the automatic sequence is required. In particular **every k-automatic union of complete components is either empty or all positive integers**. If there is more than one component, a finite automaton cannot recognize any single one in any integer base.

For a fixed positive target b, (1) also holds for its first-hit basin, allowing starting at b. Among the hits, the residues of the counts of even and odd shortcut steps before arrival are jointly uniform against every such automatic weight, up to absolute error o(X) in each fixed proportional window. For example one can impose an arbitrary fixed finite-state condition on the input digits at the same time as specifying both arrival-clock residues.

## A finite-state averaging lemma

Suppose |f(n)|≤1 and X^{-1}Σ_{n≤X}|f(n+1)−f(n)|→0. Suppose that all subsequential weak limits of f(floor(Xt)) against integrable tests on fixed bounded intervals in t>0 are constant functions (their constants may depend on the subsequence). Then (1) holds with f in place of 1_A. If the only limit is zero, mean_(I_X) f a tends to zero.

Normalize |a|≤1. Read digits from the most significant end, allowing leading zeroes by adding an initial state if necessary. Let P be the stochastic transition matrix of the automaton for a uniform random digit, and let v_0 be its output vector. The average output after appending m unrestricted digits to a state s is (P^m v_0)_s. There are finitely many closed recurrent classes. Let p be a common multiple of their periods. As m tends to infinity through multiples of p, on each recurrent cyclic subclass the entries (P^m v_0)_s tend uniformly to a constant for that subclass. Appending any digit advances the cyclic subclass by one, irrespective of the digit. The chance a uniformly sampled prefix of length l has not reached a recurrent class tends to zero with l. These facts follow by decomposing the finite Markov chain into transient states and closed irreducible classes and taking the p-step chain.

Take any subsequence of X. Choose L so that vX≤k^L and k^L/X stays bounded above and below, and extract a further subsequence on which X/k^L converges, L modulo p is fixed, and f at scale k^L converges weakly to a constant c. For fixed m, partition the L-digit inputs into blocks of size k^m. Outside at most two end blocks of I_X one may replace f throughout each block by its value at the first point, with total absolute error o(k^L): bounding changes by telescoping costs a fixed multiple of Σ|f(n+1)−f(n)|. Thus in the product sum a may be replaced by the average (P^m v_0)_s of the final m digits, with o(k^L) error; one may then restore f at its original positions.

Given ε>0 choose l so that the exceptional first-l-digit prefixes still in transient states have fraction below ε. Next choose m divisible by p such that the recurrent subclass averages are accurate to ε. For a nonexceptional leading prefix, the cyclic subclass before the final m digits is determined by that prefix and L−m−l modulo p. Therefore, up to O(ε) in the normalized product sum, the suffix average is a fixed bounded step function of the first l digits, hence of n/k^L. The same approximation applies to the unweighted sum of a by taking f=1. Weak convergence of f against these finitely many interval indicators gives mean_(I_X) f a − c mean_(I_X) a = O(ε)+o(1). Replacing the moving endpoints by their limits costs o(1) since all functions are bounded. If u=0 one discards [0,δ k^L) first and then lets δ decrease to zero. Also mean_(I_X) f→c. Let ε decrease to zero and use the subsequence principle. This proves the lemma.

## Ordinary application

The [ordinary clock-character argument](CLOCK_CHARACTERS.md) gives the vanishing adjacent differences and constant subsequential scale profiles for the basin indicator. It also gives zero profiles for every nontrivial finite character of the two arrival-clock counts. The identical proof applies to any complete-component union: its indicator is unchanged on coalescing pairs and exactly invariant on both shortcut branches. The two dilation equations at scales 2 and 3 then force a constant profile by irrationality of log2/log3. Applying the finite-state lemma, followed by finite character inversion for the clocks, proves the assertions about ordinary sample windows.

If A itself were automatic, taking a=1_A gives p_X(1−p_X)→0 for its window frequencies p_X. Every nonempty component union contains the full predecessor set of some positive odd 3-unit, since any positive orbit reaches such a unit. That predecessor set has positive lower natural density by Mazur's theorem. If the complement is also nonempty, it has the same positivity property. Using I_X=[1,X] contradicts p_X(1−p_X)→0. The synchronization and positive-density conclusions are stated in the [clock packet](CLOCK_CHARACTERS.md) and the [predecessor source audit](../external/mazur-2026/README.md).

This does not show that a hypothetical nonconvergent component is automatic. Formula (1) instead excludes any finite-state digit test with persistent predictive advantage over the component frequency.
