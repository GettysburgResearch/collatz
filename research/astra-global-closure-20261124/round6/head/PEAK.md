# Every path between two convergent residual arms can require an arbitrarily high peak

For every \(m\ge4\) there are infinitely many positive ordinary \(v=9k+2\) on the odd lift of the [rank criterion](README.md) such that **both** \(A=4v\) and \(B=8v+1\) reach 1, their left orbit satisfies \(\max O(A)=A\), and every finite undirected shortcut path from \(B\) to \(A\) contains the specified vertex
\[
                 z=3^{m+1}a-1>(3/2)^m B,\qquad
                 a=(3v+1)/2^m .
\]
Here \(a\) is an ordinary positive odd integer selected jointly with \(v\). The term “every path” includes paths of unbounded length and arbitrary reverse moves. This disproves a fixed multiplicative ceiling for certificates connecting these two arms, even if certificates are only required on known convergent pairs. It imposes no ceiling on paths from \(B\) to other smaller integers. PR192 obtains a factor 1024 for paths to **any** smaller vertex on a different progression. [PR192, complete graph theorem](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/301b84491ffafe283bd63d104c1b60ddf0e97f06/research/astra-two-centre-20261124/rewriting.md)

Fix \(m\ge4\) first. We shall find infinitely many odd \(a\) with \(2^m a\equiv7\bmod 27\), both \(a\) and \(3^{m+1}a-1\) converging, and \(\max O(a)\le5a\). For the fixed pair of forms \(a,3^{m+1}a-1\), the pair synchronization input says the proportion of binary prefixes on which the profiles fuse by clock \(H\) tends to one as \(H\to\infty\). This persists on the odd half by conditioning. For the scalar lower arm on the unconditioned full grid, the homogeneous slope \(M_i=3^{p_i}/2^i\) is a mean-one nonnegative martingale in the parity filtration. For each finite \(H\), stopping it on the first \(M_i>4\) or at \(H\) shows \(\Pr(\max_{i\le H} M_i>4)\le1/4\). Conditioning on oddness increases this upper bound to at most \(1/2\). Choose a finite \(H\) at which the conditional fusion probability is greater than \(3/4\). The two conditions intersect on at least one full \(H\)-bit residue \(a=u\bmod 2^H\), even though the conditions are dependent. Both trajectories on this residue have identical affine endpoints at clock \(H\). No Haar assertion about a particular terminating source is needed. [PR146, distributional tail and scalar transition input](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437/research/upper-bit-mergers/THREE_RAY_UPPER.md) · [PR146, general finite initial states and normalization](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437/research/upper-bit-mergers/GROWING_ADDITIVE_GAPS.md)

Let \(0<u<2^H\) be the representative, \(q\) its number of odd steps, and \(U=T^H u\). For any lift \(a=u+2^H t\), \(T^H a=U+3^q t\). The specified modulus 27 prescribes one residue \(t=t_0\bmod27\). Since \(u\) is odd, \(q\ge1\), and \(U\) is a ternary unit: an odd shortcut lands at 2 modulo 3, and even division preserves being a unit. Hence \(U+3^q t_0\) is prime to three. The powers of two run through every unit modulo \(3^{q+3}\). There are arbitrarily large \(e\) with \(2^e\equiv U+3^qt_0\bmod 3^{q+3}\). Set \(t=(2^e-U)/3^q\). These give arbitrarily large positive \(a\) with the prescribed congruences, \(T^H a=2^e\), and the exact same endpoint on the other arm. Up to clock \(H\), each lower iterate is at most \(4a+E\) for a fixed maximum of the finitely many prefix intercepts. For \(a\ge E\) the whole orbit is bounded by \(5a\), including all later halvings and the cycle. This is ordinary interpolation on a fixed finite prefix. [Related terminal power-of-two interpolation at PR146](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437/research/upper-bit-mergers/ADJACENT_DESCENT.md)

Now \(v=(2^m a-1)/3\). Starting from \(A\), the orbit reaches \(v\) after two halvings, then \(2^{m-1}a\), and then halves to \(a\). Each of these values is at most \(A\); all later values are at most \(5a\le A\) since \(m\ge4\). Starting from \(B\), the first three steps reach \(9v+2\), and (4) of the rank note gives \(T^{m+3} B=z\). For the size inequality, \(B=(8\cdot 2^m a-5)/3\), so \(z-(3/2)^m B=3^{m-1}a+(5/3)(3/2)^m-1>0\). The two orbits actually meet at \(2^e\) after the additional scalar prefix, and both reach 1.

For the path assertion let \(P_z\) be the set of positive starts whose forward orbits hit \(z\). At a vertex \(x\ne z\) one has \(x\in P_z\iff T(x)\in P_z\), by deleting or prepending the first step. An undirected path avoiding \(z\) must therefore preserve membership in \(P_z\). But \(B\in P_z\), while \(z>A=\max O(A)\) gives \(A\notin P_z\). This cut argument works for any map and allows paths with repeats.

There is a separate fixed-target consequence. Set \(a=1\) and \(m=16\bmod18\). The left arm reaches one with maximum \(A\) for every such \(m\). The right arm reaches \(z=3^{m+1}-1\), which is of order \(B^{\log_2 3}\). The same cut forces this peak on every connecting path **if any path exists** for that explicit family. The convergence of its right arm for all such exponents is open. The simultaneous construction above changes \(a\) to guarantee existence of a connecting path. The ratio \((3/2)^m\) can grow without bound there, with no asserted polynomial relation between \(a\) and \(m\).
