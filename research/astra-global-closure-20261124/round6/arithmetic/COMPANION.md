# The actual scalar and the exceptional clocks

**PROPOSED written deductions.** Use the [fixed-source definitions and denominator theorem](README.md). At a positive ordinary escape, write \(r_L=-3c_L^*/D_L^*\) for the **value of the dyadic companion of the periodically repeated prefix**. Its reduced denominator is \(d_L^*\), since \(3\nmid D_L^*\). The scalar \(\mathcal D(n)\) of the original ordinary input is irrational by the pinned PR182 theorem. The following exact formula measures their distance.

## Exact dyadic agreement at every long prefix

Let \(k_L=v_2(x_L-n)\), finite on the injective orbit. At all sufficiently large \(L\),
\[
           v_2\big(\mathcal D(n)-r_L\big)
                    = L+p_L+k_L+p_{k_L}.                          \tag{8}
\]
Here \(p_{k_L}\) is counted from the original source \(n\). To see this, the first \(L\) bits coincide, and the companion splitting multiplier has 2-adic valuation \(L+p_L\). The two remaining streams are the actual bits from \(x_L\) and the periodically repeated bits restarting at the beginning. One has \(k_L<L\) for large \(L\) since \(0<|x_L-n|\le(3/2)^L(n+1)<2^L\). Until the first difference, the periodic stream thus supplies the original ordinary bits from \(n\). Two dyadic inputs differing at valuation \(k\) have exactly \(k\) matching bits. On each matching physical branch the valuation of their difference drops by one, and a difference of valuation zero means the parities differ. At the first differing bit the two companion values themselves have opposite parity, since the constant digit is that bit and the subsequent terms are even. The \(k_L\) further matching bits contribute valuation \(k_L+p_{k_L}\), proving (8). The periodic scalar is indeed \(-3c_L^*/D_L^*\), by summing its dyadic geometric series or by solving its two-branch affine equation.

For \(i<j\), set \(h=j-i\). The two physical pairs \(x_i,n\) and \(x_j,n\) give
\[
  \min(k_i,k_j)\le h\log_2 3+\log_2(n+1).                        \tag{9}
\]
If their minimum is less than \(h\) this is immediate. Otherwise \(x_i,n\) agree modulo \(2^{k_i}\); propagation by \(h\) actual steps gives \(T^h n\equiv x_j\bmod 2^{k_i-h}\). Hence \(T^h n-n\ne0\) is divisible by at least \(2^{\min(k_i,k_j)-h}\). Use \(|T^h n-n|\le(3/2)^h(n+1)\). For every fixed \(\eta>0\), successive sufficiently large clocks with \(k_i\ge\eta i\) therefore grow by a fixed factor \(>1\). There are \(O_{n,\eta}(\log(2N))\) such clocks through \(N\), and any fixed-length late window contains at most one of them.

Combining (8)–(9) with the full companion denominator estimate yields
\[
     \frac{v_2(\mathcal D(n)-r_L)}{\log_2 d_L^*}
          \longrightarrow 1\qquad\text{in upper Banach density}.            \tag{10}
\]
Here \(d_L^*>1\) eventually if one uses PR193's individual growing denominator bound. Alternatively define the ratio arbitrarily at \(d_L^*=1\); the denominator theorem puts those clocks in the exceptional set for a fixed positive tolerance, and (10) follows directly. Indeed \(\log_2|D_L^*|=L+p_L+o(1)\), the lost logarithm is \(o(L)\) in upper Banach density, and \(0\le k_L+p_{k_L}\le2k_L\). The genuine scalar enters both (8) and (10). These estimates concern an explicit infinite sequence of wrong rational candidates on an escape. They do not produce a successful candidate. ## A periodicity criterion for every ordinary positive source

Fix \(0<\epsilon<\log2\). For **every** positive ordinary starting integer \(n\), the set of clocks with \(\max(\log G_L,\log G_L^*)\ge\epsilon L\) has positive upper Banach density if and only if \(n\) itself lies on a cycle. For such a source of least period \(r\), this set eventually equals \(r\mathbb N\) and has density \(1/r\). The same equivalence holds with the minimum in place of the maximum. For a nonperiodic start the fixed-window bound (1) holds also when it has a finite preperiod.

The main theorem handles an injective orbit. In every other case the forward states belong to a finite set. Their true companion values are rational because the physical bits are eventually periodic, as follows by summing a dyadic geometric series. Choose one positive integer \(Q\) clearing the denominators of all these values. The scalar is injective on \(\mathbb Z_2\): a first differing physical bit gives a difference of dyadic valuation equal to the preceding weighted clock, as in (8), and equal physical bits at every clock imply congruence modulo every power of two.

At every clock with \(x_L\ne n\), \(G_L\mid x_L-n\). In the other row the genuine splitting identity gives
\[
             3 Q c_L^*= b_L^* Q\mathcal D(n)-a_L^* Q\mathcal D(x_L).
\]
Modulo \(G_L^*\) the clocks agree and \(b_L^*=3^L\) is a unit. Thus \(G_L^*\mid Q(\mathcal D(n)-\mathcal D(x_L))\). Both right sides are nonzero integers in fixed finite sets. The two entire cancellations are uniformly bounded at all these clocks. This includes every clock when the source has a nonempty preperiod.

If \(n\) is periodic, exactly the positive multiples \(L=jr\) have \(x_L=n\). At these clocks the physical periodic fixed point is \(n\) and the companion rational \(r_L=\mathcal D(n)\) is fixed. Hence \(G_L=|D_L|\) and \(G_L^*=|D_L^*|/\operatorname{den}(\mathcal D(n))\). The positive physical cycle has \(2^r>3^{p_r}\) by its positive carry, so \(\log G_{jr}/(jr)\to\log2\). In the companion row the two distinct bases \(2^{r+p_r}\) and \(3^r\) are raised to the power \(j\), so \(\log G_{jr}^*/(jr)\to r^{-1}\log\max(2^{r+p_r},3^r)\ge\log3\). Together with the bounded off-return cancellations this proves the criterion. It neither identifies the possible least periods of positive cycles nor distinguishes an escaping start from a transient start merely by density zero. The individual lower bound (3) and the bounded whole-cancellation statement use their separate hypotheses.

## Complete cancellation requires prime factors outside each fixed set

Fix an injective ordinary orbit again. Let \(S\) be any fixed finite set of places containing \(\infty,2,3\) and \(a\) any fixed integer. In either row, let \(P_{L,S,a}\) be the positive part of \(|\operatorname{num}(y_L+a)|\) supported outside \(S\), including all multiplicities. Discard the finitely many clocks with \(y_L+a=0\), permitted by the curve-avoidance theorem. On any infinite subsequence with
\[
                       \log P_{L,S,a}=o(L)
\]
we have \(\log G_L=o(L)\) on that same subsequence. More precisely, for every fixed \(\epsilon>0\) the inequality \(\log G_L\ge\epsilon L\), at all sufficiently large indices where it holds, forces \(\log P_{L,S,a}\ge c_{\epsilon,n,S,a}L\) for some positive constant. The result applies to any fixed finite list of translates and prime sets simultaneously by taking the least of their constants. This gives pointwise control even if the problematic clocks in (1) are arbitrarily far apart. It imposes a condition on the actual endpoint at each such clock.

Use the two coprime linear polynomials \(f(U,Y)=U-1\) and \(g(U,Y)=Y-(s+a)\) at the torus point \((u_L,y_L+a)\). The first polynomial is nonzero at the origin, including when \(s+a=0\). For this point the joint outside-\(S\) height used in Xiao, Definition 1.2, is exactly \(\log P_{L,S,a}\). Indeed \(u_L\) is an \(S\)-unit, and the second coordinate has no denominator away from \(S\). The joint height is \(\ge h(u_L)\gg L\), while both coordinate heights are \(O_n(L)+O_a(1)\). At primes away from six, the local gcd contributions of \(f,g\) equal those of \(G_L\), by (2). This includes the primes in \(S\setminus\{\infty,2,3\}\). Xiao's global left side sums nonnegative local contributions over **all** places, so it bounds this entire \(G_L\).

For an arbitrarily small relative gcd tolerance Xiao's Theorem 1.3 gives a fixed positive outside-height tolerance and a fixed proper Zariski-closed exceptional subset of the torus. The translated points eventually avoid that whole subset by Section 2. Indeed a nonzero Laurent polynomial vanishing on the subset can be cleared of negative exponents; if its coefficients are algebraic, take the product of its conjugates over a finite normal field, and then translate the second variable. This gives a nonzero rational polynomial in the original two coordinates. The uniform height bounds now prove the assertions. In particular any clock carrying a fixed fractional cancellation forces a large part away from \(S\) in **every** fixed translate, after its corresponding threshold. There is no proof that one of these parts must always be small at a prescribed clock. [Zheng Xiao, *Greatest common divisors for polynomials in almost units*, arXiv:2110.01751v3, Definition 1.2, Theorem 1.3 and §2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.01751v3)

## The final ordinary lap

For a hypothetical primitive positive integral cycle with smallest member \(m\) and length \(L\), the two-log lower bound and the positive product identity do imply \(m=O(L^{14.3})\), hence \(h(m)=O(\log L)\). This slow movement alone does not convert (7) into a bound excluding its full-period cancellation. At the entire lap the actual physical endpoint is \(y_L=m\) identically, and \(G_L=|D_L|\). In the variables \((U,Y,Z)\) with \(Z=m\) the points satisfy \(Y-Z=0\). In a monomial embedding containing \(1,Y\), the coordinates are linearly dependent over the moving coefficient field containing \(m\). Ru–Vojta's moving-hyperplane theorem requires linear independence over that field on each infinite coherent subset in addition to slow coefficient growth. At the apex the local target is \((1,m)\) and the second linear form \(Y-m\) vanishes exactly. Restricting to the diagonal leaves the entire factor \(U-1\) whenever an interpolant also vanishes at \((1,m)\). More concretely, \(P(U,m)\) is then a univariate polynomial divisible by \(U-1\), or is identically zero. This identifies the component causing failure even if large primitive cycles had only polynomially high minima. [PR195, physical minimum bound at all contracting clocks, §1](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/1c6e750e2f6970e6d5456cba5a93c2f3de8c2a97/research/astra-global-closure-20261124/closing/cycles/README.md) · [Paul Vojta, *Nevanlinna theory and Diophantine approximation*, Theorem 7.6(ii)–(iii), p. 553](https://library.slmath.org/books/Book37/files/vojta.pdf)

For the companion on one fixed periodic orbit, (4) has a rational limit. For instance, at \(n=1\) and even \(L\) the physical raw cancellation is complete and the companion zero-trial satisfies \(W_L=-3+3/u_L^*\). Its points lie on the fixed curve \(U(Y+3)=3\), with both reductions having denominator one. This checks the rational-limit exception on repeated laps. Nor does a dyadic limit over changing sources with individually classified companions imply triadic membership at the limit, as the PR193 signed interpolation example shows. To close universal convergence one still needs a successful triadic companion at each ordinary input or both a contradiction on an escape and an exclusion at the complete return of a primitive nontrivial ordinary cycle. An escaping trajectory is compatible with the estimates even if all its long auxiliary denominators are large.
