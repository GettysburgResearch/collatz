# A bounded bank of repeated words cannot cover a fixed ternary tower

**PROPOSED written theorem using a published S-unit theorem.** Fix a positive
odd integer `w`, a finite bank of nonempty finite parity words, and an integer
`K ≥ 1`. Only finitely many exponents `r ≥ 0` have the following property:
the actual shortcut orbit of `3^r w` follows a nonempty concatenation of at
most `K` powered words from the bank, with arbitrary positive repetition
counts, and its endpoint belongs to

\[
 \{2^i3^s b: i,s\ge0,\ 0<b<w\}
 \ \cup\ \{2^i3^s w:i\ge0,\ 0\le s<r\}.
\]

The words are chronological physical parities. A zero denotes an even
input and a one an odd input to the shortcut map. All vertices are positive
ordinary integers. An all-even template is allowed in the bank. The complete
concatenation must have positive length. This avoids the empty-trace
ambiguity for a base divisible by three. In the `FirstTower` application the
base is already prime to six.

This theorem controls forward visits through a bounded number of repeated
templates. It does not control arbitrary two-arm meetings, chains with
arbitrary additional preperiods, or a number of template blocks growing with
`r`. It does not assert convergence at an uncovered input. No numerical last
exceptional exponent or priority claim is supplied.

## Exact ordinary affine equation

For a nonempty word `v` let `E` be its length, `P` its odd count and `B` its
integer affine carry. On every ordinary realization,

\[
 T^E(x)=\alpha x+\beta,
 \qquad \alpha=3^P/2^E,\quad\beta=B/2^E.
\]

Since `E>0`, unique factorization gives `α≠1`. The rational center
`C=β/(1−α)` is defined even when `P=0`; in that case `B=C=0`.
Repeating the word `ell` times gives the exact centered map

\[
 x\longmapsto C+\alpha^{\ell}(x-C).
\]

The ordinary word identity is already proved in round ten's `TowerClock`.
[TemplateChain.lean](TemplateChain.lean) checks its centered form, arbitrary
repetition and the finite telescoping identity below. It does not formalize
the external S-unit theorem.

Fix the number of blocks `k≤K`, the ordered templates, and their centers
`C_1,…,C_k`. There are only finitely many such choices. Let `ell_j≥1`,
`F_0=1`, and

\[
 F_j=\prod_{t=1}^{j}\alpha_t^{\ell_t}.
\]

Write `x_j` for the actual endpoint after block `j`. Dividing
`x_j=C_j+(F_j/F_{j-1})(x_{j-1}−C_j)` by `F_j` and summing gives

\[
 x_0-C_1+
 \sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{C_j-C_{j+1}}{F_j}
 +\frac{C_k}{F_k}-\frac{x_k}{F_k}=0.                 \tag{1}
\]

The nonempty path starts at an odd integer, so its first input is odd.
After one odd shortcut, the current state is prime to three, and all later
shortcuts preserve this property. The proposed endpoint therefore has
`s=0` and `3∤b`. It has the form `x_k=2^i b`, with `1≤b≤w`. This is a finite
set of choices for `b`; allowing all of them only enlarges the set to bound.
Substituting `x_0=w3^r` in (1) yields a sum of at most `k+3` nonzero terms
after zero coefficients have been removed.

## The S-unit input and the valuation obstruction

Use Evertse, Schlickewei and Schmidt, *Linear equations in variables which
lie in a multiplicative group*, Annals of Mathematics 155 (2002),
807–836, Theorem 1.1. For fixed nonzero coefficients, the equation
`a_1 z_1+…+a_N z_N=1` has finitely many nondegenerate solutions in a
multiplicative subgroup of finite rank in `(Q*)^N`. Their uniform quantitative
bound is `exp((6N)^(3N)(rank+1))`. The bound concerns the number of solutions,
not the maximum height of a solution. [Published statement](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/155-3/p04)
· [Author's full preprint, Theorem 1.1](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~evertsejh/00-equation.pdf)

Every non-source term of (1) has the form

\[
 a/F_j\quad\hbox{or}\quad -b2^i/F_k,
\]

with fixed nonzero rational coefficient `a` or `−b`. Since
`v_3(F_j)=Σ_{t≤j}P_t ell_t≥0`, its 3-adic valuation is bounded above by
the valuation of that fixed coefficient. In contrast the source term has
valuation `r+v_3(w)`.

For each solution of (1), choose a minimal vanishing subset of its terms
that contains the source term. It contains at least two terms. No proper
nonempty subset of it vanishes: if a vanishing subset omitted the source,
its complement would be a smaller vanishing subset containing the source.
Choose a nonsource term as reference and divide by its negative. There are
only finitely many choices of subsets and reference positions.

For each choice, the resulting equation is nondegenerate in the precise
sense of the cited theorem. The ratio variables form a multiplicative group
of rank at most `k+2`, generated by the changes of the `k` repetition
indices, `r` and `i`. Fixed rational centers and their nonzero differences
are coefficients; they do not require a group-rank bound depending on their
prime factors. Normalizing by a reference term is a homomorphism on these
ratio variables and does not increase the rank. The S-unit theorem gives
only finitely many possible projective term ratios.

If the reference coefficient is `a≠0`, the source/reference ratio satisfies

\[
 v_3\!\left(\frac{w3^r}{\text{reference term}}\right)
 \ge r+v_3(w)-v_3(a).                                 \tag{2}
\]

The optional `2^i` in a reference has valuation zero. A fixed projective
ratio has a fixed finite valuation. Equation (2) bounds `r` for that ratio,
even though the reference's own valuation and its repetition indices may
vary. Finite unions over ratios, references, subsets, endpoints `b`, ordered
templates and `k≤K` establish the theorem.

Degenerate full sums are not discarded: selecting their minimal vanishing
source-containing subset is what makes the argument cover them. No typical
orbit assumption, random parity model, signed ordinary fixed point or
bounded repetition count is used.

## What this changes in the proof search

The preceding [anchor result](../closure/README.md) allows arbitrary
preperiod anchors for one immediate transfer through a finite set of signed
periodic phases. The theorem here instead allows a bounded number of
successive powered templates, including templates with nonintegral rational
centers. These are different quantified statements. Neither subsumes
arbitrary chains with unbounded preperiods and unbounded numbers of blocks.

Thus increasing only the repetition counts in a fixed finite catalogue of
blocks cannot give complete lower-cone coverage along a fixed tower. A
successful universal construction within a finite template bank must use a
number of blocks unbounded in the tower exponent, or use another mechanism
outside this model. This is a restriction on the certificate strategy; the
ordinary Collatz conjecture remains open.
