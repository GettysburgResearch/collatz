# Actual paired fibers on directed cuts

**PROPOSED.** Write \(T(2n)=n\), \(T(2n+1)=3n+2\), and let \(P_t(n)\) count odd inputs before clock \(t\). Two positive ordinary starts have the same component when their forward orbits meet at finite, possibly different clocks. A unit is positive, odd and prime to three.

Apart from \(O_w(X/(\log X)^w)\) original sources in a dyadic octave, a unit \(x\) has many strictly smaller and strictly larger units \(u\) with \(u,x\) reaching one common ordinary vertex and \(3u,3x\) reaching a second one. The two vertices lie below a fixed power of \(\log X\). Each family shares both clocks and both odd counts. This uses the named input H. Under the additional one-arm floor F and the cited cut results, such families carry arbitrarily large flow between any two specified distinct ordinary components, even after deleting a prescribed finite initial range. A failure of Collatz would therefore produce genuinely discordant units with near-linear-sized families in exactly their own two components. A least failed unit can still be exceptional.

## R8-P1. Many sources share two actual small endpoints

Fix \(w>0\), let \(X=2^h\), and consider the octave \(X\le x<2X\). Let \(\mathcal I_h\) consist of its aligned dyadic subintervals of lengths \(L=2^j\), \(0\le j\le h\). For some \(B=B(w)\), after discarding \(O_w(X/h^w)\) original unit sources, every remaining \(x\) has one record
\[
 (t,p,y,t',p',Y),\qquad T^t x=y,\quad T^{t'}(3x)=Y,\quad
 P_t(x)=p,\quad P_{t'}(3x)=p'
\]
with all the following properties.

* Both \(y,Y\ll_w h^A\) for fixed \(A=A(w)\). Both clocks are \(c_*\log X+O_w(\sqrt{h\log h})\), where \(c_*=2/\log(4/3)\). Each odd count is half its physical clock up to \(O_w(\sqrt{h\log h})\).
* In **every** \(I\in\mathcal I_h\) containing \(x\), there are at least \(\lfloor L/h^B\rfloor\) distinct units on each strict side of \(x\) with precisely the same six-tuple. They also share \(x\bmod3\).
* If the two components of \(x,3x\) differ, every partner is genuinely discordant with the same ordered components. The full octave gives \(X/(\log X)^{O_w(1)}\) in each strict direction. The first dyadic length at least \(2h^B\) gives a strictly smaller unit within \(4h^B\) of \(x\) for large \(h\). Advancing the earlier endpoint to \(\max(t,t')\) for the whole family gives a single common clock and a common count on each respective arm. The later target remains small and the advanced target is at most \(\exp(O_w(\sqrt{h\log h}))\).

**Input H.** Shaik's written Corollary 1.2, version 4.0.2, gives the ordinary exceptional count \(O_A(N/(\log N)^{A/(2A_{\rm FP})-1/2})\) for the polylogarithmic landing and shortcut time. Choose fixed \(A\) with \(A/(2A_{\rm FP})-1/2\ge w+1\). The time-control proof of Proposition 6.4 additionally bounds the actual shortcut odd count at this witness by half the time with the indicated error. The counting proof needs only that the counts lie between zero and the physical times. This packet does not independently reprove H or rebuild its formalization. [Shaik, Corollary 1.2 and section 6](https://shaikidris.github.io/polylog/paper/)

Apply H through \(6X\) to each injection \(x\mapsto x\) and \(x\mapsto3x\). The two original-source exceptional counts add. At each eligible \(x\) select the least witness clock on each arm satisfying the height, time and count estimates with the uniform octave constants. Including the source residue, there are \(O_w(h^{2A+4})\) records, from two endpoints with \(O_w(h^A)\) choices each and four integer clocks/counts bounded by \(O_w(h)\). No distribution at a landed source is used.

Fix a dyadic length \(L\) and put \(m=\lfloor L/h^B\rfloor\). In each of the \(X/L\) intervals, mark the first and last \(m\) sources of each record class as possible exceptions. At most \(O_w((X/L)h^{2A+4}m)\) sources fail at this length. There are \(h+1\) lengths. With \(B=2A+w+7\) the union costs \(O_w(X/h^w)\), including short lengths where \(m=0\). Partners are counted in their original H-eligible record class, before removing ranks at other lengths. Thus the proof does not recursively require every partner to pass every rank test. The synchronized endpoint estimate uses \(T(z)+1\le(3/2)(z+1)\) for every positive physical step.

## R8-P2. Fixed components and actual directed transfer

Give each odd-unit edge from the component of \(x\) to that of \(3x\) capacity \((\log(2x))^{5/4}/x\). In the undirected version use \(1/x\). Fix two distinct positive ordinary components \(A_0,A_1\), a lower height \(X_0\), and a desired flow \(M>0\). Under F, H and the respective pinned cut inputs, some **finite** upper height \(Z\) supports a directed flow of value at least \(M\) from \(A_0\) to \(A_1\) on R8-P1 edges with \(X_0<x\le Z\), using the stated capacities. The reversed ordered pair satisfies the same assertion. In the undirected version reciprocal capacities suffice. Edges discarded by the unconditional [native-prefix estimate](PAIRED_PREFIX.md) may also be removed.

1. Each fixed component has positive lower ordinary density from **F**, via PR203's separate depth-bank deduction. Choose a fixed nonperiodic odd unit \(a\) in that component large enough to dominate F's bounded real offset. A unit has arbitrarily large odd unit predecessors with one accelerated step, and only finitely many can be periodic. Among \(b\) in an interval of \(\asymp\sqrt{\log X}\) central odd depths, choose totals \(s\) with \(2^sa/3^b\) strictly inside the octave. A fixed finite net for rotation by \(\log_2 3\) gives \(\gg\sqrt{\log X}\) such depths. The central window and offset bound place all selected starts inside the octave. F at this supplied \(a\) gives \(\gg_a X/\sqrt{\log X}\) at each qualifying depth. Different depths cannot count the same start because two visits to \(a\) would make \(a\) periodic. This uses the multiplicity in paper 48, Lemma 7 equation (76), and the bounded initial branch of the proof of Theorem 8. The existence of a single routing word would be insufficient. [PR203, full hypotheses and proof](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/3f2c89f12b057b8bb8655bd6fc794c772aa8b65d/research/mixed-adic-basin-density-20261124/fixed_target_depth_bank.md) · [Weaver and Wren, paper 48 v2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/48/v2)
2. For any binary component cut separating \(A_0,A_1\), if its actual **odd** tripling discrepancies had finite reciprocal sum, PR146's adjacent-variation estimate and dyadic \(L^1\) telescoping would give a binary circle limit invariant under rotation by \(\log_2 3\). Its constancy would give zero upper density to one side, contrary to step 1. The fixed affine directed return paths of PR146, inequality (3), compare each orientation to the other at comparable heights with bounded multiplicity. Both oriented odd reciprocal sums therefore diverge. [PR146, dyadic limit](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437/research/upper-bit-mergers/THREE_RAY_DENSITY.md) · [PR146, directed return](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437/research/upper-bit-mergers/DIRECTED_TRIPLING.md)
3. For the directed claim, PR197's unit-exit equation (6) applies separately to each orientation. Finiteness on units with weight \((\log(2x))^{5/4}/x\) would give finite reciprocal mass for the same orientation on all odd sources. This uses the PR146 square-root pair bound and PR197's finite 22-window pressure certificate with strict exponent \(2001/5000>2/5\). For the undirected claim, round seven's 33-row exact substitution detects each odd discrepancy at a unit of bounded relative upper height with bounded multiplicity. This gives divergent reciprocal mass on the union of unit directions. The individual directed assertion here retains the logarithmic weight. [PR197, unit-exit equation (6)](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/b0538639df0ab7edd239281bd897e705160f732c/research/ayh-resource-reset-20261124/unit-exit/UNIT_EXIT.md) · [Round seven, bounded 33-row substitution](../../round7/head/README.md)
4. Use \(w=4\) in P1 and in the optional native-prefix estimate. The total excluded directed weight on octave \(h\) is \(O(h^{5/4-4})\), summable. The union is independent of the component cut. Every separating cut retains infinite capacity even on the prescribed tail. Cuts form a compact space: enumerate the countable components, fix the two endpoint bits, and give the other bits their product topology. Cut capacity through a finite \(Z\) depends on finitely many bits and increases with \(Z\). The open sets on which this capacity exceeds \(M\) give an increasing cover. A finite subcover gives one \(Z\) for **every** cut. The cuts of the resulting finite graph extend to cuts of all components. Finite max-flow/min-cut gives the claim.

With more than two components a flow may traverse intermediate components. If there are exactly two, all its crossings in the specified direction are direct. For any nontrivial basin-of-one cut, each direction separately has infinitely many octaves with at least \(X/(\log X)^3\) good edges. Otherwise the surviving weighted sum would be bounded by a constant times \(\sum_h h^{5/4-3}<\infty\). Every such edge has one actual small endpoint on either prescribed side. Its partners belong to the identical ordered pair of merger components, a stronger conclusion than membership in the cut. The two literal targets may depend on the edge and octave. Extending a convergent arm from its common endpoint to 1 yields a common continuation for all partners, with no time bound from this argument.

**First missing inference.** A least failed unit can lie in the summable exceptional set. The targets observed at different octaves need not recur at a preassigned ordinary \(q\), and overlapping families cannot be counted as independent copies of flow. An opposing upper bound on the surviving directed flow, or descent at every possible least unit, remains open.
