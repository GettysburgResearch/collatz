# Individual cancellations and the algebraic companion

**PROPOSED written theorem.** Keep the [same ordinary escape and notation](README.md). If its actual value \(\alpha=D(n)/3\in\mathbb Q_2\) is algebraic over \(\mathbb Q\), then for every fixed integer \(d\ge1\)
\[
 M\log G_i\ \le\ d\log A_i+E\log^+ W_i+o_{n,d}(i),
 \qquad M=(d+1)(d+2)/2-1,\quad E=\binom{d+2}{3}.                 \tag{6}
\]
Here the error denotes an upper error with positive part \(o(i)\). In particular on **every subsequence** with \(\log^+W_i=o(i)\), one has \(\log G_i=o(i)\). Indeed \(\log A_i\le i\log4\), and \(d/M\to0\) after taking the limsup for each fixed \(d\). Thus positive fractional cancellation along any infinite such subsequence proves \(D(n)\) transcendental. At such clocks the log reduced denominator is \(\log A_i-o(i)\) since \(A_i/Q_i\to\infty\).

Formula (2) gives this subexponential real trial at every physical record. It gives the same conclusion at all clocks of an escape with a limit \(\lambda=\lim_i \log(x_i)/i\). Indeed \(\lambda\ge0\), and \(\max_{j\le i}\log x_j-\log x_i=o(i)\). For any \(\gamma>0\), bound the maximum by a fixed initial constant or \((\lambda+\gamma)i\) and use \(\log x_i\ge(\lambda-\gamma)i\) eventually. Let \(\gamma\downarrow0\). More generally it suffices that the displayed record deficit be \(o(i)\) along the clocks in question.

## Primitive integer inputs and the exceptional subspaces

Let \(z_i\in\mathbb Z^M\) be the row of integral degree-\(d\) evaluations from the main note. Its exact content \(G_i\) gives the primitive integer vector \(y_i=z_i/G_i\). At \(v=\infty,2,3\) use exactly the fixed bases there. Each is a set of \(M\) independent linear forms in the coordinates \(y_i\). The bases at infinity and three are rational. The basis at two has algebraic coefficients at the *actual chosen embedding* under the hypothesis of (6). We make no assertion that a conjugate tail remains integral at a different place above two. This use of rational integer input vectors is compatible with the other conjugates' growing local heights. [PR203, frozen companion height obstruction](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/3f2c89f12b057b8bb8655bd6fc794c772aa8b65d/research/mixed-adic-basin-density-20261124/companion_obstruction.md)

At a single clock \(K=0\), the product of the column bounds in the local-size proof of (3), scaled from \(z_i\) to \(y_i\), gives
\[
 \prod_{v=\infty,2,3}\prod_{j=1}^M |L_{v,j}(y_i)|_v
   \ \le\ 2^M A_i^d\max(1,W_i)^E/G_i^M .                         \tag{7}
\]
No change-of-basis determinant is needed for this product of the actual local forms; the factor \(2^M\) absorbs the real subtractions. The division uses \(G_i\) once per coordinate at infinity, with \(|G_i|_2=|G_i|_3=1\). The sup norm \(\|y_i\|_\infty\le \exp(O_d(i))\), since \(A_i\le4^i,\ Q_i\le3^i,\ C_i\le4^i-3^i\) by the positive sum in its definition.

For any fixed \(\eta>0\), Schlickewei's \(p\)-adic Subspace Theorem puts primitive integer vectors with the product in (7) at most \(\|y_i\|_\infty^{-\eta}\) in finitely many proper rational linear subspaces. This is the square case with \(M\ge2\), one independent algebraic basis per place and three fixed places. No uniformity in \(d\), in \(n\), or in the coefficient field over varying sources is used. [Schlickewei, original paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01220404) · [Evertse, Theorem 1.1, pp. 1–2, explicit primitive-integer formulation](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~evertsejh/dio2011-padicsubspace.pdf)

Every such subspace is met only finitely on this fixed orbit. A rational hyperplane containing it gives a nonzero homogeneous polynomial \(F\in V\). Its zero set on the companion chart is \(F(1,U,UW)=0\), a nonzero polynomial since homogeneous dehomogenization followed by \(C/Q=UW\) is injective on polynomials. The fixed-orbit curve-avoidance argument in round six applies. Briefly, the chart points are \(S\)-integral on \(\mathbb G_m\times\mathbb A^1\) for \(S=\{\infty,2,3\}\). Infinitely many on a curve would, after normalization and a fixed finite enlargement of \(S\), give a genus-zero component with at most two punctures by Siegel. \(U\) has a zero and a pole among the punctures and is nonconstant because \(v_2(U_i)=a_i\to\infty\). Both punctures are rational, so \(U=ct^r\) and \(W\) is Laurent in a rational parameter \(t\) tending to zero dyadically. Then \(UW=C_i/Q_i\) has finite dyadic limit \(\alpha\) by (1), and the limit of this rational-coefficient Laurent polynomial must be rational. PR182 proves \(D(n)\) irrational on an escape. The normalization, rationality of the component and extension of integrality are detailed at the frozen source. [PR195 at fbe68bf1, round-six §2](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/fbe68bf1b13b797843691080d782f4262a5c4468/research/astra-global-closure-20261124/round6/arithmetic/README.md) · [Corvaja–Zannier, integral-curve theorem](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/S1631-073X%2802%2902240-9.pdf) · [PR182, actual dyadic irrationality](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/234fd2ece3a17c8950dce452291198705b4e6e60/research/high-variance-closure-20261124/ordinary-arithmetic/DYADIC_COMPANION.md)

Thus outside finitely many clocks the left side of (7) exceeds \(\|y_i\|_\infty^{-\eta}\). Taking logs and then \(\eta\downarrow0\) gives (6). Merely knowing that the actual value is dyadically integral does not make the supplied two-adic forms algebraic. The unconditional multi-clock argument avoids that hypothesis because it estimates one rational determinant and proves its nonvanishing from actual paired physical states.

## The closing step

PR193 gives full leading denominators on expansions of certain fixed seeds and shows a fixed escaping source can have prefix seed complexity growing linearly. That result supplies neither an exceptional clock for the present source nor algebraicity of its companion. Even a transcendence certificate would concern a specified escaping source. Establishing universal convergence would instead require triadic membership of \(D(n)\) at each positive ordinary \(n\), or a contradiction for an escape together with exclusion of nontrivial ordinary cycles. The present estimates are compatible with every large companion denominator of an escape having its full leading size.
