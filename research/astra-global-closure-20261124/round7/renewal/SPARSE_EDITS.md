# Bounded changes of ordered odd positions at one exact endpoint

**PROPOSED written theorem.** Fix the number of permitted changes *before* letting the physical clock tend to infinity. In an exactly legal fixed-target row, changing this many ordered odd positions can affect only the first $o(e)$ odd ranks. The changes may be adaptive and need not occur in one consecutive segment. The result follows from the rational-input $p$-adic Subspace Theorem. A separate application below retains PR201's actual early complete marks in the pointwise concentration of R7-R1.

## A target-uniform restriction

**R7-R5.** Given an integer $s\ge0$ and $\epsilon>0$ there is $e_0(s,\epsilon)$ such that the following holds for every $e\ge e_0$, every weight $p\le e$, and every supplied positive integer $q$. Take two actual chronological first-arm parity words of length $e$ and weight $p$ ending at this same $q$. Their ordered odd positions are $0\le d_0<\cdots<d_{p-1}<e$ and $0\le d'_0<\cdots<d'_{p-1}<e$. If at most $s$ indices have $d_j\ne d'_j$, then
$$
                          d_j=d'_j\qquad(j\ge\epsilon e).             \tag{S1}
$$
Only full inverse congruence at $q$ is needed. In fact the result holds whenever the two word carries agree modulo $3^p$. It applies in particular to common total central rows and to any endpoints of a completed paired family. The metric counts displacements at fixed ordinal ranks. A change of just two chronological bits can move the ordinal ranks of many intervening odd inputs.

We first prove the Diophantine lemma. Let $N\to\infty$ index a sequence, let $S$ be a fixed finite set of rational places containing $3$ and infinity, and let $u_1,\ldots,u_l$ be signed rational $S$-units, with $l$ fixed. Suppose that every $|v_3(u_i)|=o(N)$, each numerator and denominator has logarithmic size $O(N)$, and $U=\sum_i u_i\ne0$. Then $v_3(U)=o(N)$.

The ultrametric inequality supplies the lower bound. If the upper bound fails, pass to an infinite subsequence with $v_3(U)\ge\eta N$ for some fixed $\eta>0$. Induct on $l$. For $l=1$ this contradicts the hypothesis. For $l\ge2$, multiply all coordinates by a common rational $S$-unit so that $z$ is a primitive integer vector. This scaling exists by clearing denominators and dividing by their integer gcd. Its height $\|z\|_\infty\le\exp(CN)$ for some fixed $C>0$, since $l$ is fixed. Use coordinate forms at every place except 3, and at 3 replace the first coordinate form by the sum of all coordinates. These are independent rational forms at each place. The product of their absolute values on $z$, taken over all places in $S$, is exactly $|U|_3/|u_1|_3$, since each scaled nonzero coordinate is still an $S$-unit and has product one. The ratio is at most $\exp(-cN)\le\|z\|_\infty^{-c/C}$ eventually, after decreasing $c>0$. Schlickewei's theorem places these vectors in finitely many proper rational linear subspaces. Pass to one such subspace containing infinitely many of them, choose one fixed nonzero rational relation $\sum c_i u_i=0$, and relabel with $c_l\ne0$. Then
$$
                   U=\sum_{i<l}(1-c_i/c_l)u_i .
$$
Delete zero coefficients and enlarge $S$ by the finitely many primes in the other fixed rational coefficients. At least one term remains because $U\ne0$. The other hypotheses survive, with fewer terms. This contradicts induction and also handles exact cancellations among proper subsums. No nondegeneracy assumption on the original tuple is needed. [Schlickewei, original theorem](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01220404) · [Evertse, Theorem 1.1, primitive rational inputs, $r=n\ge2$](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~evertsejh/dio2011-padicsubspace.pdf)

For (S1), the word carry is $C=\sum_{j<p}3^{p-1-j}2^{d_j}$. Equality of ordinary endpoints implies $3^p\mid C-C'$. If (S1) were false, take violating rows with $e\to\infty$. On a subsequence the number of changed ranks is a single $l\in[1,s]$ and their ordered ranks $j_1<\cdots<j_l$ satisfy $j_i/e\to\lambda_i$ for every $i$. Put $J=j_l$, $\lambda=\lambda_l\ge\epsilon$. Let $a$ be the first index with $\lambda_a=\lambda$. Dividing the full congruence by $3^{p-1-J}$ yields
$$
 3^{J+1}\mid\sum_{i=1}^l3^{J-j_i}(2^{d_{j_i}}-2^{d'_{j_i}}).
$$
If $a>1$, all terms with $i<a$ are divisible by $3^{J-j_{a-1}}$, with $(J-j_{a-1})/e\to\lambda-\lambda_{a-1}>0$. If $a=1$ there are no omitted terms. In either case the retained tail sum $V$ over $a\le i\le l$ is divisible by $3^{\lfloor c e\rfloor}$ for some $c>0$ on every sufficiently late row of this subsequence.

This tail sum cannot be zero. Fill in the unchanged ranks in the contiguous interval $[j_a,J]$ on each side. Equality of the two resulting carries with weights $3^{J-j}$ first forces equality of the least position: its term has the unique smallest 2-adic valuation on either side. Subtract these equal terms and repeat to recover all remaining positions. That would contradict the known change at $J$. On the other hand $V$ is a sum of at most $2s$ signed $\{2,3,\infty\}$-units $\pm3^{J-j_i}2^d$. They all have logarithmic size $O(e)$ and $v_3=J-j_i=o(e)$ within the retained group. The lemma gives $v_3(V)=o(e)$, contradicting its divisibility. Since a failure of the asserted uniform threshold supplied the sequence itself, the proof gives (S1) for every sufficiently large row with the fixed $s,\epsilon$. The threshold need not be effective.

For one moved rank $j$ the exact test is elementary. With $\Delta=|d_j-d'_j|>0$, the carry congruence is $3^{j+1}\mid2^\Delta-1$. The order of 2 modulo $3^{j+1}$ is $2\cdot3^j$ (cube successively from $4=1+3$). Hence $2\cdot3^j\mid\Delta$ and $j\le\log_3((e-1)/2)$. Such ranks can grow, and the chronological displacement can remain proportional to $e$. For $j\ge1$ take $e=16\cdot3^j$, $p=e/2$. Set $d_i=d'_i=i$ for $i<j$, $d_j=j$, $d'_j=j+2\cdot3^j$, and $d_i=d'_i=i+2\cdot3^j$ for $i>j$. Both words start odd and have $6\cdot3^j$ trailing zeros. Both real offsets are below $1/2$, since after the $p$ odd steps the offset is at most $(3/2)^p$ and the trailing halvings divide it by $2^{6\cdot3^j}$. Their common carry class is a unit modulo $3^p$. Among its three lifts at precision $p+1$ after multiplying by $2^{-e}$, each word forbids just one lift to make its source a unit. Choose an allowed common lift and a positive odd representative $q$. Both inverse sources are then positive odd units and have exactly those itineraries. The target here may depend on $j$. This prevents reading (S1) as a uniform assertion that all modified *physical* clocks are small.

There is an ordinary typical-source refinement. Fix $q$, consider central $(e,p)$ with $p/e\to1/2$, and restrict starts to $0<x\le qG$, $G=2^e/3^p$. Given a fixed $0<\rho<\log_2(2/\sqrt3)$, put $H=\lfloor\rho e\rfloor$. All but $O_{q,\rho}(G\exp(-c_\rho e))$ such starts have at least $H/4$ odd inputs in their first $H$ steps: the bad parity words occupy an exponentially small fraction of the $2^H$ classes, and $2^H\le G$ for large $e$. For fixed $s$, use (S1) with $\epsilon<\rho/8$. A good start has an unchanged odd rank $\lceil\epsilon e\rceil$ strictly before clock $H$, so *both* lists place every changed position before $H$. The two first-arm itineraries have a common full suffix from $H$ to $e$ and end at the same $q$; inverting that fixed suffix gives the same physical value at $H$. Also
$$
          |x-x'|\le \sum_{d_j\ne d'_j}3^{-1-j}|2^{d_j}-2^{d'_j}|
                     \le s\,2^H .
$$
This estimate concerns all bounded-rank repairs of each such original source. It leaves open algorithms that change a growing number of ranks or deliberately redesign the early joint state.

## The late fixed-target bridge

PR201's new theorem can strengthen R7-R1 and R7-R2 without changing their terminal-state qualification. Assume its additional finite-prefix renewal input R. For fixed $q$, choose the A predecessor $q_0$ and clock $u$ as in R7-R1 with $q_0>F_*+2$, $(9(F_*+1)+7)2^{-u}\le1/2$. Set $b=p-2$, $s_0=e-u$, then $|s_0-2b|\le0.55\sqrt b$ eventually on the full R7-R1 window. In PR201 (2.2) take no terminal words beyond the empty suffix, $K=1$, and front odd depth $a=\lfloor\eta b\rfloor$, $n=b-a$. Choose fixed $\eta>0$ small enough that $a+1\le\delta(n-1)$, $a\le n/10$, $\eta<1/20$, and $32a/n<1/8$ for all large rows. Restrict the front total to $|A_u-2a|\le\sqrt n/4$. Its rejected geometric mass is at most $32a/n$ by variance $2a$. Then
$$
        |s_0-A_u-2n|\le0.55\sqrt b+\tfrac14\sqrt n<\sqrt n .
$$
By R, at least $3/4$ of the chronological front law has at least $c\sqrt{a-1}$ complete marks through physical clock $a-1$, for sufficiently small fixed $c>0$. The intersection therefore has weight at least $5/8$. PR201 (2.2) supplies $\gg_q G/\sqrt e$ ordinary words to $q_0$ with these early marks and $\beta\le F_*+1$. Appending the one-arm A suffix gives the exact row at $q$ with $\beta\le1/2$. The original $mx$ count/height exceptional set of size $O_{m,q}(G/e^2)$ in R7-R1 still discards a vanishing fraction of this bank. Specialize to $m=5$. The original large concentrated family and its R7-R2 simultaneous binary translates therefore keep $\gg\sqrt p$ *actual initial* complete ratio-five returns. The final R7-R2 marked fiber still has the separately identified large ordinary target $Q$. PR201's escape criterion counts high *relative* real offsets $\beta\ge\epsilon q$. For any fixed $\epsilon>0$ and $q>1/(2\epsilon)$, all the small-offset R7-R1 starts lie outside that count. A simple path to a nonperiodic $q$ with $\beta/q\ge\epsilon$ must pass through a bounded physical state depending only on $\epsilon$, by PR201's finite-distortion bound S. For example choose $M$ with $(200/3)M^{-1/40}<\epsilon$ and use $\beta/q=1-D^{-1}\le\log D$. Such a path contains a suffix of the literal forward orbit of one of the finitely many integers at most $M$; a nonperiodic $q$ can be visited only once on each forward orbit. PR201 (4.4) shows how a single escaping small head, if present, supplies the opposing *positive* lower bound along these fixed suffixes. Neither the lower bridge nor the subspace restriction on edits bounds the high-offset counts on those supplied endpoints. Eventual periodicity and the independent nontrivial-cycle question remain open.
