# Large irreducible factors collectively carry almost the whole cycle determinant

**PROPOSED written theorem.** Let $p$ be the odd period in the bounded least-tripling-disagreement branch and $F(X)=\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}c_r X^{p-1-r}$ its rank polynomial, with $c_r\in\{1,2,4\}$. For an irreducible factor $G$ of $F$ over the rationals, let $t$ be the gcd of the positive exponents with nonzero coefficients and write $G(X)=H(X^t)$. This removes all common dilation from its exponents. Call $n=\deg H$ its degree after this substitution.

Every sufficiently large **ordinary** candidate has a noncyclotomic irreducible factor with
$$
                 n\gg \frac{p(\log\log p)^3}{(\log p)^5}.                 \tag{1}
$$
More precisely, for every fixed $\epsilon>0$, remove the integer content, all cyclotomic factors, and all noncyclotomic factors with $n\le p^{1-\epsilon}$, including multiplicities. The remaining product evaluated at the rank residue $u$ must absorb a divisor of $D=2^h-3^p$ of size $3^{p-o(p)}$. If there are no remaining factors, the reduced denominator of the rational candidate is $3^{p-o(p)}$. The bounds are uniform over the rank letters. They cover any number of factors and arbitrary dilations, including factors of large uncompressed degree.

This excludes an algebraically specified class of mixed words from complete divisibility. The first missing inference is a contradiction for a physical word whose high-degree factors do absorb the indicated divisor. The theorem imposes no general upper bound on that last gcd. A separate [all-depth basin control](BASIN_CONTROL.md) tests why short inverse paths alone do not close the argument.

## Inputs and finite divisor accounting

Take coprime $p,h$ with $p<h\le2p$ and $D>1$. Since $D$ is prime to six and $2^h=3^p\bmod D$, Bézout exponents for $p,h$ supply a unit $u$ with $u^p=2$ and $u^h=3\bmod D$. The reduced denominator is $d=D/\gcd(D,F(u))$. Indeed at the minimum the chronological carry for $4s$ is $\sum_{j<p}3^{p-1-j}2^{\lfloor jh/p\rfloor} c_{jh\bmod p}$. Modulo $D$ it is $F(u)$ times the fixed unit $u^{h(p-1)-(p-1)}$. Multiplication by four preserves the reduced denominator. For any formal candidate satisfying the physical edge rule the corresponding positive rational array has the same denominator, even if its prescribed ranks still need an order check. [Exact prior derivation](../../round9/critical/README.md) · [Prior local test for the entire determinant](../../round11/cycle/README.md)

The estimates below use the full integer gcd. If $g\mid D$ divides the two integer evaluations $H(y),P(y)$, the integral Sylvester resultant is divisible by $g$. To see this even at bad primes, evaluate the rows for the polynomial multiples in the Sylvester matrix on the power vector of $y$ modulo $g$. They all vanish. Multiplication by the adjugate gives that the determinant times the power vector vanishes; its constant entry is 1. The same conclusion holds for constant $P$ by the constant resultant formula. No field structure on $\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z$, simple-root assumption or discriminant exclusion is used. In products we use $\gcd(D,AB)\le\gcd(D,A)\gcd(D,B)$, which is $\min(e,a+b)\le\min(e,a)+\min(e,b)$ at every prime power. Shared primes and repeated factors are allowed throughout.

## Cyclotomic factors, including arbitrary orders

If $g=\gcd(D,\Phi_v(u))$, then $u^v=1\bmod g$. For $g>1$, the subgroup generated by $u$ modulo $g$ has at most $v$ elements. It contains $2$ and $3$. Put $r=\lfloor\sqrt v\rfloor$. The $(r+1)^2>v$ ordinary positive integers $2^a3^b$ for $0\le a,b\le r$ are all different by unique factorization. Two coincide modulo $g$, so $g\le6^r$. This also bounds $g=1$. In particular the cost per factor is $O(\sqrt v)$ in the logarithm, uniformly in $p,h$.

Here is a uniform bound on repeated factors at the circle. If $\zeta$ is an $m$-fold root of $F$ with $|\zeta|=1$, then $m=O(\sqrt{p\log(4p)})$. Along the straight segment from $\zeta$ to $z=e^{i\theta}\zeta$ the modulus of the $m$th derivative is at most $4p\,p^m$. Taylor's formula with its integral remainder and $m!\ge(m/e)^m$ give
$$
 |F(e^{i\theta}\zeta)|\le4p(ep|\theta|/m)^m.
$$
On $|\theta|\le m/(2ep)$ the logarithm is thus at most $\ln(4p)-m\ln2$. On the rest of the circle it is at most $\ln(4p)$. Jensen's formula gives mean logarithm at least $\ln|F(0)|\ge0$. Integrating the two bounds proves the claim, also with unit-circle zeros interpreted by an integrable logarithm.

For all $v$, $\varphi(v)\gg v^{3/4}$: for every prime $\ell\ge5$, $1-1/\ell\ge\ell^{-1/4}$, and the two remaining primes cost only a fixed factor. For $v>T$, the log gcd cost is therefore $O(T^{-1/4}\varphi(v))$. Degrees including multiplicities sum to at most $p-1$. For $v\le T$, the multiplicity bound gives total cost $O(T^{3/2}\sqrt{p\log(4p)})$. Taking $T=p^{2/7}$ bounds the log gcd of **all cyclotomic factors together** by $O(p^{13/14}\sqrt{\log(4p)})=o(p)$.

## A short relation for each noncyclotomic factor

Choose primitive integral factors with positive leading coefficients. Every endpoint coefficient of every factor is prime to three since both endpoints of $F$ are powers of two. If $G=H(X^t)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$, so is $H$. It has no positive real root since $F$ is strictly positive on positive reals. Write $n=\deg H$, $\ell=\ln M(H)$, where $M$ is multiplicative Mahler measure. Then $M(G)=M(H)$, because the $t$th roots of each root contribute its original factor $\max(1,|\alpha|)$ to the product.

For an integer $R\ge2\sqrt t$ put $B=\lceil6p/R\rceil$. There is a nonzero integer triple $(i,j,k)$ satisfying
$$
      ip+jh+kt=0,\qquad |i|,|j|\le R,\quad |k|\le B.                \tag{2}
$$
The individual coordinates may vanish. Indeed the box $[0,R]^2\times[0,B]$ maps by that linear form into $[0,3pR+tB]$. Its cardinality exceeds $3pR+tB+1$, since $t\le R^2/4$ and $R^2 B\ge6pR$. Subtract two points with the same image.

Let $y$ denote $u^t$ modulo $D$. If $j\ne0$, use the nonzero polynomial
$$
 P(Y)=2^{i_+}3^{j_+}Y^{k_+}-2^{i_-}3^{j_-}Y^{k_-},\qquad a_\pm=\max(\pm a,0).
$$
It vanishes at $y\bmod D$ by (2). Modulo three it is a nonzero monomial. If $H$ divided $P$ over $\mathbb Q$, Gauss's lemma and primitivity would give divisibility over $\mathbb Z$. Reduction modulo three is impossible because $\bar H$ has positive degree and nonzero constant and leading coefficients. Consequently $\mathrm{Res}(H,P)\ne0$.

If $j=0$, then $i$ and $k$ are nonzero. Write $t/p=a/b$ in lowest terms; $a\le |i|$, $b\le |k|$. Now $P(Y)=Y^b-2^a$ vanishes at $y\bmod D$. It is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$: if a monic factor has degree $f$, its nonzero integer constant term has complex modulus $2^{af/b}$ as every root has modulus $2^{a/b}$. Thus $b\mid af$ by integer prime factorization, and $b\mid f$. By Gauss's lemma this excludes a proper positive-degree factor. Its positive real root excludes equality with a scalar multiple of $H$. Again the resultant is nonzero.

Both polynomials have degree at most $B$ and the sum of absolute coefficients is at most $2\cdot6^R$. The product formula for the resultant, with the leading coefficient included in $M(H)$, gives
$$
 \log\gcd(D,G(u))\le\log|\mathrm{Res}(H,P)|
                \le n\log(2\cdot6^R)+B\ell .                         \tag{3}
$$
This gives an exact finite divisor for the contribution of each factor.

For each selected occurrence choose $R=\lceil2\sqrt t+\sqrt{p\ell/n}\rceil$. Let $N$ be the sum of its $n$ over all selected occurrences, and $L=\ln M(F)$. We have $\sum nt\le p$, $\sum\ell\le L$ and $L\le\log(4\sqrt p)$ by Jensen's inequality and the circle $L^2$ norm. In (3), summing $n\sqrt t$ costs at most $\sqrt{Np}$ and summing $\sqrt{pn\ell}$ costs at most $\sqrt{pNL}$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. The ceilings cost $O(N+L)$. Thus for the product $V$ of **any selection** of noncyclotomic irreducibles with multiplicity,
$$
       \log\gcd(D,V(u))\ll N+L+\sqrt{pN(1+L)}.                      \tag{4}
$$
In particular total degree *after* removing the individual dilations $N=o(p/\ln p)$ absorbs only $\exp(o(p))$. Neither physical low complexity nor a fixed number of irreducibles is assumed.

## From Mahler measure to the largest required factor

Dobrowolski's theorem on algebraic integers gives, for a monic noncyclotomic irreducible of degree $n$ (nonzero constant term), $\ln M\gg(\log\log n/\log n)^3$ for large $n$. Bounded degrees can be included with a positive absolute constant: if $M<2$ there are only finitely many polynomials of bounded degree and bounded Mahler measure, and Kronecker's theorem characterizes the case $M=1$. A nonmonic primitive factor already has $M\ge2$. Therefore the number $J$ of all noncyclotomic irreducible occurrences satisfies
$$
                  J\ll\frac{(\log p)^4}{(\log\log p)^3}
$$
for sufficiently large $p$, since $\ln M(F)\le\log(4\sqrt p)$. [Dobrowolski, *On a question of Lehmer and the number of irreducible factors of a polynomial*, Acta Arithmetica 34 (1979), 391–401](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-34-4-391-401)

For the bounded branch, Wu and Wang's homogeneous special case gives $h\ln2-p\ln3\gg p^{-4.5}$, so $D=3^p(e^{h\ln2-p\ln3}-1)\gg 3^p/p^{4.5}$. The near-critical strip also gives $h\ln2-p\ln3=O(1)$, hence $\ln D=p\ln3+O(\ln p)$. This applies, for example, to every supplied coprime pair with $0<h/p-\log_2 3\le1/p$. [Wu and Wang, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2014.03.007) · [Bounded branch order pair](../../round9/critical/README.md)

If $d=1$, (4) and the cyclotomic bound force $N\gg p/\ln p$ for the entire noncyclotomic product. Dividing by $J$ gives (1). For the stronger divisor statement choose the factors with $n\le p^{1-\epsilon}$. Their total $N\le Jp^{1-\epsilon}=o(p/\ln p)$. The content of $F$ is a power of two, hence a unit modulo $D$. The full gcd of the removed factors is $\exp(o(p))$. If $d=1$, the gcd with the remaining product is at least $D/\exp(o(p))$. If there is no remaining product, then $d=D/\exp(o(p))$. This last conclusion requires the actual rational denominator formula and does not infer ordinary realization from a single factor's cancellation.

The fixed-$S$ gcd theorem of Corvaja and Zannier motivated comparing a putative common algebraic root with two power relations. Here (2) and positivity produce a finite resultant bound with uniform dependence on the changing polynomial, which a fixed-$S$ specialization alone would not supply. If $F$ is itself irreducible of degree $p-1$ with no nontrivial dilation, (4) does not contradict full divisibility. The finite-prime switches and inverse tests in PR193 and the occupation lower bounds in PR192 supply no bound on this remaining resultant.
