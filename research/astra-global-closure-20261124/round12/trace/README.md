# Narrow source intervals on an escaping ray

**Status PROPOSED.** An escaping integer would force mass in exponentially narrow source intervals at individual endpoints of the balanced integer trace. It would also force positive mass proportional to angular width in every sufficiently wide moving dyadic mantissa interval, after both endpoint cutoffs. The source-count implications assume the source-qualified renewal floor F. Its analytic proof belongs to the external source and lies outside the repository's Lean verification. The phase discrepancy and translated-interval estimates are elementary finite-path statements. Universal convergence remains open.

## 1. An actual source interval at each terminal

Let \(q_0=a\) be an escaping ordinary odd integer prime to three, let \(q_i=U^i(a)\), and write \(A_i\) for the total valuation of its first \(i\) odd gaps. An escaping orbit has all vertices distinct. Set
\[
 D_i=\prod_{j<i}(1+1/(3q_j)),\qquad G_b=(4/3)^b,\qquad
 B_i=q_i G_b/D_i=a3^i2^{-A_i}G_b .
\]
Use the simple row with \(b\) odd gaps, shortcut clock \(2b\), and relative real offset at least \(\epsilon_a=1/(3a+1)\). Its normalized mass is its integer source count divided by \(q_iG_b\). A row of \(n\) gaps and clock \(s\) from \(x\) to \(t\) has \(t=3^n2^{-s}x+\beta\).

**Theorem 1.** Let \(N=\lfloor\sqrt b/10\rfloor\). For every sufficiently large \(b\), every \(N\le i<2N\), and every \(2\le e\le \sqrt b/4\) for which \(t=(2^e a-1)/3\) is a positive ordinary unit, the balanced row at \(q_i\) has normalized mass at least
\[
                   c_a\,2^{-e}/\sqrt b
\]
from distinct actual integers \(x\) in the one-sided interval
\[
        \frac{1}{a2^e}\ \le\ 1-\frac{x}{B_i}\
                \le\ \frac{1+3F_0}{a2^e}.                                  \tag{1}
\]
Here \(F_0\) is the absolute offset bound in F. The same lower bound holds with a prescribed unit source class modulo three. Every six consecutive candidate exponents contain an admissible \(e\), since the powers of two modulo nine permute the six units and \(2^e a\bmod9\) may be 4 or 7.

For the proof apply F **at \(t\)** with \(n=b-i-1\) and \(s=2b-A_i-e\). This is the previously stated form of Weaver–Wren's fixed-total lower floor, with \(c_0 2^s/(3^n\sqrt n)\) distinct sources and \(0\le\beta\le F_0\). The central-window condition holds with room: \(i\le A_i\le i\log_2 3+\log_2(aD_*)\), where \(D_*\) bounds simple path distortion by §2. Thus \(s-2n=2i-A_i+2-e\) has absolute value at most \(\sqrt b/2+O_a(1)<\sqrt n\). Append \(t\to a\) and the actual suffix from \(a\) to \(q_i\). A repeated vertex anywhere would make \(q_i\) eventually periodic. The gap out of \(a\) gives the required relative offset by the product for distortion. The source-to-\(a\) offset is \((1+3\beta)/2^e\), giving (1) after division by \(a\). Dividing the floor by \(q_iG_b\) gives \(3c_0/(2^e aD_i\sqrt n)\). Positivity and the bound on \(D_i\) prove the claim. No union over potential midpoint integers is used. [Qualified input F and the fixed-clock formulation](../../round10/rows/README.md) · [Weaver–Wren, paper 48 v2, Lemma 7 and Theorem 8](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/48)

**Uniformity in the moving terminal.** In (76) the absolute constants and the one cylinder \(y\bmod3^{h_0}\) are fixed before quantifying over \(n\ge n_0\), \(K\le\delta_*n\), \(|s-2n|\le2\sqrt n\), and **every** lift of that cylinder at depth \(n+K\). For an arbitrary unit terminal \(z\) and requested nonzero source residue \(u\bmod3\), choose \(r=z-3^n2^{-s}u\bmod3^{n+1}\), a unit. Theorem 8's first-letter spread chooses \(1\le v\le2\cdot3^{h_0}\) so \(r'=(2^v r-1)/3\) lies in the fixed cylinder at depth \(n\). Use (76) at length \(n-1\), extra depth one, total \(s-v\). If \(|s-2n|\le\sqrt n\), then \(|s-v-2(n-1)|\le2\sqrt{n-1}\) beyond an absolute onset. Its fixed-total probability is \(\gg n^{-1/2}\), by the negative-binomial local bound at this central displacement. Words of that clock have probability \(2^{-(s-v)}\), so (76) supplies \(\gg 2^s/(3^n\sqrt n)\) words, the bounded \(2^{-v}\) absorbed. Prepending \(v\) retains distinctness, yields offset \((1+3\beta')/2^v\le F_0\), and gives the full residue \(r\). Equation (83) with total \(s\) makes the outermost integer congruent to \(u\bmod3\). Congruence modulo \(3^n\) makes each intermediate inverse integral by successive division. Starting at positive odd \(z\), each integral \(I_e(z)\) is positive odd and each internal state allowing another inverse step is a unit. This proves F with an onset independent of \(z\), even for the growing \(t\) used here. The argument is a source-qualified consequence of (76), whose analytic proof is external.

The glued floor in round 10 extends (1) to some absolute \(c>0\) and all admissible \(e\le cb\). Its offset bound is \(2F_0\), so the upper numerator in (1) becomes \(1+6F_0\). For \(N\le i<2N\) the normalized mass is at least \(c_a2^{-e}\exp(-C_a e^2/b)/\sqrt b\). In fact its prefix excess \(d=2i-A_i+2-e\) satisfies \(d^2/b=O_a(1+e^2/b)\), and choosing \(c\) sufficiently small ensures \(|d|\) is within the linear range of that floor. Its block-by-block proof uses F at each actual terminal with the absolute onset just checked. [Round 10, Lemma 1](../../round10/rows/README.md)

The same central source-shell theorem holds in **every** prescribed ternary unit class \(u\bmod 3^k\), with lower bound \(c_a 3^{-k}2^{-e}/\sqrt b\), for \(1\le k\le\delta_*(n-1)\). Indeed use \(r=t-3^n2^{-s}u\bmod3^{n+k}\); its shifted \(r'\) is specified at depth \((n-1)+k\), so (76) at extra depth \(K=k\) loses at most a fixed factor times \(3^{-k}\). The absolute onset is uniform in \(u,k,t\) throughout this range. This assertion is for the central prefix used in Theorem 1. [Weaver–Wren, (76), (82)–(83)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/48)

**Finite obstruction to extending the ternary depth.** At fixed \(n,s\), any assertion that *every* unit terminal \(z\) has even one length-\(n\), clock-\(s\) word with outer source in *one fixed* unit class \(u\bmod3^k\) requires
\[
                  2\cdot3^{\,n+k-1}\le {s-1\choose n-1}.                 \tag{C}
\]
Vary the **positive odd** terminal through representatives of every unit class modulo \(3^{n+k}\). The required offset residue \(z-3^n2^{-s}u\) runs through the same number of classes, since \(n\ge1\). Each word of that fixed clock has only one offset residue, and there are just \({s-1\choose n-1}\) positive compositions. For \(s=2n\), Stirling gives \(k\le n\log_3(4/3)-\tfrac12\log_3 n+O(1)\). Even without (C), at one terminal all sources with \(0\le\beta\le F_0\) lie in an absolute interval of length \(F_0\,2^s/3^n\), which is also exactly the length of (1) in its original \(x\) units. At the balanced prefix clock, (C) makes the number of residue classes smaller by order \(\sqrt n\) than this interval's length, even at the highest globally possible depth. The primary source's \(\delta_*\le1/1000\) is smaller still. Therefore increasing a uniform all-terminal renewal depth cannot by itself force a vacant ternary source class by the interval-length pigeonhole at \(s=2n\). A stronger conclusion at a selected terminal could use additional arithmetic particular to that terminal.

The interval center \(B_i\) depends on \(a,i,b\). Its logarithmic phase is exactly \(\log_2 a+(i-b)\log_2 3\bmod1\). The normalized mass per unit relative width in (1) is of order at least \(1/\sqrt b\) on these supplied intervals. A bound that is little-oh of this scale, uniformly over the allowed source intervals at the relevant actual endpoints, would contradict escape. The absolute intervals still contain many candidate integers. The depth range in the theorem cannot place them at sub-integer spacing.

**A pointwise singular moment.** Write \(\mu_{b,i}\) for mass \(1/(q_iG_b)\) at each counted original integer in the high-carry balanced row at \(q_i\). For each individual \(N\le i<2N\), not only after averaging the terminal,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{x:\;0<r_x\le C_a2^{-\sqrt b/8}\\ r_x=1-x/B_i}}
                   \frac{\mu_{b,i}(\{x\})}{r_x}\ \ge\ c_a .                \tag{2a}
\]
Indeed choose one admissible residue for \(e\bmod6\) and take a fixed spacing \(m\) divisible by six with \(2^m>1+3F_0\). There are \(\gg\sqrt b/m\) such exponents at spacing \(m\) between \(\sqrt b/8\) and \(\sqrt b/4\). Their intervals (1) are pairwise disjoint. On each one the reciprocal weight is at least \(a2^e/(1+3F_0)\), so its contribution is \(\gg_a b^{-1/2}\). Summing proves (2a) at the actual endpoint. With the source restricted to any prescribed unit class modulo \(3^k\), the right side becomes \(c_a3^{-k}\) for every \(k\) no larger than \(\delta_*(n-1)\); the same disjoint layers prove this. The finite source density and the singular weight use the same ordinary \(B_i\). A uniform bounded-density estimate for a smoothed Haar trace does not address this shrinking one-sided moment.

## 2. Uniform angular counts on one simple path

We include a translated sparsity proof to make explicit the input needed after cutting off small terminals. Set \(\eta=39/40\). For every finite injective positive shortcut path, the number of its visited states in a physical interval of length \(X\ge1\) is \(O(X^\eta)\), uniformly in its initial location, source, and duration.

Indeed take \(k=\lceil\log_2(X+1)\rceil\). At most \(k\) final observation times lack a \(k\)-step image. For the others fix the number \(p\) of odd departures. The ordinary affine formula puts the common-clock image of a starting state \(y\) between \(3^p 2^{-k}y\) and \(3^p(2^{-k}y+1/3)\). Thus all such images for one \(p\) have diameter at most \(3^p(2^{-k}X+1/3)\). Different observation times have distinct images. Summing for \(p\le3k/5\) costs \(O(3^{3k/5})\). For \(p>3k/5\), the original interval has at most one integer per residue modulo \(2^k\), and each length-\(k\) parity word specifies exactly one such residue. The latter follows by induction: changing the source by \(2^k\) preserves the first \(k\) parities and changes its \(k\)-step image by the odd number \(3^p\). The high tail costs at most \((5/(2^{2/5}3^{3/5}))^k\), by multiplying the binomial sum by \((3/2)^{p-3k/5}\). Both exponents in base two are below \(39/40\), as follows from \(3^{24}<2^{39}\) and \(5^{40}<2^{55}3^{24}\). The source-independent \(O\)-constant covers bounded \(X\). Partial summation at the origin now gives \(\sum_j1/q_j\ll 1\) and \(\sum_{q_j\ge M}1/q_j\ll M^{\eta-1}\) for distinct odd inputs on a simple path. Expanding a finite odd-accelerated segment with distinct odd vertices through its last odd vertex preserves injectivity. A repeated even state must have an odd departure between its occurrences, since consecutive even steps strictly decrease. The two occurrences therefore have distinct next odd landing times with equal values, contradicting the hypothesis. Garcia and Tal proved vanishing upper Banach density, and later work obtained sharper translated estimates. [Garcia–Tal, Acta Arithmetica 90 (1999)](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa90/aa9033.pdf) · [Weaver, paper 31, §8 and cited interval bound](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/31)

Write \(\theta=\log_2 3\), \(v_j=\log_2 D_j\) and \(\phi_j=\log_2 q_j\bmod1\). Then
\[
 \phi_j=\log_2 q_0+j\theta+v_j\bmod1,\qquad
 0\le v_{j+1}-v_j\le (3q_j\log2)^{-1}.
\]
The translated estimate gives an absolute bound \(V_*\) on the total variation on each injective segment. This also supplies \(D_*=2^{V_*}\) in §1. For \(d\ge1\) fixed, any window of \(n\) observations at odd indices \(u+dj\), any arc \(I\) of length \(\delta\) on the circle, and any reduced \(p/Q\) with \(|d\theta-p/Q|\le Q^{-2}\), one has
\[
 \left|\#\{0\le j<n:\phi_{u+dj}\in I\}-n\delta\right|
       \le C\{n/Q+(1+V_*)Q\}.                                             \tag{2}
\]
Each complete block of \(Q\) observations can be matched to a permuted grid translated by its own initial physical phase. Every matched pair is within \(1/Q+\Delta v\) on the circle, where \(\Delta v\) is the variation inside that block. A rotated grid counts any arc to within two points of its expected count. Expanding and contracting the arc by the pair distance on both ends bounds the block error by \(6+2Q\Delta v\), including a full or empty trimmed arc. Across disjoint blocks the variations sum to at most \(V_*\); a partial block contributes at most \(Q\). This proves (2) uniformly in the location of \(I\) and of the window.

For \(d=1\), \(n=Q^2\) along the convergent denominators gives normalized discrepancy \(O(n^{-1/2})\). At all \(n\), Wu and Wang's Theorem 1 gives \(|Q\theta-p|\gg Q^{-33/8}\), taking zero constant coefficient and enlarging constants for bounded denominators. Reduced Dirichlet approximation at scale \(R=\lfloor n^{33/41}\rfloor\) supplies \(n^{8/41}\ll Q\le R\) and \(|\theta-p/Q|\le Q^{-2}\). Thus the normalized discrepancy is \(O(n^{-8/41})\) for all \(n\). Even without a logarithmic-form theorem, \(|3^Q-2^p|\ge1\) for \(Q>0\) gives \(|Q\theta-p|\gg3^{-Q}\) when the left side is small; Dirichlet at \(\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor\) yields \(O(1/\log n)\). [Wu–Wang, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2014.03.007)

More generally a lower form bound \(|Q\theta-p|\gg_\kappa Q^{-\kappa}\) gives \(O_\kappa(n^{-1/(\kappa+1)})\) by Dirichlet at \(R=\lfloor n^{\kappa/(\kappa+1)}\rfloor\). The homogeneous two-logarithm case of Chudnovsky's November preprint, Corollary 1.2, reports this bound for each fixed \(\kappa>2\) because 2 and 3 are multiplicatively independent. With that external theorem, the all-depth width in (3) below improves to \(C_\gamma b^{-\gamma}\) for any fixed \(0<\gamma<1/6\); its constant is ineffective. We did not replay the external formalization. [Chudnovsky, Corollary 1.2 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.30820v1)

## 3. Moving angular intervals survive both trace cutoffs

For \(L=\lceil\sqrt b\rceil\), let \(E_b(I,\epsilon)\) denote PR207's sum of normalized balanced simple-row counts for \(L\le q\le2^L\) restricted to \(\log_2q\bmod1\in I\). Under F, escape at \(a\) implies, with absolute \(C\) and a positive \(c_a\),
\[
                    E_b(I,\epsilon_a)\ge c_a\,|I|                            \tag{3}
\]
for every sufficiently large \(b\) and every arc \(|I|\ge C b^{-4/41}\). Along \(b=100Q^4\) for convergent denominators of \(\theta\), the same conclusion holds for every arc \(|I|\ge C b^{-1/4}\). Without Wu–Wang the all-depth width in (3) can be replaced by \(C/\log b\).

Indeed, observe the \(N\) consecutive actual indices \(N\le i<2N\). The large cutoff holds automatically eventually since \(q_i+1\le(a+1)(3/2)^i\). By (2), at least \(3N|I|/4\) observations fall in the arc when the absolute width constant is sufficiently large. In each octave below \(L\), the part of the arc occupies at most two intervals, each of length \(O(|I|2^j)\) in octave \(j\). The translated bound shows that small terminals remove only
\[
                O((|I|L)^\eta+\log(2L))=o(N|I|).
\]
This little-oh is uniform over all allowed widths, since \(L\asymp N\) and \(N|I|\gg\sqrt N\) in both stated regimes. Merely discarding every small state by the origin-centered bound \(O(L^\eta)\) would lose the conclusion for these shrinking arcs. The remaining \(q_i\) are distinct. Theorem 1 with one fixed admissible \(e\) gives mass \(\gg_a b^{-1/2}\) per endpoint. Summation yields (3). Keeping any common admissible \(e\le\sqrt b/4\) instead proves the joint version: mass at least \(c_a2^{-e}|I|\) has its original sources in the respective intervals (1) at their individual endpoints. [PR207, integer trace at b56c4dc](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/b56c4dc2c28a184cf55638bb80c49a9d4662fc81/research/diffusive-central-row-closure-20261124/README.md)

A sufficient and necessary escape-exclusion criterion under F is now available at the indicated cofinal \(b=100Q^4\). For each fixed \(\epsilon>0\), it suffices that along some subsequence there are arcs \(I_b\) of width at least \(C b^{-1/4}\) with \(E_b(I_b,\epsilon)/|I_b|\to0\). Necessity under absence of escape follows because a contributing simple row has an odd input \(g\le M(\epsilon)\), by the reciprocal tail bound and \(\beta/q=1-1/D\) (take \(0<\epsilon<1\)). If all such gates are eventually periodic, their finite forward orbits make the moving lower terminal cutoff impossible for all large \(b\). Conversely an escape contains a positive unit \(a\), to which (3) applies at its fixed \(\epsilon_a\). Widely distributed allowed terminal phases and the source intervals in (1) are compatible with the present upper estimates. Existence of a deficient arc for each fixed threshold remains open, as does exclusion of a nontrivial positive cycle.
