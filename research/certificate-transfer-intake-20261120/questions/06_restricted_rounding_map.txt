TWO ALLOWED REMAINDERS IN AN INTEGER ROUNDING MAP

Set

    M=274877906944,
    K=282429536481,
    c=5285712575.

QUESTION

Can there exist an infinite sequence of positive integers b_0,b_1,... and digits epsilon_n in {0,1} satisfying

    M*b_{n+1}=K*b_n+c+epsilon_n  for every n>=0?              (1)

Prove nonexistence, or construct an exact positive integer source with a verified infinite sequence.

Because 0<c<c+1<M, the same question asks whether every positive integer orbit of

    b -> ceil(K*b/M)

eventually has a remainder

    d(b)=M*ceil(K*b/M)-K*b

outside {c,c+1}.

KNOWN STRUCTURE

Every finite prescribed binary word epsilon_0,...,epsilon_{h-1} has positive integer realizations. The identity

    M^h*b_h = K^h*b_0
              + sum_{j=0}^{h-1}K^{h-1-j}M^j(c+epsilon_j)

selects one residue class for b_0 modulo M^h, because gcd(K,M)=1. It also forces integrality of the intermediate values. Positive representatives give positive intermediate states.

No positive integer source can support an eventually periodic infinite digit schedule. For a block of length h, iteration has the form

    b -> (K^h*b+B)/M^h,  B>0.

Repeating it indefinitely would force the nonzero positive integer (K^h-M^h)b+B to be divisible by arbitrarily high powers of M. The same argument applies after a finite initial prefix.

The open case is therefore one fixed positive integer source and an aperiodic infinite digit sequence. Positive realizations for all finite lengths may have different sources.

A NECESSARY REAL CONDITION

Write D=K-M=7551629537 and lambda=K/M. An infinite solution would give a real beta>0 with

    beta*lambda^n-b_n in [c/D,(c+1)/D]  for every n>=0.

The interval has width 1/D and lies in (0,1). This is a necessary condition; its sufficiency has not been supplied.

Proposed proofs may use simultaneous real and modular information, but convergence of one series in different completions does not identify its limits. Any appeal to equidistribution of all integer rounding-map orbits needs a proved theorem with the exact hypotheses.

WORK STANDARD

Treat the requested assertion as an open research target, not as an established theorem. A proof, an exact counterexample, or a precisely scoped new partial result is useful. State every additional hypothesis. Keep finite computations separate from claims for all parameters. If a proposed argument fails, identify its first unsupported implication and preserve any exact obstruction you find.
