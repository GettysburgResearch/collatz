FULL DIVISIBILITY OF AN EXPONENTIAL WORD SUM

Let q>=1, and let a_1,...,a_q be arbitrary positive integers. Put

    A_0=0,
    A_j=a_1+...+a_j  (1<=j<=q),
    C=sum_{j=0}^{q-1} 3^{q-1-j}2^{A_j},
    D=2^{A_q}-3^q.

Assume D>0.

QUESTION

Prove or refute the implication

    D divides C  ==>  a_1=a_2=...=a_q=2.                    (1)

The word need not be primitive. The case in the conclusion is admissible: if every a_i=2, then C=D.

A proof must allow arbitrary q and arbitrary positive entries. An exact counterexample consists of a full word together with the positive integer quotient C/D.

If the full statement resists proof, try to establish it for a genuinely new infinite class of words, with every restriction explicit.

CAUTION ABOUT CANCELLATION

The required condition is divisibility by the complete D. Excluding some divisors, showing that the greatest prime factor of D grows, or proving a typical estimate on gcd(C,D) does not settle (1).

Word repetition illustrates why raw denominator growth is insufficient. If a word has total exponent A and length q, repeating it h times multiplies both C and D by

    sum_{j=0}^{h-1} (2^A)^(h-1-j) (3^q)^j,

so the quotient C/D remains unchanged.

WORK STANDARD

Treat the requested assertion as an open research target, not as an established theorem. A proof, an exact counterexample, or a precisely scoped new partial result is useful. State every additional hypothesis. Keep finite computations separate from claims for all parameters. If a proposed argument fails, identify its first unsupported implication and preserve any exact obstruction you find.
