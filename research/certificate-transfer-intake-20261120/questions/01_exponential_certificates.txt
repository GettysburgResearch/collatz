EXPONENTIAL CERTIFICATES: A CLOSURE QUESTION

Let E be the set of positive integers x admitting an integer r >= 0 and an increasing integer tuple

    0 = A_0 < A_1 < ... < A_r

such that

    3^r x + sum_{j=0}^{r-1} 3^{r-1-j} 2^{A_j} = 2^{A_r}.       (1)

For r=0, the sum is empty and A_0=0, so this case represents x=1. A tuple satisfying (1) is a certificate for membership in E.

QUESTION

Prove or disprove:

    x in E and x mod 512 in {129,257,385}
        implies (x+1)/2 in E.                                (2)

Every variable here ranges over ordinary integers. The conclusion asks for the existence of a finite certificate of the same form; its length and exponents may differ from those in the supplied certificate for x.

A constructive approach may consume the whole witnessing tuple for x. If you use induction, identify a well-founded order that remains valid when auxiliary carries or exponent indices grow. No uniform bound on the output length in terms of r is assumed.

A CONCRETE FIRST LAYER

For every j>=1, the integer

    x_j = (2^(54j+8)-175)/81

has a certificate with

    r=4, (A_0,A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4) = (0,2,4,6,54j+8).

It belongs to the residue classes in (2). Thus a necessary special case of (2) is to construct a certificate for

    (x_j+1)/2 = (2^(54j+7)-47)/81

uniformly in j. This family can be used to stress-test a proposed general certificate transformation.

WORK STANDARD

Treat the requested assertion as an open research target, not as an established theorem. A proof, an exact counterexample, or a precisely scoped new partial result is useful. State every additional hypothesis. Keep finite computations separate from claims for all parameters. If a proposed argument fails, identify its first unsupported implication and preserve any exact obstruction you find.
