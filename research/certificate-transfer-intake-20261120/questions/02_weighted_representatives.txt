A POSITIVE SUM OVER AFFINE CONGRUENCE WORDS

For an integer q>=1 and a word

    w=(a_1,...,a_q) in {1,2,3,...}^q,

define

    A_0=0,
    A_j=a_1+...+a_j  (1<=j<=q),
    C_w=sum_{j=0}^{q-1} 3^{q-1-j} 2^{A_j},
    J_w=3^q / 2^{A_q}.

Let r_w be the unique odd integer in

    1 <= r_w < 2*3^q

such that

    2^{A_q} r_w = C_w  (mod 3^q).

Such a representative exists and is unique because 2 is invertible modulo 3^q and each residue modulo the odd number 3^q has exactly one odd representative in this interval.

Define

    P_q = sum_{w in {1,2,3,...}^q} J_w^4 / r_w^2.             (1)

This is an infinite nonnegative sum at each fixed q, and it is finite:

    P_q <= sum_w J_w^4 = (27/5)^q.

QUESTION

Does there exist an absolute finite constant K such that

    P_q <= K (q+1)^(21/10)  for every q>=1?                   (2)

Prove or disprove this assertion. The stronger estimate O((q+1)^2) is also useful. A still weaker alternative target is

    P_q / (q+1)^(40/19) -> 0  as q -> infinity.               (3)

Retain the actual dependence between a word's weight J_w and its representative r_w.

EXACT STRUCTURE AVAILABLE

The integer

    b_w = (2^{A_q} r_w - C_w)/3^q

is positive and odd, with 1<=b_w<2^{A_q+1}. Consequently

    r_w = J_w b_w + C_w/2^{A_q} >= J_w + C_w/2^{A_q}.

The total word weight is exponentially large; proving (2) therefore requires control of where that weight is placed among the exact small representatives.

An exact finite recurrence is available. Set H_0(t)=1 for positive odd t. For q>=1 put L_q=2*3^{q-1} and define

    H_q(t) = 81/(1-2^(-4L_q))
             * sum_{1<=a<=L_q, 2^a t = 1 (mod 3)}
                 2^(-4a) H_{q-1}((2^a t-1)/3).              (4)

In (4), evaluate H_{q-1} on the unique positive odd representative modulo 3^{q-1}. Each H_q is periodic on odd t with period 2*3^q. Then

    P_q = sum_{1<=t<2*3^q, t odd} H_q(t)/t^2,

    sum_{1<=t<2*3^q, t odd} H_q(t) = (27/5)^q.

The recurrence includes all positive exponents by an exact geometric summation. A truncated exponent menu defines a different approximation.

A useful equivalent expression, with

    F_q(y)=sum_{1<=t<=y, t odd} H_q(t),

is

    P_q = (1/4)(3/5)^q + 2*integral_1^{2*3^q} F_q(y)/y^3 dy.

One sufficient intermediate inequality for the stronger quadratic target is F_q(y)<=B(q+1)y^2, uniformly in q and y, for an absolute B. That inequality is itself unproved.

PITFALL

Replacing r_w by independent uniform odd representatives changes the weighted sum and produces exponential growth on the scale (9/5)^q. Even a uniform model retaining the elementary cutoff r_w>=J_w+C_w/2^{A_q} has exponential growth on the scale (9/7)^q. The unresolved issue is specific arithmetic dependence, rather than an independent-residue model.

WORK STANDARD

Treat the requested assertion as an open research target, not as an established theorem. A proof, an exact counterexample, or a precisely scoped new partial result is useful. State every additional hypothesis. Keep finite computations separate from claims for all parameters. If a proposed argument fails, identify its first unsupported implication and preserve any exact obstruction you find.
