# Uniform homogeneous run escape: independent modular certificates

**PROPOSED, with complete finite modular verification.** This supplements the
[binary-run core theorem](arithmetic.md). It proves uniform bounds for two
homogeneous arithmetic sequences. The bounds do not prove termination of an
arbitrary Collatz orbit. The proof here and the scanner were reconstructed
separately from the adaptive-core implementation.

Let `runs(n)` count maximal nonempty blocks of ones in the canonical binary
expansion of a nonnegative integer, with `runs(0)=0`. For positive odd `u`,
`delta` equal to 0 or 1, and integer `R>=1`, define

\[
h_R(u,\delta)=\min\{j\ge0:\operatorname{runs}(3^j u-\delta)>R\}.
\]

## M1. A complete finite certificate implies a uniform ordinary bound

Fix `m>=2R+3`. On odd residues modulo `2^m`, iterate
`x -> 3x mod 2^m`. Mark a residue bad when `runs(x-delta)>R`, using its least
positive odd representative. Let `H` be the greatest length of a cyclic
string of consecutive nonbad residues, over both multiplicative orbits.
Then

\[
\boxed{h_R(u,\delta)\le H\qquad\text{for every positive odd integer }u.}
\]

**Proof.** For `m>=3`, multiplication by 3 has exactly two orbits on the odd
residues, represented by 1 and 5; each has length `2^(m-2)`. Indeed
`v_2(3^(2^k)-1)=k+2` for `k>=1`, by squaring and induction, so the order is
exactly `2^(m-2)`. The subgroup generated by 3 consists of all residues
congruent to 1 or 3 modulo 8. Multiplication by 5 gives its complement.

Each orbit contains a bad residue. Explicit representatives are

\[
a=3+\sum_{i=2}^{R+1}4^i\equiv3\pmod8,
\qquad b=1+\sum_{i=1}^{R+1}4^i\equiv5\pmod8.
\]

Both lie below `2^(2R+3)`, and each of `a,a-1,b,b-1` has more than `R`
one-runs. Thus the cyclic maximum is finite. The low `m` bits of
`3^j u-delta` equal `x_j-delta`, since `x_j` is odd and at least one.
Deleting higher bits cannot increase the number of one-runs. At the first
bad modular residue, therefore, the ordinary integer also has more than
`R` runs. Every starting residue reaches such a residue within `H` steps.
This proves the assertion. The case `u=1,delta=1,j=0` gives zero and is
included. QED.

Without enumeration this proves the analytical bound
`h_R(u,delta)<=2^(2R+1)-1`, because each of the two orbits modulo `2^(2R+3)`
has at least one bad element. A complete scan often gives much smaller
constants.

The [independent scanner](../../experiments/fixed_integer_run_hitting.py)
traverses both cycles, starting each traversal at a bad state so a run
crossing the representative is counted correctly. It records the maximum,
a maximizing residue and its complete modular trace, a digest of every
good/bad label, and both cycle lengths. It uses exact integers and no
third-party packages.

These are **exact maxima for the stated finite modular tests**, and upper
bounds for ordinary hitting times. Optimality among ordinary integers is
not asserted: high bits can cause an earlier ordinary exit. The smaller
values at greater precision illustrate this distinction.

The complete extended report records 73,400,320 inspected states, counting
repeated scans for different caps and shifts. This is not a count of
distinct ordinary trajectories. The original modulus-`2^20` maxima were
also independently obtained in the arithmetic-core investigation; the
scanner here reproduced them before checking the larger precision.

## M3. Consequence for actual odd Collatz trajectories

For the odd map `U(n)=(3n+1)/2^v_2(3n+1)`, the core theorem proves that an
odd source outside its adaptive `R`-run core exceeds the cap within
`max_delta sup_u h_R(u,delta)` odd events. The modular certificate therefore
gives outside-core clocks 3, 6, 7, 10, 13, 21, 117, and 978 for caps 1
through 8, respectively. These are ordinary, source-independent bounds.

For cap 5, the separate complete core replay found maximum clock 68.
Combining that result with the outside-core bound 13 proves that **every
positive odd source with at most five binary one-runs reaches 1 or exceeds
five runs within 68 odd events**. It does not prove that a source leaving
the cap subsequently reaches 1.

The complete cap-6 core has 19,371,072 sources. Its maximum replay clock is
107, and its entire capped basin of 1 consists of exactly 157,728 odd
sources. The outside-core bound 21 therefore gives the analogous universal
**107-odd-event alternative for six runs**. In particular the finite replay
plus the core theorem excludes every nontrivial positive cycle whose odd states all
have at most six one-runs. It does not exclude a cycle with a larger run
count. The source 74475 attains the 107-event core clock.

The core proof also gives an effective bound on the binary length of a
trajectory which remains under a run cap for a full local window. With
separate uniform bounds `H_0,H_1` and `H=max(H_0,H_1)`, a coarse length bound is

\[
B_R=R\lfloor H_1\log_2 3\rfloor+(R-1)(2H_0-1).
\]

The logarithm here is computed exactly as `(3**H_1).bit_length()-1`. An odd
source with `runs(n)<=R` and binary length greater than `B_R` must exit
within `H` odd events. This does not bound run counts on unrestricted
orbits.

## Replay and limits

Run from the repository root:

```bash
python -X utf8 -B experiments/fixed_integer_run_hitting.py --extended --compare reports/2026-11-20-fixed-integer-descent/modular-run-hitting.json
```

The comparison reconstructs the complete finite report and rejects three
corruptions of its maxima, witnesses, and digests. The arithmetic core has
its own separate replay. These computations support the finite constants;
the transfer to arbitrary ordinary `u` is the proof of M1 above. Neither
finite verification establishes a global binary-run cap or full Collatz.
