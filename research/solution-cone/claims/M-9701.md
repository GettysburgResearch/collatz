### 1.1 Berg & Meinardus 1994 — PRIMARY NOT RETRIEVED; statements VERIFIED VIA TWO INDEPENDENT SECONDARY SOURCES

**Citation.** Lothar Berg and Günter Meinardus, *Functional equations connected with the Collatz problem*, Results in Mathematics **25** (1994), 1–12. (MR 95d:11025.)

**Retrieval attempts (primary).** `link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF03323136` → 303 redirect to `idp.springer.com` auth wall → second redirect loop (`error=cookies_not_supported`). Full text is paywalled; not read. Title/venue/pages confirmed from the Springer landing metadata via search.

**Verified content (secondary source A — read verbatim).** J. C. Lagarias, *The 3x+1 problem: an annotated bibliography (1963–1999)*, arXiv:math/0309224; I downloaded the arXiv **TeX source** (`v104-1jan11.tex`) and quote the Berg–Meinardus annotations verbatim. Lagarias's entry states (notation: `T` the shortcut map, `f_m(z) = Σ_{n≥0} T^{(m)}(n) z^n`):

- "The 3x+1 Conjecture is stated as Conjecture 1. The paper proves its equivalence to each of Conjectures 2 and 3."
- Each `f_m(z)` is rational: `f_m(z) = p_m(z)/(1−z^{2^m})^2`, `p_m` an integer polynomial of degree `2^{m+1}−1`.
- **The recursion** `f_{m+1}(z^3) = f_m(z^6) + (1/(3z)) Σ_{j=0}^{2} ω^j f_m(ω^j z^2)`, `ω = exp(2πi/3)`.
- A bivariate `F(z,w) = Σ_{m,n} T^{(m)}(n) z^n w^m` (analytic for `|z|<1`, `|w|<2/3`) satisfies `F(z^3,w) = z^3/(1−z^3)^2 + wF(z^6,w) + (w/(3z)) Σ_j ω^j F(ω^j z^2, w)`, and this equation determines `F` uniquely near `(0,0)`.
- **Conjecture 3 (the dimension-2 equivalence):** the only solutions `h(z)`, analytic in `|z|<1`, of
  `h(z^3) = h(z^6) + (1/(3z)) Σ_{j=0}^{2} ω^j h(ω^j z^2)`
  are `h(z) = c_0 + c_1·z/(1−z)`, `c_0, c_1 ∈ C`. Per the annotation, the 1994 paper **proves** Collatz ⟺ Conjecture 3 (and ⟺ Conjecture 2: each `g_n(w) = Σ_m T^{(m)}(n) w^m` rational of the form `q_n(w)/(1−w^2)`).

**Verified content (secondary source B — primary reproduction, read in full).** Opfer's 2011 preprint (§1.4 below, full PDF retrieved and text-extracted) reproduces the system it attributes to Berg–Meinardus 1994/95 as operators on the space `H` of holomorphic functions on the open unit disk `D` (his eqs. (2.3)–(2.8)): `T_1[h](z) := h(z^3)`, `H[h](z) := (1/(3z))[h(z^2) + λh(λz^2) + λ^2 h(λ^2 z^2)]`, `λ := exp(2πi/3)` (removable singularity at 0 noted), and the pair `U[h](z) := h(z) + h(−z) − 2h(z^2)`, `V[h](z) := 2H[h](z) − T_1[h](z) + T_1[h](−z)`; solution set `K := {h ∈ H : U[h]=0, V[h]=0}` contains a "simple two dimensional space, denoted by ∆2, and if one could show that ∆2 is the only solution, the Collatz conjecture would be true" (quoted from Opfer's abstract). This is consistent with, and independently confirms, Lagarias's annotation.

**Note on the two known solutions.** `h = 1` has coefficient sequence `1_{{0}}` (indicator of the 0-component) and `z/(1−z) = Σ_{n≥1} z^n` is the indicator of the positive integers, i.e. of the component of 1 *if* the conjecture holds — exactly the packet's two fixed rays.

### 1.2 Berg & Meinardus 1995 — PRIMARY NOT RETRIEVED; VERIFIED VIA LAGARIAS ANNOTATION (verbatim)

**Citation.** Lothar Berg and Günter Meinardus, *The 3n+1 Collatz problem and functional equations*, Rostock. Math. Kolloq. **48** (1995), 11–18. (MR 97e:11034.)

**Retrieval attempts (primary).** No open copy found in the sweep; the Rostock kolloquium archive was not probed further after the Lagarias source download succeeded. Not read in primary.

**Verified content (from the Lagarias TeX annotation, verbatim).** The 1995 paper (i) proves the only **entire** solutions of the `h(z^3)` equation are constants; (ii) splits the equation into the **equivalent system of two functional equations**
`h(z) + h(−z) = 2h(z^2)` and `h(z^3) − h(−z^3) = (2/(3z)) Σ_{j=0}^{2} ω^j h(ω^j z^2)`,
whose disk-analytic solutions can be studied separately; (iii) via `Φ(z) = ∫_0^z h`, `z = e^{2πiξ/3}`, transforms the second equation to `φ(3ξ) + φ(3ξ + 3/2) = φ(2ξ) + φ(2ξ+1) + φ(2ξ+2)` with `φ(ξ) = φ(ξ+3)` on the upper half-plane; (iv) adds formulas for the `f_m`. This system is the one Opfer's `U`, `V` implement (§1.1B), which cross-confirms it.

### 1.3 arXiv:2111.07882 (Siegel) — RETRIEVED (abstract page); paper WITHDRAWN

**Record.** M. C. Siegel, *Functional Equations Associated to Collatz-Type Maps on Integer Rings of Algebraic Number Fields*, arXiv:2111.07882. **Withdrawn by the author (2022-04-16)** citing "an error in section three" concerning the structure of quotients of rings of algebraic integers modulo an ideal. The abstract states it "generalizes that method" — the Meinardus–Berg 1995 single-complex-variable unit-disk functional-equation reformulation — to Collatz-type maps on rings of integers of arbitrary number fields. **No theorem from this paper may be cited or relied on.** Its value here is (i) evidence the BM functional-equation formalism has active continuations, and (ii) a second cautionary withdrawal in exactly this formalism (with Opfer, §1.4).

### 1.4 Opfer 2011 and its refutation — PRIMARY RETRIEVED IN FULL

**Record.** Gerhard Opfer, *An analytic approach to the Collatz 3n+1 Problem*, Hamburger Beiträge zur Angewandten Mathematik, Nr. **2011-09**, May 2011. Full PDF retrieved from `preprint.math.uni-hamburg.de` and text-extracted. The claimed proof: reformulate the BM95 pair as the operators `U`, `V` above, use the known general solutions `K_U`, `K_V` of each single equation, compute `U[h]` for `h ∈ K_V`, and conclude `K = ∆2` — "which implies that the Collatz conjecture is true" (abstract). **The preprint carries the author's own withdrawal note, quoted verbatim:** "Author's note: The reasoning on p. 11, that 'The set of all vertices (2n, l) in all levels will contain all even numbers 2n ≥ 6 exactly once.' has turned out to be incomplete. Thus, the statement 'that the Collatz conjecture is true' has to be withdrawn, at least temporarily. June 17, 2011." Community accounts (mixedmath.wordpress.com post of 2011-06-06, retrieved) locate the flaw in the "annihilation argument" on p. 11 — an unproved surjectivity/uniqueness claim about reverse-orbit coverage smuggled in as combinatorial bookkeeping; no repaired version is known to have appeared. (bit-player.org account: HTTP 503, UNVERIFIED.) **Methodological import for this packet:** the fatal step was not the functional equations but an unverified *coverage/faithfulness* claim about which coefficient patterns the equation system actually forces — precisely the class of claim the packet isolates in L-9708 and the truncation-artifact discipline (X-9701).

### 1.5 arXiv:2106.11859 (Neklyudov) — RETRIEVED (abstract page + full text via ar5iv render)

**Record.** Mikhail Neklyudov, *Functional analysis approach to the Collatz conjecture*, arXiv:2106.11859 (v9, 2022). A Springer page titled "Functional Analysis Approach to the Collatz Conjecture" in *Results in Mathematics* (article s00025-024-02167-7, 2024) appeared in search; I did not open the published version, so the published text may differ from v9. Content read (ar5iv render; theorem numbers per that render):

- **Operator (pushforward).** `𝒯 z^n = z^{T(n)}` (shortcut `T`), realized on Bergman/Hardy spaces as `𝒯f(z) = (Sf)(√z) + √z (Af)(z^{3/2})` with `S`, `A` the even/odd parts — i.e. on coefficients `(𝒯a)_m = Σ_{n: T(n)=m} a_n`, the **transfer/pushforward** operator dual to the packet's pullback `F`.
- Lemma 1.1: a cycle `(n_1, …, n_k)` of `T` gives the fixed point `Σ_i z^{n_i}` of `𝒯`.
- Theorem 2.2: if `T` has no nontrivial cycles then `𝒯` is hypercyclic (on a Bergman quotient mod span{1, z, z²}).
- Theorem 2.3: every `λ` with `|λ| < √2` is an eigenvalue of `𝒯` of infinite multiplicity (Bergman setting).
- Lemma 2.4: a divergent trajectory `{T^k(m)}` gives a fixed point of `𝒯` of the form `f_m(1,z) + Σ_k z^{T^k(m)}`.
- Theorem 3.1: Lebesgue measure on the circle is invariant for `𝒯` on `L²(S¹)` — an invariant-measure/transfer-operator statement in the generating-function frame.
- Proposition 4.2: the **adjoint** `ℱ = 𝒯*` is "defined by the Berg–Meinardus formula", is injective with closed range, and expansive in `H²(D)`; the **abstract** states "the adjoint operator has no non-trivial fixed points in `H²(D)`". Since `𝒯*` on `H²` is the coefficient pullback, this is exactly: `Fix(F) ∩ H² = {0}` (nontrivial part).
- Theorem 4.4: the number of cycles of `T` is bounded above by the index of `Id − 𝒯` on `H²(D)`; Theorem 4.5: a resolvent formula `(I − wℱ)^{-1}`; Theorem 4.9: a functional equation for the characteristic function of the total stopping time.

## 3. M-9701.a — machine-checked identity (the one mathematical claim)

**Claim.** For any coefficient sequence `(a_n)` and the shortcut map `T` on `N_0`, the BM94 right-hand side, interpreted coefficientwise in exact `Z[ω]` arithmetic, satisfies `f(z^6) + (1/(3z)) Σ_{j=0}^2 ω^j f(ω^j z^2) = Σ_m a_{T(m)} z^{3m}` — i.e. the BM operator **is** the packet's pullback `F`, with `(Ff)(z^3)` on the left. Proof of the identity (elementary, complete): `f(z^6)` contributes `a_n` at exponent `6n = 3·(2n)`, matching `T(2n) = n`; in the filter term, `Σ_j ω^{j(n+1)} = 3` iff `n ≡ 2 (mod 3)` and `0` otherwise, so the term contributes `a_{3r+2}` at exponent `2(3r+2) − 1 = 3(2r+1)`, matching `T(2r+1) = 3r+2`; all fractional/non-multiple-of-3 exponents receive coefficient 0. Edge cases: `n = 0` gives `a_0` at exponent 0, matching `T(0) = 0`; the `{1,2}` cycle is `a_1 ↔ a_2` consistently (`T(1)=2` via the odd branch at `m=1`, `T(2)=1` via the even branch). The 3n−1 control `U` obeys the twisted identity `(F_U f)(z^3) = f(z^6) + (z/3) Σ_j ω^{2j} f(ω^j z^2)` (filter survives iff `n ≡ 1 (mod 3)`, matching the odd preimage `(2n+1)/3`), which pins down the sign/normalization conventions: the `1/(3z)` prefactor and `ω^j` twist are specific to `+1`. Both identities verified coefficientwise in exact integer `Z[ω]` arithmetic to degree 4000 (snippet below, 3334 fully-determined coefficients each, plus vanishing of all non-integral exponents). **Consequence:** the packet's `F` and the Berg–Meinardus equation have identical solution theory on any coefficientwise-faithful space, and T-9701 is the sequence-space form of BM94 Conjecture 3.
