## Statement

Let T: N_0 -> N_0 be the shortcut Collatz map (T(n)=n/2 for n even, T(n)=(3n+1)/2 for n odd), let F be the coefficient pullback operator ((Fa)_m = a_{T(m)}), let omega = exp(2*pi*i/3), and for a sequence a = (a_n)_{n>=0} of complex numbers let f(z) = sum_{n>=0} a_n z^n in C[[z]] and u(z) = f(z^3).

(1) [Unconditional bracket identity] For EVERY complex sequence a, the formal power series B(z) := f(z^2) + omega*f(omega*z^2) + omega^2*f(omega^2*z^2) equals 3*sum_{k>=0} a_{3k+2} z^{6k+4}; in particular every exponent occurring in B is an integer >= 4 congruent to 4 mod 6, so Phi(z) := B(z)/(3z) = sum_{k>=0} a_{3k+2} z^{6k+3} is a well-defined element of C[[z]] with only integer exponents (no fractional powers occur anywhere).

(2) [Main equivalence, formal] For EVERY complex sequence a (no boundedness assumed): Fa = a (i.e. a_m = a_{T(m)} for all m in N_0) if and only if the single-valued formal identity
  (STAR)  f(z^3) = f(z^6) + (1/(3z)) * [ f(z^2) + omega*f(omega*z^2) + omega^2*f(omega^2*z^2) ]
holds in C[[z]] (with the bracket term read as Phi(z) of part (1)). Equivalently, in terms of u: u(z) = u(z^2) + Phi(z). The equivalence is exact coefficientwise: the coefficient of z^{6m} in (STAR) is the relation a_{2m} = a_m (m >= 0), the coefficient of z^{6k+3} is the relation a_{2k+1} = a_{3k+2} (k >= 0), and all other coefficients are identically 0 = 0; conversely {a_{2m}=a_m for all m} together with {a_{2k+1}=a_{3k+2} for all k} is exactly Fa = a.

(3) [Analytic version and converse for bounded sequences] If sup_n |a_n| < infinity (more generally limsup |a_n|^{1/n} <= 1), then f is holomorphic on the open unit disk D, each term of (STAR) is holomorphic on D (the bracket term after removing the removable singularity at 0, where it vanishes to order 3), and: Fa = a if and only if (STAR) holds as an identity of holomorphic functions on D. In particular a bounded sequence satisfies the functional equation iff Fa = a; the derivation is fully reversible.

(4) [Relation to the unshortened 3n+1 map; partially literature-conditional] Let C(n) = n/2 (n even), 3n+1 (n odd, n >= 1), C(0)=0, and mu = exp(pi*i/6*2) = exp(pi*i/3). whose bracket equals 6*sum_{k>=0} a_{6k+4} z^{6k+4} unconditionally, and (STARSTAR) holds iff a_m = a_{C(m)} for all m; (c) hence (STAR) and (STARSTAR) are distinct identities with the same solution set, and (STARSTAR) collapses to (STAR) exactly by the even-branch substitution a_{6k+4} = a_{3k+2} (the generating-function image of halving the odd branch, T|odd = half o C|odd); (d) LITERATURE-CONDITIONAL: the equation quoted in the task as Berg–Meinardus's, h(z^3) = h(z^6) + (h(z^2) + lambda*h(lambda*z^2) + lambda^2*h(lambda^2*z^2))/(3z) with lambda = exp(2*pi*i/3), is syntactically identical to our shortcut equation (STAR) with h = f, lambda = omega; IF that quotation is faithful, the published equation is already in shortcut form (one halving of the odd branch already incorporated), and the original-map/shortcut difference is exactly (STARSTAR) vs (STAR).

(5) [3n-1 control] For the control map U (U(0)=0, U(n)=n/2 for n even, (3n-1)/2 for n odd >= 1): a_m = a_{U(m)} for all m iff
  (STAR-U)  f(z^3) = f(z^6) + (z/3) * [ f(z^2) + omega^2*f(omega*z^2) + omega*f(omega^2*z^2) ],
whose bracket equals 3*sum_{k>=0} a_{3k+1} z^{6k+2} unconditionally (surviving exponents 2 mod 6, hence the multiplier z/3 instead of 1/(3z): the sign of the +-1 in the odd branch flips the surviving residue class 2 -> 1 mod 3 and the exponent shift -1 -> +1).

(6) [Solution-cone corollary] For any a: Fa = a iff a is constant on each weak component of the functional graph G of T. ## Proof

# L-9706: The canonical single-valued functional equation for F-fixed sequences of the shortcut Collatz map

**Conventions.** $T(n)=n/2$ ($n$ even), $T(n)=(3n+1)/2$ ($n$ odd) on $\mathbb N_0$; $(Fa)_m=a_{T(m)}$; $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$; $f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n\in\mathbb C[[z]]$; $u(z)=f(z^3)$. All series manipulations are in the formal power series ring $\mathbb C[[z]]$; substitutions $z\mapsto cz^r$ with $c\in\mathbb C$, $r\ge1$ are well defined ring maps on $\mathbb C[[z]]$ (each coefficient of the image is a finite — here single-term — combination of the $a_n$). The rigorous proof below never uses a fractional power; the classical $z^{2/3}$ heuristic appears only in Remark 2.

---

## Lemma 1 (roots-of-unity filter; unconditional)

*For every complex sequence $a$,*
$$B(z):=f(z^2)+\omega f(\omega z^2)+\omega^2 f(\omega^2 z^2)\;=\;3\sum_{k\ge0}a_{3k+2}\,z^{6k+4}\quad\text{in }\mathbb C[[z]].$$
*In particular every exponent occurring in $B$ is an integer $\ge 4$ and $\equiv 4\pmod 6$, so*
$$\Phi(z):=\frac{B(z)}{3z}=\sum_{k\ge0}a_{3k+2}\,z^{6k+3}\in\mathbb C[[z]]$$
*is well defined (division by $z$ is exact, division by $3$ is exact, all exponents are integers $\equiv3\bmod 6$).*

**Proof.** For $j\in\{0,1,2\}$, $f(\omega^jz^2)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n\omega^{jn}z^{2n}$. Hence the coefficient of $z^{2n}$ in $B$ is
$$a_n\sum_{j=0}^{2}\omega^{j}\,\omega^{jn}=a_n\sum_{j=0}^{2}\bigl(\omega^{\,n+1}\bigr)^{j}.$$
If $3\mid n+1$, i.e. $n\equiv2\pmod3$, then $\omega^{n+1}=1$ and the sum is $3$. Otherwise $\zeta:=\omega^{n+1}$ is a primitive cube root of unity, $\zeta\ne1$, and $\sum_{j=0}^2\zeta^j=(\zeta^3-1)/(\zeta-1)=0$. Odd powers of $z$ do not occur (all exponents are $2n$). Thus $B(z)=3\sum_{n\equiv2\,(3)}a_nz^{2n}=3\sum_{k\ge0}a_{3k+2}z^{6k+4}$, and the exponents $6k+4$ are integers $\ge4$, $\equiv4\pmod6$. $\square$

This lemma **defines** the right-hand side of the main equation single-valuedly: the bracket, divided by $3z$, is an honest formal power series. This is the promised cancellation of fractional powers, established before — and independently of — any fixed-point hypothesis.

## Theorem 2 (main equivalence, formal; no growth hypothesis)

*For every complex sequence $a$, the following are equivalent:*

1. $Fa=a$, i.e. $a_m=a_{T(m)}$ for all $m\in\mathbb N_0$;
2. the single-valued formal identity
$$\boxed{\;f(z^3)=f(z^6)+\frac{1}{3z}\Bigl[f(z^2)+\omega f(\omega z^2)+\omega^2 f(\omega^2 z^2)\Bigr]\;}\tag{$\star$}$$
*holds in $\mathbb C[[z]]$, the bracket term being $\Phi(z)$ of Lemma 1. Equivalently, with $u(z)=f(z^3)$: $u(z)=u(z^2)+\Phi(z)$.*

**Proof.** First rewrite (i). Every $m\in\mathbb N_0$ is uniquely $m=2m'$ or $m=2k+1$. For $m=2m'$: $T(m)=m'$, so the constraint is $a_{2m'}=a_{m'}$ (for $m'=0$ this is $a_0=a_0$, vacuous, consistent with $T(0)=0$). For $m=2k+1$: $T(m)=(6k+4)/2=3k+2$, so the constraint is $a_{2k+1}=a_{3k+2}$ (for $k=0$: $a_1=a_2$; together with the even constraint $a_2=a_1$ at $m'=1$ these are the two — mutually consistent — relations produced by the $\{1,2\}$ cycle). Hence

$$\text{(i)}\iff\bigl\{a_{2m}=a_m\ \forall m\ge0\bigr\}\ \wedge\ \bigl\{a_{2k+1}=a_{3k+2}\ \forall k\ge0\bigr\}.\tag{R}$$

Now compute the coefficient of $z^N$ on each side of $(\star)$, using Lemma 1 for the bracket term. Left side: $f(z^3)=\sum_m a_mz^{3m}$ has coefficient $a_{N/3}$ if $3\mid N$, else $0$. Right side: $f(z^6)=\sum_m a_mz^{6m}$ contributes $a_{N/6}$ iff $6\mid N$; $\Phi(z)=\sum_k a_{3k+2}z^{6k+3}$ contributes $a_{3k+2}$ iff $N=6k+3$. The residues $0$ and $3$ mod $6$ are disjoint and exhaust the multiples of $3$. Case analysis on $N$:

- $N=6m$: coefficient identity is $a_{2m}=a_m$ (left: $N=3\cdot 2m$).
- $N=6k+3$: coefficient identity is $a_{2k+1}=a_{3k+2}$ (left: $N=3(2k+1)$).
- $3\nmid N$: both sides have coefficient $0$; the identity is $0=0$, always true.

Two formal series are equal iff all coefficients agree, so $(\star)$ holds iff the family $\{a_{2m}=a_m\}_{m\ge0}\cup\{a_{2k+1}=a_{3k+2}\}_{k\ge0}$ holds — which by (R) is exactly (i). Each relation in (R) appears for exactly one $N$ ($N=6m$, resp. $N=6k+3$) and every $N$ yields either one relation of (R) or a tautology, so the equivalence is a term-by-term bijection; the derivation is reversible with no information lost. The $u$-form is a transcription: $u(z)=f(z^3)$, $u(z^2)=f(z^6)$. $\square$

**Remark 1 (edge cases).** $N=0$ gives $a_0=a_0$ (the fixed point $0$ imposes nothing). The cycle $\{1,2\}$ contributes the relations $a_2=a_1$ (at $N=6$) and $a_1=a_2$ (at $N=3$) — redundant but consistent; no coefficient identity is ever contradictory in itself, so the solution set is a nonzero linear subspace (it contains all constants: $f=c/(1-z)$ always solves $(\star)$, as does the indicator of $\{0\}$, $f=1$: check $1=1+\frac{1}{3z}(1+\omega+\omega^2)=1+0$).

**Remark 2 (the classical multivalued route, motivation only).** Splitting $f(z)=\sum a_{2k}z^{2k}+\sum a_{2k+1}z^{2k+1}$ and imposing (R) gives $f(z)=f(z^2)+\sum_k a_{3k+2}z^{2k+1}$. Formally writing $\sum_k a_{3k+2}z^{2k+1}=z^{-1/3}\cdot\frac13[f(z^{2/3})+\omega f(\omega z^{2/3})+\omega^2f(\omega^2z^{2/3})]$ uses the multivalued symbol $z^{2/3}$; by Lemma 1 (with $z^2$ replaced by the symbol $z^{2/3}$) the bracket has exponents $(6k+4)/3$, and $(6k+4)/3-1/3=2k+1\in\mathbb Z$ — all surviving exponents are integers, which is why the substitution $z\to z^3$ produces the single-valued $(\star)$. We record this only as motivation; the proof above is Lemma 1 + Theorem 2 and never leaves $\mathbb C[[z]]$.

## Theorem 3 (analytic version; converse for bounded sequences)

*Suppose $\sup_n|a_n|<\infty$ (more generally $\limsup_n|a_n|^{1/n}\le1$). Then $f$ is holomorphic on the open unit disk $\mathbb D$, and each of $z\mapsto f(z^3),\,f(z^6),\,f(\omega^jz^2)$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb D$; by Lemma 1, $B$ vanishes at $0$ to order $\ge4$, so $B(z)/(3z)$ extends holomorphically across $0$ (with a zero of order $\ge3$). With this reading:*
$$Fa=a\iff (\star)\ \text{holds for all }z\in\mathbb D .$$
*In particular, a bounded sequence satisfies the functional equation on $\mathbb D$ iff it is a fixed point of $F$.*

**Proof.** Radius of convergence of $f$ is $\ge1$; for $|z|<1$, also $|\omega^jz^2|,|z^3|,|z^6|<1$, so all compositions are holomorphic on $\mathbb D$, and their Taylor series at $0$ are obtained by (absolutely convergent, hence rearrangeable) term substitution — they coincide with the formal series used in Lemma 1 and Theorem 2. Likewise the Taylor series of $B(z)/(3z)$ at $0$ (after removing the removable singularity: $B$ is holomorphic with $B=O(z^4)$ near $0$ by Lemma 1, since $\sum|a_{3k+2}||z|^{6k+4}$ converges on $\mathbb D$) is $\Phi$. Two functions holomorphic on the connected open set $\mathbb D$ are equal on $\mathbb D$ iff their Taylor coefficients at $0$ all agree (identity theorem / uniqueness of power series). Hence the analytic identity on $\mathbb D$ is equivalent to the formal identity $(\star)$, which by Theorem 2 is equivalent to $Fa=a$. Both directions are pure coefficient extraction; the derivation is reversible. $\square$

## Proposition 4 (cross-check against the unshortened $3n+1$ map)

*Let $C(0)=0$, $C(n)=n/2$ ($n$ even), $C(n)=3n+1$ ($n$ odd), and $\mu=e^{\pi i/3}$ (primitive 6th root of unity, $\mu^2=\omega$, $\mu^3=-1$).*

**(a) Identical fixed sequences.** *For every complex sequence $a$: $\ a_m=a_{C(m)}\ \forall m\iff a_m=a_{T(m)}\ \forall m$.*

**Proof.** Even branches agree, and $0$ is fixed by both, so only odd $m\ge1$ needs checking. ($\Leftarrow$) Assume $T$-fixedness. For odd $m$: $3m+1=2\,T(m)$, so the even rule gives $a_{C(m)}=a_{3m+1}=a_{2T(m)}=a_{T(m)}=a_m$. ($\Rightarrow$) Assume $C$-fixedness. For odd $m$: $a_m=a_{C(m)}=a_{3m+1}$, and $3m+1$ is even with $C(3m+1)=(3m+1)/2=T(m)$, so $a_{3m+1}=a_{T(m)}$; hence $a_m=a_{T(m)}$. $\square$

**(b) The raw $C$-equation.** *For every complex sequence $a$,*
$$\sum_{j=0}^{5}\mu^{2j}f(\mu^jz)=6\sum_{k\ge0}a_{6k+4}z^{6k+4}\quad\text{(unconditionally)},$$
*and $a_m=a_{C(m)}\ \forall m$ iff*
$$f(z^3)=f(z^6)+\frac{1}{6z}\sum_{j=0}^{5}\mu^{2j}f(\mu^jz).\tag{$\star\star$}$$

**Proof.** Coefficient of $z^n$ in the 6-term bracket: $a_n\sum_{j=0}^5\mu^{2j}\mu^{jn}=a_n\sum_{j=0}^5(\mu^{\,n+2})^j$, which is $6$ if $6\mid n+2$ (i.e. $n\equiv4\bmod6$) and $0$ otherwise (geometric sum over a nontrivial 6th root of unity). So the bracket is $6\sum_k a_{6k+4}z^{6k+4}$; dividing by $6z$ gives $\sum_k a_{6k+4}z^{6k+3}\in\mathbb C[[z]]$ (integer exponents $\equiv3\bmod6$). Coefficient comparison in $(\star\star)$, exactly as in Theorem 2: $N=6m\Rightarrow a_{2m}=a_m$; $N=6k+3\Rightarrow a_{2k+1}=a_{6k+4}$; $3\nmid N\Rightarrow 0=0$. The relation set $\{a_{2m}=a_m\}\cup\{a_{2k+1}=a_{6k+4}\}$ is exactly $a_m=a_{C(m)}\ \forall m$ ($m=2m'$ even; $m=2k+1$ odd with $C(m)=6k+4$). $\square$

**(c) Exact relation between $(\star)$ and $(\star\star)$.** By (a) (or directly by (b)+Theorem 2) the two identities have the **same solution set**, yet they are **different identities**: their bracket terms are $\sum_k a_{3k+2}z^{6k+3}$ vs $\sum_k a_{6k+4}z^{6k+3}$, and (for a general sequence) these differ. Both equations contain the even-branch relations $\{a_{2m}=a_m\}$ as their $N\equiv0\ (6)$ coefficients; substituting the instance $a_{6k+4}=a_{3k+2}$ (the even rule at $m=3k+2$) into $(\star\star)$'s bracket turns $(\star\star)$ into $(\star)$ verbatim. This substitution is precisely the generating-function image of **halving the odd branch**: on odd inputs $T=\mathrm{half}\circ C$, i.e. $T(2k+1)=3k+2=\tfrac12(6k+4)=\tfrac12 C(2k+1)$.

**(d) Literature-conditional comparison.** The task quotes Berg–Meinardus's equation as $h(z^3)=h(z^6)+\bigl(h(z^2)+\lambda h(\lambda z^2)+\lambda^2h(\lambda^2z^2)\bigr)/(3z)$, $\lambda=e^{2\pi i/3}$. This is **syntactically identical** to our shortcut equation $(\star)$ with $h=f$, $\lambda=\omega$. Therefore, *conditional on that quotation faithfully rendering the published equation*, the published equation is already in shortcut form — one halving of the odd branch is already incorporated — and the difference the shortcut formalism makes relative to the raw $3n+1$ map is exactly the difference $(\star\star)\to(\star)$ described in (c); the shortcut derivation reproduces the quoted equation exactly rather than producing a variant. I have not consulted the original paper; any statement about what Berg–Meinardus actually printed (indexing conventions, domain of $h$, normalization at $n=0$) is literature-conditional.

## Proposition 5 ($3n{-}1$ control equation)

*Extend the control map by $U(0)=0$, $U(n)=n/2$ ($n$ even), $U(n)=(3n-1)/2$ ($n$ odd $\ge1$). Then for every complex sequence $a$,*
$$f(z^2)+\omega^2f(\omega z^2)+\omega f(\omega^2z^2)=3\sum_{k\ge0}a_{3k+1}z^{6k+2}\quad\text{(unconditionally)},$$
*and $a_m=a_{U(m)}\ \forall m$ iff*
$$f(z^3)=f(z^6)+\frac{z}{3}\Bigl[f(z^2)+\omega^2f(\omega z^2)+\omega f(\omega^2z^2)\Bigr].\tag{$\star_U$}$$

**Proof.** Filter: coefficient of $z^{2n}$ in the bracket is $a_n\sum_{j=0}^2\omega^{-j}\omega^{jn}=a_n\sum_j(\omega^{n-1})^j=3a_n[n\equiv1\ (3)]$; surviving exponents are $2(3k+1)=6k+2\equiv2\bmod6$, so multiplying by $z/3$ gives $\sum_ka_{3k+1}z^{6k+3}$ (integer exponents). Coefficients of $(\star_U)$: $N=6m\Rightarrow a_{2m}=a_m$; $N=6k+3\Rightarrow a_{2k+1}=a_{3k+1}$; else $0=0$. And $a_m=a_{U(m)}$ is exactly $\{a_{2m}=a_m\}\cup\{a_{2k+1}=a_{3k+1}\}$ since $U(2k+1)=(6k+2)/2=3k+1$. $\square$

Structural contrast worth recording: the sign of the $\pm1$ in the odd branch moves the surviving residue class ($3k+2$ vs $3k+1$, bracket exponents $4$ vs $2$ mod $6$) and flips the prefactor $\frac{1}{3z}\leftrightarrow\frac{z}{3}$. This makes $(\star_U)$ a genuine control: it is *not* the same equation, and the verification code checks each map against its own equation.

## Corollary 6 (solution structure; connection to the solution-cone program)

*(i) For any complex sequence $a$: $Fa=a$ iff $a$ is constant on each weak component of the functional graph $G$ of $T$.*

**Proof.** ($\Leftarrow$) $m$ and $T(m)$ are joined by an edge, hence lie in the same component, so constancy on components gives $a_m=a_{T(m)}$. ($\Rightarrow$) $Fa=a$ gives $a_m=a_{T^j(m)}$ for all $j\ge0$ by induction. If $m,n$ lie in the same component then (frozen definition: components = grand orbits) $T^j(m)=T^k(n)$ for some $j,k\ge0$, whence $a_m=a_{T^j(m)}=a_{T^k(n)}=a_n$. $\square$

*(ii) Hence the $\{0,1\}$-valued solutions of $(\star)$ are exactly the indicators $\mathbf 1_S$ with $S$ a union of components (a set in bijection with the power set of the set of components), the nonnegative bounded solutions form a cone whose $\{0,1\}$-extreme elements are these indicators, and:* **the Collatz conjecture is equivalent to the statement that $(\star)$ has exactly four $\{0,1\}$-valued solutions**, namely $f=0$, $f=\frac{1}{1-z}$ (all of $\mathbb N_0$), $f=1$ (the component $\{0\}$), $f=\frac{1}{1-z}-1$ (its complement).

**Proof.** By (i), $\{0,1\}$-solutions $=$ indicators of unions of components; distinct unions give distinct indicators, so their number is $2^{\#\text{components}}$ (as a cardinal). $\{0\}$ and $C_1$ are always distinct components ($T(0)=0$ confines the component of $0$ to preimages of $0$; $1\notin$ that component since $T$-orbit of $1$ never reaches $0$ — indeed the only preimages of $0$ under $T$ are $0$ itself: $2\cdot0=0$ and $(2\cdot0-1)/3\notin\mathbb N_0$, so the component of $0$ is... more carefully: the component of $0$ is the grand orbit $\{m: T^j(m)=0,\ \exists j\}=\{0\}$ since $T(m)=0$ forces $m=0$). So $\#\text{components}\ge2$ always, with equality iff the components are exactly $\{0\}$ and $C_1$ — which is the frozen statement of the Collatz conjecture. And $\#\text{components}=2\iff 2^{\#\text{components}}=4$. The four listed $f$'s are the indicators of $\emptyset,\ \mathbb N_0,\ \{0\},\ \mathbb N_0\setminus\{0\}$. $\square$

(Note the small argument inside (ii): $T(m)=0$ implies $m=0$, because $m/2=0\Rightarrow m=0$ and $(3m+1)/2=0$ has no solution in $\mathbb N_0$; hence the grand orbit of $0$ is $\{0\}$, unconditionally.)
