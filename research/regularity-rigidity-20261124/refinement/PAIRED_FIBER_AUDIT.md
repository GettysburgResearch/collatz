## Testing the prescribed target by two integer carries

Use $T(2n)=n$, $T(2n+1)=3n+2$. For integers $1\le p\le e$, let $\mathcal C_{e,p}$ consist of
$$\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}3^{p-1-i}2^{d_i},\qquad 0=d_0<d_1<\cdots<d_{p-1}<e.$$
It is the carry set of words of length $e$ and weight $p$ with odd initial source. For a positive odd ordinary unit $q$ and $0<B<q$, the bounded-offset completed paired count in PR200 is exactly
$$
\#\{C\in\mathbb Z:0<C\le B2^e,\ C,5C\in\mathcal C_{e,p},\ 3^p\mid2^eq-C,\ 3^{p+1}\nmid2^eq-C\}. \tag{1}
$$

**Proof.** In a complete pair the row identities at endpoints $q,5q$ have common length $e$ and common grade $p$. Subtracting five times the first from the second shows that the second carry is $5C$. The two divisibility conditions say exactly that the starting value $x=(2^eq-C)/3^p$ is a ternary unit. Conversely, suppose $C$ passes the tests. Its oddness and the height cutoff give an odd positive $x$. For the word represented by $C$, the full row equation forces its parity word at $x$. Reducing the equation modulo two gives the first bit. Take the specified shortcut, divide its row by two and iterate. The word represented by $5C$ likewise has full row equation $3^p(5x)+5C=2^e(5q)$, forcing the whole path at $5x$. The endpoint is an odd equal-count ratio-five root. Starting from the initial odd root, scan to the first positive root and consume its zero padding up to the next odd bit. Since the final endpoint is an odd root, all these cuts end no later than the final clock. The finite word therefore consists of complete marked blocks. A carry has at most one representation at grade $p$ since successive binary valuations recover $d_i$ after subtracting the preceding term. This also proves that each admissible $C$ contributes one distinct $x$. QED.

For a fixed $q$, (1) tests at most $1+B2^e/3^p$ integers in one progression. Membership in $\mathcal C_{e,p}$ takes at most $p$ successive binary-valuation tests. It is the joint test on $C$ and $5C$ that is missing from a scalar atom floor. A single-arm floor at either $q$ or $5q$ has no assertion about the intersection in (1).

## An attainable relative state that the real cutoff excludes

At a common clock $e\ge1$ from $(x,5x)$ with $x$ a positive odd unit, let the grades be $p,p+k$ and the real offsets $\beta,\beta_5$. Each offset is positive since both arms start odd. For $k_\pm=\max(\pm k,0)$ the integer relative state obeys
$$D=3^{k_-}Y-5\,3^{k_+}y=3^{k_-}\beta_5-5\,3^{k_+}\beta.$$
Consequently every prefix with $\beta\le B$ satisfies
$$ D>-5\,3^{k_+}B. \tag{2}$$
The ternary unit condition on $Y$ is necessary as well. A positive shortcut starting at a unit leaves a unit after both branches.

Take $B=1/2$, $K=1$, $y_0=1\bmod3$ and the singleton menu $S=\{(0,-4)\}$. At **every** positive odd $y\equiv1\bmod3$, the candidate $Y=5y-4$ is a positive odd unit, and the required menu integrality always holds. However (2) would give $-4>-5/2$. Thus the FS row is empty at every positive clock for every such $y$. The state itself appears at the exactly balanced physical row
$$x=835,\quad 5x=4175,\quad (e,p,p+k)=(10,5,5),\quad (y,Y)=(199,991).$$
The chronological words are `1100011010` and `1111001000`, with carries $871$ and $259$ and hence $259-5\cdot871=-4\cdot1024$. The first offset $871/1024$ exceeds the cutoff. A separate simpler obstruction uses $S=\{(0,1)\}$ on the same ternary class, for which $Y=5y+1$ is always a multiple of three.

PR200 states a sufficient implication for a fixed menu. The examples refute its promotion to a uniform existence claim over all integral menus. They do not refute the implication. One would have to produce **some** fixed menu and class with enough cutoff-compatible prefixes at its preassigned $y(q)$ for every supplied $q$. Checking integrality, unit-valued endpoints, (2), or occurrence on a few trajectories only supplies necessary screens.
