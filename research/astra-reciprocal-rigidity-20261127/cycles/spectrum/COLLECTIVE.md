# How many shortcut shifts can have few mismatches?

**PROPOSED written theorems.** For a primitive positive rational shortcut cycle of period $n$ and common reduced odd denominator $d$, write $m$ for the least phase, $X_i=d(x_i+1)$ for the centered positive integer numerator, $L=1+\log_2(d(m+1))$ and $\alpha=\log_23$. Indices belong to $\mathbb Z/n$. The cyclic binary parity word is $w$, with $u$ odd positions. Write $s(h)=\#\{i:w_i\ne w_{i+h}\}$, $K=\{h\ne0:s(h)\le k\}$, $B=|K|$. Assume $k\ge2$ and $B>0$. A primitive cycle has $s(h)\ge2$ at every proper shift.

The minimum mismatch bound applies to each shift individually. Here the distribution over *all* shifts has much stronger restrictions. Some follow from the full reduced denominator and the native parity coding. A separate restriction needs only convergence of the odd density to an irrational number.

## Finite height and cardinality

For $n>kL$,
$$
 B<\frac{3\alpha k^2}{2(1-kL/n)}. \tag{1}
$$
In particular $B<3\alpha k^2$ when $n\ge2kL$. Thus a positive rational cycle with $B$ small-Hamming shifts requires
$$
 \log_2(d(m+1))>\frac nk-1-\frac{3\alpha kn}{2(B+1)}. \tag{2}
$$

**Proof.** In a finite group of size $M$ with a symmetric generating set $S$ containing zero, the Cayley graph in which distinct adjacent vertices differ by an element of $S$ has diameter $D<3M/|S|$. Along a shortest path of length $D$, the closed radius-one balls at dates $0,3,6,\ldots,3\lfloor D/3\rfloor$ are disjoint. Every ball has $|S|$ vertices, which proves the bound, including graphs of diameter one.

Take $S=\{0,1,-1\}\cup K$ in the cyclic group. Hamming distance is translation-invariant, so $K=-K$. Each edge from a shift in $K$ changes $X$ by at most a factor $3^{k/2}$ in either direction by the centered ratio comparison in [the paired proof](../ALL_SHIFT.md). Each clock edge has both ratios at most $2$, because $2X_{i+1}$ equals either $3X_i$ or $X_i+d$ and $X_i\ge d$. Hence $\log_2\max X_i<L-1+3\alpha kn/(2|S|)$. For any $h\in K$, at least one run of common consecutive parity digits between the two tracks has length $R\ge n/s(h)-1\ge n/k-1$. Two distinct centered numerators agreeing on that run differ by a nonzero multiple of $2^R$. Therefore $n/k-1<\log_2\max X_i$. Combining and using $|S|\ge B+1$ proves both inequalities.

## The subgroup generated by the small shifts

Write $H=\langle K\rangle$, $M=|H|$, $g=n/M$ and $A=(B+1)/k$. Its cosets are the $g$ residue columns modulo $g$. Choose the majority bit in each column, with ties arbitrary. Repeating these $g$ bits gives a genuine $g$-periodic word. Denote its number of odd bits per block by $v$ and its number of differences from $w$ by $e$. Then
$$ e<\frac{3M}{A},\qquad |u/M-v|\le e/M<3/A. \tag{3}$$

Indeed let $a_j$ be the number of odd bits in column $j$. Summing over all $h\in H$ counts every ordered pair in each column precisely once, and gives $\sum_{h\in H}s(h)=2\sum_j a_j(M-a_j)$. Since $\min(a,M-a)\le2a(M-a)/M$, the sum of the minority counts $e$ is at most the average mismatch count on $H$. By the Hamming triangle inequality an $H$-element at graph distance $t$ from zero has at most $tk$ mismatches. The diameter for $\{0\}\cup K$ is less than $3M/(B+1)$. The odd-count estimate follows by summing the deviations over sites.

In particular, if $H=\mathbb Z/n$ then $B+1<3nk/\min(u,n-u)$. For prime $n$, every nonempty $K$ generates this entire group.

## A direct integer-cycle quantile with no two-logarithm input

For every fixed $0<\delta<1$ there are $c_\delta>0$ and $n_\delta$ such that every primitive positive **ordinary** shortcut cycle of length $n\ge n_\delta$ satisfies
$$
 \#\{0<h<n:s(h)\le c_\delta n\}<\delta n. \tag{4}
$$
This means that along any unbounded family and any $k=o(n)$ the fraction $B/n$ vanishes. The constant depends on the tolerated fraction. The statement does not identify the smallest individual distance.

Let $p=\log_32$, irrational because distinct positive powers of 2 and 3 differ. For an ordinary cycle the exact return identity gives
$$
 0<\Lambda=n\log 2-u\log3
 =\sum_{\text{odd }i}\log(1+1/(3x_i))
 \le(1+\log n)/3.
$$
The last inequality uses distinct positive integers and the harmonic-sum bound. Thus $u/n\to p$ uniformly. If $B\ge\delta n$ then $g\le n/(B+1)<1/\delta$. The finite minimum $\Delta_\delta=\min_{1\le j\le\lfloor1/\delta\rfloor}\operatorname{dist}(jp,\mathbb Z)$ is positive. At sufficiently large $n$, $g|p-u/n|<\Delta_\delta/2$ for every such $g$. Equation (3) implies $\Delta_\delta/2<3k/(B+1)$. Taking $c_\delta=\delta\Delta_\delta/6$ proves (4). The same proof works for any cyclic binary words whose densities tend to a fixed irrational. This part uses ordinary arithmetic solely to get the density limit.

More quantitatively, if $|g\log2-v\log3|\ge c_\kappa g^{-\kappa}$ for $g\ge1$, then $g\le n/(B+1)$ gives
$$
 c_\kappa\left(\frac{B+1}{n}\right)^\kappa
 \le \frac{3k\log3+\Lambda}{B+1}. \tag{5}
$$
Chudnovsky, Theorem 11.1, gives this displayed input at each fixed $\kappa>2$. Equation (4) requires none of its constants. [Versioned primary source](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28079v1#S11)

## When the approximating block must repeat contiguously

If $A\ge12$, the subgroup index and the actual centered heights satisfy
$$
   gA < 6L+3\sqrt{\alpha n}. \tag{6}
$$
Cut the $M$ successive length-$g$ blocks starting at the minimum phase. At most $e<3M/A$ blocks disagree with the majority block anywhere. There is at least one erroneous block, since otherwise the length-$n$ word has a proper period $g$. If $b$ blocks are erroneous, pigeonhole on the circle gives at least $M/b-1$ consecutive exact majority blocks. Two adjacent row starts in such a run share at least $g(M/b-2)>g(A/3-2)\ge gA/6$ parities. Both starts differ from the minimum by elements of $H$. Their centered log heights are at most $L-1+(3\alpha/2)M/A$ by the Cayley argument inside $H$. Their distinctness and dyadic divisibility give $gA/6<L+(3\alpha/2)M/A$. With $z=gA$ and $M=n/g$, this reads $z^2<6Lz+9\alpha n$ and implies (6).

For $d=1$ with polynomial minimum, (6) and the same two-log estimate yield $B\ll_\epsilon k n^{1/3+\epsilon}$. To see the exponent, split at $g=n^{1/(2\kappa+2)}$. Above it, (6) gives $A=O(n^{\kappa/(2\kappa+2)})$. Below it, the error $g\Lambda/n$ is $o(g^{-\kappa})$ by the elementary harmonic bound, so (3) forces $A=O(g^\kappa)$. This rate and (5) apply in different ranges of $k$. The quadratic bound (1) handles fixed or slowly growing $k$.

## Scope

The single-lag arithmetic in [the lag note](../LAG_SPECTRUM.md) localizes each exceptional $h$ near a low-order rational clock. The estimates here bound the count and the subgroup generated by the exceptional lags. They use finite graph geometry, rather than a prior lower bound on the autocorrelation of the parity word. The identity for the mean over all shifts gives $2u(n-u)$ for the sum, which alone permits a positive fraction of zero mismatches. General almost periods of finite words and popular differences have a substantial literature. No priority claim is made for the purely combinatorial subgroup estimate. Typical dense, irregular words can have a positive fraction of mismatches at every proper shift. The existence of nontrivial ordinary cycles remains open.

## Critical positive rational controls

There are primitive positive rational cycles with odd count $u=\lfloor n\log_32\rfloor$, unboundedly many proper shifts with $s(h)\le4$, and whole reduced denominators satisfying $\log_2 d/n\to1$. These show why the physical cardinality estimate includes $d$, even at the exact critical integer weight. The proportion of designated small shifts is $1/g+o(1)$ and vanishes, as required by the irrational-density argument.

Choose any sequence of reduced approximations $v/g$ to $p=\log_32$ with $g\to\infty$ and $\delta=|gp-v|\to0$. Continued-fraction convergents supply one. Take $M=\lfloor3/(2\delta)\rfloor$, $n=gM$ and $u=\lfloor np\rfloor$. The difference $u-Mv$ equals $+1$ if $gp>v$ and $-2$ if $gp<v$, for all sufficiently late terms. Write $e=|u-Mv|$. In each of $e$ consecutive rows use $1^{v+\operatorname{sgn}(gp-v)}0^{g-v-\operatorname{sgn}(gp-v)}$. In all remaining rows use $1^v0^{g-v}$. The row boundaries are exactly the starts of one-runs since both kinds of runs have positive lengths. Any period must be a multiple of $g$, and the exceptional rows form one nonempty proper interval in the cyclic row order. Thus the constructed word is primitive. Its exact weight gives $2^n>3^u$, hence its ordinary affine branches close at a positive rational cycle with common reduced odd denominator. For every row shift $jg$ the word and its rotation differ only at the exceptional rows' marked bit offset, so $s(jg)\le2e\le4$.

Within the run of $M-e$ exact standard rows, choose a phase that is congruent modulo $g$ to the minimum and at most $g-1$ clocks after the run begins. It and its shift by $g$ share at least $(M-e-2)g$ digits. Their centered heights are each at most $3^e d(m+1)$ by the all-shift ratio bound, since they differ from the minimum by row shifts. Their entrance differences give $2^{(M-e-2)g}<3^e d(m+1)$. Finally $\Lambda=(np-u)\log3$ stays bounded above and away from zero, since $M\delta\to3/2$. The positive cycle formula gives $m<C=O(n)$. Hence $\log_2 d\ge n-O(g+\log n)$, while $d\le2^n-3^u<2^n$. This proves the stated asymptotic. Exact recurrence checks for five such words appear in the accompanying checker.

### Large minima, vanishing reciprocal defect and rough total determinant together

One may retain every small-shift conclusion, force the least prime factor of the full raw determinant $D=2^n-3^u$ to exceed $c\log n$, and arrange
$$
   m\asymp\delta^{-4/3}\to\infty,\qquad
   \Lambda\asymp\delta^{1/3}\asymp m^{-1/4}\to0.
$$
In particular the total reciprocal correction passes the necessary ordinary upper scale $O(m^{-1/40})$, while the whole *reduced* denominator still has $\log_2d/n\to1$.

Write $L_0=1/\delta$. Choose a small absolute $c_0>0$, $R=\lfloor c_0\log L_0\rfloor$ and $Q=\operatorname{lcm}\{p-1:p\le R,\ p\text{ prime}\}$. For all large terms $Q\le L_0^{1/3}$, since $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,R)\le4^{2R}$. The last elementary bound follows by induction on dyadic scales. Every prime-power crossing in $(h,2h]$ contributes its prime to $\binom{2h}{h}$. This is exactly the lcm congruence device used for mechanical rational cycles in PR200.

If $gp>v$ take $M$ to be a nearest positive multiple of $Q$ to $L_0(1+\delta^{1/3})$. If $gp<v$ instead use $L_0(2-\delta^{1/3})$. Then $M\asymp L_0$ and the error in $M\delta$ from this rounding is $O(\delta^{2/3})$. Consequently $u-Mv=1$ or $-2$ as before, and $\Lambda=(np-u)\log3\asymp\delta^{1/3}$. For every prime $5\le p'\le R$ both $gM$ and $vM$ are multiples of $p'-1$. The determinant is congruent to $-2$ in the first case and to $1-3^{-2}=8/9$ in the second. Two and three already do not divide $D$. Since $g\le L_0$ along convergents and $n=gM=O(L_0^2)$, the least prime factor of $D$, and hence that of $d>1$, exceeds a positive absolute multiple of $\log n$.

For a row of $a$ odd steps followed by $g-a$ even ones the row map is
$$
   x\longmapsto \lambda_a x+\lambda_a(1-(2/3)^a),
   \qquad \lambda_a=3^a/2^g .
$$
Regular rows have $\lambda_v=\exp(\pm\delta\log3)$. In at most two exceptional rows an additional factor $3$ or $1/3$ occurs. Products over any contiguous collection of at most $M$ rows are therefore bounded above and away from zero by absolute constants, because $M\delta=O(1)$. Every additive row intercept is also bounded above and away from zero for large $v$. Expanding one lap from any row start gives $(1-e^{-\Lambda})x_{\rm row}\asymp M$, uniformly at all row starts. Each row first increases and then decreases, so the minimum is among those starts. It follows that $m\asymp M/\Lambda\asymp\delta^{-4/3}$. The repeated-row argument again gives $\log_2d\ge n-O(g+\log n)$. These controls are still nonordinary. The lower bound concerns the entire reduced denominator and coexists with its unit status at every small prime.

PR200 already constructs full logarithmically rough denominators for genuine mechanical positive rational cycles at large minima, with several additional approximation constraints. Here the independent property is the unbounded number of row shifts with at most four mismatches at critical binary weight.
