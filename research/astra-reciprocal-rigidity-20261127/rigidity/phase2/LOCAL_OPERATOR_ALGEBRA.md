# Finite arithmetic operators have the same image at infinity on every component

**Proposed mathematical theorem.** On the Hilbert space of square-summable functions on an ordinary Collatz component, let `C f(n)=w(n)f(Tn)` with `w=1/4,9/4` at even and odd sites. Include its Hilbert adjoint and diagonal multiplication by any bounded periodic arithmetic function of `n`. For every noncommutative polynomial `P` in finitely many of these operators, its essential norm is **identical** on every ordinary component and on the full positive integer graph. The corresponding unital C*-algebras generated in the Calkin algebra are canonically isometrically *-isomorphic, with all the indicated generators matched. In particular corresponding polynomials, and their norm limits, have the same essential spectra.

Here the essential norm is the operator norm modulo compact operators. The essential spectrum uses the image in that same quotient. An actual acyclic component, if one exists, also has its ordinary operator norm equal to that common essential norm for every such polynomial. The theorem thus concerns all these local operators simultaneously. A Euclidean shift `n↦n+1` can join different components and is outside this algebra.

[HEIGHT_OBSERVABLES.md](HEIGHT_OBSERVABLES.md) extends essential-norm equality to all bounded uniformly continuous functions of ordinary log height. It supplies an independent uniform-localization argument.

## Exact replication of a finite tree calculation

Each component contains a positive vertex coprime to 3. From a multiple of three, first halve until odd and then take one odd shortcut step. For any such target and any modulus `M`, arbitrarily high blocks of `M` consecutive starts reach that target by [the ordinary block theorem](BasinBlocks.lean). Hence each positive residue class modulo `M` has arbitrarily large representatives in every given component.

Consider a finite, connected, acyclic part of the ordinary graph, together with the full propagation collar needed for a specified computation. Orient edge labels according to the actual shortcut branch. Starting from an anchor `x`, each vertex `v` in this finite tree is obtained by an affine form `g_v(x)` with positive rational slope, following its unique simple path. The elementary maps are `t/2,(3t+1)/2,2t,(2t−1)/3`, used on their appropriate domains. Every slope denominator divides a power of 6. The ability to use the odd inverse branch is determined by the terminal residue 2 modulo 3.

Let `m` be a common period of all diagonal labels used in the polynomial. If all the paths have length at most `H`, restricting the anchor to `x'≡x mod m·6^(H+1)` preserves integrality, all vertex labels modulo `m`, the physical parities and the odd-predecessor flags. Indeed `g_v(x')−g_v(x)` is an integer divisible by `6m`. Along an oriented edge the two affine descriptions satisfy the defining affine identity for all lifts, because its path either appends that edge to the unique anchor path or retraces it. Different vertices give different values at `x`, so a collision of their affine forms occurs for at most one lift. Similarly an unwanted extra edge between two selected images can occur at most once, since after parity is fixed it would impose a nonidentical affine equality. All selected values tend to positive infinity with `x'`. This gives exact copies with all prescribed local labels, arbitrarily far out in any chosen component. Copies can be taken pairwise disjoint. At an interior vertex with a full collar, its forward and available inverse neighbors are precisely the represented ones.

A degree-`d` word in the graph operators propagates by at most `d` graph edges; diagonal factors do not move support. If a finitely supported vector `f` and everything seen through the required `d` steps fit in one such finite tree, transplant its coefficient values along the copies. The resulting vectors `f_j` have identical norm and identical `||P f_j||`. The normalized disjoint vectors are weakly null in `ℓ²`, so each compact operator sends them to zero in norm. The ratio `||P f||/||f||` therefore does not exceed the essential norm of `P` on the **receiving** component.

## Removing the cycle without assuming a finite number of components

A single functional component is an undirected tree or has one finite directed cycle with attached trees. Edges on a two-cycle are taken with their directed multiplicity. For an acyclic component any finite support and its propagation collar have a finite connected tree hull. For a cyclic component choose one cycle vertex `a` and remove from the support its finite radius-`d` neighborhood. The remaining graph after deleting `a` is a forest. Distinct forest pieces of the remaining support have disjoint outputs, since no path of at most `d` steps starting there can reach `a`. In each piece the finite support together with its required collar has a finite tree hull. The preceding estimate can be applied to each piece and squared norms summed. The initial fixed finite support deletion changes the operator by a finite-rank map. This proves the essential-norm comparison between any two single components, in both directions. On an acyclic component the same argument required no deletion even for its full norm.

On the full graph there may be infinitely many different cycle cores. For each fixed bound `L₀` there are only finitely many ordinary cycles of length less than `L₀`. A parity word of length `h≥1` gives the affine iterate with slope `3^q/2^h≠1`, hence at most one fixed point. There are finitely many words through any bounded length. Delete one fixed finite `d`-neighborhood for each of these finitely many short cycles.

For a remaining cycle of length `L≥L₀`, consider a finite input vector in its component. Averaging over all phase vertices `a` gives

\[
 \min_{a\text{ on the cycle}} \|1_{B_d(a)}f\|_2^2
       \le (2d+1)L^{-1}\|f\|_2^2 .
\]

Each vertex lies within `d` of at most `2d+1` cycle phases. Remove that low-mass piece at a chosen phase. The induced operator-norm error on this vector is at most `K sqrt((2d+1)/L₀) ||f||`, for a single uniform bound `K` for the norm of the polynomial (obtained from its coefficients and the uniformly bounded generators). The rest of the vector splits into tree pieces exactly as above. Squared errors sum over disjoint components. Thus the full essential norm exceeds the common receiving-component upper bound by at most `K sqrt((2d+1)/L₀)`; let `L₀→∞`. Conversely every transplantation into a component is already a transplantation into the full graph, giving the reverse inequality. The proof uses the one-cycle structure to cut long cycles at small vector cost. Pointwise agreement of bounded-radius neighborhoods alone is not used as a general essential-norm theorem.

Equal seminorms on all unital *-polynomials give isometric identifications of their completions in the respective Calkin algebras. Unital C*-subalgebras have the same spectra for their elements as the ambient C*-algebra. This yields the spectral assertion without assuming that the polynomial itself is normal. Prior graph right-limit theory provides context; the explicit copies, finite collars and long-cycle cut above supply the comparison for these particular ordinary graphs. [Breuer, Denisov and Eliaz, *On the essential spectrum of Schrödinger operators on trees*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10049)

## The disk is already essential on the known component

Write `a=1/4,b=9/4` and use the terminating paths in [the operator spectrum note](OPERATOR_SPECTRUM.md). For `a<|λ|<b`, take the segment with the last `k` odd predecessors of `q_k`, the odd step `q_k→2^(3^k)`, and `k` subsequent halvings. Set a test vector for the Hilbert adjoint equal to 1 at the first power of 2 on the segment. Along consecutive vertices `v_i,v_{i+1}` impose `h(v_{i+1})=w(v_i)h(v_i)/λ`, and set all other values zero. The residual `(C*−λ)h` vanishes at all interior sites. At the two ends its sizes are bounded by fixed multiples of `(|λ|/b)^k` and `(a/|λ|)^k`, respectively. There are no other residual sites because the adjoint sends a basis impulse only to its forward successor. The norms are at least 1 and bounded above by converging geometric sums.

The odd predecessor segments for different `k` lie in disjoint `q_k` predecessor trees. All their odd vertices are greater than 1, while their power-of-two segments have exponent ranges near `3^k` which are pairwise disjoint (discarding finitely many starting values of `k` would also suffice). Thus normalized test vectors are orthonormal. An invertible Calkin image would give a bounded inverse modulo a compact error, contradicting their residuals. This places `λ` in the essential spectrum of `C*`, and hence its conjugate in that of `C`.

For `|λ|<a`, use the two children of any `q_k≡2 mod3`. The children `2q_k` and `(2q_k−1)/3` are even and odd respectively. Give them values `b,−a` so their weighted images cancel at `q_k`. Extend backwards on the even-doubling ray of each child by multiplying its value by `λ/a` at every doubling. These two rays have distinct odd parts and are disjoint. They are contained in their common root's predecessor tree; for distinct `k` those trees are disjoint. The resulting vectors are in `ℓ²` and obey `C*h=λh` exactly. They are infinitely many independent adjoint eigenvectors. Closedness includes the two seams `|λ|=a,b`. The radius bound in the operator spectrum note supplies the exterior resolvent. Thus the essential spectrum of `C` is exactly the closed disk of radius `9/4`, already on the known basin and, by the transplantation theorem, on every component and the full graph.

These conclusions concern the Calkin image. The *exact kernel* at the real value 1 still depends on whether an escaping ordinary component exists. Known-basin copies of approximate vectors give no nonzero exact vector on that basin.
