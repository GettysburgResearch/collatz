# Independent reconstruction of the ordered subpower descent

**Verdict. VERIFIED against the named symbolic-absorption premise.** I checked the written statement (1), the two physical lifts, and the iterated original-parent count in [the head proof](../head/README.md), SHA-256 `78977a273b4e3dff70d602d69b0ce30c36ce235694cc108d055e7cd727f4ac1f`. The input is the **symbolic absorption** estimate proved in the paragraphs after equation (1) of PR146, commit `f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437`. Merely quoting that equation's bound on the absence of an ordinary meeting would not bound symbolic nonabsorption. Its proof explicitly estimates the probability of avoiding the absorbing state on a complete binary grid, which is the stronger event needed here. I checked its applicability and did not review its underlying native remaining-time theorem afresh. 1. **The identities preserve the imposed order.** With lower arm grade $a$ at most upper arm grade $b$, the first odd step from $(2^kA-1)/3$ and the following $k-1$ halvings reach $A$. Its triple starts at $2^kA-1$ and follows $k$ odd inputs to $3^kA-1$. The new slope grade of this latter state is $a+k$, between $b$ and $b+6$. In the other case $9u=2^kB-1$ gives $T^k(3u)=B$, while the first two odd steps on $u$ give $2^{k-2}B+1$. Each pair of steps on $4w+1$ gives $3w+1$, with the final single odd step if required. The other comparison record has grade $b+\lceil(k-2)/2\rceil$, between $a$ and $a+9$. Since the residues impose numerators $3\bmod9$ or $9\bmod27$ before division by $3$ or $9$, respectively, the selected $u$ is $1\bmod3$ and odd. Thus fusion of the two compared records supplies the stated order of ordinary components in both branches.
2. **Each trial uses the original source grid.** Fix the low $t$ bits of $n$. The remaining quotient runs through a dyadic interval whose low $h$ bits are exactly uniform whenever $t+h\le D$. The original states are affine in this quotient with slopes $3^a,3^b$. At a good checkpoint these are multiples of $27$, so the exponent menu is fixed by the past. The smaller-grade comparison record is still odd-affine in this quotient, and hence permutes its residues modulo $2^h$. After subtracting $3^g$ times that record from the other, the leading term cancels and the remaining $J$ is an integer, by evaluation at an integral quotient. Its logarithmic size is $O(1+k+\log(2+E_i))$ for fixed residual gap $g\le9$. This verifies the conditional hypothesis of the cited absorption bound, without assuming independent arms or freshly sampled children.
3. **Moment and trial budgets.** On either arm, even after fixing all initial $L$ original bits, each subsequent parity is fair conditional on the newly exposed original past. Subadditivity of square root gives the contraction $\rho=(1+\sqrt3)/(2\sqrt2)<1$. Its initial square-root factor is $O_r(2^{L/2})$, which is absorbed by $\rho^{t-L}$ uniformly since $t\ge.75D$ and $L=o(D)$. The resulting Markov loss per test is $O(\exp(-2\lambda_i))$. For each arm the remaining scalar parity sequence is conditionally iid fair, so Hoeffding applies marginally; the fixed prefix already accounts for the $L/2$ allowance. Conditional failure at each good trial is at most $1/2$, and earlier trial outcomes are measurable by the next checkpoint. Iterating with indicators of *past* goodness bounds the probability of all-good/all-fail, without conditioning a current trial on future good checkpoints.
4. **Actual-child counting.** For a fixed tuple of clock, chosen arm, grade, exponent and target octave, the marked arm is $c_t n+I$. Its lower bound on a good row is exponential in $D$, while $E_i=\exp(o(D))$. Thus $c_t X\asymp 2^{-k}Y$ on a nonempty row of parents. For fixed child $u$, the source interval has length $E_i/c_t$, so the original residue gives at most $1+O(E_i2^kX/(2^L Y))$ possible parents. The additive one is absorbed since $D-D'\ge .15D$ and $L=o(D)$. The selected child is in the particular residue $u\equiv-q_0^{-1}\bmod 2^k$ for $q_0=3$ or $9$. The next-stage estimate applies there because $k\le K_{i+1}<D'$. Its $Y2^{-k}$ possible lattice points cancel the extra $2^k$ from each source fiber. Counting all good lifts to bad children only enlarges the set being bounded. The polynomial tuple count and $E_i$ leave $d^{O(1)}\exp(-4\lambda_i)$ after division by $X2^{-L}$.
5. **Growing depth.** The chosen $m$ leaves $D\ge d^{.98}$, $K_i\le d^{.72}$ and $\lambda_i\le d^{.184}$. Consequently the $h\asymp d^{.74}$ trials fit inside the free bits, have vanishing per-trial upper bound, and number at least a constant times $d^{.24}$. The Hoeffding loss uses $M^2/d=d^{.20}$; this and the trial loss dominate every $\lambda_i$ in the backward induction. The physical height fraction before rounding lies between $1-(1-\log_2(3)/2)7/8$ and $1-(1-\log_2(3)/2)3/4$, strictly inside $[.80,.85]$. Both the added $k$ and grade errors are $o(D)$. Finally $.85^m d\le .85^{-1}d^{1+\log(.85)/12}=o(d^{.99})$. Taking the integer row endpoint into account proves the asserted absolute height cap.
