# Prescribed ancestors of two ordinary targets

**PROPOSED written theorem.** Let $T$ be the ordinary positive shortcut map. Consider the two ordered pairs
\[
 P_0(x)=(x,9x+2),\qquad P_1(x)=(x,3x).
\]
A link from one such pair to another advances the two actual coordinates by independently chosen numbers of shortcut steps between zero and $H$. Zero movement on both coordinates does not count. The line may change at each link. Call a target discordant when its coordinates have disjoint forward orbits. The target, its line, and the chain can depend on the individual source.

Take $x\in[2^d,2^{d+1})$ in one prescribed binary residue class of depth $L\le d$. Let $U\ge1$ be an upper bound on the first coordinate of the terminal target. Put
\[
 B=d-\left\lceil\log_2\max\{U+1,16\cdot6^H+1\}\right\rceil,
 \qquad k=\max\{0,\lfloor (B-L-H-6)/(H+6)\rfloor\}.
\]
For either specified initial line, the relative fraction of sources admitting **any** such chain to a discordant target is at most $(63/64)^k$. The bound applies to the union over all the prescribed targets below the floor $U$. It requires no assertion about the density of any positive predecessor set.

In particular suppose $U\le2^{(1-\delta)d}$ for a fixed $\delta>0$, and $L\le d^{3/5}$. If $H=o(d^{99/100})$, the fraction is $o(\exp(-d^{1/100}))$. Thus short inverse returns confined to these two pair relations cannot, by their number, force a prescribed discordant pair through the exceptional fraction in the [ordered landing theorem](../head/README.md). This concerns actual forward visits on both arms. General component comparisons may instead pass through a common future and then traverse an inverse edge to their next representative. Those comparisons need not be chains of the stated kind.

## Unequal clocks and observation of the return

For $h$ shortcut steps on positive $x$, write $2^h T^h x=3^p x+C$, where $p$ counts odd inputs and $0\le C\le3^h-2^h$. Appending a bit $e$ takes the carry to $3^eC+e2^h$, proving the bound by induction. Write the current line as $(x,ax+b)$ and the returned line as $(y,cy+e)$ with $(a,b),(c,e)\in\{(9,2),(3,0)\}$. Suppose the two advances have lengths $h,j\le H$, grades $p,q$, and carries $C,F$. Eliminating $y$ gives
\[
 2^h a3^q x+2^h(3^q b+F)
 =2^j c3^p x+2^j cC+2^{j+h}e .
\]
The nonnegative intercepts on both sides are at most $11\cdot6^H$. If $x>16\cdot6^H$ the two integer slopes coincide, since otherwise the difference of the linear terms has absolute value at least $x$. Their valuations at two give $h=j$, as $a,c$ are odd. The equality of the intercepts now follows too. As a result this is a return of the two **affine maps** on the entire binary cylinder fixed by the $h$ low bits. It can be detected from those bits without reading the remaining quotient. This rules out extra asynchronous returns obtained by choosing the two short clocks separately at a single large source.

At common clock $t$ the first coordinate is $3^{p_t}Q+c_t$ in the unread quotient $Q$ of the original source after $t$ bits. Its next six low bits are uniform on a complete remaining grid, since $3^{p_t}$ is odd. On the first line the class $46\bmod64$ merges the two arms after six steps, both at $27b'+20$ when $x=64b'+46$. On the second line $23\bmod32$ merges them after five steps, both at $27b'+20$ for $x=32b'+23$. Thus the conditional probability of certifying an actual merger in the next six newly read bits is at least $1/64$ at either type of observation. Every pair on a chain to a discordant target must itself be discordant.

For the count, observe the first coherent visit to either line at or after clock $L$. Then observe the first such visit at least six clocks after the previous observation. Coherence means equality as affine maps on the currently read cylinder of the original source, including the initial line at clock zero. These hitting times are determined by read bits. Along a claimed chain the original first arm has size at least $2^{d-t}$ at common clock $t$, so every link started through clock $B$ begins at a state above $U$ and $16\cdot6^H$. The chain cannot already end there and its next link is coherent by the preceding calculation, also as an identity on the composed original cylinder. The chain therefore supplies marked times with gaps at most $H$ up to $B$, and a greedy observation is available by time $L+H$ initially and at most $H+6$ clocks after its predecessor as long as the relevant interval lies before $B$. The definition of $k$ leaves six unread bits for every one of the first $k$ tests. Conditioning on the original prefix and then on each observed past gives the stated power bound. A source for which a required observation never occurs does not witness the claimed chain. The proof works on a complete finite dyadic row and needs no mixing at a retrospectively chosen endpoint.

## Exactly prescribed targets

The homogeneous line has an exact obstruction even with unlimited, unrelated clocks. If $T^j(3x)=3u$, then $x=2^j u$. An odd shortcut produces a ternary unit and all its future iterates stay units. Therefore the entire path to $3u$ consists of halvings. Conversely $x=2^j u$ reaches the exact pair $(u,3u)$ at common time $j$. Thus parents on this same line giving the two specified ordinary targets have at most one value in a half-open dyadic octave. There is also a directed asymmetry without any clock budget. No source on the inhomogeneous line can flow to the homogeneous line, since $9x+2$ starts as a ternary unit. Consequently a chain switching between the two lines can only start with homogeneous halvings before moving to the inhomogeneous line for the rest of its visits. Replacing $3u$ by a later unit in its component changes the problem and the conclusion need not persist. The inhomogeneous line really does have expanding inverse returns. For every $b\ge0$, the literal paths from $P_0(4096b+2830)$ arrive at $P_0(81b+56)$ at clock 12. Both grades are four. Restricting to $b=7\bmod9$ makes both parameters eligible in the directed $2\bmod9$ problem. Repeating a nonempty periodic itinerary of such inverse returns cannot give a growing ray of one fixed integer target. Indeed, after discarding the finite preperiod, write the first-arm action of the repeated word as $2^h z_j=3^p z_{j+1}+C$ in the outward direction. If $p>0$, set $D=2^h-3^p\ne0$. Then
\[
                   3^{pj}(Dz_j-C)=2^{hj}(Dz_0-C).
\]
Integrality for every $j$ forces $Dz_0=C$, since two is a unit at three. Every $z_j$ is then equal to $z_0$. If a positive-length word returns to the inhomogeneous line coherently with $p=0$, both grades are zero and $2=2^{h}\,2$, impossible. A homogeneous all-even inverse word gives precisely the dyadic ray above. There is also a finite certificate. With $j+1$ such blocks the absolute successive displacements satisfy $3^{pj}\mid |z_1-z_0|$, by subtracting adjacent recurrences and cancelling the coprime factor $2^{hj}$. This bounds the length of a run of identical inverse words for each nonfixed target and first lift. The periodic exclusion concerns a repeated fixed pair of branch words.
