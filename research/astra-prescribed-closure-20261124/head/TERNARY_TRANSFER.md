# Which component survives ternary contraction?

**PROPOSED closing-step audit.** On the two dyadic progressions of the [sparse unit criterion](PRESCRIBED_RESIDUES.md), the choices $\beta=-1$ and $\beta=-1/4$ force high ternary divisibility. There are genuine contractions for both choices. The ordinary endpoint identities below determine which label the new integer inherits.

## The center $-1$ and the frozen binary digits

Suppose $q_0u+1\equiv0\bmod2^k$, $q_0\in\{3,9\}$, $k\ge3$, $h=v_3(u+1)\ge3$. Write $u+1=3^hw$, and set $v=2^h w-1$. Thus $v+1=(2/3)^h(u+1)$. All $h$ initial steps of $v$ are odd, giving $T^h v=u$. The first three steps of $3v$ have parities odd, even, even and end at $9\cdot2^{h-3}w-1$. Its next $h-3$ steps are odd and end at $9\cdot3^{h-3} w-1=(u-2)/3$. Consequently the pair at clock $h$ is $(u,(u-2)/3)$.

* If $q_0=9$ then $u\equiv7\bmod8$. The last three steps from $(u-2)/3$ visit $(u-1)/2,(3u-1)/4,(9u+1)/8$. This is $T^3(3u)$, by one odd step and two halvings from $3u$. Thus the pair at clock $h+3$ is precisely $(T^3u,T^3(3u))$. The first new arm has $h$ extra odd steps. On the second the counts are $1+(h-3)+3=h+1$ versus one on the old three steps. At *every* clock $h+j$, $j\ge3$, the states agree with the old states at $j$, with the same $h$ extra odd counts on both arms. In particular, by the time $h+k$ is reached the full old grade gap at $k$ has returned. Although $v_2(9v+1)=3$, the following congruence is exact:
$$
        3^{h+2}(v+1)-2^{h+3}=2^h(9u+1)\equiv0\pmod{2^{h+k}}.
$$
For each fixed $h$ this describes one prescribed residue of $v$ to $h+k$ bits. Its initial $h+3$ steps rejoin the two old arms, then the old $k-3$ forced bits are still committed.
* If $q_0=3$ then $u\equiv5\bmod8$. The same upper state $(u-2)/3$ now visits $(u-1)/2,(u-1)/4,(3u+1)/8$. This last value equals $T^3u$, by one odd step and two halvings from $u$. Hence the new pair actually **fuses** at clock $h+3$, for every such positive input. Both new labels are the old label of $u$. An old discrepancy between $u$ and $3u$ has lost the other component in this contraction.

On the $9$ progression the contraction preserves the ordered components and their long shared-prefix history. On the $3$ progression it certifies an ordinary merger of the new pair. The identities through the specified clocks are kernel-checked in [PrescribedReturns.lean](../orbit/PrescribedReturns.lean) as `exchangedPrefix`, `exchangedNineReplay`, and `exchangedThreeFusion`. These identities hold regardless of the colors. They give no assertion about a later meeting of the old pair.

## The center $-1/4$ and the child $9u+2$

Use the notation of [PR 192, least-source size bound](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/b72cea3bcd5aa51aba8df6f5e63bc1c473e9523b/research/astra-closing-step-20261124/descent/README.md). Here $u\equiv2\bmod9$, $4u+1=3^{b+2}w$, $3\nmid w$, and $B=8\cdot2^b w-1$. The identity $T^{b+3}B=12u+2$ and two further steps give $B\sim9u+2$. In particular $B+1=(8/9)(2/3)^b(4u+1)$, so $B/u$ tends to zero as $b$ tends to infinity. Which arm of the tripling pair this represents requires an additional ordinary comparison.

On the $3$ progression write $3u+1=2^k V$, $Y=3^k V$. The actual states from $(3u,9u+2)$ after $k$ steps are $(Y-1,3Y-1)$, since $9u+2=3\cdot2^k V-1$. If $Y\equiv3\bmod4$, one even then one odd step from the first state and two even steps from the second both give $(3Y-1)/4$. Thus on this subclass $B$ indeed represents $3u$. If $u$ is good and $3u$ bad for the basin of one, the source gives a genuine failed transfer to $9u+2$.

On the $9$ progression write $9u+1=2^k V$. Let $Z=3^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}V$ and take $c=1$ for even $k$, $c=2$ for odd $k\ge3$. The pair $(u,9u+2)$ now has the states $(Z+c,3Z+c)$ after $k$ steps. For the second state use $9u+2=2^k V+1$ and the two-step formula $4x+1\mapsto3x+1$. For even $k$ and $Z=5\bmod32$, the starts $6,16$ at the base meet after five steps at 2 with odd counts 2 and 1. For odd $k$ and $Z=49\bmod128$, the starts $51,149$ meet after seven steps at 11 with counts 3 and 2. In each case adding $2^j m$ to $Z$ adds $3^p m$ to the first endpoint and $3\cdot3^{p-1}m$ to the second, so the meeting holds throughout the specified progression. Here $B$ represents $u$ itself. It can therefore provide no comparison to the opposite color of a genuinely discordant $3u$.

These small binary conditions may be imposed in the single-stage sparse sampler with $\beta=-1/4$. The branch, $k$ and the trit exponent are selected from the past, and $V$ is the selected physical state, odd-affine in the remaining original quotient. Thus the indicated condition on $Y$ or $Z$ uses at most seven fresh bits and has conditional probability at least $1/128$. Reduce the constant $\delta$ in the companion proof so that the conditional failure of the entire physical comparison, **before** imposing those bits, is less than $1/256$. The probability of both the designated residue and a successful comparison is at least $1/256$ by subtraction. Taking a sufficiently large fixed multiple of $\log d$ disjoint trials again gives a summable row exception. This places the extra ordinary identities on the constructed landings. It gives no upper bound relative to the least nonconvergent integer $N$ for the particular $B$ so obtained.

In contrast, the PR 192 conclusion $N\le B(U)\le JN$, $J<171$, selects *some* failed transfer $U$ from a finite directed path at $N$. It does not assert the same upper bound for every failed transfer or place its chosen $U$ in either of these sparse progressions. Even when $B(u)\sim3u$ and $B(u)<u/171$, one has found a small representative **relative to $u$**. PR 192 would require, for the witness it selects, either a convergent child or a still smaller representative **relative to $B(U)$**. A transfer discharging that particular ordered child with that height bound remains open.
