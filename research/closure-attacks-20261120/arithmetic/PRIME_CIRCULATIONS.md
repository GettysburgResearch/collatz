# Exact all-prime circulations that lose the order of a trajectory

**Status: PROPOSED elementary certificates, independently checkable in
small integer arithmetic. These are not Collatz cycles.** They are
controls for an attempted global arithmetic proof based on summed prime
flux or completely additive ranks. The broader semigroup obstruction
has classical prior art; no novelty claim is made for that general fact.

Use the positive odd map
\(U(n)=(3n+1)/2^{v_2(3n+1)}\), and write
\(v(n)=(v_p(n))_p\) for the vector at **all** primes.

## AR-CIRC-001: an extremal five-state balance is not a cycle

The following are exact ordinary transitions:

| Source | Successor | Division exponent |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 19 | 29 | 1 |
| 25 | 19 | 2 |
| 29 | 11 | 3 |
| 55 | 83 | 1 |
| 83 | 125 | 1 |

Let \(B=\{19,25,29,55,83\}\). Then

\[
 \prod_{n\in B}n=62\,882\,875
 =\prod_{n\in B}U(n),
 \qquad \sum_{n\in B}v_2(3n+1)=8.
 \tag{1}
\]

After common factors are removed, the product identity is simply
\(25\cdot55=11\cdot125\). Thus

\[
 \sum_{n\in B}\bigl(v_p(U(n))-v_p(n)\bigr)=0
 \quad\text{for every prime }p,
\]

and equivalently
\(\prod_{n\in B}(3n+1)=2^8\prod_{n\in B}n\).
All states are ordinary positive odd integers, all sources are distinct,
and every local branch is exact. Nevertheless the set is not invariant:
11 and 125 are outputs outside \(B\). Its edges form two open paths,
\(25\to19\to29\to11\) and \(55\to83\to125\).

The positive balance is also inclusion-minimal and spans an extreme ray
of the all-prime balance cone on these five edges. Indeed a real relation
with coefficients \((c_{19},c_{25},c_{29},c_{55},c_{83})\) is forced by
the prime coordinates 19, 29, 11 and 83 to satisfy, respectively,

\[
 c_{25}=c_{19},\quad c_{29}=c_{19},\quad
 c_{55}=c_{29},\quad c_{83}=c_{55}.
\]

The prime-5 coordinate then vanishes as well. The kernel is precisely
the span of \((1,1,1,1,1)\). Hence passing to an extremal or
inclusion-minimal positive all-prime relation does not recover an
ordered cycle. This is not a claim of globally minimum support size.

## AR-CIRC-002: nonincreasing completely additive potentials are not proper

A slightly larger certificate includes the transition \(5\to1\):

| Source | Successor | Division exponent | Multiplicity |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5 | 1 | 4 | 2 |
| 7 | 11 | 1 | 2 |
| 25 | 19 | 2 | 1 |
| 55 | 83 | 1 | 3 |
| 65 | 49 | 2 | 1 |
| 83 | 125 | 1 | 3 |
| 95 | 143 | 1 | 1 |

The source and successor products, using these multiplicities, agree:

\[
 5^2 7^2 25\,55^3\,65\,83^3\,95
 =11^2 19\,83^3\,49\,125^3\,143.
 \tag{2}
\]

Both sides factor as
\(5^9 7^2 11^3 13\,19\,83^3\).
The weighted total division exponent is 21.

Let \(F\) take values in any totally ordered abelian group, and suppose
it is completely additive on positive odd integers:

\[
 F(ab)=F(a)+F(b),\qquad F(U(n))\le F(n)
 \quad\text{for every positive odd }n.
 \tag{3}
\]

Complete additivity gives \(F(1)=0\). Apply \(F\) to (2).
The positive weighted sum of all edge drifts is zero. Since every drift
is nonpositive in the ordered group, each drift is zero. In particular
\(F(5)=F(1)=0\). Therefore

\[
 \boxed{F(5^k)=0\quad(k\ge0).}
 \tag{4}
\]

Such an \(F\) cannot have finite sublevel sets, because its zero sublevel
already contains all powers of five. This rules out a proper,
nonincreasing completely additive rank even if it is allowed to take
arbitrary real prime weights or a finite lexicographically ordered vector
of such weights. The seven-state certificate alone proves this; no
assumption that arbitrary orbits converge is used.

The five-state certificate separately rules out strict decrease at every
nontrivial odd state for every completely additive potential. A bounded
or state-dependent nonadditive correction is outside these conclusions.
The certificates do not address a potential that only needs to decrease
after a selected variable number of actual steps.

## 3. Prior-art boundary

Applegate and Lagarias,
[*The 3x+1 Semigroup*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411140),
Journal of Number Theory 117 (2006), 146--159, identify the multiplicative
semigroup generated by the backward shortcut ratios and by 2. Taking
reciprocals gives the corresponding wild-semigroup description. The
author-hosted paper is
[available here](https://websites.umich.edu/lagarias/doc/3x%2B1semigroup.pdf).
This is a much broader prior result about the loss of ordered trajectory
information under multiplicative combination. No claim that these small
certificates discover that general obstruction is intended.

The present controls are useful because they retain exact accelerated
ordinary branches, give a five-edge extreme positive circulation, and
force \(F(5)=0\) with seven explicit source values. They can be checked
without importing the semigroup theorem. They also clarify why checking
every prime simultaneously is still weaker than checking the actual
cyclic ordering.

## 4. What the ordered continuation actually added

`CLOCK_HEIGHT.md` keeps index distances and ordinary heights. Its
increasing clock \(t_i=3i-\log_2 n_i\) charges a repeated prime power
to a real interval of the actual path. That information is absent from
(1)--(2). It yields a sharper height-concentration estimate and an
all-cycle maximum-height versus prime-support theorem.

The ordered theorem still allows growing support. No certificate here
converts full-denominator divisibility into an impossibility, extracts
one ordinary orbit from changing rational witnesses, or proves a
contradictory upper bound on the support of every candidate cycle.
