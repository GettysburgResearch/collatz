# A finite union of translated multiplicative groups cannot have bounded return gaps

**PROPOSED, source-qualified on the unit-equation theorem.** Let F be a finite union of sets b_i+u_i G_i, where b_i and u_i are rational, u_i is nonzero, and each G_i is a finite-rank subgroup of the positive rationals. Every interval of sampled values below concerns ordinary positive odd states under U. There is no infinite distinct odd orbit whose visits to F have bounded gaps and occur infinitely often. In particular any odd orbit lying eventually in F must be periodic from some point on.

Translating the group is significant: the odd states of an arbitrarily long initial run of shortcut odd steps from 2^h−1 belong to −1+⟨2,3⟩. Simple sparsity in arbitrarily translated finite time windows would fail for this fixed set.

## Rigidity of a bounded-odd-clock return

Fix a positive gap k and two indexed charts i,j. There is a finite exceptional set of positive **endpoints** with the following property. If x∈b_i+u_iG_i and y=U^k(x)∈b_j+u_jG_j lies outside this set, then

    2^(s_k)(y−b_j) = 3^k(x−b_i),                         (1)

where s_k is the sum of the k actual valuations. Equivalently the ordinary affine word traced from x to y sends b_i to b_j by the same affine formula. Its parity need not be postulated for the rational centers.

**Proof.** Use the odd-clock telescoping identity in [the rank packet](README.md). The following tuple sums to zero:

    2^(s_k)(y−b_j),  −3^k(x−b_i),  2^(s_k)b_j,  −3^k b_i,
    −3^(k−1−l)2^(s_l)    (0≤l<k).

Remove zero terms (only the two pure center terms can vanish). Every remaining term belongs to the fixed finite-rank multiplicative group generated by the two G's, signs, 2, 3, the two scales and the nonzero centers. Choose a minimal vanishing subsum containing the first coordinate. Such a subsum can be found by partitioning the tuple into minimal zero sums. Within a minimal zero sum, every ratio of coordinates ranges over a finite set depending only on the group and the number of coordinates. Indeed, divide by one coordinate and apply the nondegenerate unit-equation theorem to the others. There are only finitely many choices of subsum.

If this subsum consists just of the first two coordinates, (1) holds. Otherwise it contains a coordinate of the form c·2^v, where c belongs to a fixed finite list and 0≤v≤s_k. The ratio bound therefore bounds |y−b_j| by a fixed constant: its absolute value is a bounded ratio times |c|·2^(v−s_k). An ordinary positive y in this bounded interval has finitely many possibilities. This proves the stated rigidity without a bound on any intermediate valuation. The argument provides no numerical upper height for the exceptional endpoints. ∎

## The binary divisibility obstruction

Suppose an infinite sequence of visits of a distinct orbit has gaps at most K. After finitely many visits all its endpoints avoid the finite union of exceptional lists for k≤K and all chart pairs. At each visit choose any witnessing chart. Multiply (1) along the visits. If x_* is the first value on this tail, the subsequent sampled endpoints satisfy

    x_*−b_* = 2^S 3^(−J)(y−b_j),

where J is elapsed odd time and S is elapsed shortcut time. The 2-adic valuations of y−b_j are bounded below uniformly because y is integral and the list of rational denominators is fixed. S tends to infinity. Hence the fixed rational x_*−b_* would be divisible in the 2-adic sense to arbitrarily high order. This forces x_*=b_*, contradicting the nonzero multiplicative coordinate u_*g. ∎

For a single center b having no rational affine return word with at least one odd step, (1) is impossible for any positive k. On an infinite distinct orbit the gaps between visits to that translated chart then tend to infinity. In particular this applies when b has even denominator in lowest terms, since the fixed point of any binary word with q≥1 odds and t positive steps, when it exists, has denominator dividing the odd number 2^t−3^q. For resonant centers the long-run example above explains the need to retain the exceptional alternative (1).

This excludes models in which finitely many affine families of finite multiplicative rank control ordinary odd states at bounded odd intervals. No argument here puts an arbitrary hypothetical divergent orbit in such a family.
