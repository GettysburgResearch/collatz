# Polynomial digit-state rigidity over commutative rings

**AR1--AR5: PROPOSED, self-audited.** These are exact source-classifier
statements, not termination theorems for arbitrary Collatz orbits. The
finite-dimensional linear invariant result in parent pass2/RIGIDITY.md is
credited. This pass enlarges the class to nonlinear polynomial digit machines
in every integer base, and classifies invariance outside finitely many edges.

## Definition: one fixed finite polynomial description

Fix a base B>=2, a commutative ring R with identity, a finite dimension d,
an initial state v in R^d, polynomial maps P_0,...,P_{B-1}:R^d -> R^d,
and a polynomial output H:R^d -> R. Read the CANONICAL most-significant-first
base-B digits of n>0, starting at v and applying the corresponding maps.
Write f(n) for H of the final state. Finite vector outputs are treated
coordinatewise. The zero ring is harmless: all outputs are identical.

Coefficients and v are fixed once for all inputs. Polynomial degree is
arbitrary but finite. There is no sign test, floor, extraction of a state
digit, arbitrary rational division, or input-length-dependent change of
program. Polynomial evaluation can use ring operations and fixed coefficients.
This is a finite algebraic recurrence class, not the class of all algorithms.

Use the shortcut T(n)=n/2 if n is even and (3n+1)/2 otherwise.

## AR1. Finite commutative quotients separate nonzero elements

Let S be a commutative ring finitely generated as a ring. For every nonzero
g in S there is a homomorphism pi:S -> Q to a FINITE commutative ring with
pi(g)!=0. We need element separation, not a claim that every quotient is finite.

Here is a proof with its standard inputs identified. S is a finite-type
Z-algebra and hence Noetherian [A]. Choose a maximal ideal m containing
Ann(g), which is proper. The element g/1 is nonzero in S_m: otherwise an
annihilator of g outside m would exist. Krull intersection in the Noetherian
local ring S_m gives an integer k>=1 for which

    g/1 is not in (m S_m)^k.                          (2)

Thus g is not in m^k. The field S/m is finite: Z is Jacobson, finite-type
maps send closed points to closed points, and the corresponding residue
extension is finite [B]. Since m is finitely generated, each m^i/m^{i+1}
is a finite-dimensional vector space over that finite field. The finite
filtration shows S/m^k is finite. This quotient preserves g, proving AR1.
Krull intersection is the standard input [C]; residual separation itself is
standard algebra, not a claimed new theorem about rings.

Finite FIELD quotients alone would be insufficient for a general R:
nonzero nilpotents map to zero in every field. For example e in
F_2[e]/(e^2) is separated by the finite ring itself, but not by its residue
field. The executable retains an explicit nilpotent-valued noninvariant
sequence as a control.

## AR2. All-base nonlinear polynomial invariants are constant

**Theorem.** For every machine in the definition, the identities

    f(T(n))=f(n) for all n>0                          (3)

force f constant on the positive integers.

Let S be the subring of R generated by every coefficient of the P_d and H,
and every coordinate of v. It is finitely generated, and all reachable
states and outputs lie in S. If f(a)!=f(b), apply AR1 to their nonzero
difference. Reduction by pi makes a deterministic digit machine on the
finite state set Q^d, with finite output set Q. It is therefore B-automatic.
All the identities (3), for ALL inputs at once, survive the same quotient.
FD3 of FINITE_DEFECT.md at N=1 forces its outputs constant, contradicting
pi(f(a)-f(b))!=0. No growth bound or finite-dimensional linearization of
polynomial iterates has been assumed.

The enlargement is strict over the real numbers. Take one state, initial
value 2, P_0(x)=x^2, P_1(x)=x^2+1, H(x)=x. On the binary all-one word of
length ell the output is at least 2^{2^ell}. A fixed real linear digit
representation has output norm at most C M^ell, by a matrix norm bound.
Thus this one-state polynomial sequence is not in the old finite-linear
class. It is NOT invariant; the new theorem excludes it and other genuinely
nonlinear machines from being nonconstant exact component classifiers.

The same method extends any finite-output automatic obstruction expressed
by equalities of finitely generated ring values. It does not transfer
inequalities or approximate equalities through a finite quotient.

## AR3. Complete finite-defect normal form for polynomial machines

Suppose instead that (3) holds for every n>=N, for a fixed N>=1, and put
K=ceil(N/2). Then the normal forms in FD3 hold with values in R:

* If B is not a power of two, f is constant for every n>=K.
* If B=2^r, f is constant for n>=K except for independently constant values
  on the truncated rays {2^j m >=K}, where m<N is odd and divisible by three.
* In both cases the values below K are unrestricted.

To prove necessity, choose one good positive odd ternary-unit source a>=N
and set c=f(a). Any failure of the asserted equality between a good source
and a, or between two points on one exceptional ray, gives a nonzero output
difference in S. AR1 preserves it in a finite quotient, contradicting FD3.
There are finitely many exceptional roots, so the displayed form is exhaustive.

Conversely all the displayed assignments have finite-state representations
in their specified bases, including arbitrary labels from R. A DFAO with
states 1,...,s can be implemented on the one-hot vectors e_i in R^s by
linear digit maps e_i -> e_{delta(i,d)} and output H(e_i)=label_i. Hence they
are polynomial machines and are invariant above N by FD3's direct proof.
When R is the zero ring the assignments all coincide and the conclusion
remains true. This proves both directions, not just a necessary obstruction.

The finite defect set may be any finite set: take N greater than its largest
source. The normal form may enlarge the list of permitted low exceptions;
it does not assert that every n<N is actually defective.

## AR4. Polynomial automorphisms and invertible linear/projective models

If every P_d is a polynomial automorphism, eventual invariance implies
GLOBAL constancy, including all inputs below N. Include the finitely many
inverse-polynomial coefficients in S. The inverse composition identities
survive every finite quotient, so the reduced digit maps are permutations
of Q^d. FD4 forces global constancy. AR1 then rules out every unequal output
pair in R. The required hypothesis is polynomial inverses, not an unproved
assumption that arbitrary injective maps remain injective after reduction.

In particular this covers invertible linear digit maps over a field, with
the inverse matrix coefficients included in S. Noninvertible tables can have
finite-defect ray exceptions, so the reversibility distinction is necessary.

There is a related projective statement. Let A_0,...,A_{B-1} be fixed
invertible r-by-r matrices over a field F and let v!=0. The entire projective
state

    f(n)=[A_{d_ell} ... A_{d_1} v]

(with the product ordered according to the chosen update convention) cannot
be a nonconstant eventually Collatz-invariant function. If two projective
states differ, one two-by-two wedge coordinate g is nonzero. Form the
finitely generated DOMAIN containing the matrix/vector coefficients, and
adjoin inverses of g, all matrix determinants, and one nonzero coordinate
of v. A maximal ideal of this finite-type Z-algebra has finite residue
field [B]. Reduction preserves the unequal projective pair, matrix
invertibility and validity of every projective state. The finite projective
space carries permutation transitions, contradicting FD4.

This projective corollary is a written proof, not a separate executed
projective-machine census. It does not cover arbitrary rational maps with
input-dependent poles, or arbitrary discontinuous functions of the state.

## AR5. What would still be needed for Collatz

The convergence indicator chi(n)=1_{n eventually reaches 1} is an exact
invariant and chi(1)=1. If it had ANY fixed finite polynomial digit-machine
representation of this kind, AR2 would force chi=1 and prove Collatz.
No such representation is established here. In the presence of a hypothetical
counterexample the theorem instead proves that this entire representation
class is unavailable for chi. Even arbitrary finite dimension, polynomial
degree, coefficient field and input base do not escape the obstruction.

This is not a proof that a prescribed positive source converges. It is also
not a result about the complexity of one orbit's temporal parity sequence.
The domain throughout is the original integer source.
