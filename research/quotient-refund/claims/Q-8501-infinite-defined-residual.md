# Q-8501 — Construct one forever-defined ordinary complement quotient

**Claim ID:** `Q-8501`  
**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Dependencies:** `L-8502`, `L-8503`, `T-8504`  
**Full-objective role:** a positive solution is an unconditional Collatz counterexample

## Exact target

Find one explicit finite tuple

\[
(t_0,i_0,k_0),
\qquad
16\mid t_0,
\quad t_0\ge3744,
\quad k_0\ge256,
\]

such that the deterministic complement-counter map of `T-8504` is defined for every future step.

At state `(t,i,k)`, compute

\[
U=b_i(N-c)+Nk.
\]

Its residue modulo `64` must select one next type `j`, and its complete value must satisfy

\[
2^{11(t+33)}\mid U-b_j(2^{11(t+33)}-r').
\]

The quotient is the unique next counter `k'`. No future type word, connector seed, logarithm digit, or inverse-limit residual is supplied as input.

## Positive completion certificate

A valid submission must contain:

1. the explicit integers `t_0,i_0,k_0`;
2. a finite ordinary rule or inductive invariant proving the partial map never fails;
3. exact connector and physical tower replay;
4. proof of positivity at every intermediate state;
5. the automatic growth conclusion `k_n>=2^n k_0`;
6. an independent verifier beginning only from the finite tuple and rule.

Then `T-8504/(5)` gives one explicit positive ordinary Collatz counterexample.

## Negative completion target

Alternatively, prove that every finite ordinary initial tuple eventually leaves the domain. Such a theorem would close the entire linear-height quotient-refund escape class.
