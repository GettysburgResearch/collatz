## L-9507: Finite prime support exclusion

**Claim ID:** L-9507  
**Title:** A nonperiodic exact survivor must introduce infinitely many odd primes  
**Status:** PROPOSED  
**Authoring agent:** prior thread; consolidated by `gpt56-h-01`  
**Reviewing agents:** none  
**Created:** 2026-07-21  
**Dependencies:** standard S-unit finiteness theorem

### Statement

Write exact states as

\[
p_i=2^{3r_i+2}u_i,
\]

so

\[
2^{3r_{i+1}+2}u_{i+1}-3^{2r_i+1}u_i=1.
\]

If all odd `u_i` are supported on a fixed finite prime set, then the orbit is
eventually periodic. Hence every nonperiodic infinite exact orbit introduces
infinitely many odd primes.

### Proof

Both terms in the displayed equation are units in the multiplicative group
generated by the fixed prime set together with 2 and 3. The standard S-unit
finiteness theorem gives only finitely many solutions of `X-Y=1`; hence only
finitely many adjacent transitions occur. Determinism then forces repetition.

### Gap audit

This claim imports the S-unit finiteness theorem and should receive a citation
review before promotion.

## L-9508: Prime reuse through affine offsets

**Claim ID:** L-9508  
**Title:** Reused core primes divide intervening block offsets  
**Status:** PROPOSED  
**Authoring agent:** prior thread; consolidated by `gpt56-h-01`  
**Reviewing agents:** none  
**Created:** 2026-07-21  
**Dependencies:** D-9501

### Statement

For a segment `i<j`, write

\[
p_j=\frac{3^{S_{i,j}}p_i+A_{i,j}}{2^{E_{i,j}}}.
\]

If an odd prime `q != 3` divides both cores `u_i` and `u_j`, then

\[
\boxed{q\mid A_{i,j}.}
\]

In particular, adjacent cores are coprime.

### Proof

Modulo `q`, both `p_i` and `p_j` vanish, while the power of 2 is invertible.
The segment formula therefore gives `A_(i,j)=0 mod q`. For adjacent states the
offset is a power of 2, so no odd common divisor exists.

## L-9509: Two-sided exact-window density and subpolynomial toll

**Claim ID:** L-9509  
**Title:** Exact past/future windows have product density  
**Status:** PROPOSED  
**Authoring agent:** prior thread; consolidated by `gpt56-h-01`  
**Reviewing agents:** none  
**Created:** 2026-07-21  
**Dependencies:** L-9502, L-9504

### Statement

Let `L_(h,k)` be the set of positive exact states that admit an exact future of
length `h` and an exact past of length `k`. Then

\[
\boxed{
d(\mathcal L_{h,k})=
\frac1{12}\left(\frac27\right)^h\left(\frac38\right)^k.
}
\]

If `p_j` are distinct states on a bi-sided-interior portion of an infinite exact
orbit and `Q_L=p_L/M_L`, then, for every fixed `h,k`,

\[
Q_L\le C_{h,k}L^{\delta_{h,k}+o(1)},
\qquad
\delta_{h,k}=\frac1{12}(2/7)^h(3/8)^k.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{Q_L=L^{o(1)}.}
\]

### Proof

For a fixed future word `f`, the starting cylinder has modulus
`3*2^(E_f+2)`. For a fixed past word `b`, increasing its start by
`3*2^(E_b+2)` increases its endpoint by `12*3^(S_b)`, so the endpoint
progression has modulus `12*3^(S_b)`. These moduli have gcd 12, and the
intersection is nonempty because the concatenated finite word is realizable.
Thus a fixed past/future pair has density

\[
2^{-(E_f+2)}3^{-(S_b+1)}.
\]

Summing independently over words gives

\[
\frac14(2/7)^h\cdot\frac13(3/8)^k.
\]

Standard arithmetic-progression counting and partial summation show that the
sum of `1/(n-1)` over any `L` distinct members is at most
`delta_(h,k) log L+O_(h,k)(1)`. Apply this to interior orbit states and the
Euler product of L-9504. Since `delta_(h,k)` can be made arbitrarily small by
fixing a sufficiently large window, `Q_L=L^(o(1))`.

### Gap audit

For an actual nonperiodic infinite orbit, T-9501 and T-9502 strengthen this to
bounded `Q_L`. This claim remains useful as a finite-window estimate that does
not assume harmonic summability of the full infinite-survivor set.

## L-9510: Arbitrarily long expanding shadows and constant-word exclusion

**Claim ID:** L-9510  
**Title:** Long expanding exact prefixes exist, but an expanding constant itinerary has no positive integer start  
**Status:** PROPOSED  
**Authoring agent:** prior thread; consolidated by `gpt56-h-01`  
**Reviewing agents:** none  
**Created:** 2026-07-21  
**Dependencies:** L-9502, L-9503

### Statement

For every `r>=3` and `k>=1`, there is a positive exact start realizing `k`
consecutive letters `r`, and every one of those `k` transitions increases `p`.
No positive ordinary integer realizes the constant itinerary `r,r,...`
forever.

### Proof

L-9502 realizes every finite word, including `r^k`. Since

\[
a_r=\frac34(9/8)^r>1
\]

for `r>=3`, every exact transition satisfies `p^+=a_rp+1>p`.

An infinite constant itinerary would be fixed under the affine map in the
2-adic limit, hence its start would equal

\[
p^*=-\frac1{a_r-1}<0
\]

as a rational number. It is therefore not a positive ordinary integer.

### Consequence

No bounded-depth descent theorem and no finite itinerary forbidden-factor list
can settle the infinite problem.
