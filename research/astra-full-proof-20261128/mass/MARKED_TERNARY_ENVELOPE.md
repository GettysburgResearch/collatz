# What a component-restricted ternary trace would require

Valbuena's positive arithmetic trace $h$ vanishes on multiples of 3, is positive on every positive integer prime to 3, and solves

$$
h(n)=\tfrac12h(2n)+\tfrac32\mathbf1_{n\equiv2\ (3)}h((2n-1)/3)=:(Lh)(n).
$$

His greatest lower semicontinuous stationary density on $\mathbf Z_3$ agrees with $h$ at every positive integer. The construction takes the infimum of $h$ over ordinary integers in each ternary cylinder, then increases the conductor. The periodic marked first-hit mean assumes a periodic source mask. Both the trace and the regularity statements are external. [Valbuena, theorem, marked first-hit proposition and envelope construction at a pinned revision](https://github.com/x1xhlol/collatz-two-coordinate-obstruction/blob/a7275d5a73814d3d9a400c6fe6577de9542b03a3/paper/positive-stationary-envelope.tex)

**Elementary density.** For every odd positive unit $y$ and every class $a\bmod 3^j$, choose $e>0$ such that $2^e y\equiv1+3a\pmod{3^{j+1}}$. Such an $e$ exists since 2 generates the units modulo every positive power of 3. For example, $v_3(2^{2\cdot 3^i}-1)=i+1$ follows from $4-1=3$ and $v_3(u^2+u+1)=1$ for $u\equiv1\pmod3$. Then $x=(2^e y-1)/3$ is an odd positive integer in the prescribed class, and its single accelerated odd step lands at $y$. Thus the predecessors of each fixed odd unit meet every ternary unit cylinder. Every ordinary component contains an odd unit, by following odd departures and halving a unit successor as needed.

If one could construct a lower semicontinuous density agreeing at ordinary integers with $h\mathbf1_C$ for the component $C$ of 1, universal convergence would follow. In the presence of a second component its odd unit predecessors force the infimum of the proposed density to vanish in every cylinder. Its positive value at 1 then contradicts lower semicontinuity. Two calibrations identify the strength of this proposed construction.

**One step and actual inverse subtrees.** Let $A$ and $B$ be the complete shortcut predecessor cones of 5 and 85. Their forward paths meet at 8; neither target reaches the other. Hence the cones are disjoint and both meet every ternary cylinder. On the positive unit integers define the inverse Markov kernel by $P(n,x)=w(x,n)h(x)/h(n)$ where $T(x)=n$, with $w=1/2$ on an even predecessor and $w=3/2$ on an odd predecessor. The displayed harmonic identity makes the row sums one. Define $H(n)=h(n)\Pr_n(\text{the inverse path eventually stays in }A)$ on units and extend it by zero on multiples of 3. The event is unchanged by deleting a finite initial segment. Consequently $LH=H$ and $0\le H\le h$. Every inverse path started in $A$ remains in $A$, and every inverse path started in $B$ stays in $B$. Therefore $H=h$ on $A$ and $H=0$ on $B$. Its ordinary lower envelope is identically zero despite $H(5)>0$. The construction uses a genuine positive one-step harmonic and physical convergent subtrees. The ratio $H/h$ can vary at the finite attachment to the cycle and is not a one-step component indicator.

**Entire two-step components.** On the standard basin put $p(n)=(-1)^{\tau_1(n)}$, where $\tau_1$ is the first hit of 1. Set $p=0$ elsewhere. The identity $p(Tn)=-p(n)$ also holds across $1\mapsto2$. Put $H_\pm=h\mathbf1_{p=\pm1}$. Then $LH_+=H_-$ and $LH_-=H_+$ on all positive integers, so each is exactly harmonic for $L^2$. The sets $p=+1$ and $p=-1$ are the two entire asynchronous components of $T^2$ that lead to its fixed points 1 and 2. The immediate odd predecessors of 1 described above have an even gap $e$ and positive phase. Those of 5 have an odd gap and negative phase, since $\tau_1(5)=4$. Each phase therefore meets every ternary unit cylinder. Neither $H_+$ nor $H_-$ has a lower semicontinuous extension agreeing with its ordinary values. These examples satisfy the exact two-step component identities regardless of all unknown orbits.

The unmarked envelope, domination by it, one-step inverse harmonicity, and exact complete-component invariance for a fixed power each leave the obstruction visible. A proof for the one-step component of 1 must supply a marked ordinary trace theorem specific to that component. Haar $L^p$ regularity alone cannot prescribe values on the countable set of ordinary roots. All arguments after the external trace identity are written deductions.
