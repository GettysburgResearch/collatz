# The native boundary of the first-repeat survival product

Let $T$ be the shortcut map and define the physical one-step weight $c(n)=1$ at even $n$, $c(n)=3n/(3n+1)$ at odd $n$. Put $P f(n)=c(n)f(Tn)$. For an ordinary positive source, let $G_R$ be the product stopped at the earlier of its first repeated state and its $R$-th odd departure, multiplied by the indicator that it has yet to visit $1$ at that odd clock. The test of visiting $1$ uses the original trajectory. The product and indicator both decrease in $R$, so $L(n)=\lim_R G_R(n)$ exists. [Finite repeat-stopped observable](../descent/SURVIVAL_WINDOWS.md)

**Exact source term.** For a nonstandard positive cycle $C$ put $\pi_C=\prod_{x\in C}c(x)$. At every positive integer,

$$
 L(n)-P L(n)=g(n),\qquad
 g(n)=
 \begin{cases}
   (1-c(n))\pi_C,& n\text{ lies on a nonstandard cycle }C,\\
   0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                                        \tag{1}
$$

Every source reaching $1$ has $L=0$. For a transient source, removing its first step removes exactly its first factor from the product through the initial visit to a repeated state, or from the whole infinite product on escape. At a cycle vertex the first-repeat product contains exactly one lap, hence equals $\pi_C$ at every phase. In particular $L(2n)=L(n)$ everywhere, including on a cycle.

Write $w_t(n)=\prod_{0\le j<t} c(T^j n)$, with $t$ counting shortcut steps. Unrolling (1) gives $L(n)=\sum_{j<t} w_j(n)g(T^j n)+w_t(n)L(T^t n)$. On an orbit reaching a nonstandard cycle the terminal term tends to zero because each full lap multiplies it by $\pi_C<1$. Thus the accumulated cycle term supplies all its weight. On escape the successive positive states are distinct, and the uniform orbit-set bound $\#(O\cap[1,Y])\ll Y^\eta$ for some $\eta<1$ makes $\sum_j(1-c(T^j n))$ finite. It follows that $w_\infty(n)>0$ and $L(T^t n)\to1$ as $t\to\infty$. The terminal term then tends to $w_\infty(n)$ and the entire cycle sum is zero. The same orbit packing up to the first repeat gives a uniform positive lower bound on $L$ over bad ordinary sources. [Distinct-state estimate, §10](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/177/v1)

**Elementary uniform native lower bound.** Here is the distinct-state argument of Weaver §10 and its application to this particular product. Put $j=\lceil\log_2Y\rceil$ for $Y\ge2$, and $\eta=H_2(3/5)<1$, with $H_2$ the binary entropy. Each length-$j$ parity word occurs in at most one source through $Y$, since it prescribes one residue class modulo $2^j$. The words with at least $3j/5$ odd steps number at most $2^{\eta j}$ by the binomial entropy bound. With fewer odd steps, the affine formula bounds the positive integer endpoint by $Y3^{3j/5}2^{-j}+(3/2)^j\le3^{3j/5}+(3/2)^j$. The intercept bound follows by maximizing each branch over the positive input. Both terms are $O(2^{\eta j})$, since $5^5>4\cdot3^6$ gives $(3/5)\log_2 3<\eta$ and $\log_2(3/2)<(3/5)\log_2 3$. Consequently the entire image $T^j([1,Y]\cap\mathbb N)$ has at most $C Y^\eta$ elements. On any one nonrepeating orbit $T^j$ is injective. On an eventually periodic orbit, remove the last at most $j$ transient vertices before entry to its cycle; the remaining transient images are distinct transient vertices and the cyclic images are distinct cyclic vertices. Thus every complete forward orbit $O$ obeys $\#(O\cap[1,Y])\le j+C Y^\eta$. Partial summation from one yields a universal $C_*$ with $\sum_{z\in O}z^{-1}\le C_*$, counting distinct vertices only. The departures through the first repeat are distinct, so $\log(1+1/(3z))\le1/(3z)$ gives the explicit form $L(n)\ge e^{-C_*/3}$ on every bad orbit, and $G_R(n)\ge L(n)$ at every finite clock. This consumer requires no finite verification floor or asymptotic input beyond the displayed elementary proof. The further fact that a single bad component has positive native source density is a separate cited input. **Natural mean from finite target data.** Separately assume the established existence of each shortcut target's predecessor natural density $d_z$. The first-repeat product yields the exact set formula $L(n)=\mathbf1_{1\notin O(n)}\prod_{z\in O(n),\ z\ {\rm odd}}c(z)$, counting distinct vertices. Truncating the product to $z\le M$ has uniform error at most $C M^{\eta-1}$ by partial summation of the orbit count above. The truncated mark is a polynomial in finitely many true target-hit indicators, including the one at $1$. Every nonempty intersection of finitely many specified predecessor cones is itself one of those cones: on a trajectory visiting all targets, the first one encountered has all the others in its future. Thus the truncated mark has a natural mean expressible in finitely many $d_z$. Uniform approximation proves that $L$ has an ordinary natural mean $m_L$, equal to its Abel mean. If the bad set has minimum $a$, the same tail bound, now applied to each bad orbit, gives $(1-C a^{\eta-1})(1-d_1)\le m_L\le1-d_1$. This is an existence and comparison statement; numerical values of the target densities are not supplied by it. The uniform lower-bound finite consumer above only uses positive lower density at a single bad target. [Valbuena, basin-density theorem and external verification scope](https://github.com/x1xhlol/collatz-two-coordinate-obstruction/blob/a7275d5a73814d3d9a400c6fe6577de9542b03a3/README.md)

**Abel boundary.** For $s>1$ set $A_s=\sum_{n\ge1}L(n)n^{-s}$ and $\Xi_s=\sum_{y\ge1}(3\mathbf1_{y\equiv 2\ (3)}-1)L(y)y^{-s}$. Then, as $s\downarrow1$,

$$
 \Xi_s=\log(4/3)(s-1) A_s
     -2\sum_{\substack{x\text{ odd and on a}\\\text{nonstandard cycle }C(x)}}
                \frac{\pi_{C(x)}}{x(3x+1)}+o(1).                    \tag{2}
$$

This compares two scalar quantities at the same $s$, without assuming that the native Abel mean has a limit. For each odd $x$ write $y=(3x+1)/2$. Its trit-two contribution is exactly

$$
 3L(y)y^{-s}
 =3(2/3)^s x^{-s}(1+1/(3x))^{1-s}\,[L(x)-g(x)] .
$$

Replacing the factor $(1+1/(3x))^{1-s}$ by one costs $O(s-1)$ after summation because $0\le L(x)-g(x)\le1$. Doubling invariance gives $\sum_{x\ {\rm odd}}L(x)x^{-s}=(1-2^{-s})A_s$. Thus the coefficient on $A_s$ in $\Xi_s$ is $3^{1-s}(2^s-1)-1=(s-1)\log(4/3)+O((s-1)^2)$. The remainder tends to zero since $A_s\le\zeta(s)$. At odd cycle vertices $g(x)=\pi_{C(x)}/(3x+1)$, so dominated convergence evaluates the absolutely convergent cycle series. This proves (2).

**Source-qualified physical limit.** With the signed native endpoint estimate of PR 215, the series $\Xi=\sum_y(3\mathbf1_{y\equiv2\ (3)}-1)L(y)/y$ converges in increasing physical order. Indeed (1) and $0\le g\le1-c$ give $|L(n)-L(Tn)|\le1-c(n)\le1/(3n)$. On $Y\le y<2Y$ take $t=\lfloor(\log_2Y)/4\rfloor$. Since $T^i y\ge2^{-i}Y$, the substitution of $L(T^t y)$ for $L(y)$ costs $O(2^t/Y)$ per source. The cited total-variation bound with the bounded true endpoint test $z\mapsto L(z)$ makes the signed trit sum on every subinterval of this octave $O_A(Y(1+\log Y)^{-A})$ for every fixed $A$. Partial summation and summation over octaves give the physical-series tail $O_A((1+\log Y)^{1-A})$ for $A>1$. Abel summation then gives $\Xi_s\to\Xi$. In particular the native Abel mean $(s-1)A_s$ has a limit by (2) under this written input. The error rate is in powers of the logarithm of the lower cutoff. It cannot be used as a uniform bound below the reciprocal scale of a least bad source.
