# Attempting the universal closing step

**Full theorem: OPEN.** The aim of this pass was a proof for every positive
ordinary integer. The deductions below advance specific routes, but their
last universal implication has not been established. This document keeps
the actual source and its quantifiers explicit.

## 1. Complete the random relative-section argument

The relative section of `x,5x` is described by a slope `5*3^k` and a carry
`c`. The new Foster proof controls both quantities jointly. The `k`
coordinate has zero conditional drift and uniformly renewed variance;
the normalized square-root carry has a strict contraction. A logarithmic
function of `k` plus a small weighted carry is proper on the exact lattice
and has strictly negative three-step drift outside a finite set.

This closes recurrence to that finite set. PR146's latest finite control
words then close communication to the exact section root. Repeated root
visits follow for fair binary input. A law of large numbers makes the
common affine slope arbitrarily small along those visits. Finite cylinder
unions convert that result into the ordinary paired-descent density
statement in this packet.

The pointwise transfer is where the proposed full proof fails. Once one
specified ordinary source reaches one, its future parity drive is the
fixed periodic sequence of the `1,2` cycle. It is not a fresh fair drive.
Indeed every convergent source has only finitely many section-root
returns: a later root return puts the target at five or ten, and after
the target reaches its `1,2` cycle ratio five is impossible. This fact
holds whether or not convergence of the target was initially known.

Thus the probability-one infinite-return event actually omits the very
certified-source class to which forward-five closure must be applied.
For each fixed delay the successful ordinary set can have density one
while its countable intersection contains none of those sources. This
is a proved quantifier distinction, not an unexplained appeal to
randomness or to general difficulty.

Numerical equality at one source is also different from an affine
identity on its entire cylinder. The section root guarantees the latter;
an accidental equality at a single integer need not be the root.

## 2. Use the full return dictionary to finish energy renewal

For an actual invariant metric label, put

\[
e(x)=d(f(5x),f(x)),\qquad
E_s(X)=\sum_{\substack{x\le X\\x\text{ odd},\ 3\nmid x}}x^{-s}e(x).
\]

A paired ordinary return preserves `e(x)` exactly and can move it to a
smaller endpoint. The partial dictionaries in PRs166–169 and the
independent two-residue calculation here give useful inequalities
`E_s(X)<=R_s(X)+kappa*E_s(theta*X)` with `kappa<1`.
They prove a comparison with the uncovered energy `R_s`; they do not
prove `R_s(infinity)<infinity`.

The random-section theorem supplies a full-measure return dictionary,
so it is natural to try to complete that positive kernel. The attempt
can now be tested exactly. The section root has infinite mean return
time. A uniform exponential deviation bound for the parity count shows
that the typical long return has large inverse affine slope. PR167's
finite-family averaging identity then forces an infinite supremum of
the inverse-fiber column mass for every `0<s<1`. This is stronger than
merely losing a convenient numerical margin below one.

It rules out this unrestricted, label-independent positive-column
completion. It does not rule out a support-sensitive estimate for actual
ordinary invariant labels, signed cancellation, different weights, or
separately certified zero-defect sources. Establishing one of those
mechanisms is the first unsupported step of this energy route.

PR146's new construction transports every possible five comparison into
every prescribed arithmetic progression while preserving the two
endpoints. Consequently a large collection of successful residue rules
cannot be interpreted as a percentage of the universal theorem proved.
The failed comparisons, if any exist, can be replicated throughout
the remaining arithmetic domains.

## 3. Force an actual coefficient excursion

The first-crossing lemma improves the affine remainder from a crude
exponential allowance to less than one third per preceding odd input.
For a fixed eligible `k` this makes a linear number of actual parity
steps useful in the least-failure reduction. The simple-prefix Mellin
budget goes further: above an explicit floor it pays the entire
multiplicative correction independently of the observation time.

The missing implication is a forced low coefficient excursion for the
specified source, or another argument excluding sources without that
excursion. On a hypothetical nonrepeating positive orbit, the existing
orbitwise product bound instead implies full coefficient escape to
infinity. That behavior is compatible with never crossing the required
lower threshold. On a cycle, repeatedly reusing a distinct-state budget
is invalid. The new tests therefore do not by themselves eliminate
either class of counterexample.

Extending the time horizon also does not extend uniform binary
independence. At clock `h` the actual parity word is fixed by the source
modulo `2^h`. An interval containing only `N` ordinary sources does not
sample all those words when `h` greatly exceeds `log_2(N)`. A new
distribution theorem on that sparse set of residues is required for a
stronger counting conclusion.

## 4. Finish the supplied-certificate transformer

The additional valuation-one identities are actual ordinary paths.
They preserve the two prescribed endpoints and compose with the older
ladders. The source refresh showed that several parallel packets had
already obtained those identities, so they are retained here as
cross-checks with corrected attribution.

Their complete balanced architecture is insufficient even on known
convergent inputs. The example `85,425` has first-hit shortcut times
eight and thirty-nine. There is no common clock with endpoints `(1,5)`.
Appending source-cycle padding cannot change that mismatch. Other
ordinary certificates may certainly prove both endpoints convergent.

The special PR146 sufficient progression begins with one followed by
threes. The correction decoder cannot transport a whole prefix of the
form `(1,3 repeated r times,b)` with equal clock and equal odd count.
This does not exclude later prefixes containing additional general
letters. The alternative two-known-orbit
proposal simplifies to the ordinary question whether `u,5u` convergent
forces `5u-2` convergent in the relevant class. Convergence of the
original source adds no independent premise after its first odd step.
No well-founded rule closing that last component was found.

## 5. Combine the sharp orbit count with the arithmetic moments

The critical interval theorem in PR146 includes a logarithmic saving,
so the critical reciprocal-power sum over one distinct orbit is finite.
PR169's moment inequality retains the same fixed original source and
lower-bounds `Q_q` by a fixed positive multiple of its squared endpoint.
Combining them gives the full finite-series obstruction
`sum_q Q_q^(-rho/2)<infinity` whenever a nonperiodic positive orbit exists.

The new [critical interface](CRITICAL_NEGATIVE_MOMENT_INTERFACE.md)
therefore reduces nonperiodic exclusion to divergence of that arithmetic
series, or to its stated critical quantile-excess variant. Neither
statement is proved for the actual moments. PR169's unconditional
fifth-denominator summability allows mass to move to larger endpoints;
it does not bridge the three endpoint powers needed for this conclusion.
A synthetic aggregate array in the note demonstrates that exact
limitation without pretending to satisfy the actual word congruences.
Repeated cycle visits also invalidate the distinct-state summation, so
the cycle obligation would survive even a successful moment estimate.

## 6. Role of the allowed GRH assumption

The new state recurrence, transport identities and stopping estimates
are unconditional. The previously allowed GRH hypothesis remains an
available assumption, but no implication from it to the required
pointwise parity, uncovered-energy, or moving-endpoint moment estimate
has been derived. Constructing primes in prescribed finite progressions
does not control the entire trajectory of the one original integer.

## Present outcome

There is no full proof in this packet. The strongest new results are
the exact relative-section recurrence and its return/density/pressure
consequences, together with stronger source-preserving stopping tests.
The residue-rule simplification and the exact residual families help
identify where a different certificate mechanism must enter. A valid
completion must supply an ordinary pointwise selector, a suitable
actual-label energy bound, or another argument that handles every
remaining source and nontrivial cycle.
