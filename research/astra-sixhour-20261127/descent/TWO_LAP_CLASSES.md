# Physical completion classes for two cycles

Let a periodic lower orbit have primitive shortcut clock and odd count $(h,p)$. Let the upper orbit from each source under consideration visit one. The upper cycle has clock and odd count $(2,1)$. Write $x_i$ for the $i$-th odd lower phase, $0\le i<p$, and $A_i$ for the elapsed physical clock from $x_0$ to $x_i$. Let $S$ be the set of these indices for which $3x_i$ visits one, with $J_i=H_i-2P_i$ on its actual first-hit word. In the bounded least-disagreement case $D=2p-h>0$.

**Proposition.** Two indices $i,j\in S$ admit ordinary paths from their lower phases to a common lower phase and from their triples to one, with the *same intersource difference in clocks and odds on both coordinates*, exactly when
$$
              A_i-2i+J_i\equiv A_j-2j+J_j\pmod D.             \tag{1}
$$
Neither the upper first hits nor their difference are bounded in this assertion.

Take the common lower target $x_0$, with initial lower vectors $L_i=(h-A_i,p-i)$. Their other completions include $L_i+n(h,p)$ for all nonnegative integers $n$. The upper vectors are $U_i=(H_i,P_i)$ and their completions $U_i+m(2,1)$. Thus the required differences agree precisely when
$$
                 (L_i-L_j)-(U_i-U_j)\in
                        \mathbb Z(h,p)+\mathbb Z(2,1).
$$
Whole-lap differences of either sign are realized by adding the desired number of positive physical laps to the appropriate member. Applying $(t,b)\mapsto t-2b$ kills $(2,1)$ and sends $(h,p)$ to $-D$. Its integer kernel is generated by $(2,1)$. This proves (1). A meeting at another lower phase can be advanced along one common suffix to $x_0$, preserving both differences. These actual words also lift to identities in one increasing arithmetic-progression parameter after clearing their finite binary and ternary branch denominators. Ordering of their slopes on that progression does not follow from the ordering of the two original numerical sources.

There are exactly $D$ possible residues, of which at most $D$ are occupied. All but at most $D$ members of $S$ therefore have a **strictly smaller numerical phase in $S$** with the displayed physical completion. On the distinguished bounded bad cycle the good-neighbor theorem gives $|S|\ge h-p$, so at least $2h-3p>(2\log_2 3-3)p$ phases have such partners. This count permits arbitrary first-hit clock and odd count at every upper. The PR195 completed-grade estimate instead keeps the upper first-hit budget or measures its failure while allowing lower laps. Both arguments retain counted meetings. The second independent lap vector gives the finite quotient here. The original canonical index need not have a smaller *canonical* partner by (1). The available count of at least $(2h-3p)/4$ canonical phases is below $D$ at the relevant slopes. The local comparison also exposes the direction problem. A canonical cycle entry $x$ starting a run of at least two exponent-two edges has an incoming exponent-one phase $y=(2x-1)/3<x$. Put $x_2=U^2(x)$ for the second odd successor. Its odd-map iterates satisfy $T^4(3x)=3x_2-1$. Since $3y=2x-1$, the two successive exponent-two edges propagate $2x-1$ to $2x_2-1$ in four shortcuts with two odds. One further odd shortcut gives $3x_2-1$. Thus the uppers meet after respectively $(4,2)$ and $(5,3)$, exactly matching the lower difference from $T(y)=x$. The smaller $y$ is three modulo four, hence outside the required class. No conversion of that phase or of the general partners into an earlier canonical bad integer has been proved.
