# One orbit field at all primes, and an exact normalized trace

**PROPOSED written theorems.** Let \(x_i\) be any finite or infinite trajectory of the positive odd map, omitting an initial multiple of three. The definitions of \(E_x,F_x,t_x,U_x\) are those of [CUBIC_HEIGHT_COST.md](CUBIC_HEIGHT_COST.md). Put
\[
 z_x=E_x^{1/3},\qquad
 w_x=\frac{t_x+1}{t_x-1}=1+\frac{2(z_x+z_x^2)}{E_x+1},\qquad
 q_x=\frac{3x}{3x+1},\qquad U_x=w_x^3/q_x .
\]
Use the positive real cube root. Let \(B\) be the physical real field generated by all \(t_x\) along the specified trajectory. All statements about one selected finite place per rational prime refer to the **same entire field** \(B\).

## C. Simultaneous outside-prime cancellation along the entire future

For each \(p\ge5\) there is one embedding \(B\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) such that
\[
       v_p(w_x)=0,\qquad v_p(U_x)=v_p(x')-v_p(x) \tag{4}
\]
on every edge \(2^a x'=3x+1\). There are also two choices, obtained by rotating the three cube roots coherently, which give \(v_p(U_x)=-\tfrac12(v_p(x')-v_p(x))\) on every edge. These choices include repeated occurrences of the same Pell field. At two one embedding gives \(v_2(w_x)=0,\ v_2(U_x)=a\). At three every embedding has \(v_3(w_x)=1/3,\ v_3(U_x)=0\). In particular the entire set of \(w_x\) has zero valuations at the selected places over all primes other than three. This is a claim about one place over each rational prime, not about integrality at all places.

To prove it, fix a Pell field \(F\) occurring anywhere in the sequence, and let \(\eta_F>1\) generate the totally positive norm-one units of that real quadratic field. Write \(E_x=\eta_F^{e_x}\) with \(e_x>0\). As in the Eisenstein argument of §1 of the height theorem, \(3\nmid e_x\). Fix once an embedding of the multiquadratic compositum enlarged by \(\mu_3\) into \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\). If \(p\mid x(3x+1)\) for any state of this field, then \(E_x\equiv1\) or \(-1\) respectively. This follows from \((E_x-1)^2/E_x=12x\) and \((E_x+1)^2/E_x=4(3x+1)\). The residue order \(L\) of \(\eta_F\) divides \(2e_x\), so \(3\nmid L\). Select the cube root \(z_F\) of \(\eta_F\) whose reduction equals its unique cube root in the cyclic subgroup of order \(L\). Such a choice exists and is unique among the three roots because \(p\ne3\). At every occurrence with \(E_x\equiv\pm1\), the same selected \(z_F^{e_x}\) reduces to \(\pm1\) with that sign. Hence \(t_x\equiv\pm2\) at each such occurrence. These are precisely the simple roots used for (4). If no occurrence of that field meets \(p\), any of the three choices works. Rotating \(z_F\) by \(\zeta_3\) or \(\zeta_3^2\) puts every relevant \(t_x\) at the respective nonsimple roots, because \(3\nmid e_x\).

The Kummer character argument in the height theorem applies to \(\eta_F\) itself. It is a noncube since one of its powers \(E_x\) is a noncube. Hence the root choices are independent for every finite set of labels and define a compatible embedding on their directed union. Equivalently the finite tensor-product embeddings agree on all common generators. Restrict to the physical field \(B\). The two-place dyadic and ternary formulas follow from \(P_x(t_x)=0\) and the factorizations used in the height theorem. At two, \(\eta_F\equiv1\) at its quadratic place because \(E_x\equiv1\), \(3\nmid e_x\), and the residue unit group has order one or three. Choosing the cube root reducing to one makes \(t_x\) even simultaneously. At three \(t_x-2\) has valuation \(1/3\), so \(t_x+1\) also has valuation \(1/3\), and \(t_x-1\) is a unit.

This construction works even on the field generated by **all** positive odd \(3\)-units. Thus the selected-place conclusion alone cannot distinguish an escaping source from any other set of such integer states.

## D. Normal-basis rigidity and the exact rational full trace

For a nonempty finite sequence, let \(f\) be its number of distinct Pell labels. Put \(W=\prod_i w_{x_i}\), and take the multiquadratic \(K\) and cubic \(M/K\) as above, using \(\eta_F\) as generator for each label. Then \(W\) is a normal basis generator of \(M/K\). Its \(3^f\) conjugates over \(K\) are linearly independent over \(K\). For any subgroup \(H\) of the cubic rotation group \(G\),
\[
 [K(|H|^{-1}\sum_{g\in H}gW):K]=3^f/|H|. \tag{5}
\]
In particular averaging the three diagonal conjugates retains degree \(3^{f-1}\) over \(K\) for \(f\ge1\).

Indeed each factor is \(1+2(z_F^{e_x}+z_F^{2e_x})/(E_x+1)\). Reducing the exponents modulo three gives three strictly positive coefficients in the physical embedding, one at each exponent. This remains true for the grouped product of all factors with a given label, since reduction uses the positive \(\eta_F\). The product therefore has a nonzero coefficient on *every* monomial \(\prod_F z_F^{r_F}\), \(r_F=0,1,2\). They form a \(K\)-basis. Each is an eigenvector for a distinct character of the full cubic rotation group. The character table is invertible, proving the normal-basis assertion. Averaging over \(H\) preserves exactly the monomials in its annihilator. Their common stabilizer is \(H\), proving (5). This works also when the quadratic labels satisfy multiplicative relations.

The full normalized trace is rational:
\[
 A=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}gW=\prod_F A_F,\qquad
 A_F=\tfrac13\operatorname{Tr}_{B_F/\mathbb Q}\prod_{i:F_i=F}w_{x_i} . \tag{6}
\]
All physical cubic fields inside a label coincide, since \(t_x=P_{e_x}(t_F)\), \(t_F=z_F+z_F^{-1}\), and each \(t_x\) has degree three. Hence each factor in (6) is the rational trace over that cubic field. The product formula follows by independent averaging over labels. It is also the normalized trace over the compositum of the physical cubic fields, of degree \(3^f\). A single factor \(w_x\) has normalized trace exactly one, by the expansion above. Equivalently its polynomial is
\[
    w^3-3w^2+3q_x w-q_x=0 .
\]
For a label with two or more occurrences, in addition to the product of constant ones there is a strictly positive constant Fourier contribution from two opposite nonzero exponents. Thus \(A\ge1\), with equality precisely when the labels are pairwise distinct. Appending one state increases \(A\) strictly exactly when its label has appeared before.

## E. All repeated-field correlations have summable size on simple paths

The following absolute estimate is independent of the path. In the set of all distinct positive odd integers \(x\) coprime to three, put
\[
 \mathcal D=\{x:e_x\ge5\}.
\]
There is at most one \(x\notin\mathcal D\) in each Pell field, and
\[
 \#\{x\in\mathcal D:x\le X\}=O(X^{1/5}),\qquad
 \sum_{\substack{x\in\mathcal D\\ x\ge X}}x^{-1/3}=O(X^{-2/15}). \tag{7}
\]
Indeed \(b=\eta_F+\eta_F^{-1}\) is an even integer at least three by the dyadic residue argument in C. All the traces \(P_{e_x}(b)=12x+2\) equal six modulo eight. If \(b\equiv2\bmod4\), then \(P_j(b)\equiv2\bmod4\) for every \(j\). Every even power trace \(P_{2j}(b)=P_j(b)^2-2\) is therefore two modulo eight, so \(e_x\) must be odd. If \(b\equiv0\bmod4\), every odd degree trace is zero modulo four by the recurrence, and \(P_{4j}(b)\equiv2\bmod8\) since every \(P_{2j}(b)\) is two modulo four. Thus \(e_x\equiv2\bmod4\) in this case. Since \(3\nmid e_x\), there is at most one allowed \(e_x\le4\) for either value of \(b\bmod4\). For (7), \(\eta_F>b-1\) implies \((b-1)^e<12X+2\) for each counted \(x\), with \(5\le e=O(\log(2X))\). The \(e=5\) count is \(O(X^{1/5})\), all others total \(O(X^{1/6}\log(2X))\). Each \(b,e\) specifies at most one integer \(x\). Dyadic summation proves the tail estimate.

For **any finite sequence of distinct** such ordinary states, whether or not consecutive, with minimum \(X\),
\[
                1\le A\le \exp(C X^{-2/15}) \tag{8}
\]
for an absolute \(C\). Every conjugate of \(w_x\) has distance \(O(x^{-1/3})\) from one, directly from its positive two-term Kummer expansion. For each label separate the possible single \(e_x\le4\) factor. Its mean is one and its absolute value is uniformly bounded. The product of its remaining factors differs from one in every embedding by at most \(\exp(C_0\sum_{x\in\mathcal D,F_x=F}x^{-1/3})-1\). Thus \(A_F\le\exp(C_1\sum_{x\in\mathcal D,F_x=F}x^{-1/3})\), increasing the absolute constant since the total sum is bounded by (7). Multiply and apply (7). On an entire injective trajectory, the prefix traces increase to a finite real value while the full traces of *every* sufficiently late finite interval are uniformly close to one. These assertions do not need any assumed odd-state frequency or growth rate.

A genuine simple ordinary interval prevents an integrality argument in (8). Starting at \(43681=209^2\), take each odd departure through the first visit to 1, including that departure at 1. There are 58 distinct departure states, and the terminal odd value is 1. Only the Pell label \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\) is repeated, at \(43681\) and \(1\). With \(E=E_1\), \(\operatorname{Tr}(E)=14\), one has \(E_{43681}=E^5\). The two-factor constant coefficient in (6) gives
\[
 A=1+\frac{4(E^2+E^4)}{(E+1)(E^5+1)}
  =1+\frac{4\cdot14}{P_3(14)+P_2(14)}
  =\frac{369}{362}.
\]
The denominator prime \(181\) occurs at the intermediate state \(32761=181^2\), and divides neither endpoint. Hence even an exact visit to 1 with simultaneous selected-place cancellation allows a nonintegral normalized trace and an internal odd denominator.

**First unproved inference.** One might hope that the upper bound in (8) forces exact equality on all sufficiently late intervals of one escaping source. For this one needs a lower separation from one for its positive rational traces. Neither positivity nor the all-time selected-place construction gives such separation. The ordinary example exhibits precisely how an internal prime survives the averaging. Even equality throughout a tail would give distinct Pell labels there, a possibility the current arguments allow. These results do not prove finite trapping or positive cycle uniqueness.
