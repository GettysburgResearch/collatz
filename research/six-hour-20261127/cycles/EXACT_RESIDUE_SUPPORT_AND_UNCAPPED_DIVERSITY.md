# Exact scaled-residue populations and an uncapped physical diversity bound

Let a primitive positive rational Collatz cycle have least odd period $P$, odd phases $x_i=N_i/d$ in lowest terms with common $d$ coprime to six, positive accelerated valuations $e_i$, total $A$, and maximum $X$. Write $R$ for the number of actual residues $N_i\bmod d$, equivalently the number of actual odd-phase fractional parts. Two elementary results give its relationship to the scaled rank residues and its minimum growth when the denominators grow. The second uses no grade, valuation, width, order or Diophantine assumption.

## All scaled rank residues, including a deficient clock order

Set $g=(A,P)$, $q=P/g$, $a=A/g$, $S=|\langle2,3\rangle_d|$,
$$
 \omega=3^q2^{-a}\bmod d,\qquad k=\operatorname{ord}_d(\omega),\qquad
 T=S/k,\quad m=g/k.
$$
At $d=1$ take the trivial group and $k=S=1$. Rotate to a canonical maximum, put $z_i=\lfloor ia/q\rfloor-E_i$, $H=\max z_i$, and $K_i=2^{H-z_i}N_i$. The scaled integers satisfy $2^{m_i}K_{i+1}=3K_i+d2^{H-z_i}$ with $m_i=\lfloor(i+1)a/q\rfloor-\lfloor ia/q\rfloor$. Modular iteration gives $K_{i+q}\equiv\omega K_i$ and $k\mid g$.

**Exact distribution.** The numbers of dates with each possible $K_i$ residue in $K_0\langle2,3\rangle_d$ are as follows. Write $q=nT+u$, $0\le u<T$. Exactly $ku$ residues have $m(n+1)$ dates and the remaining $k(T-u)$ residues have $mn$ dates. In particular the occupied scaled support has size $\min(kq,S)$ and the largest atom has $m\lceil q/T\rceil$ occurrences. These formulas include the possible zero populations and the case $k<g$.

For $q>1$, choose $l$ with $la\equiv1\bmod q$, $1\le l<q$, and define $b=\lfloor la/q\rfloor$, $\beta=3^l2^{-b}\bmod d$. The identity $la-qb=1$ gives $2=\beta^q\omega^{-l}$, $3=\beta^a\omega^{-b}$. Thus the quotient of $\langle2,3\rangle_d$ by $\langle\omega\rangle$ is generated by the coset of $\beta$ and has order $T$. At the $g$ chronological copies of rank $t$ the full list is
$$
 K_{lt+jq}\equiv K_0\beta^t\omega^j\pmod d,\qquad
 0\le t<q,\quad 0\le j<g.
$$
The floor is $\lfloor lta/q+ja\rfloor=bt+ja$ because $t/q<1$. These indices cover all chronological dates modulo $P$ once. Within a rank each residue of its $\omega$-coset occurs $m$ times. The $q$ consecutive cosets $\beta^t\langle\omega\rangle$ give the displayed distribution. When $q=1$, all scaled residues advance by $\omega$ itself, giving one coset uniformly with $m$ copies of each residue, exactly the same formula.

Let $V$ be the number of distinct factors $2^{H-z_i}\bmod d$ on the actual cycle, so $V\le H+1$. A fixed $N$ residue contributes to at most $V$ scaled residues. Consequently
$$
 R\ge\frac{\min(kq,S)}{V},\qquad
 \max_r\#\{i:N_i\equiv r\bmod d\}
 \le V\,\frac gk\left\lceil\frac{qk}{S}\right\rceil.
$$
For any chosen subset of grades, the per-residue bound for the population at those grades uses the number of distinct scaling factors there. This permits unbounded grades on the complementary dates. Under canonical height at most two, or physical ratio at most eight, the previously proved denominator-order theorem gives $k=g$. In particular
$$
 \frac{\min(P,S)}{H+1}\le R\le\min(P,S),\qquad
 \max_r\#\{i:N_i\equiv r\bmod d\}
 \le (H+1)\left\lceil\frac PS\right\rceil.
$$
The upper bound on $R$ follows directly from $N_i\equiv 3^i2^{-E_i}N_0\bmod d$. All starting values and their residues in this section are actual physical phases. Scaled-support uniformity does not force equal occupancies for the unscaled residues.

## A physical maximum and the size of the full denominator

The following all-height conclusions use only the physical recurrence. If $d>3^R$, then
$$
 X+1<\left(\frac32\right)^R
       \left(1+\frac1{d^{1/R}-3}\right). \tag{1}
$$
In particular every such cycle, with no condition on $d$, satisfies at least one of
$$
 d<4^R,\qquad
 P<R\left(\left(\frac32\right)^R+\frac12\right). \tag{2}
$$
Also, if $R=1$ and $d>1$, then $P=1$.

Consider the $R$ edges ending at a chronological occurrence of the global maximum $X$. Write $y$ for the minimum of the $R+1$ states on that interval. Among their $R+1$ numerator residues two agree. If their positive time separation is $b\le R$ and the division sum of the intervening edges is $K'$, then $d$ divides the nonzero integer $2^{K'}-3^b$ by invertibility of the equal starting residue. If $d>3^R$, a negative determinant would have absolute value below $d$. Thus $d\le2^{K'}-3^b<2^{K'}$. If $K$ is the division sum on the whole interval, product of the physical equations gives
$$
 2^{K'}\le2^K
   =\frac{x_{\rm start}}X \prod_{j<R}\left(3+\frac1{x_j}\right)
   \le(3+1/y)^R.
$$
This proves $y<1/(d^{1/R}-3)$. Every accelerated step satisfies $x_{j+1}+1\le(3/2)(x_j+1)$ since $e_j\ge1$. Applying this from a point attaining $y$ to the terminal maximum proves (1).

At $d\ge4^R$, (1) gives $X+1<2(3/2)^R$. Each fixed raw residue can have at most $1+X/2$ physical phases: two distinct odd numerators in the same class differ by an even multiple of their odd denominator. Consequently $P\le R(1+X/2)$, proving (2). In the separate case $R=1$, every edge gives $d\mid 2^{e_i}-3$. For $d>1$ none of the $e_i$ can be one or two. Therefore $8X\le3X+1$, so $X\le1/5$. Distinct positive phases below one have different fractional parts, whence $P=1$. These remaining singletons are $x=1/(2^e-3)$.

Along any sequence with $P\to\infty$ and $\liminf\log d/\log P\ge\delta>0$ one obtains
$$
 \liminf \frac R{\log P}\ge
 \min\left\{\frac{\delta}{\log3},\frac1{\log(3/2)}\right\}. \tag{3}
$$
For a contrary subsequence choose $L$ strictly between its limiting ratio and the right-hand side. Then $R\le L\log P$ and $d^{1/R}$ is uniformly above three. Formula (1) gives $\log(X+1)\le R\log(3/2)+O(1)$, whereas $P\le R(1+X/2)$ would imply $1\le L\log(3/2)$ after division by $\log P$. The same argument gives the second constant in (3) when $\log d/\log P\to\infty$.

The finite checker reconstructs 590 true primitive rational cycles, including a full-denominator example $d=431,P=8,g=2,k=1$ with only four occupied scaled residues despite eight different raw fractional parts. It tests 338 large-denominator cases of (2) and eight actual maximum-ending intervals forcing a returning-residue determinant. Separate group enumeration tests 16,020 schedules, including 10,680 examples of order deficiency. No ordinary convergence or new fixed-denominator cycle exclusion follows from (1)–(3).
