# Exact inverse decoding, two-sided clock bounds and ordinary finite lifts

This arithmetic example tests the proposed passage from prediction of the preceding gap to deep bounded-height rational phases of a stationary law. It includes a genuine ordinary-integer realization of every prescribed finite experiment. The sources of the finite experiments can change. The complete harmonic weight on any hypothesized escaping component remains an additional requirement.

Use two-sided forward gap sequences formed by concatenating independent equally likely blocks 111 and 121. Randomize the initial block phase uniformly among three choices for stationarity. Consecutive occurrences of 2 are at least three places apart. A block boundary is adjacent to two ones. At every such boundary any prefix of the future and any prefix of the reversed past have gap sum at most 3j/2 for length j: at j=1 the letter is one, and at j≥2 at most ceil(j/3)≤j/2 letters are twos. Thus at least one third of the times satisfy both all-length bounds, with a rational slope strictly below log₂ 3. The stationary mean gap is 7/6.

For any such bi-infinite gap sample define a ternary state at time i by the convergent backwards expansion of the forward maps F_e(z)=(3z+1)/2^e from its infinite past. The last d maps determine the state modulo 3^d. Conversely z modulo 3 determines the last e, since the two possible e have different parity and 2^e z≡1 mod 3. Then (2^e z−1)/3 is known to one fewer digit. Iteration recovers the entire length-d past from d ternary digits. Thus the preceding letter is predicted exactly from one digit, in a stationary process with positive label entropy (log 2)/3 per step.

At fixed block phase a prescribed d-letter gap word has probability at most 2^(−floor(d/3)), since at least floor(d/3) free middle positions occur. Therefore the probability of a specified residue modulo 3^d has that same bound. The uniform mixture over the three phases preserves it. For N≥1 and H≥1 there are at most (2H+1)H signed reduced anchors of height ≤H. A unit-denominator anchor specifies at most one residue in the condition v₃(z_i+t)≥d; an anchor with 3 dividing its denominator contributes none. A union bound gives

    Pr(some i∈[0,N], some height≤H signed anchor has depth≥d)
       ≤ (N+1)(2H+1)H · 2^(−floor(d/3)).                            (D1)

Consequently for every fixed b≥0 almost every sample, simultaneously at all sufficiently large N, has score O_b(log N) for **every** clock i∈[0,N] and **every** signed rational height ≤N^b. Apply (D1) at dyadic N, H=(2N)^b, with d a sufficiently large multiple of log N and sum the resulting geometric errors. A countable intersection over integer b covers every fixed polynomial exponent. This is stronger quantitative shallowness than the upper bound derived from Yu, and it coexists with exact one-digit prediction and permanent small mean gaps in both directions on positive mass.

## At positive ordinary integers

Choose a finite good block sample containing d prior letters for every tested clock, plus as many future letters as the finite test requires. Complete its ends to block boundaries. If the resulting word has length L and division sum E, write its ordinary affine formula as 2^E y=3^L x+C. Its terminal residues satisfying integrality form the single class y≡2^(−E)C mod 3^L. In particular one can take the least positive odd representative y<2·3^L. Reconstruct all previous ordinary integers by x_prev=(2^e x_next−1)/3. Successive divisibility follows by stripping the last letter from the affine congruence and dividing by three. Every such integer is positive and odd; the output's oddness verifies that the physical valuation is exactly e. Every earlier state is below y: for its suffix of length j ending at the final block boundary, the reverse multiplier is 2^sum/3^j≤(2^(3/2)/3)^j<1, and the inverse affine maps also subtract strictly positive terms. The entire ordinary realization therefore lies inside the principal positive window (0,2·3^L). Its tested ternary low digits agree with the stationary labels past the d-letter warmup. The two finite Pliss tests at the recorded block boundaries remain literal physical tests.

There is a separate realization guaranteeing eventual arrival at 1. Choose an arbitrarily large E′ with 2^E′≡3y+1 mod 3^(L+1). This is possible since 2 generates the units modulo each power of three: v₃(4^(3^s)−1)=s+1 by cubing induction, and 2 has order two modulo three. The exponent E′ is even. Replace the endpoint by (2^E′−1)/3, a positive odd integer in the same ternary class which goes to 1 in one accelerated step. The same positive inverse lift supplies every finite observation. This realization need not lie in the principal window for its original length L.

## The absent mass condition

For a complete L-letter sample of independent blocks, with L a multiple of three, an individual ordinary starting integer can realize only one observed word. Consequently the probability that its least positive ordinary source is ≤B is at most B·2^(−L/3). One can impose its complementary event together with (D1) when the sum of their failure bounds is less than one. For example the least source can exceed exp(cL), for any fixed 0<c<(log 2)/3, while d=O_b(log L) controls all polynomial heights on a segment of order L. Use central clocks at least d from the initial time. This source bound applies to the principal-endpoint realization too, since its source is positive.

In the original independent-geometric ternary law the contribution of the observed word to ρ_L(y)/y is 3^L/(2^E y). At its ordinary source x this equals (1/x) times the finite product of 3x_j/(3x_j+1), hence is at most 1/x. No other word from the two-block grammar can reach the same ternary target class at length L, by one-digit decoding. On the good large-source event the *entire contribution of this grammar* to that target is ≤exp(−cL). The complete ρ_L(y) includes words outside the grammar and has not been bounded. This comparison identifies precisely why positive integer realizability of the tests does not manufacture the macroscopic original-law amplitude used in PR 193/201.

The fixed-source question that survives is whether positive original-law escape charge can support such predictability and height avoidance on one unbounded positive orbit. The stationary construction and the two finite ordinary lift variants do not resolve that question. The low-mean stationary realizability results in PR 200 already give broad fixed-window terminating examples; (D1) and the explicit growing-depth height control are the additional test here.
