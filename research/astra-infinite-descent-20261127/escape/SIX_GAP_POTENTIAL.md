# Six gap lengths suffice for a uniform high-odd-count inverse ray

For every positive odd ternary unit \(y\), one can recursively select actual positive odd unit ancestors \(x_0=y,x_1,\ldots\) with
\[
  3x_i+1=2^{e_i}x_{i-1},\qquad 1\le e_i\le6,\qquad
  6\sum_{i=1}^d e_i\le13d+48\quad(d\ge0).                            \tag{1}
\]
Thus the original absolute mass of the selected depth-\(d\) finite word is at least \(2^{-8}(3/2^{13/6})^d\). Its exponential base is strictly larger than \(2/3\), since \(3^{12}=531441>524288=2^{19}\). At a nonperiodic target every such visit is a first visit. The individual-gap cap six is the **least uniform cap** that permits this conclusion for all positive unit targets at all grades with an absolute prefactor. This statement concerns controlled reverse paths; it puts no upper bound on unrestricted forward trajectories.

The classical Applegate–Lagarias certificate gives the larger unrestricted odds-per-clock ratio \(14/29\) with certificate blocks of length at most 60. Their strong certificate at \(28/59\) yields branching paths. The finite calculation here certifies a weaker average slope with a sharp maximum allowed individual gap, a uniform additive clock bound, and ordinary reconstructions from a small table. [Applegate and Lagarias, Theorems 3.1, 4.1 and 4.2](https://websites.umich.edu/lagarias/doc/3x%2B1bound.pdf).

## Exact finite certificate

Put \(q=3^{12}=531441\). The [potential table](../../../reports/astra-infinite-descent-20261127/escape/INVERSE_POTENTIAL.b85) has one byte \(V(s)\in[0,48]\) per residue \(0\le s<q\). For **every** unit \(r\bmod 3q\) it satisfies
\[
 \exists e\in\{1,\ldots,6\},\quad u=(2^e r-1)/3\in\mathbb N,
 \quad 3\nmid u,\quad 6e+V(u\bmod q)\le13+V(r\bmod q).             \tag{2}
\]
The [independent integer verifier](check_inverse_potential.py) tries the six exponents at each of the 1,062,882 unit representatives and checks the exact integer division. The uncompressed table has SHA-256 `ca7c5f953992887eceb13b181e5db946b0959ddc6f23c66db3a609377bb30562`; its zlib bytes have SHA-256 `62e169c1d97002dafb71e83bff9f333eb11f80975d4fd6fc3d88cf880d018151`. The stored file uses ASCII base85 encoding. Normal and optimized Python independently checked the same bytes. [Finite receipt and ordinary path reconstructions](../../../reports/astra-infinite-descent-20261127/escape/INVERSE_POTENTIAL_CHECKS.json).

The [constructor](construct_inverse_potential.cpp) starts at zero. For each unit state \(s\bmod q\), maximize over its three possible next trits, minimize \(6e+V(u\bmod q)-13\) over the two legal gaps in \([1,6]\), and take the maximum with zero. The iterates stabilized at sweep 283. Running the verifier with `--regenerate` recompiles this C++17 constructor and checks every regenerated byte. Validity of (2) uses only the exhaustive local verification.

For any actual positive target \(y=r+3qj\), lift the chosen finite witness to \(z=u+2^e qj\). Then \(3z+1=2^e y\), \(z\bmod q=u\bmod q\), and \(z\) is a positive odd ternary unit. These are genuine shortcut paths with exactly one odd departure in \(e\) steps. Applying (2) repeatedly gives \(6\sum e_i+V(x_d\bmod q)\le13d+V(y\bmod q)\), hence (1). The [Lean bridge](InversePotential.lean) formalizes the finite-table predicate, physical lift and arbitrary-grade induction. Its premise is checked by the separate exhaustive integer program; the large table is not kernel-evaluated.

To see sharpness of the gap cap, choose for each \(d\) an actual positive odd \(y\) with \(13y\equiv1\pmod{3^{d+1}}\). The first residue is \(y\equiv7\pmod9\), whose only positive unit predecessor exponent at most five is four. Its unit parent \(z=(16y-1)/3\) satisfies \(13z-1=16(13y-1)/3\); one trit of the same condition is lost. Thus the first \(d\) reverse gaps are **all four** in every allowable word with cap five, and every such multiplier is precisely \((3/16)^d\). The positive targets may vary with \(d\); a uniform upper clock slope below four under cap five is impossible. This argument applies only to the requested all-target uniformity.

Already at one odd step there are roots, such as seven modulo nine, with no unit gap in the range one through three. With cap four or five the minimax clock at every grade is exactly \(4d\): the shadow just exhibited forces \(4d\), and the least allowed unit gap is always at most four by the six unit residues modulo nine.

## A smaller certificate for the sum over all capped words

A separate five-trit integer table controls the total multiplier of **all** legal unit words with gaps at most six. Give each valid first choice of exponent \(e\) integer weight \(3\cdot 2^{6-e}\), so the product along a grade-\(d\) word equals \(64^d 3^d2^{-m}\). For 243 residues, the table values \(f\) are zero on nonunits and lie in \([52,1000]\) on units. At all 729 lifted residues the sum of the integer-weighted child values is at least \(48 f(y)\). Iteration shows, for every positive odd unit target,
\[
          \sum_{w:\,|w|=d,\ e_i\le6}3^d2^{-m(w)}
                       \ge (52/1000)(3/4)^d .                            \tag{2a}
\]
The [Lean transfer](CappedMass.lean) evaluates all 729 inequalities in the kernel and proves (2a) after clearing denominators. Its [clock-coefficient companion](CappedClock.lean) proves that the exact-clock coefficients sum to this total and that one clock in the range zero through six times the grade receives at least the corresponding average. This is also a lower bound on the original positive source-weighted law, since every unit terminal has original weight at least one. At nonperiodic \(y\) all of these are genuine first visits. There are only \(5d+1\) possible clocks \(d\le m\le6d\). In particular the global first-row maximum \(W_d\) from [the target-row criterion](TargetExtinction.lean) satisfies
\[
              W_d\ge \frac{52(3/4)^d}{5000(5d+1)}
\]
for every grade, by retaining just \(y=5\). Distinct odd words at fixed grade correspond to distinct ordinary sources because the forward odd trajectory is deterministic. The raw positive first-row maximum therefore cannot have the polynomial-times-\((2/3)^d\) decay of the signed original criterion on any unbounded subsequence. This leaves open whether \(W_d\to0\), which is the boundedness question.

## Uniform branching below the critical clock slope

After every 4095 controlled steps in a complete block allow **both** positive unit exponents from \(1,\ldots,6\) on its last step. There are always two because two of the six powers of two times a unit are \(4\) or \(7\bmod9\). They give distinct odd predecessors. For \(d\) steps, the incomplete last block uses only the controlled strategy. The clock of every resulting path satisfies
\[
         6m\le13d+71\lfloor d/4096\rfloor+48.                          \tag{3}
\]
Indeed a group of controlled steps costs its baseline plus at most 48 by (1), and substituting one unrestricted unit letter costs at most \(36-13=23\) extra units. These paths furnish at least \(2^{\lfloor d/4096\rfloor}\) **distinct ordinary sources** at grade \(d\), since a given odd start has exactly one forward odd word of that length, including at recurrent targets. Put \(\kappa=13/6+71/(6\cdot4096)\). The exact integer check \(2^{13\cdot4096+71+6\cdot4096}<3^{12\cdot4096}\) gives \(\kappa<\log_2(9/2)\). Thus all these individual atom multipliers exceed an absolute constant times \((3/2^\kappa)^d\), whose base still exceeds \(2/3\).

## First arrivals of finite patterns at both prescribed parameters

Given a finite pattern \(F\) of nonnegative offsets and any positive source modulus \(q'\), there are constants \(g_0,C\) depending only on this pattern and modulus with the following property. For every positive unit target \(y\), every prescribed grade \(d\ge g_0\), and **every supplied integer clock** \(M\ge(13/6)d+C\), one certificate at \((d,M,y)\) contains a translated copy of \(F\cup\{0\}\) beginning in each source class modulo \(q'\). All members first reach \(y\) at exactly that clock and grade. Allowing \(M\ge\kappa d+C\), (3) yields at least \(2^{\lfloor(d-g_0)/4096\rfloor}\) disjoint certificates of this kind. Overlap between the copies for different residue classes inside *one* certificate is allowed.

Use [the fixed-clock ordinary block router](../merger/BlockClock.lean) for the finite pattern obtained by packing \(F+j\) for all \(0\le j<q'\). At any unit endpoint \(w\), every routing clock \(H\ge B\) achieves the same fixed grade \(g\), independent of \(w,H\). From the final target take its positive odd unit branch \(b\) outside the forward orbit of \(y\); it first reaches \(y\) in \(e\le10\) steps of grade one. Build a controlled reverse path from \(b\) with \(d-g-1\) letters, ending at odd \(w\) at clock \(m\). Give the block router \(H=M-m-e\), which is at least \(B\) once \(C\) is chosen. The combined paths have exactly the two specified parameters. An earlier visit to \(y\) would place \(w\) or \(b\) in its forward orbit; the inverse prefix to \(b\) is simple because \(b\) is nonperiodic. Each required source residue occurs among the \(q'\) consecutive translates. In the branching construction the different \(w\)'s are distinct odd states. A shared member of two certificates would have two different odd landings immediately after its first \(g\) odd departures, which is impossible. Taking \(g_0=g+1\) proves both statements. The [formal strategy interface and ordinary first-arrival transfer](../merger/FixedGradeCone.lean) and [potential-to-strategy theorem](InversePotential.lean) cover the single-certificate assertion under the finite table premise. The reported constants do not give an arbitrary relative real interval at a specified grade and clock.

Finally the single path at \(y=5\) in (1), combined with the disjoint exact ternary valuations of consecutive original mark supports, yields a fully finite arithmetic obstruction to exponential decay of the **unrestricted two-sided CDF distance** there, at every grade.
