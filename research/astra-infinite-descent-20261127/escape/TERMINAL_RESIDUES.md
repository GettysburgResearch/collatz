# Quantitative terminal source-residue mixing in the original all-visit law

Fix an integer modulus \(q\ge1\) and a label \(a(n)\) on its residues. Use the true odd source selector \(\psi(n)=\mathbf1_{n\ {\rm odd}}(n\bmod3)\) from the [original PR193 law](ORIGINAL_TRACE_VARIATION.md). Let \(p\) be the probability vector of the labels within one complete source period \(P=\operatorname{lcm}(6,q)\), weighting points by \(\psi\) and normalizing. At grade \(d\) and positive odd target \(y\), write \(R_{d,y}\) for the vector of actual original masses \(\pi(y)\rho_{d+1}(y)\) partitioned by the label of their terminal integer source. Set \(|R_{d,y}|=\pi(y)\rho_{d+1}(y)\) for the total. Then, with an absolute constant independent of the labels, their number and the modulus,
\[
 \sum_{D\le d<2D}\sum_{y\ {\rm positive\ odd}}
       \|R_{d,y}-p\,|R_{d,y}|\|_1
       \le C\big[\sqrt{D\log(2q)}+\log(2q)\big].                      \tag{1}
\]
The sum includes the recurrent target one, or one may remove it. The target and grade are kept before taking the norm. This is an absolute total-mass estimate, with no normalization by a small conditioning probability.

For proof put \(\Phi(n)=\psi(n)(\boldsymbol\delta_{a(n)}-p)\). This vector has period \(P\), zero mean over each period, and \(\ell^1\) norm at most four. With \(v_n(d,y)\) the genuine all-visit losses, the vector at \((d,y)\) on the left side of (1) is exactly \(\sum_n\Phi(n)v_n(d,y)\). At an octave \(I_N=[N,2N)\) of integer sources, \(N\ge P\), compare \(v_n\) to each translate \(v_{n+s}\), \(0\le s<P\). At a common physical clock \(L\) with equal odd counts, all future vectors agree at every grade and target. The two unmatched prefixes contain at most \(2(L+1)\) landings and cost \(O((L+1)2^L/N^2)\) per source by the true mark bound. On a failed comparison the total cost per source is at most \(2/N\). The [scalar estimate](SCALAR_RETURN_PROOF.md) for initial distance \(s\) bounds the failed fraction by \(C\sqrt{\log(2P)/(1+L)}+O(2^L/N)\). Indeed the two runs and their two counts at clock \(L\) are determined up to a common affine translation by \(n\bmod 2^L\), and each full period has the scalar survival proportion. Equal counts are required in this argument. The case \(s=0\) is already fused. Take \(L=\lfloor (\log_2 N)/2\rfloor\).

Replace \(v_n\) by the average of \(v_{n+s}\), retaining \(\Phi(n)\). Interior columns vanish by periodic zero mean. The two end intervals have at most \(2P\) sites and cost \(O(P/N)\) after averaging, by \(\|v_n\|_1\le1/n\). At smaller octaves \(N<P\), the full vector cost is \(O(1)\) by the same bound. Summing all octaves below \(X=2^{20D}\) gives \(O(\log(2P)+\sqrt{D\log(2P)})\); the geometric boundary and unmatched-prefix errors are summable. The comparison bound concerns the full vector across all grades and targets simultaneously.

To restore sources \(n\ge X\) at \(D\le d<2D\), first bound targets \(y>2^{4D}\) by the uniform one-letter row bound and the summable target kernel. At remaining targets the true affine relation forces physical clock at least \(16D\), so the unconstrained positive-gap moment gives \(O(D\theta^D)\) across the block, as in the original proof. Label norms cost only an absolute factor. Coordinates can first be restricted to finite sets; increasing those sets and using nonnegativity of the summed absolute errors justifies the full vector estimate.

In particular, let \(q_d=2^{h_d}3^{k_d}\) where \(h_d+k_d=o(d)\). The true binary and ternary source residues together have vanishing total error in natural-density mean over grades, summed across all positive odd targets as in (1). On each dyadic grade range use the common finer modulus with the maximum of the two respective bit lengths there; its log is still \(o(D)\). Projecting its label law down to each individual coarser modulus contracts total variation, and projects the reference law to the correct coarser reference. Markov's inequality gives vanishing error outside a density-zero set of grades (or a single density-one subsequence with error tending to zero). The comparison provides no relative control for a target whose original row mass tends to zero. Choosing a modulus whose logarithm is a small fixed fraction of \(D\) gives a correspondingly small absolute mean error, with no limiting independence asserted for a fixed positive fraction.

## Keeping the true mark and comparing full distribution functions

The same right side in (1), with an additional \(O(\log(2D))\) absorbed by \(O(\sqrt D)\), applies to
\[
 \sum_{D\le d<2D}\sum_{y,r}
   \sup_{0\le t\le1}|R_{d,y,r}([0,t])-p_r R_{d,y,\mathrm{total}}([0,t])|.     \tag{2}
\]
Here \([0,t]\) tests the original normalized mark \(b_d(x)=x h_d(x)\), and even the thresholds for different labels may differ. In the translation argument on a successful pair, the common target and clock give the same \(h=3^d2^{-m}/y\), but the true marks are \(nh\) and \((n+s)h\). For arbitrary threshold \(t_{d,y,r}\), a mismatching indicator therefore selects at most \(s+1\) integers at any one clock \(m\). Within \(I_N\), all eligible \(h\le1/N\). Their distinct clock weights form a geometric progression, so their sum is at most \(2/N\). The total successful-pair threshold error at a fixed \((d,y,r)\) is at most \(8(P+1)/[N(3y+1)]\), including the bounded coefficient. For \(N\ge P\) all active targets satisfy \(y\le3N2^{2D}\), by the ordinary height upper bound; hence summing \((d,y,r)\) costs \(O(q(P+1)D(D+\log(2N))/N)\). Sum this over dyadic \(N\ge N_0=\lceil q(P+1)(D+\log(2P)+2)^3\rceil\) at cost \(O(1)\). Earlier octaves cost \(O(\log N_0)=O(\log(2q)+\log(2D))\) by the full true loss per source, also when the threshold depends on every coordinate. Failed pairs, premerger prefixes, end intervals and source cutoffs retain their previous bounds, because deleting any coordinate by a threshold only decreases their nonnegative majorants. Supremum over thresholds follows by making independent approximating choices on each finite coordinate set and increasing the sets.

In particular the consecutive **whole-interval**, two-sided original CDFs satisfy
\[
  \sum_{D\le d<2D}\sum_y \pi(y)
     \sup_{0\le t\le1}|L_{d+1,y}(t)-L_{d,y}(t)|\ll\sqrt D.             \tag{3}
\]
Use the [exact marked gap-three identity](MARKED_GAP_THREE.md). A synchronized pair only contributes a possible strip between \(b h\) and \((c-1/2)h\), of source width less than three. The other two strips have width one half. For all of them the preceding calculation with a fixed number of residues bounds the high-octave charges by \(O(D(D+\log(2N))/N)\); low octaves up to \((D+2)^3\) cost \(O(\log(2D))\). Failed pairs at grade \(d\ge D\) can be discarded when fusion occurs by \(\min(D,\lfloor(\log_2 N)/2\rfloor)\), and the full combined true loss bound on the actual failed sources sums to \(O(\sqrt D)\) over \(N<2^{20D}\). The absolute all-parity high-source and high-target tails have the previously proved exponential bound. This is a consecutive-law estimate and includes every ordinary target, even one.

There is a strict obstruction to replacing the right depth scale in (3) by the sought exponential scale, even at a single target along a subsequence. For a positive odd unit target \(y\), every grade-\(d\) original true mark is \(3^d x/(2^m y)\) for an odd unit \(x\). Its exact 3-adic valuation is \(d\). The mark supports at depths \(d\) and \(d+1\) are consequently disjoint. A jump of mass \(a\) in either distribution, at a mark with no atom from the other, implies a supremum CDF difference of at least \(a/2\) by its two one-sided values. This also applies at mark one using the left limit and the value at one.

Take \(y=5\). The published Applegate–Lagarias finite certificate in Theorem 4.1, with the concatenation in Theorem 3.1(i), gives a reverse ray made of successive pruned unit blocks. Each block has physical length at most 60 and at least \(14/29\) odd letters per clock. It applies at 5, which is outside a cycle since \(5\to8\to4\to2\to1\to2\). At the \(d\)-th reverse odd letter the current source is an odd unit; any even inverse steps since the previous odd letter are exactly its physical gap. Completed blocks use at most \(29d/14\) clocks and the final partial block costs at most 60. Thus **at every grade** some original atom has mass at least
\[
    2^{-60}(3/2^{29/14})^d .
\]
The base exceeds \(2/3\), since \(3^{28}>2^{43}\). In particular even the unrestricted two-sided consecutive CDF difference at this one fixed ordinary target cannot satisfy \(O(d^A(2/3)^d)\) along any unbounded subsequence. The published 350,688,758-class certificate was not independently regenerated here. The disjoint-valuation argument compares the two original finite-depth laws themselves. It concerns the **two-sided, unrestricted** CDF and gives no lower bound for the actual narrow one-sided rate statistic.
