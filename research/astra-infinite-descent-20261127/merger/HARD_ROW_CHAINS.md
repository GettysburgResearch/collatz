Equal labels persist at every later common postpeak clock. For \(j\ge0\) let \(D_h(j)\) be the largest \(K\le j\) for which the peaks at \(m_j,m_{j-1},\ldots,m_{j-K}\) all have the same label. ## Median chain length at a logarithmic postpeak clock

Fix an integer step \(b\ge1\), an initial row index \(a\ge0\), and any \(N\ge2\) consecutive nonnegative integers \(s\). Sample \(j=a+bs\) over that interval. Put \(e=3+v_2(b)\) and \(h=e+\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor\). There are absolute constants \(C,c>0\) such that, for all integers \(K\ge1\),
\[
 \Pr(D_h(j)<K)\le K/N+C2^e K/\sqrt h,\qquad
 \Pr(D_h(j)\ge K)\le C2^e\exp(-cK^2/h).                 \tag{1}
\]
The first \(K/N\) also covers indices \(j<K\), including intervals beginning at zero. Thus the median is of order \(\sqrt{\log N}\) as \(N\to\infty\), with comparison constants depending only on \(v_2(b)\), uniformly in the position of the interval and in \(a\). For \(K=o(\sqrt{\log N})\) the entire chain succeeds on a proportion tending to one. Each successful positive depth \(i\le K\) gives a distinct smaller canonical source at the original parameter, with
\[
  2^{162i}z_{j-i}<z_j, \qquad
  T^{7+m_j+h}z_j=T^{7+m_{j-i}+h}z_{j-i},\qquad
  T^{m_j+7-162i}(3z_{j-i})=1.                              \tag{2}
\]
The last displayed clock is the first visit on the triple, with exactly three odd departures. The strict height bound follows by multiplying the defining numerator for \(z_{j-i}\) by \(2^{162i}\). This construction supplies many *specific earlier members of the same hard row*, each with its original certificate.

For adjacent earlier and later hard exponents, the later peak is exactly \(3^{162}x+(3^{162}-1)\) at the earlier peak \(x\). The numerator on the right in (3), with this fixed pair, is less than 164. To sample a single edge at \(j-i\) for \(0\le i<K\), first omit the at most \(K\) indices with \(j<K\). As \(s\) increases, the earlier peak plus one is multiplied by \(3^{162b}\). Since \(3^{162}\equiv9\bmod16\), raising it to an odd power preserves the exact valuation three of its difference from one. Each subsequent squaring adds exactly one to that valuation. Thus
\[
 v_2((3^{162b})^n-1)=e+v_2(n)\quad(n>0).
\]
At precision \(2^h\) the powers traverse every member of the subgroup \(1\bmod2^e\) once per \(P=2^{h-e}\) consecutive indices. The earlier peaks therefore sample *all* of one class modulo \(2^e\), with no independence assertion across the different edges. Each residue appears at most twice over the retained consecutive interval because \(P\le N<2P\). An ambient exceptional fraction \(\epsilon\) at precision \(2^h\) contributes at most \(2^{e+1}N\epsilon\) failures on an edge. Apply (3), sum over the \(K\) edges and use persistence to obtain the common label at the one clock \(h\). This proves the first estimate in (1).

For the second estimate, equal extreme labels require
\(p_h(U_{m_{j-K}})-p_h(U_{m_j})=162K\). At least one marginal grade deviates from \(h/2\) by at least \(81K\). Each binary parity word occurs for one start modulo \(2^h\), so either marginal on all residues has the binomial \((h,1/2)\) law. The same residue-fiber count and the elementary Hoeffding bound give the second inequality. The estimate applies also to any independent-clock peak meetings through \(h\) between the two extreme large exponents: if the earlier exponent exceeds \(2h\) and \(h\ge2\), the affine coefficient identity forces the two clocks and the augmented grades to agree. ## A sparse decidable check for convergence throughout this hard row

Keep \(j=0\). For \(j\ge1\) put \(h(j)=3+\lceil\log_2(2+j)\rceil\), and keep \(j\) exactly when the two adjacent peak labels disagree at \(h(j)\). Every interval of \(N\ge2\) consecutive nonnegative row indices contains \(O(N/\sqrt{\log(2N)})\) retained indices, uniformly in its position. Discard the at most \(\sqrt N+1\) indices below \(\sqrt N\). Every remaining retained index also missed a single clock \(3+\lfloor\tfrac12\log_2 N\rfloor\), by persistence. The residue-counting argument for one edge at this clock still applies: its period is at most \(N\), so representation multiplicity is at most twice \(N\) divided by the period. This proves the bound. By strong induction on \(j\), every \(z_j\) reaches one if and only if this holds at every retained \(j\). A nonretained \(z_j\) shares a future with \(z_{j-1}\), so it inherits the latter's eventual hit. The membership test is finite even though the literal exponents and starting values can be enormous.

The same proof applies to **all positive Mersenne exponents** without the hard-parent restriction. Keep exponent one, and keep any \(m\ge2\) whose peak fails the adjacent comparison with \(U_{m-1}\) at \(h(m)\). The pair state is now \((1,2)\). Split exponent parity, so the ratio of successive peak powers *within a sampling progression* is nine, again with valuation three. The retained list has the same uniform counting bound, and convergence of all Mersenne numbers is equivalent to convergence on this computable retained list. Prior work exhibits arbitrarily long finite delays for comparisons with every earlier peak, and the sparse test leaves the retained exponents to be resolved. ## Positive progressions and sparse fixed parameters

For every finite list of nonnegative exponent offsets and every arithmetic progression \(a+bs\), \(b>0\), there is a full subprogression on which *every* selected peak has one ordinary endpoint and one augmented grade at a single fixed postpeak clock. This qualitative assertion has a direct deterministic proof. Choose \(e\ge2\) such that \(q=3^{2b}=1+2^e c\), \(c\) odd, and fix \(A=3^a\). After the \(e\) fixed low bits, the input lifts \(3^d(A+2^e w)-1\) for each selected \(d\) form ordinary affine profiles whose slopes are powers of three and whose grades already include \(d\). Refine \(w\) by the finite-profile coalescence theorem. Any required new residue of \(w\) is attained by \(Aq^i=A+2^e w\) on an entire arithmetic progression of \(i\), since the increment \(q^{2^h}-1\) has exact valuation \(e+h\). Taking sufficiently large lifts keeps all affine coordinates nonnegative.
