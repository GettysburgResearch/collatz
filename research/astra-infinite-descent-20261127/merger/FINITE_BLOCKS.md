# Finite families of ordinary starts in a prescribed binary cylinder

**PROPOSED, with the all-clock ordinary first-hit finite-family theorem strictly compiled in Lean.** Fix a finite set of nonnegative integer offsets $F$ containing zero, binary precision $v$ and residue $r$, and ternary precision $s$. There are integers $B,g$ such that for **every** $D\ge B$, **every** ordinary $y$ prime to three, and **every** $c\bmod3^s$, a positive integer $x$ satisfies
$$
x\equiv r\pmod{2^v},\qquad x\equiv c\pmod{3^s},\qquad
T^D(x+i)=y,\quad O_D(x+i)=g\quad(i\in F).
$$
Each $x+i$ first reaches $y$ exactly at $D$. The two moduli and the entire finite pattern are fixed before the target, source trits and clock are supplied. Taking $F=\{0,\dots,M-1\}$ gives arbitrary fixed block lengths. The same argument allows any finite list with repetitions. The core finite-list induction is [AffineProfiles.lean](AffineProfiles.lean), and [BlockClock.lean](BlockClock.lean) proves the complete mixed-cylinder ordinary first-clock statement as first_cover.

## A finite refinement that gives one extra odd departure to a chosen arm

A binary cylinder with free high parameter $t$ represents an ordinary arm by the pair $(p,a)$, where $p$ is its cumulative odd count and its value is $A(t)=3^pt+a$. The ordinary dyadic lift identity reads
$$
T^j(n+2^j u)=T^j(n)+3^{O_j(n)}u,\qquad O_j(n+2^j u)=O_j(n).
$$
Thus on replacing $t$ by $t_0+2^j u$ the new profile has grade $p+O_j(A(t_0))$ and constant $T^j(A(t_0))$. Refinements compose, and equal profiles remain equal on every later refinement.

Suppose $A(t)=3^pt+a$ and $B(t)=3^qt+b$ with $p<q$. Since their slopes differ, choose $t_0\in\{0,1\}$ with $A(t_0)\ne B(t_0)$. Write their absolute difference as $2^j u$ with $u$ odd. The lift identity applied to the smaller of these values shows that their additional odd counts through clock $j$ agree, while their endpoints at that clock have opposite parities. Replacing $t_0$ by $t_0+2^j$ toggles both endpoint parities and preserves both counts up to that clock. Indeed each endpoint moves by the odd integer $3^{p+O_j(A(t_0))}$ or $3^{q+O_j(B(t_0))}$. Choose the orientation in which the first arm is odd and the second is even. After one more step the grade difference has fallen from $q-p$ to $q-p-1$. Repeat until grades agree.

At equal grade $p$, the starts have a fixed ordinary difference $d=|a-b|$ as $t$ varies. Apply the fixed-gap construction with precision $p$ and lower-source residue $\min(a,b)\bmod3^p$. Its endpoint can be chosen so large that its lower start is at least $\min(a,b)$. The selected lower start is then $3^pt+\min(a,b)$ for a nonnegative integer $t$. Both arms have the same endpoint and the same grade at this clock. Their refined profiles agree identically. This proves finite synchronization for arbitrary two profiles. Given a finite list, synchronize its first two members and then add one member at a time, always retaining earlier identifications.

## Closing the common profile at a supplied clock

Start with the binary cylinder $x=r+2^v t$. Run its $F$-arms for $v$ clocks and apply the finite refinement just proved. At some $H\ge v$, with $R\equiv r\bmod2^v$, every start $R+2^H u+i$ has the same grade $p$ and the same value $a+3^p u$, for every $u\ge 0$ and every $i\in F$.

Given $c$, solve $R+2^H u_0\equiv c\bmod3^s$. For any large enough remaining clock $L$, route an ordinary $w$ to $y$ in exactly $L$ steps and two odd departures while imposing
$$
                w\equiv a+3^p u_0\pmod {3^{p+s}}.
$$
The two-gap theorem works as soon as $L>6\cdot3^{p+s}$. The elementary height bound $2^L y\le16w$ ensures $w>a$ once $L\ge16(a+1)$. Thus $w=a+3^p u$ with $u>0$ and $u\equiv u_0\bmod3^s$. The decoded ordinary start realizes the entire prescribed pattern. Both the threshold and grade $p+2$ are independent of $y,c,L$. Use the off-orbit predecessor from the first-arrival theorem to make the final clock the first visit simultaneously for all arms. This adds at most ten to the clock threshold and one to the common grade.

The finite argument above uses an explicit deterministic one-count adjustment and makes no efficiency claim for $H$. Its role here is to match the exact-clock two-gap router, mixed source residues, and first arrivals. ## A consecutive 1,024-member example

The [exact certificate](FINITE_BLOCK_1024.json) gives a 429-bit lower source $R$. At clock 429, all 1,024 consecutive starts from $R$ through $R+1023$ share one 354-bit endpoint and grade 223. That endpoint first reaches 1 after 1,474 more clocks and 707 more odd departures. Each original member therefore reaches 1 for the first time at clock **1,903** with **930** odd departures. The [replay program](check_finite_patterns.py) regenerates the common cylinder, checks all 1,024 prefixes for three different free parameters, checks the endpoint tail with a 100,000-step budget, and separately verifies all 1,948,672 ordinary steps in the 1,024 first-hit traces. This is a finite numerical certificate.

The same cylinder gives an explicit unbounded family with exactly 223 odd departures throughout each block. The fixture records the integer $j_0$ satisfying $2^{j_0}\equiv z\bmod3^{223}$, where $z$ is the displayed common endpoint, and the period $P=2\cdot3^{222}$. For $j=j_0+kP$, $k\ge0$, take $t=(2^j-z)/3^{223}$. This is a positive integer. The 1,024 starts $R+2^{429}t+i$ all reach $2^j$ at clock 429, then first reach 1 at clock $429+j$. No earlier visit to 1 could be followed by the value $2^j>2$. Their common grade remains 223 and their excess time $429+j-\log_2(R+2^{429}t+i)$ tends to $223\log_2 3$. The script verifies both modular-power identities without constructing a number with $j_0$ binary digits.

## Relative windows and the fixed-source requirement

The common grade-$g$ clock theorem above can replace the two-arm certificate in the [relative-window construction](FAST_FIRST_HIT_PAIRS.md). At its one fixed initial clock, all $x+i$ hit the same chosen unit $w$ with that grade, and the start $x$ satisfies the same two-sided affine bounds. At most $\max F$ is added to its position to cover the whole family. For every fixed $\delta>0$ the entire pattern therefore fits inside each $[X,(1+\delta)X]$ once $X\ge C y$. Its common first-hit clock is at most $\log_2(X/y)+K$, with equal odd counts bounded by $G$. Here $C,K,G$ depend only on $F,v,r,s,\delta$. [FastBlocks.lean](FastBlocks.lean) now kernel-checks every interval and first-visit condition on every member, with the common grade bound and exponential time estimate. [MarkedProgressions.lean](MarkedProgressions.lean) closes the marked visitor theorem unconditionally. For every fixed pair of positive moduli $M,Q$, all target windows admit the first clock $D$ in any prescribed class modulo $M$ and its odd count in any independently prescribed class modulo $Q$. The threshold, speed constant and grade bound are uniform in both classes.

The same construction covers every source residue modulo any positive integer $m$ simultaneously. Apply the block theorem to the finite family of offsets $j+i$ with $0\le j<m$ and $i\in F$. It returns a single $z,D,g$. For each desired $c\bmod m$, choose $j$ in that range with $z+j\equiv c\bmod m$. The translated block $z+j+F$ then has exactly that certificate. The different blocks may overlap. This argument works both at each sufficiently late prescribed $D$ with one common fixed grade, and in every sufficiently high relative window with independently marked near-fast time and grade. The all-clock statement is [AllProgressions.all_clocks](AllProgressions.lean), and the unconditional window statement is [MarkedProgressions.all_residues](MarkedProgressions.lean). The larger synchronized family depends on $m$.

If a positive component misses $1$, it contains a unit $y$ and then has arbitrarily long consecutive blocks in each such macroscopic window, with prescribed mixed residue of the lower endpoint. The unit follows by halving to an odd state and taking its next step whenever that state is a multiple of three. Thus it would suffice to establish, for one fixed block length and one fixed residue of its first member, a sequence of unbounded macroscopic windows in which every such block contains a source that reaches $1$. This pointwise covering statement remains open. The constructed block's first member varies with the window.


## The dyadic limit of all finite refinements

For each finite set of natural shifts, the set of 2-adic parameters on which those shifts eventually synchronize in both value and odd count is open. A successful finite prefix certifies its entire binary cylinder. The finite-profile theorem makes this set dense, since it starts inside any supplied binary cylinder. Taking the intersection over all finite sets gives a comeager set of 2-adic parameters for which every natural translate eventually synchronizes with the same reference parameter. Equivalently, one can nest the successful cylinders, adding the next translate at each stage and refining one additional bit whenever needed. The complete 2-adic integers contain their limit. This Baire argument only uses the finite synchronization assertion; PR146 gives separate probabilistic estimates for ordinary finite cylinders.

No positive ordinary integer $n$ has this all-translates property: the shift by $n$ adds the input $2n$, and after positive clock $h$ its value is $T^{h-1}n$. Equality with $T^h n$ would yield a positive fixed point of the shortcut map. There is no such fixed point, and at clock zero the inputs already differ. For any nonpositive ordinary integer its natural translates include 0 and 1, whose orbits never synchronize. If they each synchronized with the reference at possibly different clocks, both synchronizations would persist at the larger clock, a contradiction. Thus this specific coherent dyadic limit construction excludes every ordinary integer.

In fact every rational 2-adic integer is excluded. Write $t=b/q$ in lowest terms with $q$ odd and positive. Choose $k>0$ with $q$ dividing $2^k-1$, and choose a nonnegative integer shift $n$ large enough that $u=t+n>0$. Then $2^k u=t+m$ for a nonnegative integer $m$. If both translates synchronized with the reference with equal odd counts, choose a common later time $H\ge k$. The value $2^k u$ first undergoes $k$ even halvings. The asserted equality at $H$ therefore gives both $T^H u=T^{H-k}u$ and $O_H(u)=O_{H-k}(u)$. Every one of the intervening $k$ steps is even, so the common positive rational state would equal itself divided by $2^k$. This contradiction places the entire comeager fusion set among the 2-adic irrationals.
