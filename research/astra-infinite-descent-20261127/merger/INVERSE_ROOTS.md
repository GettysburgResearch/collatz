# Minimal odd-unit ancestors in each individual target basin

Call an odd positive $z$ prime to three *minimal among its odd-unit ancestors* when any positive odd $w$ prime to three that eventually visits $z$ satisfies $w\ge z$. Every supplied ternary-unit target $y$, even or odd, has arbitrarily high such ancestors $z$. Each constructed $z$ lies outside the forward orbit of $y$. This conclusion applies separately in the inverse basin of any supplied target, including one on a hypothetical divergent orbit. [InverseRoots.roots_before](InverseRoots.lean)

Choose an odd-unit predecessor $b$ of $y$ outside its forward orbit, using the elementary first-arrival lemma. An odd unit $w$ visiting $b$ also lies outside the forward orbit of $y$. For any cutoff $N$, consider each $n\le N$ which visits $y$, and fix one of its visit times $k_n$. Any occurrence of such a $w$ on that orbit must come before $k_n$, since a later occurrence would put $b$ in the forward orbit of $y$. The union of these finitely many finite initial segments is bounded. The two-gap router gives arbitrarily large odd-unit $w$ visiting $b$ with two odd departures, because $2^D b\le16w$ for its arbitrarily large chosen clocks $D$. Choose $w$ beyond the bound and let $z$ be its least odd-unit ancestor. Every odd-unit ancestor of $z$ visits $w$, so $z$ has the stated minimality. The finite prefix bound gives $z>N$. [FirstArrival.branch](FirstArrival.lean) and [TwoGapRouting.route](TwoGapRouting.lean)

For **every ordinary** $n$ visiting such a $z$, even when $3\mid n$, one has
$$
                             2z\le 3n+1.
$$
Follow the initial halvings until the first odd $k\le n$. If $k$ is a unit, minimality gives $z\le k$. Otherwise its next state is $(3k+1)/2$, a unit. The subsequent initial halvings reach an odd unit at most this large before, or upon, the first visit to odd $z$. Minimality gives $z\le(3k+1)/2$. As $n\ge1$, in either case $z\le2n$. For an ordinary arrival at clock $D$, the forward affine inequality $3^{O_D(n)}n\le2^D z$ now gives
$$
             3^{O_D(n)}\le 2^{D+1},\qquad
             3^{O_D(n)}\le 2^D \quad\text{if $n$ is an odd unit}.
$$
The first source bound coupled with the forward inequality also gives $(2z-1)3^{O_D(n)}\le3\cdot2^D z$, retaining the sharper factor near $3/2$ for large $z$. These inequalities refer to every depth and every true ancestor. Taking $y=1$ places arbitrarily high examples entirely among known convergent integers. A search for a smaller point in the same component can still use a shared *later* forward endpoint. [InverseRoots.all_predecessors and charge_roots](InverseRoots.lean)

## Counting distinct minimum labels

The following counting argument is written. After one off-orbit step to $b$, a backward odd step has the form $(2^e v-1)/3$. For odd $v$ prime to three, exactly two $e\in\{1,\ldots,6\}$ give an odd unit. This follows from the six powers of two modulo nine. Every chosen odd predecessor is positive, and the deterministic next odd iterate recovers its parent. The vertex $b$ cannot lie on a cycle, since it reaches $y$ and lies outside the forward orbit of $y$. All $2^h$ paths of depth $h$ therefore have distinct leaves, all reaching $b$ for the first time after exactly $h$ odd departures. If two leaves had the same least odd-unit ancestor, its forward odd orbit would visit both. The first-arrival odd depths to $b$ would differ by a positive integer. Thus their least odd-unit ancestors are also distinct. All these minima are at most their respective leaves.

Every inverse step grows by less than $64/3$, and $b\le (2^{10}/3)y$. Hence at least $2^h$ distinct minimal odd-unit ancestors of $y$ lie below $(2^{10}/3)y(64/3)^h$. A sharper elementary count uses the two exponents' sum, always at most ten. Over the uniform $2^h$ paths, the sum of all chosen exponents has mean at most $5h$. The mean bound and integrality imply that at least $2^h/(5h+1)$ paths have total exponent at most $5h$. Their leaves and their distinct minima are all at most $(2^{10}/3)y(32/3)^h$. In particular the lower count is $(X/y)^{1/\log_2(32/3)-o(1)}$ below $X$ as $X/y\to\infty$, with absolute constants. This estimate controls minima up to $X$. Their actual locations can lie well below the chosen leaves.

Minimum ancestor labels and the conversion from an odd nonunit minimum to a nearby unit already appear in paper 102, Section 6. Paper 202, Theorem 1, gives a stronger asymptotic supply of disjoint predecessor basins with common first dates in a fixed target tree. Taking its fixed small parameters and selecting minimal odd-unit ancestors also yields many distinct minima, conditional on that paper's analytic inputs. Paper 177, Section 8, studies the all-time density of first-descent excursions with no smaller visitor. The elementary argument above uses a finite off-orbit prefix, and the counting argument needs only the six residue classes of ordinary odd units.
