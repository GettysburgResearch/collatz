# A supplied ordinary target requires large primitive returns

The central-row estimate considered in PR200 asks, at a fixed odd unit $q$, for unbounded rows $(D,p)$ with $p/D\to1/2$, scale $G=2^D/3^p$, and $G^{1-o(1)}$ distinct positive odd unit sources. Each $(x,5x)$ reaches $(q,5q)$ in exactly $D$ shortcuts with $p$ odd departures per arm. The first-arm real affine offset $\beta=q-x/G$ belongs to $[0,1/2]$, so all the sources have comparable height $X\asymp_q G$. The question remains open. The following estimates give necessary structure at the supplied integer $q$. ## The exponent of a fixed bank

**Written theorem.** Fix any finite collection $\mathcal W$ of primitive ratio-five return records starting on an odd input. Include all nonnegative common-halving paddings after each record, with arbitrary many records and arbitrary initial common halvings. A record has binary clock $h(w)$ and determines one length-$h(w)$ first-arm parity word. Write
$$
 E(z)=\frac{\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}z^{h(w)}}{1-z}.
$$
For every positive fixed integer $q$ and $z\in(1/2,1)$ satisfying $E(z)<1$, the number of positive $x\le X$ reaching $(q,5q)$ from $(x,5x)$ through this bank, at any clock and grade, is $O_{q,\mathcal W,z}(X^{-\log_2 z})$. A term permitting the empty concatenation only enlarges the language. There is always an eligible $z>1/2$. Since every fixed grade cap on primitive records yields a finite bank, this excludes all uniformly bounded primitive grades as a source of the requested near-linear row, at each individual $q$. [Fixed-grade primitive finiteness](NATIVE_FIVE.md)

To see strictness at $1/2$, the initial odd cylinders of all the selected first returns are disjoint. Let $H\ge3$ bound their lengths. The source $x=1$ has no positive return. At clocks one and two the endpoint pairs are $(2,8)$ and $(1,4)$. From clock three the companion is in $\{1,2\}$ and the lower source is at least one. The entire odd binary cylinder $x\equiv1\bmod 2^H$ also misses every selected record. Indeed the grade pair is fixed by the low $H$ bits, and whenever the two grades agree the difference of the second endpoint and five times the first is fixed as well. This follows directly from the two affine lifts, whose parameter slopes at equal grades are $3^p$ and $5\cdot3^p$. Thus $E(1/2)=\sum_w2^{1-h(w)}\le1-2^{1-H}$. Continuity gives the interval above $1/2$.

For the counting step, a full section is a record followed by $b\ge0$ zero parity bits, of total clock $e=h(w)+b$. The total $z$-weight of all formal concatenations is bounded by $(1-E(z))^{-1}$; counting duplicates can only enlarge this upper bound. The number of complete concatenations of clock $m$ is therefore $O(z^{-m})$. At a fixed later prefix length, an incomplete final section has at most $2|\mathcal W|+1$ possible bit strings. It consists either of the initial part of its selected record or of that entire record followed by as many zeros as needed. At the end of a section the incomplete portion can be empty. Initial independent zero padding has total $z$-weight $(1-z)^{-1}$. Summing over the last completed boundary $m\le L$ now bounds the number of admissible length-$L$ first-arm parity strings by $O(z^{-L})$. This argument permits a variable final physical clock arbitrarily far beyond $L$.

The mapping from a length-$L$ first-arm parity word to $x\bmod2^L$ is a bijection. Indeed a binary lift by $2^L$ preserves the first $L$ parities and changes the next one, since the current affine slope is an odd power of three. In a height shell $[X,2X)$ reaching $q$ takes at least $L=\lfloor\log_2(X/q)\rfloor$ clocks, by $x\le2^Dq$. Each admitted binary prefix hence gives $O_q(1)$ actual sources in the shell. Summing the resulting positive power across dyadic shells proves the theorem. Endpoint, clock, and grading restrictions may only decrease this ordinary count.

The PR204 finite bank takes every primitive return through clock 27 with both grades at most eight. Its 2,276 records have conditional mass $E(1/2)=2923925/33554432$. Rebuilding the two-arm binary tree and evaluating the resulting polynomial at a rational point gives exactly

 E(17/28)=
 \frac{228726486040445967436698285962972757271}
 {232525991228180563449170925246818025472}<1 .
$$
Also $28^{25}<2^{18}17^{25}$. The number of its ordinary sources at *each fixed target*, even with all padding lengths allowed, is therefore $O_q(X^{\log_2(28/17)})$ with exponent below $18/25$. Its optimal bound from this particular polynomial is the root exponent approximately $0.718267$. The earlier PR204 lower exponent is $1/5$ with its selected clocks and a nonconstant ternary potential. ## Ordinary occupancy of the full primitive dictionary

**Written theorem.** Fix a primitive grade cap $K$ and $\theta>0$. In any putative near-linear central row at a fixed $q$, all but $O_{q,K,\theta}(X^{1-c})$ sources, for a fixed $c=c(K,\theta)>0$, spend at least $(1-\theta)L$ of their first $L=\lfloor\log_2(X/q)\rfloor$ clocks inside primitive words of grade exceeding $K$. Count the portion of a primitive word crossing clock $L$, and exclude subsequent common-halving padding from this occupancy. This includes the full dictionary with unbounded primitive grades and clocks.

Start an odd source and mark its initial time and every subsequent time at which the paired orbit again has ratio five, equal cumulative grades, and an odd lower state. In the uniform binary source model the first primitive record of length $h$ occupies one cylinder of conditional probability $2^{1-h}$. Its endpoint's next $b$ parities being even and the following one being odd costs $2^{-b-1}$. Those bits are fresh because after fixing the previous $h$ bits the lower endpoint has odd affine slope in the remaining parameter. Thus the mass of a particular consecutive marked section $(w,e=h+b)$ is $2^{-e}$. Successive waiting times have the same law and are independent up to any infinite waiting time. The odd bit shared at a mark is already conditioned upon at the start of the next section. PR146 proved the sharp tail of order $n^{-1/2}$ for this waiting time, together with regeneration at odd marks. [PR146 first-return theorem and regeneration](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437/research/upper-bit-mergers/FIVE_RETURN_CLOCK_AND_TERMINALS.md)

Only the infinite mean is needed for exponential upper bounds on excess marks. One elementary proof, also underlying PR146's lower bound, observes that the grade difference is a martingale in the successive source bits. At a step the two newly exposed endpoint parities each reverse under the alternative next source bit. Thus the difference increment is either identically zero or equally likely $+1,-1$. Conditioning on $x\equiv1\bmod8$ gives grade difference $-1$ at clock three and no earlier positive return. The time to regain grade difference zero has infinite mean. Otherwise the bounded increments would give dominated convergence for the martingale stopped at that time, contradicting the initial value $-1$. Failure ever to regain zero already gives infinite mean. Hence the marked waiting time $J$ has infinite mean. Set $e^{-\lambda\infty}=0$. As $\lambda\downarrow0$, either $\Pr(J=\infty)>0$ or monotone convergence gives $(1-\mathbb E e^{-\lambda J})/\lambda\to\infty$.

For any $a>0$ choose $\lambda>0$ so that $e^\lambda(\mathbb E e^{-\lambda J})^a<1$. At least $aL$ positive completed marks by time $L$ requires the first $\lceil aL\rceil$ waiting times to sum to at most $L$. Exponential Markov bounds its probability by
$$
 e^{\lambda L}(\mathbb E e^{-\lambda J})^{\lceil aL\rceil}\le e^{-c_a L}.
$$
The event is determined by at most $L+1$ binary source bits, including the parity at the terminal mark. This gives $O_q(X^{1-c'_a})$ actual sources in $[X,2X)$, by counting full binary periods and a possible partial period; the bounded number of representatives per cylinder follows because $2^L\asymp_q X$.

For the padding, fix $t>0$ and choose $a>0$ sufficiently small. On the event of at most $aL$ completed sections, the zero padding up to $L$ is a union of at most $aL+2$ disjoint intervals. The number of unions of so many intervals in $\{0,\ldots,L-1\}$ is at most $\sum_{j\le2aL+4}\binom{L+1}{j}=\exp(o_{a\downarrow0}(1)L+o(L))$ by specifying their boundaries. Each specified union of size at least $tL$ requires at least $tL$ particular first-arm parities zero, an event of probability at most $2^{1-tL}$ conditional on initial oddness. Union bounding over masks shows that excess padding has exponentially small probability. No independence of the padding intervals selected by an actual path is needed.

The grade cap $K$ bounds the length of each primitive word of that grade or lower by $H_K<\infty$ from the carry argument. At most $(aL+1)H_K$ clocks belong to such short-grade words when there are at most $aL$ completed sections, including a possible word crossing $L$. First choose $t<\theta/3$, then $a$ small enough for the padding bound and $aH_K<\theta/3$. This proves the stated first-$L$ occupancy, with the finitely many small scales absorbed in the constant. The conclusion concerns an exceptional *count of ordinary sources*. It does not assert that any supplied individual source follows the binary probability law.

## Transfer through the whole central clock

**Source-qualified written deduction.** Let $\kappa=1-\log_2\sqrt3>0$ and $D_*(X)=\log_2 X/\kappa$. Inselmann's power-saving trajectory theorem says that for each fixed $\xi>0$, outside $O_\xi(X^{1-c_\xi})$ ordinary starts in $[X,2X)$ one has, simultaneously at every integer $0\le j\le D_*(X)-1$,
$$
 X^{-\xi}U_j\ll T^j(x)\ll X^\xi U_j,\qquad U_j=X\,2^{-\kappa j}.
$$
Changing $\xi$ by a fixed factor absorbs the endpoints of the octave. This is the power-saving form in Theorems 2.18 and 3.1 and Definition 2.6 of the cited paper. We use its one-arm conclusion only. The result below remains a written deduction with this stated prior input. [Inselmann, *An approximation of the Collatz map and a lower bound for its average total stopping time*, arXiv:2402.03276v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03276)

Fix $\eta>0$. For every bounded finite-window binary event with length $\ell=\lfloor\delta\log_2 X\rfloor$ and probability $\ll e^{-c\ell}$ under uniform low bits, its occurrences on the lower arm $u=T^j(x)$ at any $0\le j\le(1-\eta)D_*(X)$ concern only $O(X^{1-c'})$ original $x\in[X,2X)$, provided $0<\delta<\eta/3$ and the constants $c,\eta,\delta$ are fixed. The event may depend on the entire pair $(u,5u)$ and may prescribe that $u$ is odd. It must be a function of at most $\ell+1$ binary bits of $u$. In particular it applies at an actual odd paired return, where the upper state is exactly $5u$.

To prove the transport, first choose $\delta,c$, then take $\xi>0$ sufficiently small. For an Inselmann-regular $x$, the affine formula for its first $j$ clocks reads $u=\alpha_j x+\beta_j$, where $\alpha_j=3^{p_j}/2^j$ and $\beta_j\ge0$. The additive contribution of the odd step at $i$ to $\beta_j$ is half the multiplier from time $i+1$ to $j$. That latter multiplier is at most $T^j x/T^{i+1}x$ since all subsequent offsets are nonnegative. Thus
$$
 \beta_j\ll X^{2\xi}\sum_{i<j}2^{-\kappa(j-i-1)}\ll_\kappa X^{2\xi}.
$$
Meanwhile $u\gg X^{-\xi}U_j$ and $U_j\ge X^\eta$. Hence $\alpha_j^{-1}=x/(u-\beta_j)\ll X^{1+\xi}/U_j$. For fixed $j,p_j,u$, all original regular $x$ lie in the interval $[(u-CX^{2\xi})/\alpha_j,u/\alpha_j]$. Its integer count is $O(1+X^{1+3\xi}/U_j)$. Among $u\ll X^\xi U_j$, the short event occurs for $O(X^\xi U_j e^{-c\ell})$ integers by counting all admissible residues modulo $2^{\ell+1}\ll U_j$. Multiplying these bounds, choosing $\xi$ sufficiently small relative to $c\delta$, and summing over $O((\log X)^2)$ possible $j,p_j$ proves the claim.

In a central row, $X\asymp_q G$ and $p/D\to1/2$ give $D=(1+o(1))D_*(X)$. Partition all but a chosen small final fraction of this clock into consecutive blocks of length $\ell$ (the last incomplete block can be discarded). If any such block contains at least $a\ell$ marked times for a fixed $a>0$, its first mark $j$ has at least $a\ell-1$ subsequent marks by $j+\ell$. At the actual pair $(u,5u)$ there this is an exponentially sparse finite event by the renewal bound. The transfer excludes it with a power saving, uniformly over the mark time. Thus for each fixed $\theta>0$, apart from $O(X^{1-c_\theta})$ original inputs of a central row, its *entire* $D$-clock path has at most $\theta D$ marked sections. Choose the discarded fraction, end block, and $a$ in that order small enough compared with $\theta$.

The same transport handles any union of at most $a\ell+O(1)$ intervals of local first-arm times. For fixed $t>0$, if $a$ is sufficiently small, the probability that their total parity discrepancy exceeds $t\ell$ is $\ll e^{-c_t\ell}$. Here discrepancy means the absolute difference between the number of ones in the union and half its size. There are at most $\exp(o_{a\downarrow0}(1)\ell+o(\ell))$ possible boundary masks, and each prescribed mask has the usual exponential binary-sum tail by the finite binomial Chernoff inequality. This also bounds all-zero padding occupying a fixed positive fraction of the local block. Covering preterminal time by the fixed number of $\ell$-blocks, each intersection with a union of at most $a'D$ whole-path intervals has at most $a'D+O(1)$ local intervals. Take $a'$ small relative to $a\ell/D$, then absorb the discarded final portion. Consequently, for every $t>0$ there exists $a'>0$ such that a central row, apart from $O(X^{1-c_t})$ starts, has total parity discrepancy at most $tD$ on **every** union of at most $a'D$ intervals anywhere on its entire first arm. This quantifier includes intervals chosen from the actual path. In particular the whole-path padding and the time in primitive words of any fixed bounded grade together occupy an arbitrarily small fraction of $D$, up to a power-saving exception.

### A necessary single primitive fiber

Suppose a supplied $q$ has near-linear central rows as above. Then there exist individual primitive-return rows of clocks $h\to\infty$, grades $a/h\to1/2$, and scales $G_h=2^h/3^a$, with at least $G_h^{1-o(1)}$ distinct ordinary starting values on a *single* such primitive row. The selected ordinary endpoint may change between rows. It lies on a paired itinerary leading to the original supplied $(q,5q)$. Its first-arm real offsets on the selected primitive row are at most $2^{o(h)}$. The following proof retains that endpoint dependence.

Pass to a diagonal subsequence of the asserted central rows. The polynomially exceptional sets for each fixed error parameter omit only a vanishing fraction of a near-linear row. Thus a subfamily of size $G^{1-o(1)}$ has $m=o(D)$ complete primitive sections, total common-even padding $o(D)$, and total first-arm parity discrepancy $o(D)$ on every union of at most $2m$ intervals. One may choose deterministic bounds $m\le a_DD$ and discrepancy $\le t_D D$ uniformly on each subfamily, with $a_D,t_D\downarrow0$ sufficiently slowly. Every source and endpoint in the stated PT row is odd, so its entire path factors into completed sections.

Index those sections chronologically. Let $e_i$ be total padded duration, $h_i$ primitive duration, $p_i$ grade, $G_i=2^{e_i}/3^{p_i}$, and $\beta_i$ the forward affine offset over the whole section. Positive and negative section discrepancies each form a union of at most $m$ intervals, so
$$
 \sum_i |p_i-e_i/2|=o(D),\qquad
 \sum_i|\log_2G_i-\kappa e_i|=o(D).
$$
Also $\sum_i\log_2(1+\beta_i)=o(D)$. In fact the affine contribution of a suffix of length $s$ with $p$ odd steps has multiplier $2^{-\kappa s}3^{p-s/2}$. For each section take a suffix maximizing its positive discrepancy, including the empty suffix. These disjoint suffixes form at most $m$ intervals. Their total excess above half their lengths is $o(D)$. The section offset is bounded by $(e_i+1)$ times three to this maximal discrepancy, and $\sum_i\log_2(e_i+2)=o(D)$ by concavity since $m=o(D)$.

Choose $b_i=\lceil\log_2(1+\beta_i)\rceil$, so $\sum b_i=o(D)$. The number of possible tuples $(m,(e_i,h_i,p_i,b_i)_i)$ is $2^{o(D)}$ by stars and bars, since $m=o(D)$ and the arrays have total respectively $D$, at most $D$, at most $D$, and $o(D)$. Retain one tuple with $G^{1-o(1)}$ ordinary full sources. In the backwards prefix tree from the fixed $q$, a node of level $i$ with odd parent $u$ has at most
$$
 A_i=1+\lceil G_i\,2^{b_i}\rceil
$$
distinct sources for this one specified section. Each is an integer in $[G_i(u-2^{b_i}),G_i u]$. Its primitive endpoint is the same even-or-odd integer $2^{e_i-h_i}u$ for every child of that node. Each child gives one actual primitive $h_i$-clock word and the source determines its trajectory. Since the negative parts of $\log_2G_i$ sum to $o(D)$ and $\sum b_i=o(D)$, the product of these level capacities is at most $G\,2^{o(D)}$.

Let $N_i$ be the largest actual number of children of any node at the corresponding level of the retained tree. The products satisfy $G^{1-o(1)}\le\prod_i N_i\le\prod_i A_i\le G\,2^{o(D)}$, hence $\sum_i(\log_2 A_i-\log_2 N_i)=o(D)$. Weight the choice of an index $i$ by $e_i/D$. For this choice each of
$$
 1/e_i,\quad (e_i-h_i)/e_i,\quad |p_i-e_i/2|/e_i,\quad
 b_i/e_i,\quad (\log_2 A_i-\log_2 N_i)/e_i
$$
has mean tending to zero. Select one index where their sum tends to zero and a node attaining $N_i$. Now $h_i/e_i\to1$, $p_i/h_i\to1/2$, and $\log_2 N_i\ge\log_2 G_i-o(e_i)=\kappa h_i-o(h_i)$. Removing the common terminal padding increases each affine offset by $2^{e_i-h_i}=2^{o(h_i)}$. This proves the claimed necessary primitive row. It leaves open both the full-target estimate and any independent opposing upper bound on these high-grade primitive fibers.

## Rich primitive rows in the actual predecessor tree of every supplied target

**Source-qualified positive theorem.** The necessary intermediate-parent conclusion above can in fact be attained for every positive unit $q$, using the sharp prior first-return tail of PR146 and the elementary trit router in this packet. For arbitrarily large $h$ there is a common $p=h/2+O(\sqrt{h\log h})$ and at least $3^p/h^C$ classes of unit parents modulo $3^{p+1}$ with the following property, for an absolute $C$. Every sufficiently large parent $v$ in one of those classes has at least
$$
 \frac{2^h}{3^p h^C}
$$
distinct positive odd unit sources $x$ for which the physical pair $(x,5x)$ makes its *first positive equal-grade ratio-five return* at clock $h$, with common grade $p$, ending at $(v,5v)$. Its first-arm real offsets are at most $h^C$; the companion offsets are five times the first-arm offsets. A single eligible odd unit parent can be chosen in the actual paired predecessor tree of the specified $(q,5q)$, with a four-grade suffix of clock $O(3^p+h)$. No source in this argument is a 3-adic limit.

PR146 proves that the first primitive return $H$ from an odd root has $c/\sqrt n\le\Pr(H>n)\le C_0/\sqrt n$. Choose an absolute $B$ large enough that the difference between the lower bound at $n$ and upper bound at $Bn$ is at least $c/(2\sqrt n)$. Some $h\in(n,Bn]$ has $\Pr(H=h)\gg h^{-3/2}$. At clock $h$ the first-arm grade differs from $h/2$ by at most $C_1\sqrt{h\log h}$ outside a set of probability $O(h^{-4})$ by the ordinary binomial bound (conditioning the first bit odd costs at most two). Also, outside $O(h^{-4})$ probability, every suffix of length $s\ge A\log h$ has at most $3s/5$ odd bits, for large absolute $A$. Sum the binary Chernoff bound over $s$. Each long suffix multiplier is therefore at most $(3^{3/5}/2)^s$, and shorter suffix multipliers are at most $(3/2)^{A\log h}$. Summing the contributions of the odd steps gives a bound $h^{C_2}$ for the real affine offset $\beta$ of the first arm. Pigeonhole the remaining $\gg h^{-3/2}$ probability over at most $h+1$ grade values. Conditional on an odd first input, each complete length-$h$ first-return record has mass $2^{1-h}$. We obtain at least $c_1 2^h h^{-5/2}$ distinct primitive records of that same $h,p$, all with $\beta\le h^{C_2}$. [PR146 sharp primitive tail, first section](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437/research/upper-bit-mergers/FIVE_RETURN_CLOCK_AND_TERMINALS.md)

For such a record let $C_w=2^h\beta$. This is an odd ternary unit and the unpadded inverse is exactly $x=(2^h v-C_w)/3^p$. Among all unit classes $v\bmod3^{p+1}$, exactly two make $x$ an integer prime to three: these are the two good lifts of its single required residue mod $3^p$. The average number of available records per unit parent class is thus at least $c_1(2^h/3^p)h^{-5/2}$. For any fixed sufficiently large representative $v$ all these inverses are positive, satisfy the specified binary words by the affine lift formula, and are distinct, since a single input has a unique primitive word. They lie in the interval $[(2^h/3^p)(v-h^{C_2}),(2^h/3^p)v]$, so at most $1+(2^h/3^p)h^{C_2}$ of them inhabit any parent class. At least $3^p/h^{C_3}$ classes therefore reach half the average, after enlarging the absolute constants. Positivity for every qualifying class follows already for $v>h^{C_2}$. At these $h,p$ the inverse dilation $G_h=2^h/3^p$ grows exponentially in $h$.

Choose one rich trit class and apply the native two-$A$ router at precision $p+1$ to hit $q$ from a positive $v$ in that class. Given the split $e$, its equation is $81v+63+7\cdot2^e=2^{D_0}q$. Use exponents $20\le e<54\cdot3^{p+1}+20$ in its complete ternary period, and take $D_0=54\cdot3^{p+1}+h+O(1)$ so $D_0-e\ge20$. Increasing the absolute constant if necessary gives $v>2^h q+h^{C_2}$ for all sufficiently large $h$. Both padded $A$ legs are genuine flights. Each preceding rich primitive has $x=G_h(v-\beta)>2^h q$, so all its states before clock $h$ exceed $q$, since $2^jT^j(x)\ge x$. The companion exceeds $5q$ along that prefix by the same argument. Thus all these *complete* two-arm visits to the supplied $q,5q$ are also literal first arrivals, with one common full clock $h+D_0$ and common full grade $p+4$. The grade/clock balance and near-linear exponent apply to the primitive prefix of clock $h$. The long attachment changes both exponents on the full path.

A sufficient remaining estimate is now explicit. For an unbounded sequence of the rich rows, suppose some rich parent class has an actual paired ancestor $v$ of the specified $(q,5q)$, with $v>h^{C_2}$, on a native suffix of clock $o(h)$ and real offset at most $1/4$. If $v\ge 4q h^{C_2}$, the prefix offset transmits at most $1/4$ more, since the suffix slope is at most $q/v$. Then these genuine odd-unit sources satisfy the desired $G_{\rm full}^{1-o(1)}$ count, $p_{\rm full}/D_{\rm full}\to1/2$ and full $\beta\le1/2$. If a first-arrival guard is also needed, a fixed jointly off-future terminal child as in the narrow-window transfer enforces first visits. The available $O(3^p+h)$ routing does not give this sublinear attachment to a rich class at every supplied $q$. The cardinality of the rich class set alone places no bound on its gaps along that particular ordinary inverse orbit.
