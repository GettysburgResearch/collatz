# Delayed and paid mergers at the same fixed binary valuation

Fix \(1\le k\le192\), put \(J=18k+4\), and vary \(m=12\cdot2^{18k}r-1\) through positive odd cofactors \(r\equiv1\bmod27\). Thus \(v_2(3^{m+1}-1)=J\) is fixed and \(m=11+162j\) is on the ordinary hard-parent row. For every \(H\ge J\) there is an entire arithmetic progression of these cofactors, with \(m\ge4H+4\), such that

1. No \(3^t-1\), \(1\le t<m\), shares any physical iterate with \(3^m-1\) when both clocks are at most \(H\).
2. On the very same progression, the specific earlier peak \(3^{m-162}-1\) does meet \(3^m-1\) at a common postpeak clock \(H'<C(H+162)\), with the earlier grade larger by 162. Here \(C\) is absolute. Thus \(2^{162}z_{j-1}<z_j\), these two specified canonical sources share a future, and the earlier triple reaches one with exactly three odd departures 162 clocks earlier than the later triple.

One can choose such a cofactor with \(H\ll\log_2 r\ll H+162\). In particular at every one of the 192 fixed valuations, infinitely many original positive sources realize a least two-clock deadline for any earlier peak of order \(\log r\), and admit an explicitly priced earlier row at that order. PR192 established obstruction through \(J\) at cofactor one. PR146 proved unbounded early-clock obstruction by varying an exponent and choosing the signed center \(-6\) at valuation four. The finite valuation test below and the controlled successful refinement keep \(J\) fixed. These are written results with an exact finite integer certificate. ## A finite exact test that controls every postrelease clock

Write \(U=(3^{m+1}-1)/2^J\) and compare the upper peak with the peak of exponent \(m-d\), \(d\ge1\). Use the usual shortcut branches on signed rationals of odd denominator. The upper initial signed value at \(U=-1\) is \((1-2^J)/3-1\). Its first two departures are even and odd. The resulting value is \(-2^{J-2}\). After \(J-2\) further halvings it reaches \(-1\), where every departure is odd. Thus it has exactly \(J-1\) even departures in its entire infinite path.

The lower initial signed value is \(\gamma_d-2^J/3^{d+1}\), where \(\gamma_d=3^{-d-1}-1\). The first \(J\) parities equal those of \(\gamma_d\). Let \(p\) be their odd count and \(s=p-d-1\). At clock \(J\) the exact lower signed value is
\[
                 w=T^J(\gamma_d)-3^s.
\]
This follows from the affine coefficient \(3^p/2^J\) on those common parities. If a counted equality occurred before \(J\), it would persist to \(J\). A counted equality at or after \(J\) requires exactly \(s\) further *even* lower departures before equality, since all further upper departures are odd. Hence \(s\ge0\), in particular \(d<J\). At \(s\ge0\) the number \(w\) is an integer: the initial denominator \(3^{d+1}\) loses one power at each odd step until it is integral. If \(3^{d+1}>2^J-1\), the lower initial value lies strictly between \(-2\) and \(-1\), with even numerator. Its first half is greater than \(-1\), a region preserved by either branch. It cannot reach \(-1\). For all remaining candidates, \(-2^s\le w\le-1\) is necessary: on negative integers an odd shortcut never reduces absolute value and each even shortcut halves it.

The finite test uses every \(J=18k+4\), \(1\le k\le192\), and every \(1\le d<J\). It computes the exact numerator of \(T^J\gamma_d\) against the remaining denominator \(3^{\max(d+1-p,0)}\). An even step halves the numerator. An odd step at remaining exponent \(a>0\) replaces the numerator \(n\) by \((n+3^{a-1})/2\), or by \((3n+1)/2\) after the denominator becomes one. Of the 334,080 pairs, 166,182 pass the real-interval and nonnegative-credit tests. Only 58 pass the integer size test, and every one of them already has \(w=-1\). Their credits are one in 44 cases and three in 14. At \(-1\) there are no more even steps available, so none can repay its credit. This computation excludes counted equality for all common clocks, including unbounded clocks, at this signed center. It proves the finite family \(k\le192\). ## Returning the two signed calculations to the same ordinary inputs

Let \(A=3^{12\cdot2^{18k}}\). We have \(v_2(A-1)=J\) by \(v_2(3^{12}-1)=4\) and successive squaring. For \(r>s\ge0\),
\[
 v_2\left(\frac{A^r-1}{2^J}-\frac{A^s-1}{2^J}\right)=v_2(r-s).
\]
Thus the quotient map permutes the residues modulo every \(2^L\), taking odd residues to odd residues. Choose \(U\equiv-1\bmod2^{\max(1,H-J)}\). This also enforces odd \(r\) when \(H=J\). CRT allows \(r\equiv1\bmod27\) simultaneously. Both true initial peaks now differ from their corresponding signed centers by a multiple of \(2^H\) in the odd-denominator rationals. For any proposed common clock \(i\le H\), their parity words agree with their signed words. If the two augmented counts agree too, their affine coefficients on the common parameter agree, so *endpoint equality would transfer exactly to the signed center*. Only matching low binary digits of the endpoints would not suffice for this implication.

For independent clocks \(i,l\le H\), an actual collision with \(t=m-d>2H\) forces \(i=l\) and the matching augmented grades. Indeed clearing the two affine endpoint formulas gives an integer slope difference times \(3^t\), with the remaining absolute offset bounded by \(2\cdot6^H<3^t\). For \(t\le2H\), the initial height ratio exceeds \(3^{m-t}>4^H\), whereas positive shortcut steps lie between one half and twice their input. This proves assertion 1 on the entire cofactor class above the displayed exponent floor.

To get assertion 2, use the earlier peak \(x=3^{m-162}-1\) as the binary parameter. The selected signed-center condition fixes one class of \(x\) at precision \(2^{\max(H,J+1)}\), and the later peak is exactly \(3^{162}x+3^{162}-1\). The constructive affine-ray theorem extends *this very class* to one with a counted merger at \(H'=O(H+162)\) total postpeak precision and clock. Its deterministic proof forces the level to zero, then contracts a fixed additive offset. The odd slopes have equal 2-adic valuation, so both clocks agree, and their initial power-of-three discrepancy forces the grade difference 162. If the subclass fixes fewer digits than the clock, refine it further to that clock. Choose \(r\) realizing these new digits by the same quotient permutation: \(3^{m-162}=A^r/3^{163}\), with odd denominator. The selected residue still has the original value modulo \(2^J\), and the fixed next bit preserves odd \(r\). Adding a multiple of \(27\cdot2^{O(H+162)}\) preserves all conditions. Choosing the first such positive lift beyond \(2^H\) establishes the logarithmic size estimates.
