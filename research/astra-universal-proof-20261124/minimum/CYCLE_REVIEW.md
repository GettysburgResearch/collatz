# Independent check of the cycle occupation bound

**VERIFIED as a written theorem at the scoped version.** The [cycle occupation argument](../critical/CYCLE_OCCUPATION.md), reviewed at Git blob `ae868b136ffc9a41dbfd0e3bc08d31538354a5ee`, establishes its asymptotic lower bound on the fraction of the odd phases of an ordinary positive primitive cycle above twice the minimum. The uniform positive fraction for nontrivial short cycles uses the single-octave cycle exclusion in the [source calculation](../../astra-global-synthesis-20261124/arithmetic/proof.md).

**Moving-octave capacity.** Write $A_j$ for the sum of the first $j$ stripping exponents and $B_j=2^{A_j}x_j$. At common octave $[H_j,2H_j)$, the unique integer whose rescaling of that octave contains $B_j$ is $A_j$. The exact update is $B_{j+1}=3B_j+2^{A_j}$. This update strictly preserves order of the lifted values and commutes with doubling, which adds one to the octave index. Two distinct ordered starts $x<x'<2x$ therefore have $B_j<B'_j<2B_j$ throughout. In particular $A_j\le A'_j\le A_j+1$. The sum of the $k$ noninitial clock coordinates strictly increases between distinct ordered words, spanning at most $k$. This gives at most $k+1$ words on one prescribed profile.

For fixed word and total $s$, the carry $C$ in $2^s x_k=3^k x+C$ is independent of the source. Since $2^s$ is a ternary unit, all terminal states agree modulo $3^k$. Their terminal interval has length $m\le3^k$, hence contains at most one such value. The affine equation then recovers the initial state uniquely. This argument works even when the earlier octaves have different widths. The [independent Lean proof](MovingOctaveOrder.lean) checks the complete $k+1$ capacity on integral octave lower endpoints, including the clock order, propagated source difference, terminal spacing, and final count. Its path hypotheses are just the physical step equations, the initial zero clock, the accumulated stripping exponents and the prescribed octave constraints. Relaxing oddness and exact stripping valuations in those hypotheses strengthens the statement. The [source Lean file](../../astra-global-synthesis-20261124/arithmetic/OctaveProfiles.lean) separately supplies the ordered-vector count and fixed-word terminal parity spacing.

**Window count.** The union estimate for positive endpoints discards at most $2p/21$ windows. Counting positive interior sites with multiplicity before applying Markov's inequality discards at most $75p/84$ more, leaving at least $p/84$. This remains valid if a circular window wraps. The source code for schedules telescopes since the positive-next indicators sum to $r$ and the boundary levels cancel. Its backwards recurrence reconstructs each level from the terminal zero. Independently, with $r$ positive interior levels there are $k-r$ gaps between zeros. A positive run within one gap starts at depth one and can rise by at most one, so its preorder depths reconstruct one ordered rooted tree including its implicit root. The degree list of the ordered forest determines the original depth schedule, giving the stated weak-composition bound. Multiplying by the per-profile capacity, then inserting the polynomial height bound, gives the claimed strict exponent below one. The form estimate in Theorem 1 permits zero constant coefficient and measures the height by the maximum of the two logarithmic coefficients. [Wu and Wang, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2014.03.007)

**Test against the minimum-cycle restrictions.** In the odd rational model the exact primitive cycle with ordered numerators $(283,431,653,493,373)$ and common denominator 13 has exponent word $(1,1,2,2,2)$. Its minimum $s=283/13$ gives $q=(2s+1)/3=193/13$, which equals one modulo 12 in rationals with denominator prime to 12. Its maximal odd runs have lengths three, one and one with one even input following each. Their entries $(283,493,373)/13$ all lie below $2(2s+1)$. Its period ratio $8/5$ is an upper continued-fraction convergent to $\log_2 3$. Yet one of five odd phases exceeds $2s$. The complete return denominator is $256-243=13$, so ordinary integrality fails. The strip shape, run bounds and upper-convergent condition alone thus allow occupation exceeding $1/21$ in the rational relaxation. They yield no contradiction for the remaining ordinary cycle.

## Synchronization on the same physical cycle

**VERIFIED as a written theorem** for [the synchronization argument](../critical/COMPANION_SYNCHRONIZATION.md) at Git blob `f80fcd6f291318161f3b7c8db191797e04db7ce3`. Positive drift over each lap sends the centered walk to minus infinity in the remote past. Its running maximum exists, translates by the drift, and has a nonempty cyclic set of strict increase times. In a gap between consecutive strict records the intermediate centered heights may equal the first record. They never exceed it. The next record overshoots by at most one odd centered increment. Subtracting the drift correction from physical log-height increments leaves a loss of at least $c$ per clock once the minimum is large. These facts give both height bounds on the entire gap.

For a strict height threshold, if the terminal record exceeds it, its positive excess is at most the initial positive excess plus $V-c(b-a)$, covering all high clocks in the half-open gap. Otherwise a nonempty set of $h$ high times has its last clock at least $h-1$ clocks after the initial record. The initial excess must thus strictly exceed $c(h-1)$ and is itself high. If neither a terminal high record nor any high clock occurs, the charge is zero. These cases include equality with the threshold at either endpoint. The potentials cancel over a lap and the incoming and outgoing record sets agree cyclically. High odd phases also count as high physical phases, and $L\le2p$ gives the stated physical fraction.

The physical parity word is primitive: if it were a proper power, the physical seed would be fixed by a positive iterate of the strictly increasing affine map for the shorter word. Such a fixed point is already fixed by the shorter map, contradicting physical primitivity. All backward companion slope products at a strict record decay geometrically, giving the uniform bound by summing the positive affine series. Multiplication by the common reduced denominator produces bounded ordinary integer numerators. A fixed short past determines that numerator modulo the specified power of three, so two marked occurrences with the same past would give identical companion phases. The even and odd companion numerators, divided by their common odd denominator, occupy disjoint dyadic branches. Equality of phases therefore forces equality of every future symbol, impossible at distinct positions of the primitive word. This argument does not equate the physical and companion denominators.
