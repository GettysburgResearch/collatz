# The root defect is an exact ordinary coboundary

**PROPOSED, unconditional.** Let T be the positive shortcut map, and set H(x)=1 if 3 divides x and H(x)=0 otherwise. Directly from T(2u)=u and T(2u+1)=3u+2,

    H(x) − H(Tx) = 1_{x odd and divisible by 3}.

This is an identity at every ordinary input, independently of its basin. For any probability law μ on positive ordinary integers, with total variation defined by a supremum over events,

    TV(μ,T_*μ) ≥ μ({x odd: 3 divides x}).

Indeed the event of multiples of three realizes this discrepancy exactly. The same inequality applies to the second marginal of any law on pairs, comparing the law to its simultaneous forward image; use the event that the second coordinate is divisible by three. An ordinary inverse Markov law supported on genuine inverse edges gives the same obstruction: if X has law μ and Y is its sampled inverse then T(Y)=X, so TV(μ,law(Y)) is at least TV(T_*μ,μ). This uses contraction of total variation by the deterministic map.

In particular **every** probability law supported on the original defect pairs (q,3q) with q odd, including PR 197's reverse exits (32t+11,96t+33), has one-step total variation discrepancy exactly 1. This remains true after conditioning on an invariant ordering of the two actual basin labels, and regardless of whether the two coordinates eventually merge. An invariant law supported on genuine forward or backward trajectories cannot put positive mass on these pairs. For the empirical law along L simultaneous forward clocks starting at any such pair, the root occurs at the starting clock only: the right coordinate immediately exits divisibility by three forever. Its mass on the root event is exactly 1/L. Thus averaging the trajectory restores approximate stationarity precisely while its ordinary root weight vanishes. The same coboundary removes the multiple 3q in both alternatives of PR 205's ordinary excursion theorem. An ergodicity statement for such a stationary law can give no conclusion at this atomic root without a separate theorem transporting the conclusion to a null entrance set.

**A pointwise exclusion on the reverse class.** If q is the globally least positive odd basin tripling disagreement, the PR 205 ordinary floor gives that every bad x=1 mod 6 in [2,2n], n=3q, satisfies x≥(n−1)/2. Suppose q=43 mod 64. Then n=1 mod 64. On an odd y=1 mod 4 the two shortcut steps give A(y)=(3y+1)/4. Thus A^j(n)=1+3^j(n−1)/4^j for j=1,2,3. Each step is ordinary, and the third value is a bad integer equal to 1 modulo 3. When that value is even, divide by its full power of two to reach its bad odd part. **The odd part need not be 1 modulo 3**, so the floor cannot automatically be applied there. Nevertheless the third value itself is a positive multiple of three plus 1 below (n−1)/2. If it is odd, the floor gives a contradiction. If it is even, its half is strictly less than (n−1)/4<q, also impossible. Consequently q is not 43 modulo 64. The comparison holds throughout this class since n≥129. This uses the separate fact that every positive integer at most q is good. It rules out one half of the reverse-exit class as globally least; PR 197's class reduction controls counts of all defects and does not assert that the globally least defect lies in this class.
