# Balanced ordinary killing and its sharp bounded-correction limit

**Status.** Exact identities and a written minimax theorem on the tail of each fixed ordinary predecessor basin. The exponential bound (4) is OPEN. The minimax concerns a uniform additive one-step defect after a bounded change of real coordinate; it is not optimality over all orbit-dependent or multi-step methods.

Let $T$ be the positive ordinary shortcut map, $A_n=3^{n\bmod2}/2$, $a=1/4$ and $X_i=T^i m$. Then
$$Tn+a=A_n(n+2a),\qquad
\phi_s(n)=\left(\frac{n+a}{n+2a}\right)^s.\tag{1}$$
This holds on both branches. The accompanying Std Lean module proves $4Tn+1=3^{n\bmod2}(2n+1)$ on every natural input and the finite telescoping product with denominators cleared. It also checks the actual endpoints and odd counts of arbitrary all-odd and all-even prefixes, and that every positive start below 20 hits 1 within 31 shortcut steps.

## Forward atomic mass and the numerical improvement

Use the same inverse transfer $L_s$ as in ORDINARY_MOMENT.md, at $1<s<2$. Define $J_k(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}(n+a)^{-s}L_s^k1(n)$. For any bounded nonnegative terminal test $v$,
$$\sum_{n\ge1}v(n)(n+a)^{-s}L_s^k1(n)
=\sum_{m\ge1}(m+a)^{-s}v(X_k)\prod_{i<k}\phi_s(X_i).\tag{2}$$
Indeed the transfer word weight is $\prod_{i<k}A_{X_i}^s$. Multiplying (1) and dividing by $(X_k+a)^s$ gives the identity. Relative to the original moment $I_k$, one has $(5/4)^{-s}I_k\le J_k\le I_k$. The two have identical liminf and limsup rates of their k-th roots; no existence of a rate limit is assumed. The finite measure on the left, normalized by $J_k$, is denoted $\eta_k^a$. Its evolution before normalization pushes each atom deterministically to $Tn$ with killing multiplier $\phi_s(n)$, which is independent of input parity. Thus
$$\frac{J_{k+1}}{J_k}=\mathbb E_{\eta_k^a}\phi_s(n)
\le 1-\eta_k^a([1,M])\,[1-\phi_s(M)].\tag{3}$$
For any fixed $M$, the unnormalized mass in $[1,M]$ tends to zero. For each fixed source its index eventually avoids this set or the orbit enters a cycle, in which case its product in (2) tends to zero. Dominate by $(m+a)^{-s}$. On an escaping source with a finite reciprocal sum the same product has a positive limit: $-\log\phi_s(n)\le sa/(n+a)$. The fixed-orbit reciprocal-summability input of PR192, FIXED_SOURCE, was stated for the odd states; the reciprocal sum over the even states of one halving run is at most the reciprocal of its final odd state. Consequently, for some fixed M, the condition limsup as k tends to infinity of η_k^a([1,M]) > 0 excludes escape under that input. The mass must be bounded away from zero along a subsequence; mere positivity at finite dates does not suffice. This condition is not proved by ternary Haar contraction.

If all inputs below $B$ are known to converge, a nonconverging $m$ remains at least $B$ at all dates, hence $J_k\ge(m+a)^{-s}\phi_s(B)^k$. Therefore
$$\liminf_{k\to\infty} I_k(s)^{1/k}<\left(\frac{B+1/4}{B+1/2}\right)^s\tag{4}$$
would exclude both escape and other cycles. For $s=3/2,B=20$ the cutoff is $(81/82)^{3/2}=0.98176\ldots$, improving the uncentered per-step estimate $(60/61)^{3/2}=0.97551\ldots$. The true Haar rate $2^{-3/2}(1+\sqrt3)=0.96592\ldots$ would suffice if it controlled the ordinary trace. Such a transfer remains unproved. A uniform inequality $I_k\le C\rho^k$ with $\rho<\phi_s(B)$ would imply more: if $m$ avoids inputs below $B$ through the first $k$ input states, (2) forces $k\le [s\log(m+a)+\log C]/\log(\phi_s(B)/\rho)$. Thus this particular exponential strategy would give a uniform logarithmic first-entry time bound.

## A sharp obstruction even on the actual basin selected by a fixed target

Let $q$ be any fixed positive integer prime to three, let $P(q)=\{n:T^j n=q\text{ for some }j\ge0\}$, and take any finite floor $B$. For any bounded real function $b$ defined on that entire basin, put
$$e_b(n)=\frac{Tn+b(Tn)}{A_n}-n-b(n)$$
on its internal edges (only use edges with $n,Tn\in P(q)$). Then
$$\inf_{b\text{ bounded}}\ \sup_{\substack{n>B,\,n,Tn\in P(q)}} e_b(n)=\tfrac14.\tag{5}$$
No lower bound or sign condition on the individual defects is required. The upper bound is attained by the constant $b=1/4$ by (1).

For the lower bound suppose $e_b(n)\le d$ on the displayed edges. For every $m>B$ in the basin all positive doubles $2^j m$ are in it. On their even edges $e_b(2v)=2b(v)-b(2v)$, so
$$b(m)\le 2^{-J}b(2^Jm)+d(1-2^{-J})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b(m)\le d.$$
There are arbitrarily large starts in this *same fixed basin* with any prescribed length $k\ge1$ of consecutive odd shortcut steps. To see this choose arbitrarily large exponents $e\ge1$ satisfying $2^e q\equiv-2\pmod{3^{k+1}}$. The units are generated by 2: $v_3(4^{3^h}-1)=h+1$ by induction, using $(u^3-1)/(u-1)=u^2+u+1$ of valuation one when $u\equiv1\bmod3$, and 2 has order two modulo three. Then $y=(2^e q-1)/3$ is a positive odd predecessor reaching $q$ and $3^k\mid(y+1)$. The integer $x=2^k(y+1)/3^k-1$ can be arbitrarily large. Its first $k$ inputs are odd and $T^i x=2^{k-i}3^i(y+1)/3^k-1$ for $0\le i\le k$, ending at $y$. All these states belong to $P(q)$ and lie above the chosen floor for sufficiently large $e$.

On an odd edge $b(n)\ge 1/3-d+(2/3)b(Tn)$. Applying this to $x$ and using the even-ray bound at $x$ gives
$$d\ge b(x)\ge (1-3d)(1-(2/3)^k)-\|b\|_\infty (2/3)^k.$$
Letting $k$ increase yields $d\ge1/4$. Every positive component contains a ternary unit (an odd step has a unit output). The obstruction therefore still applies if one selects a particular hypothetical nonconvergent component first and permits its own bounded correction. The exhibited long prefixes and even rays are actual predecessors of the fixed target; they are not asserted to lie consecutively on its own forward trajectory.

## Killing the trivial cycle and restoring the real affine shift

PR192, `045be43632f274dd64faf15714e42cc8de27a2e9`, [CENTRAL_CLOSURE.md](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/045be43632f274dd64faf15714e42cc8de27a2e9/research/astra-high-variance-20261124/CENTRAL_CLOSURE.md), already gives a shifted inverse-word moment at the *odd* clock that converges to the entire Dirichlet mass of all nonconvergers. It also gives the cycle loss for its unshifted moment, strict Haar contraction and failure of the fixed-ancestor Green potential. We make no priority claim for these reductions. At the shortcut clock the absorbing set is $C=\{1,2\}$. To retain the full mass of a hypothetical cycle, the target observable should be $\sum_{m:X_k\notin C}(m+a)^{-s}$ rather than the distorted $J_k$.

For a shortcut word of odd count $j$, slope $A=3^j/2^k$, real shift $c$ in $X_k=Am+c$, least positive source $t$, and least positive endpoint $r=At+c$, the exact shifted terminal denominator for any endpoint $y$ is $y-c+Aa$. On multiplying its reciprocal $s$-power by $A^s$, one obtains exactly $(m+a)^{-s}$. The other endpoints are $r+h3^j$, $h\ge1$, and their total contribution over all words is at most $\zeta(s)2^{-(s-1)k}$. Kill the words/lifts with endpoint in $C$. The remaining complete sum decreases to the centered Dirichlet mass of all nonconverging ordinary sources, by dominated convergence. On any fixed surviving source $m$, when $2^k\ge m$ it is exactly its word's least source, so that single least-endpoint observation contributes $(m+a)^{-s}$.

The Haar tilt can still be inserted: the word probability is $p(w)=A^s3^{-j}/\lambda_s^k$, and the shifted term at the least endpoint inside the expectation is $3^j(r-c+Aa)^{-s}$. Multiplying by $\lambda_s^k p(w)$ again gives precisely $(t+a)^{-s}$. Thus the balanced unshifted correction sharpens the numerical spectral threshold, but restoring the full real shift to eliminate the cycle penalty also restores the exact reciprocal word likelihood at each fixed surviving atom. Neither averaging the Haar tilt nor changing to a bounded ordinary center proves the required disappearance of those atoms.
