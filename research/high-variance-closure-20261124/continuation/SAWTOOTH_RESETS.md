# An integer-exact phase correction and the centered infinite-sum test

**Status.** Exact ordinary identity, with an obstruction to using a renormalized family to obtain the physical debit in QUANTITATIVE_RESETS.md. Neither rules out arbitrary height-dependent irregular ranks.

Put $\alpha=\log_3 2$, $a=1/4$, $\theta_n=\log_3(n+a)$ and $\eta_n=\log_3((n+1/2)/(n+1/4))>0$. The quarter-shift identity gives $\theta_{Tn}=\theta_n-\alpha+(n\bmod2)+\eta_n$. For an integer $q\ge1$, let $h_q(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\{t-j\alpha\}$, and put $\delta_q=1-\{q\alpha\}$. Fix a positive integer phase anchor $A$. If $n\ge A2^q$ then
$$[-h_q(\theta_n-\log_3 A)]-[-h_q(\theta_{Tn}-\log_3 A)]
=\delta_q-{\bf1}_{[1-\delta_q,1)}(\{\theta_n-\log_3 A\})+q\eta_n.\tag{1}$$
In fact $\{t-q\alpha\}-\{t\}=\delta_q-{\bf1}_{[1-\delta_q,1)}(\{t\})$. In each of the $q$ fractional parts in $h_q(t-\alpha)$, the subsequent perturbation by $\eta_n$ crosses no jump. A crossing would give $A2^j3^l\in(n+1/4,n+1/2]$ for some $1\le j\le q$, with $l\ge0$ since $n\ge A2^q$. This is an integer in a gap free of integers. Thus the positive arithmetic surplus in (1) is exact. The bad arc displayed there is just below the chosen anchor; for $A=7$ it is not the selector's above-anchor band. No identification of these two reset sets is assumed. A fixed $q$ yields surplus only $O(q/n)$.

## Renormalizing the high frequencies

The natural nonnegative primitive $q-h_q$ is asymptotic to $q/2$ uniformly in its phase by equidistribution. Consequently pointwise finiteness of a positive sum of these particular primitives forces its coefficients to have finite $q$-weighted sum. This observation is specific to the uncentered primitives.

A serious alternative is to subtract their divergent common offsets. Along convergent denominators $q$ of $\alpha$, one has $|h_q(t)-q/2|\le C$ uniformly in $t,q$. To see this, choose the convergent numerator $p$: the points for $0\le j<q$ are within $1/q$ of a translated exact mesh of size $q$. Nonwrapping point displacements sum in absolute value to at most one; a bounded number of mesh points can wrap at the fractional-part jump, and the mesh sum differs from $q/2$ by at most one. Thus for $\sum_q|b_q|<\infty$, the series $\Psi(t)=\sum_q b_q(q/2-h_q(t-\log_3 A))$ converges uniformly. It defines a bounded function continuous at any phase outside the countable cut set $\log_3 A+j\alpha\bmod1$, $j\ge0$. This normalization allows $\sum_q|b_q|q=\infty$, so the fixed-frequency surplus bound by itself is insufficient to dismiss it.

## Endpoint test for the global ordinary debit

More generally, take any $V(n)=G(n)+\Psi(\theta_n)$, with $G$ nondecreasing in ordinary height and $\Psi$ one-periodic and having a finite left limit at $\log_3 d\bmod1$ for some fixed even integer $d\ge2$. No global smoothness assumption is needed. This potential cannot satisfy (4) of QUANTITATIVE_RESETS.md. For the centered series with $A=7$ one may take $d=10$ since $10\ne7\cdot 2^j3^l$ for all relevant integers; for a different fixed integer anchor choose an even $d$ with a prime factor absent from $6A$.

Choose increasing $k$ with $\{3k\alpha\}\to1$ by irrationality. The ordinary odd states $x_i=d8^{k-i}9^i-5$, $0\le i\le k$, give exactly $k$ expanding $(2,1)$ valleys. Their endpoint phases both tend to $\log_3 d$ from the left on the circle: the initial additional phase is $3k\alpha$ plus the strictly negative vanishing correction from $d8^k-5+1/4$, and the final additional phase is $2k$ plus the strictly negative vanishing correction from $d9^k-5+1/4$. Hence $V(x_0)-V(x_k)\le o(1)$. On the other hand all $3k$ physical source states have logarithmic heights between $c_1 k$ and $c_2 k$ for fixed positive constants, so their total $w(n)=1/\log(n+2)$ cost is bounded above and below by positive constants. Partition this finite physical block into successive actual blocks of length $\lfloor\sqrt{\log(n+2)}\rfloor$ from their own current sources. Equation (3) of QUANTITATIVE_RESETS.md bounds their combined weighted reset toll by $O(k^{\delta-p})$ times the bounded total cost; the terminal incomplete block contributes at most $O(k^{\delta-1/2})$. Both vanish for $0<\delta<p=1/(2\kappa)\le1/2$. Telescoping the proposed debit on these complete actual valleys imposes a positive lower bound on $V(x_0)-V(x_k)$, a contradiction.

These finite sources need not share a prescribed basin. They refute a proposed inequality claimed on every ordinary input. RESET_COSTS.md gives a separate oscillation requirement that retains a single prescribed ordinary target basin. The argument does not exclude an infinite sum that has no finite left limit at any admissible $d$, or a potential with additional genuine height dependence beyond $G$.
