# Simultaneous ordinary reset blocks and the cost of compressing the rank

**Status.** Written local obstruction and fixed-orbit summability theorem. No proof of universal descent. The sparse selectors and their ranks are those of the separate SPARSE_RESET_MEASURE packet. The conclusions apply to any finite bank of ordinary selectors whose output occurs at a shortcut time bounded by h_i(y)=o(log y), including arbitrary phase translations. Attribute a hit to the valley source whose half-open shortcut interval contains it. Let F_i(y)=τ_i(y)+u_i(y) be the full shortcut clock to the next marked valley plus its numerical source value, with τ_i=0 on a mark. In particular τ_i≤h_i(y).

## Simultaneous resets on actual positive inputs

For every fixed K, there are arbitrarily large actual odd sources with K successive (a,1) valleys, where a can be taken arbitrarily large, such that every one of those K sources is marked by every selector. Furthermore, throughout these valleys the numerical sources grow by a factor greater than 3^a/2^(a+1) at every edge. No independence of section choices is involved.

Prescribe K repetitions of the shortcut parity word consisting of a ones and one zero, followed by one further one. A word of L=K(a+1)+1 binary shortcuts selects exactly one residue class modulo 2^L: revealing each additional source bit toggles the next parity because the slope in the unexposed parameter is odd. Choose y in [2^L,2^(L+1)) in that class. The final prescribed odd input makes the last halving run exactly one step as well. Each valley has formula z=(3^a y+3^a−2^a)/2^(a+1). For a≥3 this is expanding. Each intermediate source y_j lies between 2^L and (3/2)^L(y+1), hence log y_j=O_K(a). For a large enough depending on K and the finite bank, all h_i(y_j)<a. The selected ordinary hits from those same sources fall strictly within their current valley. Each hence belongs to every marked set, regardless of which shifted physical section its selector chose. Allowing any fixed bounded displacement of marking by valley index does not help in the interior: prescribe that many extra valleys on both ends and retain the middle K.

In particular for adjacent sources within the block every F_i grows from y_j to y_(j+1) exactly. No adaptive choice among those F_i, nor any pointwise convex combination of them, can make a favorable choice there; all values coincide. For K arbitrarily large the cumulative raw increase is y_K−y_0 when the extra final source is also arranged to be marked. These are changing finite ordinary witnesses. The theorem does not put infinitely many such blocks on one fixed escaping orbit.

Even a single simple family suffices for an unbounded one-edge increase: for odd a≥3, y=2^a−1 has the (a,1) valley to z=(3^a−1)/2. Here F_i(y)=y while F_i(z)≥z/2^{h_i(z)}=z^{1−o(1)}. Thus min_i F_i(z)/y tends to infinity. This estimate needs no assumption that z itself is marked.

## Why summable local corrections do not yet finance a uniform debit

Suppose a bank-based rank R is nonnegative and uniformly bounded by A whenever all its sections mark the source. A global estimate R(y)≥R(z)+δ−C/y with constants δ>0,C≥0 is impossible. Fix K>(A+1)/δ, use a simultaneous block, and choose its source so large that KC/y_0<1. Summing the estimate contradicts the bound at its first source. This also excludes any universal error tending to zero at large sources by the same fixed-K argument. A source-dependent total allowance without a uniform bound over these changing sources is not ruled out.

One bounded change of variable does have *actual* summable positive reset increments on each fixed hypothetical escape. For G=1−1/F, an increase leaving a marked s is at most 1/s. The strict-power orbit-counting input, PR182 at a7fd3ac9e5bff7412858df23d776bb847e9c1371, GLOBAL_ORBIT_COUNT.md, applies to the forward-invariant set of distinct odd inputs on a single escaping orbit. Therefore Σ 1/s is finite there. But even the full debit of log F on its unmarked sources is summable. Indeed an unmarked source y has valley clock l≤h(y), F(z)=F(y)−l, and F(y)≥u(y)≥y/2^{h(y)}=y^{1−o(1)}. Consequently log F(y)−log F(z)≤l/(F(y)−l)=y^{−1+o(1)}. The orbit-counting theorem has exponent θ=H₂(log₃2)<1, so summing these bounds over dyadic shells of that same odd orbit gives a finite sum. The debit for G is pointwise no larger since (1−1/t)'≤(log t)' for t≥1. Finiteness of both signs supplies no contradiction.

Putting the original clock outside the compression gives a different exact accounting. Let G(y)=τ(y)+ψ(u(y)) with bounded ψ. On an unmarked valley of length l the debit is exactly l. At a marked s with next valley z and next marked return w, let L be the entire shortcut clock from s to w. Then G(z)−G(s)=L−l+ψ(w)−ψ(s). The *defect relative to the requested debit l* is L+ψ(w)−ψ(s). Over successive complete marked excursions these defects sum to the total elapsed clock plus a bounded endpoint difference. The same identity holds for a fixed weighted sum of finitely many sections, up to its initial and final partial excursions; it does not assert that those partial clocks are uniformly bounded. A new nonlocal source-dependent argument would have to control the actual numerical marked return, or supply a different source resource. Neither sparse density nor bounded terminal charges do that.
