# Ordinary mergers across periodic dyadic boundary rays

**PROPOSED written theorem.** A positive ordinary component boundary cannot follow a periodic binary address if one signed rational seed on each side enters a cycle containing an odd step. The two cycles can be disjoint and have different odd frequencies. By comparing *different dyadic levels*, the proof constructs a common future for two actual positive integers of opposite proposed colors, with their original root held fixed. In particular the nonterminal address with repeating digits `1011` is impossible, although for every fixed coefficient below `19/15`, its adjacent endpoints at all sufficiently late levels miss every meeting through that coefficient times the level. A constructive recurrence also bounds every new run of such a periodic block, uniformly over ordinary roots whose preceding block differs. The triadic-denominator application recovers an exclusion already implied by the earlier no-even-gap rule. The qualitative theorem uses no probabilistic synchronization or predecessor-density hypothesis.

A persistent oriented boundary for a two-color function $f$ with $f(Tn)=f(n)$ consists of positive integers $n_{i+1}=2n_i+d_{i+1}$ whose pairs $n_i,n_i+1$ always have colors $L,R$ with $L\ne R$. For a nondyadic address $\alpha$, $n_i=\lfloor2^i\alpha\rfloor$. A finite starting prefix can be discarded by rescaling the address by the corresponding power of two. The convention at a dyadic endpoint can use either terminating expansion and is immaterial to the stated triadic exclusion.

## 1. Matching in every binary residue class

For any integers $s\ge0,D$, take the ordinary pairs $u,3^su+D$, for sufficiently large positive $u$. In EVERY prescribed binary residue class of $u$, a sub-residue class has a fixed common-clock merger for all sufficiently large positive lifts. In fact the same certificate works for every lift whose two starting integers are positive. Here and below clocks stop only after actual equality.

For completeness, orient the lower-grade arm as $v$ and the other as $3^sv+D$. At a binary split, the parity of $v$ can be chosen: it is affine in the unexposed quotient with odd slope. After either choice the new anchored arm still has odd free slope. This follows by writing the quotient as $2z+b$ and applying the two branches; swapping anchors preserves the property. Any prescribed original residue first fixes a finite initial sequence and a current integer state $(s,D)$.

At positive grade, if $D\ne0$ is even choose the even anchor repeatedly to halve $D$ until it is odd. If $D=0$, choose the odd anchor to make it $(1-3^s)/2\ne0$, then do the same. At odd $D$, choosing the odd anchor makes the updated state $(s-1,(D-3^{s-1})/2)$. This reaches grade zero in finitely many steps. At grade zero an even nonzero offset can again be halved with an even anchor. For odd $|D|>1$, choosing the words $01$ for $D=3\bmod4$, or $11$ for $D=1\bmod4$, returns to grade zero with offset $(3D-1)/4$ or $-(3D+1)/4$, respectively. Both strictly decrease the magnitude. The offsets $1,-1$ absorb on $001,101$, respectively. Thus a finite extension reaching $(0,0)$ always exists. These are anchor parity words; they need not equal the ordinary binary digits in the same order. This proof uses the finite transition table of [PR146, f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437, THREE_RAY_UPPER.md](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437/research/upper-bit-mergers/THREE_RAY_UPPER.md), and only its elementary reachability argument.

## 2. Two cycles generate an actual merger for one fixed ordinary root

Let a rational address be positive, nondyadic, and of odd denominator after discarding its finite initial dyadic part. At phase $i$ put $x_i=-\{2^i\alpha\}$; then $n_i=2^i\alpha+x_i$, $n_i+1=2^i\alpha+x_i+1$. The signed rationals have odd denominators; their parity is their residue modulo two. For $0\le u\le i$, exact rational arithmetic gives
$$
 T^u(n_i+e)=2^{i-u}3^{p_{i,e}(u)}\alpha+T^u(x_i+e),\qquad e=0,1,
$$
where $p_{i,e}(u)$ counts odd steps of the signed seed. Indeed the current difference is even up to the next split, so the two quantities follow the same parity there. No negative rational is used as an ordinary orbit. The endpoints on the left are the original positive ordinary endpoints throughout.

Suppose some left phase and some right phase have eventually periodic rational seed orbits, with at least one odd input per period on each chosen orbit. As indices revisit a given phase, the seed is literally the same rational. On a progression of indices above its preperiod with stride a multiple of the binary phase period and its physical orbit period, its terminal residual $T^i(x_i+e)$ is constant and its odd count increases by a fixed positive integer each stride. Multiplying the two strides by suitable positive integers gives two sequences of levels $i(h),j(h)$ such that their ordinary endpoints at the respective native clocks are
$$
  U_h=3^{Ch+a}\alpha+c, \qquad V_h=3^{Ch+b}\alpha+d,\quad h\ge0.
$$
Here $C>0$, and we can double both strides to take $C$ even. After interchanging the two sequences if necessary, $b\ge a$. Thus $V_h=3^{b-a}U_h+D$ for a *fixed integer* $D=d-3^{b-a}c$; evaluate at $h=0$ to see its integrality.

Write $q=3^C$, $s_0=v_2(q-1)\ge2$. Modulo any power of two the powers $q^h$ attain every residue $1\bmod 2^{s_0}$ at that modulus. Indeed $v_2(q^{2^t}-1)=s_0+t$ follows by successive squaring since each new sum factor has valuation one. The order modulo $2^K$ for $K>s_0$ is exactly $2^{K-s_0}$, equal to the size of the indicated subgroup. Consequently $U_h$ hits every finer residue in the ball $U_0+2^{s_0+v_2(\alpha)}\mathbb Z_2$. The nonzero rational multiplier $3^a\alpha$ has odd-denominator unit part; multiplying the complete congruence by it is bijective after the indicated power of two is removed. This congruence holds at arbitrarily large $h$.

By section 1 this particular ball contains a fixed refining residue on which the two actual arms merge. Some single ordinary value $h$ hits it. The original left endpoint at level $i(h)$ and right endpoint at level $j(h)$ therefore have a common ordinary future. They cannot have opposite invariant colors throughout. This is an exclusion for each fixed positive ordinary root, even though the meeting levels and subsequent finite physical clock may depend on it.

## 3. Every higher triadic denominator

Suppose the reduced denominator of the address after the finite shift is $3^d$ with $d\ge2$. The phase numerators run through *all* ternary units modulo $3^d$. For clarity $2$ has order $2\cdot3^{d-1}$: $v_3(4^{3^t}-1)=t+1$ follows by cubing $1+3^k u$, $3\nmid u$, and reduction modulo three forces an even power. Thus multiplication by the unit numerator permutes the phase set.

For the left side take $x_d=-1+2^{d+1}/3^d\in(-1,0)$. At depth two, $-1/9\mapsto1/3\mapsto1$. For $d\ge2$, $x_{d+1}=(2x_d-1)/3$ is an odd preimage of $x_d$. Its reduced denominator is exactly $3^{d+1}$. For the right side start $y_1=1/3$. From $0<y_d<1$ of exact denominator $3^d$ choose $e\ge1$ with $1<2^e y_d\le2$. Then $y_{d+1}=(2^e y_d-1)/3$ is positive, at most $1/3$, odd, and has exact denominator $3^{d+1}$; one odd shortcut followed by $e-1$ halvings returns to $y_d$. Both prescribed signed seeds are present, potentially at different phases, and enter the $1,2$ cycle of positive odd count. Section 2 rules out the address.

The preceding triadic-denominator exclusion also follows immediately from the earlier no-even-gap rule in BOUNDARY_RAYS.md. The same unit-residue cycle reaches fractional part $1/3^d<1/4$, so its periodic digit sequence contains a run of at least two zeros preceded by a one. The new application outside that old binary restriction is the admissible-looking periodic word in section 5.

At reduced denominator three the two negative seeds $-1/3,-2/3$ hit zero. At dyadic centers one side can itself have zero seed. Those degeneracies yield no growing grade on that side. An arbitrary rational denominator prime to six can preserve that denominator throughout a seed orbit, so positivity of the other seed does not establish periodicity. An aperiodic address need not revisit an exact rational phase. Neither the terminal nor the aperiodic case has been eliminated.

## 4. Long periodic blocks require the correct preceding low digits

Fix a purely periodic word $w$ of length $l$ and word integer $W$, whose phases satisfy section 2. Put $t=W/(2^l-1)\in(0,1)$. Uniformly over all positive ordinary starting indices $n$ with $v_2(n+t)\le E$, a fixed number of periods of $w$ forces a cross-level merger. In section 2 put $\alpha=n+t$. The possible balls for $U_h$ have depths $s_0+v_2(n+t)\le s_0+E$, hence form a finite menu; $s,D$ are fixed. Select one merging subball of some depth $M$ for each possible ball and increase $M$ to cover the menu. The exponent congruence attains each required subball within a complete finite period modulo $2^M$, independent of $n$. The maximum of the resulting two dyadic levels yields a uniform finite word bound. Those finite prefixes equal the actual integer endpoints even if the subsequent digits of the proposed address differ.

The 2-adic expansion of $-t=W/(1-2^l)$ repeats $W$ from its least significant block. If the preceding length-$l$ block is $v\ne w$, the starting integer $n$ ends in $v$, hence $v_2(n+t)<l$. Consequently every word $v w^{N(w)}$ with $v\ne w$ is uniformly forbidden for a computable finite $N(w)$. No compactness of an ordinary-orbit family is assumed. For the initial run of a fixed starting integer $n$, $n+t\ne0$ because it is positive, and the possibly larger finite valuation provides a bound as well. An aperiodic word can still avoid long powers.

## 5. An exact long-delay example

Let $w=1011$, $t=11/15$, $\alpha=n+t$ for one fixed positive integer $n$, and $n_i=\lfloor 2^i\alpha\rfloor$. In the four phases the left seed numerators over 15 are $-11,-7,-14,-13$, and the right numerators are $4,8,1,2$. The signed numerator map is $F(a)=a/2$ on evens and $(3a+15)/2$ on odds. Each left seed enters $(3,12,6)$ with one odd input every three steps. Each right seed enters $(57,93,147,228,114)$ with three odd inputs every five steps. The two complete forward seed sets are disjoint and all residuals have absolute value at most $228/15$.

At phase zero the left seed has grade five and terminal numerator 12 at clock 8, and the right has grade five and numerator 57 there. Thus with $i=8+36h$ on the left and $j=8+20h$ on the right the terminal signed numerators are again 12 and 57 and both grades are $5+12h$. The actual two values are $u_h,u_h+3$, where
$$
  5u_h-4=3^{12h+4}(15n+11).
$$
This exhibits the asynchronous choice of *dyadic levels* which makes section 2 work.

The obstruction at early physical clocks is uniform in the number of dyadic levels. If $n\ge17$, the left ordinary trajectory at any level $i$ through clock $i$ is disjoint from the right ordinary trajectory at *every* level $j$ through clock $j$. Section 2's two leading coefficients of $\alpha$ are integers. Distinct integer coefficients differ in their contributions by at least $\alpha>17$, whereas any two signed residuals differ by at most $(228+14)/15<17$. Equal coefficients leave two distinct residuals. This does not assert that the true orbits fail to meet at later clocks.

In fact for each fixed positive ordinary $n$, every $\delta<4/15$ with $\delta\ge0$, and all sufficiently large $i$, the endpoints $n_i,n_i+1$ have no common future with *either independent physical clock* at most $(1+\delta)i$. Through clock $i$, use the signed residuals above. Beyond $i$ follow the actual parities, giving at any clock $u$ the leading term $\alpha 2^{i-u}3^{P_e(u)}$ with the *actual* odd count and a remainder $O((3/2)^{\max(0,u-i)})$. This follows by the two affine updates after clock $i$ and the bounded signed residual before then. Up to $i$, the two grades are $u/3+O(1)$ and $3u/5+O(1)$, with absolute constants uniform over the four phases. All leading terms through $(1+\delta)i$ are therefore $\gg_n 3^{i/3}2^{-\delta i}$. If the two leading monomials differ, the fixed positive bases 2 and 3 give
$$
 \big|(P_0(u)-P_1(v))\log 3-(u-v)\log 2\big|\gg (1+i)^{-C}
$$
for an absolute $C$ (Gouillon, Corollary 2.3, used at the preceding [CLOCK_WINDOW checkpoint](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/a30402b7849c437edf245974d9f436d1f105e08e/research/high-variance-closure-20261124/CLOCK_WINDOW.md)). Equality of the actual endpoints would instead give an exponentially small value, since the error-to-leading ratio is $O_n(3^{(\delta-1/3)i})$. If the leading monomials agree, unique factorization gives $u=v$ and equal grades. For $u\le i$ the two rational residuals differ; for $u\ge i$ the grade gap at $i$ is $4i/15+O(1)$ and can fall by at most one per physical step. This proves the stated bound. Only this strengthened quantitative delay uses a two-logarithm estimate.

The finite replay checks an ordinary merger for root 17: the level-224 left start and the level-128 right start reach $u,u+3$ at their respective native clocks, and meet exactly ten additional steps later.
