# Prior results and scope of the local estimates

This comparison uses the rStation Collatz listing and linked versions available on 18 November 2026. The listing contained 166 papers. The following statements compare the named theorems and quantities. They do not establish absolute priority, and the cited external proofs and computational certificates were not independently rebuilt.

## Packing and survivor words

Paper 28, v3, Theorem 1 already states the effective $m^h/(1+\log m)^{3/2}$ estimate for the *labelled* image of every translated interval at a common squared-logarithmic clock. Theorem A improves the power to $m^{h-\gamma}$ ineffectively; §8.1 gives translated orbit counts and distinct-state zeta convergence below $h$. Injectivity on a forward-invariant set or discarding at most the clock length at the boundary of a finite simple segment gives the same estimates in those formulations. Consequently the critical Mellin summability, its logarithmic tail, and the height-cap bound in this directory are already implied by Paper 28, including our stated finite-segment and finite-cycle scales. Our elementary curved-barrier and shorter-clock recursion provide alternative proofs. The labelled logarithmic-clock consequence below uses Paper 28's arithmetic estimate. Paper 87 gives the forward survivor count beyond the native source bit horizon; Paper 89 proves an excursion law, peak and area estimates, and small-ball profile complexity for actual shell survivors. A native ballot word count is not a new beyond-horizon or orbitwise experiment. [Paper 28, v3, Theorems A and 1, §§6 and 8](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/28/v3) · [Paper 87](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/87) · [Paper 89](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/89)

### A linear-clock consequence of the published two-window estimate

Retain Paper 28's notation $k=\lceil\log_2m\rceil$, $p=\lfloor k/8\rfloor$, $\alpha=\log_3 2$, $h=H_2(\alpha)$ and $z=\alpha/(1-\alpha)>1$. Its equations (S33)–(S37) bound the number of high/high original sources on each aligned width-$2^k$ interval by
$$
 C k^{1/\beta'} z^H 2^{hk-\sigma p}.
$$
Here $\sigma>0$ and $\beta'>1$ are fixed by its arithmetic input; the $H$ dependence displayed explicitly is the sum in (S35). The computation requires every retained first weight $r>\lfloor\alpha k\rfloor-H$ to satisfy $r/16\le p\le r/4$ and exceed the fixed arithmetic onset. These conditions still hold with $H=\lfloor\varepsilon k\rfloor$ for sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$, since $r\le k$ and $\alpha>1/2$. Take also $\varepsilon\log_2 z<\sigma/32$. The bad original sources are then $O(2^{(h-\sigma/16)k})$ for large $k$ (decrease the gain if needed).

The two low cases in (S38)–(S41) put each fixed prefix label into an interval with binary scale
$$
 \ell\le \max(k-H\log_2 3,\ p\log_2(3/2),1)+O(1)\le (1-c)k
$$
for a fixed $c>0$. There are $O(k)$ labels and covers in total, per aligned input interval. The two prefix lengths are $k$ and $k+p$. Pick $D$ so large that $Dck>k+p+2$ for large $k$. An inductive common clock $\lceil D\ell\rceil$ at each new scale now fits into $\lceil Dk\rceil$ after either prefix. Padding preserves the endpoint-and-count labels via deterministic iteration. Strong induction at any fixed $h-\sigma/16<\eta<h$, with $\eta>0$, costs at most $O(k)K2^{\eta(1-c)k}$ for all good covers plus the exponentially smaller bad-source count. The factor of two for an unaligned interval and all bounded scales are absorbed in the induction constant. Thus, conditionally on Paper 28's proved arithmetic input, the number of distinct endpoint-and-odd-count pairs is $O(m^\eta)$ already at a common $O(\log m)$ shortcut clock. This is a clock refinement of its stated Theorem A, not a new proof of its strict entropy gain. It also supersedes the unlabelled $O(\log m\log\log m)$ bound here when that input is allowed.

## Inverse minimum comparisons

Papers 1 and 139 prove minimum and division-rigidity statements for particular reference languages at matched odd depth, including finite arithmetic certificates and counterexamples. Paper 148 permits unrestricted valuation letters and competitors through every depth up to the reference depth; it explicitly distinguishes deeper alternatives. Paper 65 gives a genuine unrestricted-depth obstruction to a smaller source on a particular negative-cycle shadow, recording the extra even-step cost. Paper 108 minimizes over depths up to $R$ at special targets approaching a negative rational cycle, with an effective bounded terminal dictionary. Paper 150 concerns the minimum total valuation at a prescribed growing depth over ternary-unit paths; its typical critical slope and policy memory parameter differ from the multiplier cutoff at an unrestricted depth. Paper 102, Proposition 6, controls the size of a parent's least ancestor, with the parent sampled under a natural or canonical inverse law at a fixed endpoint; Paper 105 studies the related physical last-parent tail. These are material precedents for minimum ancestry, with different quantifiers. [Paper 1, v3](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/1/v3) · [Paper 65](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/65) · [Paper 102](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/102) · [Paper 105](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/105) · [Paper 108](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/108) · [Paper 139, v2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/139/v2) · [Paper 148](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/148) · [Paper 150, v2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/150/v2)

The result addressed here samples an ordinary odd *target* by natural density, allows arbitrary predecessor depths and valuations, and asks whether its least positive ancestor is below $\theta$ times that target. The local FS-010 reduction identifies this with existence of **any legal word at one Haar target** with $J=3^q/2^A\ge1/\theta$. The [renewal equivalent](MINIMUM_CONSTANT.md) gives $d(\theta)\sim c_*\theta^2$. None of the minimum statements above supplies that distributional equivalent. This comparison does not turn a population theorem into a fixed-source convergence result.

## Pressure and stationary-density tails

Paper 146 already proves the **exact** sharp quadratic tail of the Haar density of the stationary geometric Syracuse offset, with a branch-interaction expression for its positive constant and linear growth of finite-level collision energy. Its proof uses the critical tilt, bounded real offsets, fixed-total numerator spacing, summable later-prefix overlap and nonarithmetic implicit renewal with dependent branches. Paper 9 contains prior fixed-total estimates and real-scale moment bounds. Our stationary-density lower bound is an alternative, weaker corollary; neither its conclusion nor the general proof toolkit should be called new. The real random maximum controlled in Paper 146 is sampled along one random geometric word. Our $M$ is the supremum over *every legal word at one given ternary target* and is then sampled in target Haar measure. [Paper 146](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/146) · [Paper 9, v2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/9/v2)

Our pressure is the Green sum $\mathcal H_s(\alpha)=\sum_{w\text{ legal at }\alpha}J_w^s$ over all finite inverse words. It differs from the stationary geometric density, and from the maximal exponent on a sampled path. Valbuena already constructs positive-integer inverse Green sums and identifies their normalized limit as $s\downarrow1$. On odd targets, adding arbitrary terminal inverse doublings converts the odd-word Green sum into the shortcut one by the factor $(1-2^{-s})^{-1}$. Paper 43 concerns a dyadic fixed forward-clock Dirichlet series with a weight depending on parity, not this all-depth ternary Green sum. The local contribution is the all-$s>1$ tail equivalent for this Green sum at Haar targets, in particular the [explicit critical constant](PRESSURE_ENDPOINT.md) $[2\log(3/2^{4/3})]^{-1}$. FS-014 supplies the associated ordinary *limiting distribution*, with the ordinary height limit taken first. The cited stationary-density tail constant in Paper 146 is different.
