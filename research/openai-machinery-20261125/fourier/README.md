# Fourier rigidity and ordinary inverse-fiber multiplicity

**Status.** Proved finite, deterministic obstructions to a proposed transfer. The rank estimate below is a conditional finite-interface result. The all-time Collatz assertion, the high-relative-carry tail, and the summability of the retained paired disagreement counts are open.

## A fixed-total ordinary collision theorem

Write U(x)=(3x+1)/2^v₂(3x+1) on positive odd integers. Let w=(a₁,…,aₖ) be the positive gap sequence, Sᵢ its prefix sums with S₀=0, and

    C(w) = Σ_{i=0}^{k−1} 3^(k−1−i) 2^Sᵢ .
    2^Sₖ y = 3^k x + C(w),        y = U^k(x).

Induction using C(wa)=3C(w)+2^Sₖ proves the affine identity. On a single cylinder the lift x→x+2^(Sₖ+1)t preserves all gaps and sends y→y+2·3^k t. The free cylinder coordinate t maps bijectively modulo every M coprime to 3 to the odd output coordinate (y(t)−1)/2, whereas y(t) itself is constant modulo 3^k. Neither block is a full exotic Fourier transformation in the natural basis.

**Theorem.** For each fixed positive r, all sufficiently large k have an ordinary positive odd endpoint y admitting Ωᵣ(k^r) distinct ordinary starts of exactly k odd steps with total S=k+⌊k/2⌋. The endpoints may be chosen in the basin of 1, and all starts may be required to lie in one prescribed odd-compatible residue modulo a fixed power 3^d. When a source class modulo 3^d is prescribed, the implicit lower-bound constant may also depend on d. The same statement holds at the central total S=2k. Consequently no fixed-degree polynomial bounds the maximum unweighted inverse row over all ordinary endpoints at either total.

**Construction.** Set B=S−k. For k≥2r distribute B as evenly as possible among nonnegative budgets B₁,…,Bᵣ. At positions (2j−1,2j) use gaps (aⱼ,2+Bⱼ−aⱼ) with 1≤aⱼ≤1+Bⱼ and aⱼ≡1 mod mⱼ, where mⱼ=2·3^(2j+d−1). Finish the word with k−2r ones. Only S_(2j−1) varies with the split of pair j. Euler's theorem gives 2^mⱼ≡1 mod 3^(2j+d); multiplying by its coefficient 3^(k−2j) makes the corresponding variation of C zero modulo 3^(k+d). All words thus have the same C modulo 3^(k+d), and the same S. Their number is exactly

    M = ∏_{j=1}^r (1+⌊Bⱼ/mⱼ⌋).

For fixed r,d this is Ωᵣ,𝑑(k^r). Even with d fixed, set r=⌊(log₉ k)/2⌋ for large k. Then all mⱼ=O𝑑(√k), all Bⱼ≥c k/r, and M≥(c𝑑√k/r)^r=exp(Ω((log k)²)). The multiplicity is also at most (k+1)^r, hence subexponential for this choice of r.

**Ordinary realization and uniqueness.** Choose an integer residue u for the source modulo 3^d and solve 2^S y≡C+3^k u mod 3^(k+d). Infinitely many arbitrarily large positive odd y realize this class. If desired, choose y=(4^n−1)/3, which is odd and has U(y)=1: by the binomial expansion v₃(4^(3^i)−1)=i+1 inductively, so the powers of 4 run through the entire subgroup 1 mod 3 at modulus 3^(k+d+1). Choose y with 2^S y>max C. For each word the number x=(2^S y−C)/3^k is a positive odd integer in the prescribed class. To check every step, reduce 3^k x+C=2^S y modulo 2^(a₁+1) when k≥2. After dividing the sum into its first affine step one obtains the same terminal equation for x₁=(3x+1)/2^a₁ with the tail word: divisibility follows modulo 2^a₁ since 3^(k−1) is odd. The tail equation forces x₁ odd because its first offset term is odd and its right side is even. At k=1, x₁=y is odd directly. Iterate; positivity follows at every step and each claimed valuation is exact. Distinct words cannot have the same start since U is deterministic. The special endpoint choice puts all these trajectories in the ordinary basin of 1.

Already at k=12, gaps (1,7,1,…,1) and (7,1,1,…,1) have the same total 18 and the same terminal residue modulo 3^12. In the first pair the offset difference is 2^7−2=126, a multiple of 9. The long examples are collisions of distinct sources at one endpoint, never repeated independent trials along one source.

## Weighted and retained-source tests

Each exact length-k word has mass 2^(−S) in the normalized odd 2-adic cylinder model: each odd state is restricted to one residue modulo 2^(S+1). Disjointness adds the masses. The family constructed above therefore contributes M2^(−S), exponentially small at both totals. Ordinary counts and the normalized cylinder model have different input domains. The family does not obstruct an exponentially loose degree budget or a weighted average.

For PR 201's two-end escape test at supplied q, β=C/2^S satisfies β(wa)=(3β(w)+1)/2^a and β≤(3/2)^k−1. Choose the ordinary representative y≥3^k; then β/y<2^(−k) uniformly over this entire family, including at S=2k and any prescribed source unit class. For each fixed ε>0 the construction in these large representatives is excluded by β≥εy for large k. Endpoints y with U(y)=1 are also excluded by sufficiently long nonconvergence forward tests. This does not rule out smaller representatives or prove a uniform bound for all supplied q. A modular cycle-phase degeneracy alone cannot disprove this high-carry test.

PR 201's head problem fixes x=81y+r with y in a common octave, then retains original y through the same shortcut clock tⱼ for both arms, until canonical absorption. It asks about *different* actual convergence colors subject to the level and relative-offset restrictions at that very clock. Our family varies starting sources across one terminal, uses odd-step clocks, imposes no companion head constraint, and in fact all sources in the family have the same convergence status. It supplies zero evidence for opposing counts inside that filtered set. The first unsupported transfer would identify a coordinate of the Fourier list with the retained ordered pair while preserving source identity, the native shortcut clock, and the actual row weight. ## Actual use of the rank lemma and its boundary

In a finite layered graph, take sources as columns, states (including their times) as rows, C as a strictly lower triangular propagation matrix and T as the input injection. States solve V=CV+T. A diagonal deletion matrix D with entries 0 or 1 gives the same-source trimmed system W=(I−D)(CW+T); both systems have a unique solution. The adapter accepts general scalar diagonal entries and a nonzero determinant, so acyclicity is sufficient rather than necessary. For any common readout R the upstream resolvent identity gives rank(RV−RW)≤rank(D)=#nonzero mask coordinates. This works over any field. It uses no independence, positivity, or norm comparison. Ordinary gaps from 3 and 13 reaching 5 provide the two-column one-row incidence example. An all-ones readout error on p×q coordinates has rank at most one and pq nonzero entries. Thus a count bound cannot follow from this resolvent inequality alone. Retaining source labels in a diagonal disagreement matrix would turn rank into a count; the unresolved step is representing that diagonal matrix by a transfer with a provably summable state-deletion budget. Observable tests for such a representation are equality of its off-diagonal zeros and agreement of every diagonal entry with its original source at the selected clock. Conditioning or weighting must be demonstrated on the same labeled matrix.

[collisions.py](collisions.py) replays five exact finite examples with arbitrary-precision integer arithmetic, checking every forward gap from every distinct reconstructed source, the common endpoint, the clock, and the relative-carry bound. Run `python -B research/openai-machinery-20261125/fourier/collisions.py` from the repository root. These replays do not test an infinite tail.

## Two related transfer tests

**Integer multiplication on tapes.** The companion's Theorem 1.1 builds one deterministic machine for the full product at every input length n in n(1+log n)^(1/2+o(1)) time and O(n) visited tape cells. Its tape schedules pay for moving data and certify arithmetic precision. The matching worst-case Ω(n√log n) lower bound in Theorem 1.2 (with a low-n-output-bit strengthening in the companion formalization) assumes variable-count Weak ETH: for each fixed δ>0 below 1, 3-SAT has no 2^O(v^(1−δ)) algorithm times a polynomial in the input size. The lower proof freezes the coarse blocks visited by heads during epochs; there are at most exponentially many traces in the number of epochs, so one trace still carries enough independent successful inputs for a partial matrix-transposition obstruction. Weak ETH is used to choose a geometric placement separating many terminal pairs. This is an input-batch lower argument. Collatz multiplication by the fixed constant 3 is computed by shifting and adding, and is already linear in the binary input length. A hard instance of general integer multiplication need never arise on a particular Collatz orbit. An analogous grouping by gap trace would need a lower bound on the number of mutually independent data coordinates surviving at a supplied ordinary start; the deterministic start and clock instead determine the entire trace. [Tape manuscript, §§1.3–1.4 and Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/paper.pdf)

**Sunflowers.** For r≥3, a finite family of distinct sets of size at most k with more than Bᵣ^k members has r sets with common pairwise intersection. In a coordinate-labeled word encoding, this means that at every coordinate the r symbols are either all equal or all distinct. The proof tensors a test that suppresses partial repetitions: shortening labels may turn a partially repeated column into a false constant, so structured correction terms must remove those cases; Euclidean tensor placement bounds the total correction cost uniformly in alphabet size. Two deterministic controls show the limitation here. First, all 2^k binary shortcut traces encoded by their k pairs (time,bit) are sunflower-free for r≥3: an all-distinct column is impossible, so a sunflower would repeat the same complete word. One cannot force a common-core family merely from all binary traces being present. Second, fixing all but the first split in the collision construction and varying a₁ over r allowed values gives an actual r-sunflower of gap words: both split entries are all distinct and the remaining entries all agree. Every path can nevertheless meet the same ordinary endpoint in the basin of 1. This pattern detects neither convergence-color disagreement nor the rarity of high-relative-carry words.
