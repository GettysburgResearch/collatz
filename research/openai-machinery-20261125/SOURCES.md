## Headline qualifications that matter

The matrix manuscript's printed rectangular corollary gives `1/2≤α≤2/3`; the accompanying Lean `ExactExponent.lean` and scope document state the stronger exact `α=1/2` and full rectangular formula. The two should not be quoted interchangeably. The division-free model requires formal polynomial identities even over finite fields. The exponent allows positive epsilon slack. Its value does not by itself assert an endpoint `O(n^(9/4))` algorithm. The manuscript's nonscalar gate bound at each finite side length is a separate, unformalized clause in the scope ledger.

The Fourier model is a binary **linear circuit** model: each gate forms a weighted sum of two wires, with arbitrary field coefficients. The field must have the appropriate roots of unity and invert the group order. Its lower bound is not a generic mixing estimate, a statement about Fourier coefficients of the Collatz transition, or a time lower bound for a single trajectory. The formal low-rank/sparse-error statement concerns the jointly labeled batch. The polynomial-multiplication lower bound remains outside the formal scope, even for one product.

Weak ETH is an explicit premise of the tape lower bound. No other route in this packet assumes it. The LP formal scope is rational input with an actual RAM model; the broader real-data and applications discussions in the manuscript are not silently added to the compiled local theorem.

## How to evaluate a suggested transfer

An agent proposing a new application should write the actual ordinary input, source and endpoint projection, clocks, allowed deletions, function space and source measure before naming an operator or tensor. For a batch argument, explain which inputs remain independently variable. For a limiting argument, give one calibration valid across the queried family. For a rank-to-count step, prove the diagonal or independence condition. For a finite potential, prove its transition cover and its uniform validity on each whole source family.

Then identify the exact target theorem and its first unproved hypothesis. A useful negative result should exhibit an ordinary positive source or a family of such sources, with the appropriate clocks and filters. Completion points and incompatible finite lifts cannot replace one ordinary trajectory. The adjacent tensor, Fourier, clock and LP notes carry out these checks for the routes implemented here.
