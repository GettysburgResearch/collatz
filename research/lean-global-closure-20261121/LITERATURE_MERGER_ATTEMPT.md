# Literature attempt at a global ordinary merger repair

This note continues the exact-head PR183 inspection recorded in
`LATE_MERGER_REFRESH.md`. The Lean packet already delivered to PR175 remains
unchanged. The deductions below are written mathematics, not additional
Lean-checked declarations. No universal ordinary merger or Collatz proof is
claimed.

## 1. What the termination paper actually supplies

The primary source is Yolcu, Aaronson, and Heule, *An Automated Approach to
the Collatz Conjecture*, arXiv:2105.14697v3, especially Theorems 2.6 and 2.15,
Lemma 3.18, and Section 4.3:

<https://arxiv.org/html/2105.14697v3>.

The paper proves termination of each of the eleven systems obtained by
deleting one rule from its mixed-base Collatz system. Example 4.3 is the
`f1 -> 0t` deletion used by PR183. Rule removal requires more than this:
the deleted rule must terminate **relative to the full system**. A relative
matrix interpretation strictly decreases the selected rule while weakly
decreasing every remaining rule in compatible orders. The other rules
cannot spend an unlimited amount of the selected rule's resource.

Lemma 3.18 allows relative *top* termination for the leading boundary rules,
or for the reversed dynamic boundary rules. Its argument depends on those
rules occurring at a boundary. It does not turn the internal `f1 -> 0t`
rule into a boundary rule.

Thus, writing `R0` for PR183's retained subsystem and `rho` for `f1 -> 0t`,
the remaining termination target is

\[
\operatorname{SN}(\{\rho\}/(R_0\cup\{\rho\})).
\]

This would forbid infinitely many blocked-rule uses while allowing all
retained rewrites between them. The existing rank only proves
`SN(R0)`. Selecting different ranks for different individual steps does not
establish the displayed statement.

## 2. What the semigroup proof actually supplies

The primary source is Applegate and Lagarias, *The 3x+1 Semigroup*,
arXiv:math/0411140v2, especially Theorem 1.1, Section 2, and Lemmas 2.2--2.3:

<https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0411140>.

For the shortcut map `T`, the semigroup generated by `n/T(n)` consists of
the positive rational numbers whose reduced denominator is not divisible
by three. The inverse, or wild, semigroup is generated by `T(n)/n`.
The constructive proof permits an integer state `x` to be multiplied by
a wild integer `m`, followed by ordinary iterations; these multiplications
may also occur between iterations. Membership justifies the step in the
semigroup. It does not assert a physical orbit connection between `x` and
`m*x`.

The infinite multiplier family is substantive: for suitable `j` and a
source `x` congruent to minus one modulo a sufficiently large power of two,
the proof uses `m=(2^j+1)/3` and the exact ordinary identity

\[
T^j(mx)=x+\frac{x+1}{2^j}.
\]

This controls the orbit beginning at `m*x`. It does not provide the missing
ordinary connection from the original `x` to that starting point.

## 3. Exact state matching for a generator

Here is the direct test for lifting the multiplicative argument. For a
positive odd `u`, its wild generator is

\[
g_u=\frac{T(u)}u=\frac{3u+1}{2u}.
\]

At a positive odd current state `x`,

\[
g_ux=T(x)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{(3u+1)x}{2u}=\frac{3x+1}{2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=u.
\]

At a positive even current state, `g_u*x > 3*x/2`, whereas its actual next
state is `x/2`, so equality is impossible. Divisibility of `x` by `u` is
therefore insufficient. If `x=c*u` with positive odd `c`, the exact defect
is

\[
cT(u)-T(cu)=\frac{c-1}{2}.
\]

By contrast, a generator supplied by an even `u` is `1/2`, which is an
actual forward step precisely at an even current state.

An ordered factorization can certify an ordinary forward walk only when
every factor is matched to the current state with these conditions.
Commuting factors or cancelling their prime factors does not preserve that
information. To obtain an ordinary merger instead of a single forward
walk, one must construct two actual legal walks with a common endpoint;
the same state conditions apply on both arms.

For the PR183 blocked erasure, the proposed pair is

\[
n=3^k(6q+1)+r,
\qquad m=3^k(2q)+r,
\qquad 0\le r<3^k.
\]

A rational factorization of their ratio does not supply either arm of
this merger. The positivity of both starts is already established by the
word semantics. What is missing is the legal ordinary history, not an
inequality between the two values.

## 4. A uniform bounded-clock multiplier repair is impossible

The following obstruction applies to a proposed repair of the form
"replace multiplication by a fixed wild integer by a bounded menu of
ordinary merger certificates."

**Proposition.** Let `m` be a positive integer with a prime factor other
than two or three. For each fixed pair of nonnegative clock bounds
`H,K`, only finitely many positive integers `x` satisfy

\[
\exists h\le H\;\exists t\le K,
\qquad T^h(x)=T^t(mx).
\]

In particular, no fixed bounds can certify such a merger for every
positive source. There are at most

\[
(2^{H+1}-1)(2^{K+1}-1)
\]

possible sources in the displayed set; this is a coarse upper bound and
need not be attained.

**Proof.** A parity word `w` of length `h` has an odd count `a(w)` and an
integer affine correction `c(w)` such that, whenever it is the actual
parity word of an integer `z`,

\[
2^hT^h(z)=3^{a(w)}z+c(w).
\tag{1}
\]

This follows by induction on the word. The empty word has odd count zero
and correction zero. Appending an even input leaves the previous numerator
unchanged and multiplies the denominator by two. Appending an odd input
multiplies the previous numerator by three and adds the old denominator.
In particular the coefficient and correction depend only on the finite
word, not on the chosen source realizing it.

Fix `h,t` and parity words `w,v` for the two arms, with respective odd
counts `a,b` and corrections `c,d`. Applying (1) to `x` and `m*x`, their
meeting condition is

\[
(2^t3^a-m2^h3^b)x=2^h d-2^t c.
\tag{2}
\]

The coefficient of `x` in (2) is nonzero. Otherwise

\[
m2^h3^b=2^t3^a,
\]

which is impossible: a prime factor of `m` different from two and three
would divide the left side and not the right side. Therefore (2) has at
most one rational solution, and in particular at most one positive
integer source for this clock/word pair. Actual parity legality can only
remove that possible solution.

There are `2^h` parity words of length `h`, including the one empty word
when `h=0`. Hence the total number of clock/word pairs with `h<=H` and
`t<=K` is

\[
\left(\sum_{h=0}^{H}2^h\right)
\left(\sum_{t=0}^{K}2^t\right)
=(2^{H+1}-1)(2^{K+1}-1).
\]

Taking their finite union proves both claims. Equivalently, if a bounded
menu served infinitely many sources, some fixed pair of clocks and parity
words would serve two distinct sources. Subtracting their instances of
(2) would force the impossible equality of slopes. QED.

This is a restriction on **bounded** uniform multiplier repair. It does
not rule out arbitrary ordinary mergers, an unbounded source-dependent
selector, or a finite recursive rule system whose total running time is
unbounded. It also gives no converse for multipliers supported on the
primes two and three. No conclusion about Collatz convergence follows
from this proposition alone.

## 5. The attempted unbounded physical repair

I tried to replace the missing multiplication by an actual ancestor
construction. The intended intermediate state would be an ancestor
`M=m*x` of the prescribed original source, with an ordinary route from
`M` to an endpoint below that source. The exact orbit identity in Section
2 would then be physically useful, because both outgoing paths would
start at the same ordinary integer `M`.

This requires three simultaneous facts: an actual ancestor with the
needed divisibility, the required low-bit residue of its quotient `x`,
and a size or matrix-rank budget for the endpoint after the ordinary
route. Semigroup membership establishes none of these facts for a
prescribed original source.

Our inverse-carry and maximal-reserve recovery preserves an actual
ancestor when one is supplied, including its exact remainder after an
odd-budget overflow. It cannot choose the parity history needed by the
three simultaneous conditions. When its leading coefficient is exhausted,
the retained shadow is the full unresolved endpoint. I obtained no
decreasing bound for this residual state.

The remaining constructive target is therefore an **unbounded,
state-matched ordinary selector** that carries its positivity, residue,
and rank certificate through every repair. A multiplicative identity, a
normal-word rank decrease, and an inverse-path completeness theorem are
individually insufficient to supply that selector. Their missing
compatibility has not been asserted as a lemma.
