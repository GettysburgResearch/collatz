## 1. Direct canonical-charge calculation

[The charge attempt](DIRECT_CANONICAL_CHARGE_ATTEMPT.md) fixes one
hypothetical escaping component throughout. Its actual last-contact
measure is \(\eta(y)=\nu(y)\mathbf1_\Lambda(y)\), with total mass
\(C>0\). For the genuine inverse-contact reset \(Q\), the exact density
after one reset is

\[
Q_*\eta(y)=\nu(y)H(y),\qquad
H(y)=\sum_{t\ge1}\beta(U^t y),\qquad
\sum_y\nu(y)H(y)=C.
\]

The ordinary arithmetic supplies a strict one-step loss. Accounting for
all subsequent contact arrivals gives only

\[
H(y)\le1-\frac{a}{Uy\,\nu(Uy)}
       +\log\frac{C}{\nu(Uy)},\qquad a=3\gamma/2^{54}>0.
\]

The proposed contradiction needs the final positive term to be smaller
than the loss after integration against the actual boundary measure.
That inequality was not obtained. The canonical weighting contributing
\(D^2/C\) is \((D/C)^2\eta\); its componentwise constant factor
does not change this balance.

The final optional-stopping test does have a uniform density floor under
the pinned normalization, but its valid conclusion is

\[
\Pr_q^G(\tau_M^0\le s)
\le\chi M\,D_t(q)/G(q),\qquad t\ge s+1.
\]

Small density bounds early entrance from above. This permits a long
entrance delay. An old fixed contact cannot simultaneously be included
in that stopping event and excluded by a higher predecessor-floor
hypothesis at the same depth.
