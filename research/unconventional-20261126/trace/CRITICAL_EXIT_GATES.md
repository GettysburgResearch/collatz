# The logarithmic endpoint of the finite physical exit gates

**Status.** Proposed, source-qualified. PR201 at [3eb251f, EXIT_GATES](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/3eb251fae9082fef33c047079a511d1c3f4c5969/research/unconventional-20261126/operator/EXIT_GATES.md) proves a sorted gate limit for summable source weights \(x^{-s}\), \(s>1\). PR193 at [23b7d32, TILTED_TRACE](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/23b7d326a43feb21a827a9024fc24eb10d944338/research/astra-unconventional-20261126/boundary/TILTED_TRACE.md) discusses earlier summable tilted tests. The following bridge uses ordinary source densities and first-passage control to treat the nonsummable endpoint \(s=1\).

Let \(g_X(x)\) be the first physical shortcut state at least \(X\) from \(x\), with an immediate hit if \(x\ge X\), and omit a source with no hit. Let \(w_{g_X(x)}(x)\) be its product of \(3a/(3a+1)\) over odd departures before the first exit. Define the finitely supported vector on gate labels
\[
 J_X(z)=\frac1{H_{X-1}}\sum_{1\le x<X,\ g_X(x)=z}\frac{w_z(x)}x,
 \qquad H_N=\sum_{x=1}^N\frac1x .
\]
Each entry is rational. Each path is followed to exit or the first repetition below \(X\), so the vector uses only finite ordinary computation. Choose one source \(a_C\) in each escaping component, and put \(\beta_C=b_C/c_*\). Then
\[
 \left\|J_X-\sum_{C\ {\rm escaping}}\beta_C\delta_{g_X(a_C)}\right\|_1
 \longrightarrow0.                                               \tag{1}
\]
Consequently its decreasing rearrangement converges in \(\ell^1\) to the sorted charges \((\beta_C)\). Its total converges to \(\sum_C b_C/c_*\). No monotonicity or effective convergence rate is claimed here. In particular a single small computed total gives no zero-limit certificate.

**Abelian comparison.** In PR201's notation, \(L(x)=x/W(x)\) on an escaping component, where \(W\) is the entire future survival product. Thus \(Z_C(s)=\sum_{x\in C}W(x)^s x^{-s}\). The natural mean of \(\mathbf1_C W\) equals \(\beta_C\) by fixed-root truncation and the canonical density theorem, as justified in [the annular current](ANNULAR_ESCAPE_CURRENT.md). Partial summation gives \((s-1)\sum_{x\in C}W(x)x^{-s}\to\beta_C\). Replacing \(W\) by \(W^s\) changes this normalized sum by \(o(1)\): on \([0,1]\), \(\sup_t|t^s-t|=O(s-1)\), and \((s-1)\zeta(s)\to1\). The same statements hold for the union of all escaping components by component-law tightness. This explains the relation of the endpoint to PR201's atoms. It does not allow substituting \(s=1\) in PR201's dominated-convergence proof.

**First the easier, separated cutoffs.** Sample odd \(q\le Y\) with probability proportional to \(1/q\), take \(X=Y^3\), and normalize by the odd harmonic sum through \(Y\). The counterpart of (1) holds. Fix \(M\); up to an asymptotic probability \(e(M)\to0\), the orbit hits an odd \(a\le M\), by tightness of the global minimum. For each of the finitely many such targets, Valbuena's fixed-target proposition **ct-time-law** says that within its basin, outside an odd-logarithmic-density-zero exceptional set, the first-hit odd clock is at most \(1.25\log q/\log(4/3)\) at large \(q\). This is an absolute exceptional-density statement. The union over fixed \(M\) is still null. Before first hitting such an \(a\), the shortcut peak is at most \((q+1)(3/2)^{k_a(q)}\), since the first shortcut after each odd departure is the largest of its ensuing halving chain. Because \(1+1.25\log(3/2)/\log(4/3)<3\), these sources reach the fixed finite core before exiting. The last paragraph of the proof below now applies. This separated assertion only uses the fixed-target clocks and minimum law, not a uniform moving-target clock. For odd normalization, write \(F_C=\mathbf1_C W\), so \(F_C(2q)=F_C(q)\). If \(A_C(Y)=\sum_{x\le Y}F_C(x)/x\), then \(\sum_{q\le Y,\ q\ {\rm odd}}F_C(q)/q=A_C(Y)-\tfrac12 A_C(Y/2)\). Since the odd harmonic sum is asymptotic to \(\tfrac12\log Y\), this transfers the full harmonic mean and component tightness to odd sampling.

**Peak bound for the unseparated cutoff.** We spell out the additional analytic input for \(X=Y\). In Valbuena's pinned [basin article](https://github.com/x1xhlol/collatz-two-coordinate-obstruction/blob/a7275d5a73814d3d9a400c6fe6577de9542b03a3/paper/tao-basin-densities.tex), use the lemma **ct-local**, its proof's displayed **ct-local-log-path**, and the proof of **ct-time-law** through **ct-stage-TV**. They use \(\alpha=1001/1000\). The source gives, for odd logarithmic samples \(Q\) on either \([z^\alpha,z^{\alpha^2}]\) or \([z^{\alpha^2},z^{\alpha^3}]\), outside probability \(C\exp(-c(\log z)^{1/5})\),
\[
 |\log U^h(Q)-\log Q+\lambda h|\le C(\log z)^{3/5}
 \quad(0\le h\le\lfloor0.1\log z\rfloor),\qquad \lambda=\log(4/3).
\]
On this event passage below \(z\) occurs within the displayed horizon. In particular any subsegment up to that passage rises by at most a factor \(\exp(C'(\log z)^{3/5})\) over its own first odd state, by subtracting two path estimates and using the negative drift. The source's external analytic chain was not independently rebuilt here.

Fix a sufficiently large bottom \(M\), set \(x_i=M^{\alpha^i}\), and start on a top window \([x_j^\alpha,x_j^{\alpha^2}]\). The top passage satisfies the just-stated peak estimate. For each lower segment \(x_i\to x_{i-1}\), the source compares the actual landing law at \(x_i\), including its prescribed default, to \(\nu_{x_i}=\mathcal L(\operatorname{Pass}_{x_i}(Q'))\), \(Q'\) odd logarithmic on \([x_i^\alpha,x_i^{\alpha^2}]\), with total-variation error \(O((\log x_i)^{-c})\). Under this reference law, take the preceding local path estimate with \(z=x_{i-1}\). Its second allowed source window is exactly the interval for \(Q'\). On its good event both \(x_i\) and \(x_{i-1}\) are reached in order (possibly at the same step). The intervening odd segment consequently has peak at most \(x_i\exp(C'(\log x_{i-1})^{3/5})\). Success and the latter peak bound for the lower segment are a property of its starting landing state, so they transfer by the stated TV estimate. Induction from top success makes all uses of actual passage on this event legitimate. Union bounding the exponential and TV errors over the ladder gives \(e_1(M)=o(1)\), uniformly in the top rung, because \(\log x_i=\alpha^i\log M\). Every lower starting bound \(x_i\) is at most the original \(q\). Shortcut intermediate states cost at most one additional factor two. Hence, except for this error, a source reaches the bottom \(M\) with entire prelanding peak at most \(2q\exp(C'(\log q)^{3/5})\).

These top windows tile all sufficiently large \(q\). Covering a final partial top window by its complete window multiplies its upper harmonic mass bound by at most a fixed constant in the normalized cumulative estimate: the next upper endpoint is at most \(X^\alpha\) for a cutoff \(X\) in that last window. Shared endpoints have finite total reciprocal mass. Thus the exceptional set has upper relative logarithmic density \(O(e_1(M))\). Among odd \(q<X\) the additional strip \(2q\exp(C'(\log q)^{3/5})\ge X\) has normalized logarithmic mass \(O((\log X)^{-2/5})\). Almost every source in the required iterated sense therefore hits the chosen finite core **before** its first exit. For all positive starts \(x=2^j q<X\), the initial halvings introduce no exit or loss and the subsequent orbit begins at odd \(q\). Sum over finitely many \(j\) with weights \(2^{-j}\), then discard the geometric tail. This gives the same upper error for the full logarithmic source law.

**Passage to the gate vector.** For each of the finitely many possible core states \(r\le M\), a periodic future has a finite maximum. In an escaping component its forward orbit merges with that of the chosen \(a_C\) at finite height. For sufficiently large \(X\), in the latter case both have the same gate \(z_C=g_X(a_C)\), and in the former no gate. For a source \(x\) that first reached the core before exiting, its contribution at that same \(z_C\) differs from \(W(x)/x\) by at most \((1-W(z_C))/x\), since \(W(x)=w_{z_C}(x)W(z_C)\). For each of the finitely many represented escaping components \(W(z_C)\to1\), by convergence of its positive reciprocal tail. The normalized total error from every other source is at most twice its sampling probability. Finally the sampled component weights \(\mathbf E[\mathbf1_C W]\) converge in \(\ell^1\) over escaping components to \(\beta_C\): use fixed-component convergence from the weighted density theorem, then truncate the component law in total variation. Gates of distinct components are distinct. Combining these estimates, then letting \(M\to\infty\), proves (1). The rearrangement conclusion follows from the elementary \(\ell^1\) contraction of decreasing rearrangement.

The missing global upper bound can now be stated using only finite positive rational numbers: it would suffice to show a sequence \(X_j\to\infty\) with \(\|J_{X_j}\|_1\to0\). The source clocks control the routes up to entry into a finite core. They do not prevent a fixed member of the core from escaping after that entry.
