# Conservative regular sector of the compulsory log-height law

**Status.** Proposed actual-source occupation theorem. Part of the compulsory logarithmic-height law has a conservative invariant transverse measure even when the expected number of record boundaries per unit logarithmic height is infinite. Its dyadic and ternary atomic sectors coincide and consist of explicitly weighted negative signed cycles. Other atoms can only occur on a specified boundary. The regular sector can have zero mass.

Use any compulsory probability limit $\rho$ from [OP-G6](GATE_SUSPENSION.md). A dyadic odd phase is **regular** here if its true future parses into infinitely many complete strict coefficient ladders and the sum of their positive logarithmic overshoots diverges. This is a Borel set $\mathcal V$, since each finite first-ladder itinerary is read on a finite clopen native cylinder. Every positive odd integer with an escaping forward trajectory is regular by bounded simple-path distortion. Positive periodic-basin integers have at most finitely many strict coefficient records.

## OP-G7. A stationary suspension on the regular sector

On $\mathcal V$ the next record label and its roof $a(v)>0$ are fixed by the starting dyadic point and locally constant there. The full vertex $v=(x,z,\phi)$ maps deterministically to the next vertex $Fv$ by that block, including its contracting ternary affine map and the circle rotation by $a(v)$. The cycle-normalized real-log coordinate is the one in OP-G6. Let $\lambda$ be the starting vertex marginal of $\rho$ restricted to $x\in\mathcal V$. Then
$$
 \pi(dv)=a(v)^{-1}\lambda(dv),\qquad F_*\pi=\pi,\qquad
                          \int a\,d\pi=\lambda(1).
$$
This measure is $\sigma$-finite and conservative. In particular the expectation of the total number of boundaries per unit log height does not need to be finite for this conclusion.

To prove this, sample a real time uniformly in the combined intervals of the first $n$ coefficient record blocks and keep the edge and its fractional age $u$. The factor $u$ is exactly uniform on $[0,1)$ and independent of the sampled edge, also in the weak limit. For each fixed $t\ge0$, sample the mark a further time $t$ later on the same infinite source. The marginals of the two samples differ by at most $2t/A_n$ in total variation, where $A_n$ is their total original length. A joint subsequential limit has identical marginals. At a regular initial phase the limiting later mark is the genuine suspension evolution $\Phi_t$ from the initial vertex and its age. Indeed at that phase finitely many subsequent positive roofs cover the prescribed interval, and their complete labels are forced by a finite clopen future cylinder. Exact coincidence of the later time with any boundary is a conditional age-null set. The affine local coordinates and the circle evolution pass through that finite list of labels by continuity. The future is still regular. Equality of the two full marginals therefore implies that the restricted finite measure on the regular sector is $\Phi_t$-invariant. This argument works for each $t$ and does not transfer a finite label across the complementary boundary.

The resulting suspension has coordinates $(v,h)$, $0\le h<a(v)$, and measure $\pi(dv)\,dh$. A forward realization has finitely many boundaries in each bounded time interval by regularity. For a measurable set $B$ of starting vertices let $N_B(0,t]$ count departing intervals marked in $B$. Its expected count $\mu(B)$ for $t=1$ is a measure, and its expected count over integer $t=T$ equals $T\mu(B)$ by invariance. If $a\ge\delta>0$ on $B$, choose $0<\epsilon<\delta$. A traversed interval in $B$ spends exactly $\epsilon$ time in its last $\epsilon$ units of age. Integrating this indicator before time $T$ differs from $\epsilon N_B(0,T]$ by at most $2\epsilon$ at the endpoints. Its expectation is $T\epsilon\pi(B)$. Hence $\mu(B)=\pi(B)$. Exhaustion by $\{a\ge1/j\}$ proves $\mu=\pi$ everywhere and gives $\sigma$-finiteness. Counts of departures from $F^{-1}B$ differ from counts of departures from $B$ by at most one because they mark consecutive intervals. Divide by $T$ for finite $\pi(B)$, then use monotone convergence, to prove $F_*\pi=\pi$.

For any $B$ of positive $\pi$-measure the union of intervals marked in $B$ has positive finite suspension measure, as $\lambda$ and $\pi$ are equivalent. Poincaré recurrence for integer log-time translations brings almost every such initial point into these intervals infinitely often. Each interval has bounded length and each finite elapsed duration crosses only finitely many boundaries. It follows that $\pi$-almost every vertex of $B$ returns to $B$ under a positive iterate of $F$. This proves conservativity.

## OP-G8. Rational rigidities under logarithmic-height sampling

Consider the two local marginals of $\lambda$. Their purely atomic parts are carried by the **same** countable collection of negative signed rational cycles on the exact diagonal. On a contributing cycle $C$, take its consecutive strict record phases $u_0,\ldots,u_{r-1}$ with corresponding complete-block roofs $a_i$. Conditional on this cycle, log-height sampling assigns the starting phase $u_i$ probability
$$
                            \frac{a_i}{a_0+\cdots+a_{r-1}}.
$$
Both adic coordinates at that vertex equal $u_i$, and its circle coordinate is independent Haar. This is weighted continuous-height occupancy. In particular for any fixed positive ordinary escaping seed $b$ the dyadic marginal of the **whole** compulsory law assigns zero mass to $b$, since $b\in\mathcal V$. The same is true of every positive odd rational dyadic input whose genuine coefficient records are cofinal.

Here are details for both coordinates. If $x$ is an atom of the regular dyadic marginal, the positive-measure set of states with precisely that $x$ returns to itself after finitely many $F$-iterations, by conservativity. The resulting finite genuine odd word has strictly superunit coefficient since its roofs sum to a positive number. Its unique fixed point is a negative rational of odd numerator and odd denominator prime to three. It is periodic under the record map from its marked phase. The union of vertices over the finitely many dyadic phases of its record cycle is forward-invariant in the suspension, hence its finite restricted measure is invariant. Starting there, the ternary difference from the matching rational phase acquires a factor $3^m/2^s$ on each complete record block, with $m\ge1$. After logarithmic time $t$ there are at least $t/\log(3/2)-2$ such complete blocks. Invariance and contraction force this difference to vanish almost surely at the starting phase. Thus its ternary coordinate equals the dyadic rational.

Conversely let $P$ be the countable set of atoms of the regular ternary marginal. Transition starting and ending measures agree for $\pi$, which is equivalent to $\lambda$. Starting in $P$ leads into a countable union of affine images of $P$ over the finite words, and ending in $P$ comes from a countable union of inverse images. Countable nonatoms have zero measure, so a transition starts in $P$ exactly when it ends there up to null sets. Draw an arrow $z\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}z'$ for each positive-measure transition between atoms with that label. Conservativity of this atom-arrow event implies that it lies on a finite closed walk of such arrows. Along the closed walk the coefficient is strictly superunit and the affine carry strictly positive. Its fixed point, and by rotation all its phases, are negative rationals with odd numerator and odd denominator prime to three. The equalities between successive rational phases are literal equalities in $\mathbb Q$, since they hold in $\mathbb Q_3$. With the terminal phase a dyadic odd unit, the supplied finite word is the genuine native parity prefix at its rational start by the exact parity congruence. Thus its outgoing first-ladder label is unique. The recurrent atom graph is a union of finite cycles. The true dyadic address in the suspension has that entire prescribed future, so the dyadic parity-cylinder bijection makes it the same rational phase. This extends the signed atomic-sector proof of [the odd-time record-flow theorem](../renewal/RECORD_FLOW.md) to a conservative, possibly infinite transverse measure on the regular logarithmic-height sector.

Finally the suspension over one such cycle has phase-around-the-cycle $s\in\mathbb R/A_C\mathbb Z$, $A_C=\sum a_i$, and lifted real-log phase $\varphi=\phi+h\in\mathbb R/(\log2)\mathbb Z$. Both translate by the same elapsed real time. Here $A_C/\log2=m\log_2 3-S$ for the positive total odd grade $m$ and the lap clock $S$, which is irrational. A nonconstant Fourier character cannot be fixed by this continuous translation. The only invariant probability is product Haar, proving the formula and the real-circle independence.

Every atom on a rational diagonal starting address in the full compulsory law is covered by this classification. Starting ternary addresses at coefficient records have backwards critical scripts. [PR210, R1](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/592328fecf8ea2cf41736294856d95f890a1ba98/research/critical-record-rational-rigidity-20261125/README.md) identifies any rational point there that is a dyadic odd unit as a negative signed periodic phase with the critical backwards prefix property; its genuine future has divergent roof and is regular. An entire law supported on the rational diagonal is therefore the indicated countable mixture. This still allows log-height occupation entirely on divergent block lengths at a mixed or irrational limiting address, or a diffuse regular sector. A proof that ordinary escape forces neither type nor signed-cycle mixtures is missing.
