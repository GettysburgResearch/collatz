**Primitive word and denominator.** A shorter native period $d$ would have an integral number of ones $qd/m$, so $m\mid d$. At macro boundaries the two flags are readable separately and force $r,t\mid d/m$. For $U=1w0,V=0w1$ the normalized translations differ by $b_w+(2-R_w)/4$, positive of order $L$ by bounded discrepancy. Multiplying by $2^L$ gives the precise congruence for $C_w$. Adding translations independently is valid because the slopes of the alternatives coincide, and propagation through the other identical motif gives the gain ratio $c_1/c_2=\mu$. Summing a single periodic pulse before the pulse at macro time zero gives exactly $c_i A^{h-1}/(B^h-A^h)$. The lower-denominator test removes all primes common to $Q_r$ and $B-A$; the only possible one is $r$, with valuation one. Each remaining prime gives order $r$ of $B/A$ and cannot divide the other terms' denominators. Since $Q_r\ge B^{r-1}$ and $c_1\ll BL$, such a denominator survives actual reduction in both regimes.

**Full sieve.** The patched modulus is the complete prime-power part of $B^{rt}-A^{rt}$, including all tested primes and their full valuations. The independent $w$ and $v$ patches respectively cancel both switch increments and then $C_0$. Their coefficients are units modulo that entire coprime-to-six modulus. Thus each full-period numerator is divisible by it; a test only on the single-macro determinant would have been insufficient. The Fourier patch's window map is injective because its $H$ nonzero grades give a power of 3 exceeding the modulus and the window start is a unit. Hölder applies over the same nonzero characters across the independent windows. Arbitrary in-block choices preserve the slope discrepancy, and the needed patches fit in $w$ and $v$ since $P=o(L)=o(m)$. I inspected the actual formulas on Yu's printed pages 241–242. For the two rational bases the two heights may be chosen $O(1+\log \ell)$ as required; their product absorbs $(\log\ell)^{-2}$, and the last maximum is $O(1+\log\ell)$. **Distinct offsets.** The virtual averaged motif can be realized along either order by replacing all its positive odd translations with a common positive multiple. The multiplier remains the true one, the full motif endpoint agrees across both choices, the scaling ratios are uniformly bounded above and below, and every subarc of at most one macro has bounded multiplier. The periodic virtual input has height comparable to $m/\delta$ at every offset. The actual pulse response differs from its average by $O(L)$ at all times; the range of $a\lambda^v/(1-\lambda^h)$ is at most $a$, uniformly even when $r,t$ grow. Propagation and changes to interior offsets preserve an $O(L)$ total error. For compatible choices both virtual cuts lie on one common system. A long expanding complementary arc has additive increment $\gg m$, while for a short arc its nonunit multiplier differs from one by $\gg\delta$, enough to dominate its $O(h)$ translation. Incompatible cuts strictly inside the same motif compare positive prefix slopes from the same virtual boundary. The exact exponent difference falls under convergent best approximation at unequal lengths. At equal interior lengths the first digit differs and the remaining traversed part of $w$ is identical, so the slope ratio is exactly 3 or $1/3$. Boundary cuts themselves are consistently handled by whole processed motifs. This accounts for offsets on opposite orders without assigning both to a single incompatible virtual orbit.

**Equal offsets.** Dividing by the common prefix slope converts a fully processed motif into exactly $c_i/A$, independent of the cut's later position. Its track response becomes $u_{j+1}/\lambda$, whereas an unprocessed track remains $u_j$. Identical flags at a partial motif give identical added constants there. In each track, either choice of before/after is a bijection on the residue of the macro time. Uniform Taylor error for the normalized difference is $O(h\delta)$ when $h\delta=o(1)$, since the numerator differs from $\delta(v'-v)$ by $O(\delta^2 h^2)$ and the denominator is $\delta h(1+O(h\delta))$. Pairwise coprimality leaves a rational gap at least $1/(rt)$ before the $\mu-1$ and Taylor errors. The parameter choices make their sum $o(1/(rt))$. Running first at fixed $r,t$ and then taking a diagonal sequence in the endpoint regime avoids asking for uniformity of the cutoff constants there.
