# The commutative quotient and the compact ideal of the branch representations

**PROPOSED written results.** Use the shortcut map, counting Hilbert space $H$, unweighted operators $V_i$ and periodic diagonal multipliers of [the component norm theorem](COMPONENT_OPERATORS.md). Two exact calculations distinguish this concrete algebra from its height-weighted variants and from an induced Cuntz-algebra representation.

## The unweighted algebra has precisely a circle of characters

**Theorem O3.** The abelianization of $\mathcal A$ is $C(\mathbb T)$. Its characters $\chi_\lambda$, $|\lambda|=1$, satisfy
$$
 \chi_\lambda(V_0)=\lambda,\qquad \chi_\lambda(V_1)=0,\qquad
 \chi_\lambda(M_h)=h(0)
$$
for every periodic $h$. Here $h(0)$ denotes its value on the residue class of zero for any period of $h$.

For existence, fix a finite polynomial $P$, common coefficient period $d$ and maximum word length $r$ (counting only edges). Choose any long ordinary dyadic chain $x_j=2^j(3d)$ for positive indices $j$. At all $x_j$ with $j\ge1$, the next state is the previous point, the even predecessor is the next point and there is no odd predecessor, since $3\mid x_j$. Every periodic coefficient in $P$ takes its zero-class value at every $x_j$. For any finite segment with at least $r$ spare vertices at each end, the matrix entries on its interior agree exactly with the same words in a bilateral shift $S$ on $\ell^2(\mathbb Z)$, with $V_1$ set to zero and $M_h$ set to $h(0)I$. The shift sends $\delta_j$ to $\delta_{j+1}$. Any finite test vector for that bilateral polynomial may be placed sufficiently high in the chain with its full radius-$r$ halo. Thus
$$
   \|P(S,0,h(0)I)\|\le \|P(V_0,V_1,M_h)\|.
$$
Substitution consequently extends to a surjective *-homomorphism $\mathcal A\to C^*(S)\simeq C(\mathbb T)$. This constructs all displayed characters.

Conversely, $V_0^*V_0=I$ and $V_0V_0^*$ is the even projection. A character therefore sends $V_0$ to a unit complex number and the even projection to 1. As $V_1V_1^*$ is the odd projection, the same character vanishes on $V_1$. On the algebra of sequences of period $d$ it selects one residue $a\bmod d$ because the residue projections are orthogonal with sum $I$. The identity
$$
                     V_0^* M_h V_0=M_{h(2\,\cdot)}
$$
forces $h(a)=h(2a)$ for every such $h$, hence $a=0\bmod d$. Every character thus factors through the displayed quotient. The abelianization, a commutative unital C*-algebra, is determined by its characters. Equivalently, its remaining generator is a unitary with all of $\mathbb T$ as spectrum. This proves O3. The quotient is nontrivial and $V_1$ belongs to its nonzero kernel.

## Every nonzero polynomial weighting produces exactly the component compacts

Let $H^{(\sigma)}=\ell^2(\mathbb N_{>0},n^{-\sigma})$, with real $\sigma\ne0$. Define $\mathcal A_\sigma$ on counting space by unitary conjugation of the algebra generated by the same branch *formulas* and periodic diagonals on $H^{(\sigma)}$. Write $W_i$ for the conjugated branch operators. Set
$$
  \mathcal J=\bigoplus_C^{c_0}\mathcal K(H_C),
$$
the norm closure of finite sums of finite-rank operators supported in individual component blocks. This is exactly the algebra of compact operators on $H$ that are block diagonal for all components: compact block operators have compact restrictions, and infinitely many blocks of norm bounded below would give orthonormal inputs whose images have no convergent subsequence.

**Theorem O4.** For every $\sigma\ne0$,
$$
       \mathcal A_\sigma=\mathcal A+\mathcal J,\qquad
       \mathcal A_\sigma\cap\mathcal K(H)=\mathcal J,\qquad
       \mathcal A_\sigma/\mathcal J\simeq\mathcal A.
$$
The quotient is split by the inclusion of $\mathcal A$. Thus the compact ideal is independent of the sign and size of the nonzero exponent. In particular it has one minimal nonzero closed two-sided ideal for each ordinary component.

Conjugation gives $W_0=2^{-\sigma/2}V_0$. On odd integers, $W_1$ is $V_1$ multiplied on the output side by $t(n)=((3+1/n)/2)^{\sigma/2}$. Therefore $W_1W_1^*$ is diagonal with value $t(n)^2$ at odd $n$ and zero at even $n$. The odd values are pairwise distinct, strictly monotone with $n$, and accumulate only at $(3/2)^\sigma>0$. Every individual odd value is isolated in the spectrum. The continuous functional calculus produces the rank-one coordinate projection $p_n$ for each odd $n$. Applying $V_0p_nV_0^*=p_{2n}$ gives every coordinate projection.

The same functional calculus recovers $V_1$ from $W_1$: choose a continuous function on the spectrum of $W_1W_1^*$ that is $u^{-1/2}$ on its positive part and zero at zero, and multiply $W_1$ on the left by the resulting diagonal. The positive spectrum is bounded away from zero. Thus $\mathcal A\subset\mathcal A_\sigma$. For $x,y$ in the same component, choose a finite labeled graph path between them. The corresponding word in partial isometries and their adjoints carries $\delta_x$ to $\delta_y$ when read in the appropriate direction. Multiplication by $p_x,p_y$ isolates the matrix unit $|\delta_y\rangle\langle\delta_x|$. It follows that $\mathcal J\subset\mathcal A_\sigma$.

Conversely $W_1-(3/2)^{\sigma/2}V_1$ is compact and block diagonal, by $t(n)\to(3/2)^{\sigma/2}$ on its odd support. It belongs to $\mathcal J$. All generators therefore lie in $\mathcal A+\mathcal J$. This sum is closed: within the C*-algebra of all bounded block diagonal operators it is the preimage of the image of $\mathcal A$ in the quotient by $\mathcal J$, and the image of a C*-algebra under a *-homomorphism is closed. By O1, $\mathcal A$ contains no nonzero compact operator, giving the remaining assertions. The minimal ideals of a $c_0$ direct sum of compact-operator algebras are precisely its individual summands.

This describes a precise distinction between compact and essential information. For $\sigma\ne0$ one can recover every single coordinate by norm functional calculus. The algebra modulo compact operators retains the common representation norms of O1. Showing that every coordinate lies in the ideal generated by $p_1$ would require connecting every ordinary vertex to 1, which this calculation leaves open. The compact ideal is simple exactly if there is one ordinary component. This last observation interprets the structural result rather than proving simplicity.
