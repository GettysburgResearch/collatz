# Review of equality at the original cube-root gauge

**Verdict.** VERIFIED at the written-proof level for Theorem E in `../renewal/CRITICAL_GAUGE_ENDPOINT.md`, SHA-256 `9e55dc5e81e9617b0ced6ef55ebaea135bc9e81b1676aa1ccc19fa2ce5871a50`, using `SHARP_GAUGE.md` at `0dc36211f63059e5be36ea8ddf136602b062032553993ef6c796fa8ac4dea899`. In particular the original critical cube-root gauge has infinite measure. The stronger lower assertion through $b=1$ also follows. This review does not validate a separate proposed upper theorem for $b>1$.

I checked Theorem 1, equation (1.1) in Komlós–Major–Tusnády (1976). It gives the exponential tail for the maximum integer-time coupling error uniformly in the time horizon, under the finite moment generating function hypothesis. This Part II theorem allows the geometric increment distribution after centering and variance normalization. Choose $\Delta=C(\log n)^6$ large so the coupling failure is smaller than a fixed fraction, indeed little-oh, of the minimum relevant Brownian survival probability. The maximum displacement during the Brownian unit intervals contributes $O(L\exp(-c\Delta^2))$, needed for the upper comparison. Changing a strip of width $w\asymp n^{1/3}$ by $O(\Delta)$ changes its log-cost by $O((\log n)^6\Delta/w)=o(1)$. Center starts and endpoints remain uniformly away from either shifted boundary. [KMT, original theorem, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00532688)

For the variable-width construction, a stage meeting a terminal prefix only partially may be charged in full at a logarithmic cost $O((\log n)^6)$. The count of complete stage normalizers is $O(n^{1/3}/(\log n)^6)$. In a dyadic interval at scale $x$, each left-rectangle error costs $O(x^{-1/3}(\log x)^{12})$ and there are $O(1+x^{1/3}/(\log x)^6)$ such stages. Summing costs through all dyadic scales gives the claimed $O((\log n)^7)$ error. The fixed starting prefix and the ternary carry projection add at most $O(\log n)$ after the usual compensation $h>\log2$.

Finally, with $t=\log n$ two integrations by parts produce $F-3F'+O(t^{-3})$ for the normalized width integral. Since $3\kappa/f^2=hf/2$ and $A=3hf/2$, the linear width term cancels and the relative remainder is $u^2+2u'+O(t^{-3})$. For $u=(1+1/\log t)/t$ the leading expression is exactly $-t^{-2}(1+1/(\log t)^2)$. Its additional negative term dominates both the $O(t^{-3})$ Taylor error and all accumulated normalization errors after multiplying by $n^{1/3}$. The uniform ball mass divided by the $b=1$ gauge tends to zero, which gives infinite measure directly.
