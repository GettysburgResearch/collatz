**Probability and interval constants.** With $\alpha=\log_2 3$ and $u=1-1/\alpha$, the iid geometric letter has mean $\alpha$ and variance $\alpha(\alpha-1)$. Substituting the prescribed word clock $s=\alpha n-d_n$ into $(1-u)^n u^{s-n}$ gives exactly $e^{-Hn+hd_n}$. Scaling Donsker's interpolated walk to $[0,T]$ gives variance-rate $\sigma^2$ and Dirichlet interval eigenvalue $\kappa=\sigma^2\pi^2/2$. The sine expansion bounds the survival probability by $C e^{-\kappa T}$ for every starting position, including endpoints. Integrating its principal strictly positive term over the prescribed central endpoint interval and bounding the remaining terms by $O(e^{-4\kappa T})$ supplies a positive lower prefactor for bulk starts. Fix $T$ large enough to absorb both prefactors with strict slack before taking the width limit. A sequence of offending starting positions has a subsequence converging after division by the width. Portmanteau applies to the enlarged closed interval for the upper bound and the open inside-plus-endpoint event for the lower bound. Neither a strong approximation nor a uniform-in-$T$ invariance principle is required. [Mörters and Peres, Theorem 5.22](https://www.mi.uni-koeln.de/~moerters/book/book.pdf)

**Moving stages and upper cover.** If $W_j=f n_j^{1/3}$ and the stage length is $\lfloor T W_j^2\rfloor$, then $n_{j+1}^{1/3}-n_j^{1/3}\to Tf^2/3$. This yields the factor $3/f^2$ in the exponent, including time $n$ between stage endpoints. The ratio $W_{j+1}/W_j\to1$ permits the upper stage event to enlarge its interval. Successive conditional probabilities remain bounded by the same per-stage supremum, regardless of their actual start. Taking the countable union over fixed confinement-start times and then over rational $(a,f)$ covers finite positive limsup. The cases zero and infinity use distinct covers already specified in the text. Summing their variable-depth costs is justified by a strict negative multiple of $n^{1/3}$.

**Lower construction.** Every proper partial sum $S_i-\alpha i$ is distinct from every other such sum, since $\alpha$ is irrational. Rotating after the largest among $0\le i<n$ makes all subsequent sums negative: the nonwrapped endpoint at the end of the unrotated word is negative relative to the chosen nonnegative maximum, and every wrapped prefix has the strictly negative total added to a nonpositive difference. Taking $s=\lfloor\alpha n-W/2\rfloor$ puts the final deficit in $[W/3,2W/3]$ for large $n$. The bulk of each stage maps strictly into the next stage's accepted start range once their ratio is near one. Conditioning on the whole last stage still bounds the mass of a prescribed partial stage by the raw prefix probability divided by the full normalizer, since its possible completions have conditional probability at most one. Those normalizers may depend on the start, and their uniform lower bound is enough. A fixed initial prefix changes only a constant.

**Projection and measure.** At fixed $n,s$, $C_n=2^{e_n}C_{n-1}+3^{n-1}$ and $C_{n-1}$ is odd. Consequently $v_2(C_n-3^{n-1})=e_n$ for $n\ge2$, and $s$ recovers the last undetermined first gap. The clock-carry map is injective. For an individual ternary cell and clock, carries occupy one residue modulo $3^n$. From $C_n/3^n=(1/3)\sum_i2^{d_i-d_n}$ their multiplicity is at most $1+O(n)2^{f n^{1/3}-d_n}$. The conditioning probability for one such word has the complementary term $h d_n$. Since $h>\log2$, their product costs at most $\exp(hf n^{1/3}+o(n^{1/3}))$ beyond the common stage cost. Summing over the $O(n)$ possible clocks preserves that exponent. The ternary image is measurable (the critical script space is compact since every depth bounds every gap there, and the coding is continuous). A cell mass bound controls arbitrary covering sets by the ultrametric diameter property. The gauge-ratio argument supplies infinity above the threshold.
