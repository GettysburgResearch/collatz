### H and the local valuation

For each cyclic phase I recovered its fixed rational using the affine map $(3^p x+A)/2^h$. The denominator $2^h-3^p$ is odd and nonzero even at $p=0$. Rotating a word and applying its first affine branch gives the next fixed point by uniqueness. Since all phase outputs are dyadic integers, the indicated branch is its genuine parity branch. A first wrong choice at zero-based position $j$ takes an integral dyadic state to valuation $-1$. Every remaining prescribed affine branch decreases the valuation by one. Clearing $2^d$ and subtracting an integral endpoint reference therefore gives valuation exactly $j$. When all choices match, the residual is a finite phase difference times $2^dD$. For example two different terminal choices after the all-odd phase $-1$ can give $v_2(c)>d$, which explains the fixed additive slack.

On a putative countersequence to H, the elementary bound on $x$ and the failure of every growing endpoint constant force $L\to\infty$. Merging holes across bounded internal gaps increases the exceptional clock by $O(1)$ on the selected subsequence. Recomputing the merged physical defect restores its valuation bound. Removing zeros can only increase separations between the retained blocks. At infinity and 3 use coordinates; at 2 use the sum in place of the source coordinate. Multiplication over the three places removes the powers of 2 and 3 in each switch term. The surviving factor is bounded by $2^{-L}|Y|\prod|c_j|$, which is exponentially small against an at-most-exponential integer vector norm. Bilu's Theorem 2.3 explicitly allows integral vectors. Alternatively, dividing the vector by $g$ multiplies the whole local product by $H_S(g)^{-d}\le1$ in dimension $d$, and decreases its norm. [Bilu's formulation, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.2098)

The recurring rational hyperplane for H must involve the source. For a source-free relation, the first nonzero fixed multiple of a retained term has uniquely minimal dyadic valuation, even after the bounded shifts introduced by fixed rational coefficients. After eliminating the source, the first surviving term must end within $O(1)$ of $L$, since the left side has valuation at least $L$. No further retained term can then follow it. Its real size after division by $2^L$ is $O((3/2)^{d_j})$, giving the claimed endpoint alternative. Zero source, zero endpoint shift, and no retained holes are each handled before this argument. For all-odd $x=2^L-1$, both the dyadic scale and $q=3^L-1$ are literal, giving the stated sharpness example.

### A and the counting hypothesis

For an actual subpath the affine offset divided by its endpoint is $1-\exp(-\sum_{\rm odd}\log(1+1/(3x_j)))$. This is at most the entire late reciprocal tail. Also the following suffix multiplier times its initial ordinary height is at most the final height, by positivity of the suffix offset. These two facts give $|X_j|/(D2^Lq)\le2\max|Z|/m_i+e_i$, while $X_0/(D2^Lq)\to1$. The bound is uniform over the window length and the original, unmerged holes.

The cited T-A3-651 at ab7a62cbbb83860ea9436c5d3cf6cf7ce501066a indeed treats arbitrary finite simple ordinary paths, including their terminal-boundary correction. A shorter independent way to obtain the summability needed here is to take $k=\lfloor(\log_2 X)/2\rfloor$ steps at each visited value $\le X$. Windows with at most $3k/5$ odd steps have distinct endpoints at most $X(3^{3/5}/2)^k+(3/2)^k$, by the affine carry bound. There are $O(2^{kH_2(3/5)})$ parity words of larger weight, each accounting for at most $X/2^k+1$ sources up to $X$. Since $3^{3/5}<2$ and $H_2(3/5)<1$, this gives $O(X^{1-\eta})$ for some $\eta>0$ on an infinite simple orbit, sufficient for dyadic reciprocal summation. This independent derivation does not claim the cited exponent or its constant.

Under a fixed fractional failure of A, each switch contributes its integer prime-to-$S$ part exactly, after the monomial cancellation. The integer source at all places except its replaced 2-form contributes at most $|Y|$. The raw source bound under failure and the carry/clock bounds give exponential upper height at every rank. This time every fixed relation has **zero** source coefficient by real dominance. Eliminating a switch preserves the dyadic form's leading coefficient one, every omitted prime-to-$S$ factor is at least one, and the new form coefficients are fixed on the selected subsequence. Iterating gives $2^L\mid Y$ at rank one, contradicting the raw source bound. The order of these eliminations is logically different from H. For the refined entrance charge the raw-height assumption remains necessary in the proof. The charge is bounded by $|Y|=O_W(x_i+1)$ and may exceed $x_i+1$ by a fixed factor. The corrected writing says this explicitly. For moving libraries the two subexponential/real-smallness hypotheses control $D_i,\max|Z_i|$ in exactly the two uses above; the linear forms themselves remain fixed at each step.

The suffix-minimum bound uses $q\ge a$ and $L\sim\lambda\log_2(a+1)$. Equating the two branch bounds yields $1/(\log_2 3+\log_2(3/2))$. On full prefixes the discarded initial $\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$ clocks cost $o(N)$ in logarithmic height. For a Hausdorff limit, a fixed finite relatively open cover of the compact limit contains all sufficiently late discrete sets, and rounding costs $O(1)$ per interval. The inference controls macroscopic covering length. It supplies neither a positive density for the mismatch bits nor an upper bound on the number of exceptional intervals on an arbitrary orbit.
