The adaptive pool theorem, nonperiodic density-one case, bilateral statement and nonresonance refinement pass independently. This concerns one fixed ordinary injective source, with the adaptive pool fixed across its whole length-$N$ prefix. A fresh pool per clock would not be covered.

* For two hits of the same shift $z$ and prime power, the exact subtraction is $2^\ell Y_j(z)-3^bY_i(z)=D(3^bz+C_w-2^\ell z)$. This is an integer identity even when the prime is 2, 3, or divides the common denominator. The uniform bound $|E|\le C_Z3^\ell$ follows from $0\le C_w\le3^\ell-2^\ell$. If $E=0$, reversing the specified affine letters from the dyadic integer $z$ makes each stage integral with precisely the specified parity. The word is therefore a genuine return to $z$, with length a multiple of its native period.
* For a bad departure, some pair from the single bounded pool and the fixed shifts has $v_p(Y)\log_2p\ge\epsilon H/(2r)$, using the uniform asymptotic $\log_2|Y|/h\to1$. Rounding the corresponding valuation threshold up is valid at arbitrary prime size, including primes larger than $2^H$. Two successive such hits separated by less than $cH$ have $p^k>|E|$ unless $E=0$, uniformly for fixed parameters. A length-$O_K(H)$ interval thus produces only boundedly many exact periodic return groups per pair, including singleton hits. Marking the last departure of each group by the appropriate one-letter template and splitting all overlaps gives a bounded number of literal template/exception intervals; the unmarked length is at most the number of good clocks.
* The randomized height bands are chosen after the fixed trajectory prefix. A change contributes expected starting log-height at most $h_t|\ln h_t-\ln h_{t-1}|/\ln K\le 2/\ln K$. Capping each uninterrupted band after $\lceil K^2H\rceil$ clocks charges at most its preceding full chunk length divided by $K$. The sum of all starting charges is bounded by (C3), while the number of pieces is $o(N)$ at fixed $K$ because the individual heights tend to infinity along the one simple source. Thus short pieces have negligible total length and bounded-start pieces have uniformly bounded lengths. The window theorem is invoked only with fixed $K,\epsilon,r,Z$ and its fixed finite template library and budget, through its **length-only** consequence. Its Diophantine set of places is not varied with the user's adaptive prime pool. Summing the resulting inequalities and sending $K\to\infty$ gives exactly $\beta=\log_3 2$.
* For nonperiodic $z$ every group is a singleton by the true-return argument; boundedly many per piece give $o(N)$. For a periodic shift, removing a fixed number of clocks at each group's ends also costs only $o(N)$. Each remaining bad hit then sits on an exact bilateral native-word segment for its marked phase. The known dyadic finite cylinder enforces the stated forward precision. A nonzero period has a 3-integral rational phase (its denominator divides $2^\ell-3^b$ with $b>0$); thus forward contraction of congruences from the past piece gives every fixed ternary precision. The all-zero period lacks this argument as recorded. At positive lower growth one can instead discard $t<\delta N$ and cover the remaining clocks at fixed scale $H\asymp_\delta N$. The odd-frequency limit implies $\sup_{j\le N}|p_j-dj|=o(N)$, hence any of the boundedly many periodic spans with frequency different from $d$ has length $o(N)$. Removing $\delta$ proves density one.
* A consequence for the compulsory height law follows at fast horizons. Up to native time $N$, write $A_N$ for the cumulative coefficient-record height. Each terminal record contributes at most $\log(3/2)$ in natural logarithms. Therefore at horizons with $A_N/N\to v>(1-\beta)\log(3/2)$, the limiting lower fraction of this weight at simultaneously prime-rough native record phases is at least $1-(1-\beta)\log(3/2)/v$. This uses the count of **all** nonrough departures as an upper bound on nonrough record marks. It does not extend a positive fraction to arbitrarily slow escape or exclude statistical accumulation at signed rational phases.
