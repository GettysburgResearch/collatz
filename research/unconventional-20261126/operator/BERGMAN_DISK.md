# The spectrum and Fredholm essential spectrum of the classical Bergman transfer

**Proposed theorem O12.** For Béhani's shortcut transfer A on the classical Bergman quotient by degrees 0, 1 and 2, write R for the spectral radius bounded in O9. On every ordinary weak merge component, with the quotient convention in the basin of 1, both the full spectrum and the Fredholm essential spectrum are the closed disk of radius R. The same holds on the full quotient. The proof identifies the shape and component independence while O9 gives the numerical bounds on R.

## Arithmetic copies of the norm

In the normalized H=Bergman coefficient basis let S=A* be the forward weighted shift along inverse edges. It sends each basis vector at a to a positive weighted sum of its children j with T(j)=a; the squared edge weight is (j+1)/(a+1). Distinct parents have disjoint children. O9 and Béhani's Proposition 4.2 identify the squared global kth power norm as the maximum of the limiting slope sums S_k on residues modulo 3^k. A multiple of 3 has only the even inverse edge at every depth, giving S_k=2^k. Some unit residue has S_k>2^k for k≥1 because the Haar mean is (20/9)^k.

The powers of 2 traverse every unit modulo 3^k. Indeed 2 has order 2 modulo 3 and 4=1+3. If u=1+3^d c with d≥1 and 3 not dividing c, binomial expansion gives v_3(u^3-1)=d+1. It follows that 4 has order 3^(k-1) modulo 3^k and 2 has order 2·3^(k-1). For any ordinary unit vertex b, the descendants 2^j b realize every maximizing residue at arbitrarily large heights.

Consider any rooted inverse subtree below an ordinary unit b whose descendants form a tree, in the quotient graph. Such subtrees are constructed in every component below. It contains every inverse descendant of each of its vertices. For a fixed k the norm of S^k on this subtree is at most its global norm; disjoint kth-generation descendant sets give the usual squared norm as the supremum of the weighted column sums. At targets 2^j b in a maximizing residue these column sums approach S_k by the ordinary limiting slopes in O9. Thus **every power norm** on this single subtree equals the global power norm, and its spectral radius is R.

## The rooted shift has disk spectrum

The finite levels of a rooted subtree grade its Hilbert space, and the restricted forward shift W raises the level by one. Multiplication by exp(i theta times level) implements circle symmetry. The shift is injective for every nonzero weight system here by the positive even child and disjoint children. W−zI is also injective at every z≠0: inspect the root and then successive levels in the homogeneous equation.

Suppose a circle of radius s strictly between 0 and R were in the resolvent set. On that circle let x=(zI−W)^(-1)y. Write P_N for projection onto levels 0 through N and W_N for the truncation there. Since W raises level, (zI−W_N)P_N x=P_N y. The finite truncation is nilpotent, so its resolvent is the finite sum of W_N^j/z^(j+1), j=0,...,N. Integrating counterclockwise around the circle gives P_N times the Riesz projection equal to P_N. This holds for every N, hence the Riesz projection equals the identity. The spectral decomposition by that contour would put the whole spectrum inside the circle, contradicting R>s. The root coordinate shows 0 is in the spectrum. Circle symmetry, closedness and the spectral radius now give precisely the full disk.

For z in this disk, W*−conjugate(z)I fails to be bounded below. If it were bounded below, the Hilbert closed-range theorem and its trivial kernel would give closed and dense range for W−zI. The preceding injectivity of W−zI would make it invertible, a contradiction. Each such subtree therefore supplies unit vectors with arbitrarily small adjoint residual.

## Disjoint forks give the essential disk

Every ordinary component contains a unit vertex a: from a multiple of 3 repeatedly divide by 2 until odd, after which the odd shortcut is a unit. Its infinite even-inverse spine is a,2a,4a,... . For infinitely many j, the spine vertex 2^j a is 8 modulo 9. Its odd child b_j=(2^(j+1)a−1)/3 is then a unit. After omitting a finite initial part, these side branches are disjoint rooted trees. To check this, positive shortcut outdegree is one and a component has at most one forward cycle. If a is off cycle its ancestors cannot lie on a cycle; each side child's first meeting with the spine is its own parent. If a is on a cycle, discard the finitely many spine or side vertices on that cycle; after a side child reaches the initial vertex a it can visit only the cycle. In the basin of 1 take a=4, whose orbit stops in the quotient. Equivalently, one can begin in every component at an off-cycle unit vertex, available arbitrarily far along its even-inverse spine, and then the descendant graph is immediately a tree.

Inside each side tree double its unit root until reaching a vertex v=8 modulo 9. Its two children b=2v and c=(2v−1)/3 are both units, and their descendant subtrees are disjoint. On each of these two subtrees choose a unit vector with internal adjoint residual at most epsilon at the chosen scalar lambda in the disk. Extending each by zero to the full component introduces just one further output, at their common parent v. There are two coefficients and only one complex linear cancellation equation for these boundary values. Choose coefficients of Euclidean norm one solving it. The resulting vector is normalized (the supports are disjoint), and the squared norm of its remaining residual is at most epsilon squared. Across the infinitely many side trees these vectors have disjoint supports. Taking epsilon to zero gives an orthonormal approximate null sequence for A−lambda I in the component.

A Fredholm operator is bounded below on the orthogonal complement of its finite-dimensional kernel. The projections of an orthonormal sequence into a finite-dimensional subspace tend to zero, contradicting Fredholmness here. The component and full Fredholm essential spectra therefore contain the entire disk. Their spectra are contained in that disk by the global power norm bound. This proves all assertions.

Source: V. Béhani, *Linear dynamics of an operator associated to the Collatz map*, Prop. 2.1 and 4.2, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.03203>. All other arguments above are independent of any positive-integer orbit classification.
