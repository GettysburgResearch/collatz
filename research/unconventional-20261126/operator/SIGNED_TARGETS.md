# The mixed root's next slope and the rational unbounded-slope targets

**Status.** PROPOSED, with proofs. The signed finite-path input below is credited to PR210. Its critical all-prefix rational trace is a separate set.

## OP-D4. The isolated mixed minimum is sharp

On the mixed component of $(-1,1)$ in [OP-D2](HYBRID_DESCENT.md), the least slope product of a nonempty reduced walk from the root is exactly $4/3$. In particular the finite ordinary copies in OP-D3 defeat even a relaxed search for a nonempty reduced walk with product strictly below $4/3$ in the stated radius.

Write a reduced walk as $j$ forward letters, then $k$ inverse letters. If $k\le j$ and $e$ of its inverse letters are $E$, its product is $(3/2)^{j-k}3^e$. This is at least $3/2$ except when $j=k,e=0$, in which case the entire path retraces and the reduced representative is empty. If $k>j$, after the first $j$ inverse letters the product is $3^e$ and the second coordinate is a positive ordinary integer $v$, by OP-D2. At the final endpoint $(x,z)$ we have $x\le-1$ and $z$ a positive integer. A total slope strictly below $3/2$ forces $z-x<3$, hence $z=1$. The final $\ell=k-j>0$ inverse steps now give a genuine ordinary segment read backwards from $v=T^\ell(1)$ to 1. Its inverse product is $(4/3)^{\ell/2}$ for even $\ell$ and $(2/3)(4/3)^{(\ell-1)/2}$ for odd $\ell$, using the two edges of the ordinary cycle. If $e\ge1$ the full product is at least 2. If $e=0$, the matched forward and inverse prefix cancels entirely, so a reduced walk has $j=0$, $v=1$, and $\ell$ even. The smallest possibility $\ell=2$ is realized by $E,O$ with endpoint $(-5/3,1)$. Preservation of adjacent edge cancellations under the congruence comparison follows since the two inverse letters are distinguished by the parity of their child.

## OP-T3. Rational points of the unrestricted exceptional set

Let $y\in\mathbb Q\cap\mathbb Z_3$ have reduced denominator $2^e d$ with $d$ odd. Then $B(y)=\infty$ if and only if some legal inverse script of length exactly $e$ reaches a negative rational cycle vertex of the ordinary signed shortcut map on $\mathbb Q\cap\mathbb Z_2$. In particular a negative integer has $B=\infty$ exactly when it lies on a negative cycle. For example the positive rational $1/8$ has $B=\infty$: $O,E,E$ take it successively to $-1/4,-1/2,-1$ and the all-odd negative cycle can then repeat. These identities do not classify the negative cycles or give a general cycle-membership algorithm.

Indeed each inverse step lowers the binary denominator exponent by at least one until it reaches zero. The ternary guard ensures division by 3 cancels in the numerator when the denominator is prime to 3. After $e$ steps we have finitely many possible aligned roots $z\in d^{-1}\mathbb Z$ with odd denominator, and all deeper paths stay there. Both-adic integrality now makes all the inverse paths genuine reversed signed parity paths. Every finite prefix before alignment has a finite slope, and thereafter unboundedness can be tested separately at the finitely many aligned roots.

For fixed odd $d$ write the signed integer numerator $u=d z$. The forward numerator maps on even and odd inputs are $u/2$ and $(3u+d)/2$. On every simple segment their product of principal slopes is at most $K_d(1+|u_{\rm end}|)/(1+|u_{\rm start}|)$. Here is a derivation using the signed reciprocal estimate rather than a positivity assumption. At odd departures the ratio
$$
 \frac{1+|(3u+d)/2|}{(3/2)(1+|u|)}
$$
differs from one by $O_d((1+|u|)^{-1})$, is positive, and is bounded away from zero at the finitely many small $u$. For even departures the analogous ratio is $(2+|u|)/(1+|u|)\ge1$, so only the odd departures can cost in the lower direction. On a simple segment the odd numerator magnitudes on either sign give a simple segment of the positive-magnitude accelerated map with parameter $d$ or $-d$, apart from at most boundedly many terminal or sign-changing odd departures. There is at most one forward sign crossing, from negative to nonnegative. The signed path count (S) of [PR210, section 2](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/592328fecf8ea2cf41736294856d95f890a1ba98/research/critical-record-rational-rigidity-20261125/README.md) bounds the number of its odd magnitudes below $X$ by $O_d(X^{39/40})$. Summing dyadically gives a uniform reciprocal sum on these odd vertices. The product of the displayed distortion factors is therefore bounded below by a positive constant depending only on $d$, as needed.

For a fixed target $z$ the last inequality uniformly bounds all simple incoming slopes. Repeats can occur only if the target is itself cyclic, by deterministic forward iteration. Then an arbitrary path consists of a first-arrival simple path and extra forward laps at the target. A nonzero cycle is sign-constant, with at least one odd departure, so its positive affine carry gives lap slope above one for a negative vertex and below one for a positive vertex. The zero cycle has slope $1/2$. Exactly the negative case admits arbitrarily large incoming slopes. The rational classification for $B$ allows arbitrarily negative log-slope dips, whereas PR210's critical trace imposes its prefix inequalities throughout.
