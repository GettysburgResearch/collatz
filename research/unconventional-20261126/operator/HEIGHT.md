# A global ordinary height barrier and its failed reverse

**Status.** PROPOSED, with exact finite inequalities for the upper barrier. Write $h$ for the inverse-tree first-parent probability, $f(n)=G(n,2)/G(2,2)$ on positive units, and $\beta$ for its doubling-ray exponent. The [construction and proofs](IMPEDANCE.md) fix these normalizations.

## OP-H1. A nearly optimal power upper bound on every ordinary unit

Put $s=9691/25000=0.38764$. Then **every** positive unit satisfies
$$
 f(n)\le 2n^{-s}.
$$
Define $s_*=\sup\{t\ge0:\sup_{3\nmid n} n^t f(n)<\infty\}$. The ordinary good doubling ray from 2 in OP-I3 supplies the opposite constraint, so
$$
 0.38764\le s_*\le\beta\le0.387892 .
$$
Neither equality $s_*=\beta$ nor an upper bound at that endpoint is asserted. OP-I2 and $Q(n,2)\asymp f(n)\log(1/f(n))$ for small positive $f$ also give $Q(n,2)\ll n^{-s}\log(2n)$, since $v\log(1/v)$ increases for small $v$.

Here is a finite certificate for the first inequality. On the 1,458 unit residues modulo $3^7$, let $a_r$ be the OP-I3 rational **upper** bound for $h$ there. Division by 2 in a residue subscript uses its inverse modulo $3^7$. The recorded positive integer weights $w_r$, reproducibly generated by the checker, satisfy in every row
$$
 w_r\ge a_r\max\left\{\,b_0 w_{r/2},\ b_1 w_{(3r+1)/2}\,\right\},
 \qquad b_0\ge2^s,\quad b_1\ge(2/3)^s .
$$
The rational upper multipliers have denominator $10^{12}$ and numerators $1308251578800$ and $854554930666$. The checker verifies each by raising positive integers to the powers $25000$ and $9691$ and clearing denominators. The weights have minimum $10^{12}$ and maximum $1379275763162$. Starting all entries at $10^{12}$, take the maximum with the ceiling of the displayed rational row until the integer array stabilizes. The code requires stability by 2,500 sweeps and independently checks every inequality on the resulting array. The actual fixed point was reached on sweep 972. The decimal proposal of candidate rational multipliers plays no part in the exact verification. [Checker](controls.py) · [Exact receipt](CONTROLS.json)

For an off-cycle ordinary child $y$ of a unit parent $x=Ty$, $y/x=2$ for an even child and $y/x<2/3$ for an odd child. Hence
$$
 h(y)(y/x)^s w_{x\bmod3^7}\le w_{y\bmod3^7}.
$$
Multiplication along the physical path of a good unit $n\ge3$ down to 2 gives $f(n)\le(2/n)^s w_n/w_2$. The numerical rational bounds on $b_0$ and the weight ratio give $2^s\max w/\min w<2$ by an integer multiplication. The values at 1 and 2 satisfy the stated bound directly, and $f=0$ on every other component.

The same telescope is uniform in the *starting target*. For an off-cycle unit $a$ and its ordinary descendants $n$, the product $f_a(n)$ of the $h$ values from $n$ to $a$ (with $f_a(a)=1$) satisfies $f_a(n)\le(\max w/\min w)(a/n)^s$. The mass cut of OP-I4, now rooted at $a$, has absolute constants because $C(a)\in[1/7,1]$. Taking $t=(\max w/\min w)(a/X)^s<1$ gives $\gg(X/a)^s$ distinct unit predecessors of $a$ below $X$. For a cyclic unit $a$ at least one of its two depth-four unit predecessors is off its cycle and at most $16a$; use that predecessor as the new root. Thus the lower count applies for every unit target once $X/a$ exceeds an absolute constant. The exponent is weaker than classical dedicated predecessor-count estimates. The purpose of the barrier is the nearly sharp global bound on the marked Green entry.

## OP-H2. The reverse one-step barrier is impossible

A strict positive bounded function $u$ satisfying $u(n)\le h(n)u(Tn)$ off a finite boundary, with suitable boundary values, would be a candidate for a lower comparison along finite good paths. No such one-step inequality can be supplied at all sufficiently large good units by a positive function comparable there to a nonincreasing radial envelope. Specifically, assume $0<c\,v(n)\le u(n)\le C\,v(n)$ on good units for a positive nonincreasing function $v$ of the numerical integer. Use the arbitrarily large good all-odd $k$-step prefixes from OP-I3. Every point on such a path exceeds the boundary; writing $y=T^k n\ge n$, the proposed inequalities give
$$
 c\,v(n)\le u(n)\le (7/8)^k u(y)\le (7/8)^k C\,v(n).
$$
Large odd $k$ contradict this. This includes every bounded positive finite-residue multiplier of $n^{-t}$ for $t\ge0$, even if its moduli involve other primes. For a multiplier using only $2^a3^b$, one step already suffices: choose large good $n\equiv-1\pmod{2^{a+1}3^b}$ that is a unit (increasing the exponent of 3 to one if needed). Both $n$ and $Tn$ represent the same multiplier class, $Tn>n$, and $h(n)<1$.

This refutes a proposed **local sufficient inequality**. A polynomial lower bound for the actual $f$ would have a different quantifier and remains open. The upper barrier above is consistent with zeros on an unknown component.

The same obstruction reaches adaptive blocks of limited length. Suppose each sufficiently large good unit is assigned a positive length $\ell(n)$ and the lower comparison is required after multiplying the $\ell(n)$ actual edge weights. For any fixed $c<1/\log3$, even the upper allowance $\ell(n)\le c\log\log n$ eventually is impossible for a function comparable to a nonincreasing envelope there. For odd $k$ put $j=3^{k-1}$ and take the explicit good source
$$
 n_k=2^k(2^j+1)/3^k-1 .
$$
The quotient $(2^{3^{k-1}}+1)/3^k$ is an integer congruent to one modulo three: raising the preceding power of two $z$ to its cube multiplies the numerator by $z^2-z+1$, and $(z^2-z+1)/3\equiv1\bmod3$ for $z\equiv-1\bmod3$. Hence $n_k$ is a positive unit for odd $k$. It has $k$ consecutive odd steps ending at $2^j$, and $\log\log(T^i n_k)=k\log3+O(1)$ uniformly for $0\le i\le k$. Every allowed block starting in this prefix therefore has length at most $(c\log3+o(1))k$. Starting at $n_k$, use consecutive blocks up to the last endpoint still in the prefix; at least $(1-c\log3-o(1))k$ steps have elapsed, since the next block would exceed the prefix. If a block ends exactly at the end, take all $k$ steps. The endpoint is larger than $n_k$, so the same contradiction follows. This includes every bounded-depth selector and proves a lower cost for this particular proof strategy.

## OP-H3. A sharper error term along doubling rays

With the executed finite inequalities below, the error in OP-I3 improves to $O(k^{1/6})$, uniformly over starting units. This refinement uses no ordinary height-return hypothesis.

Write $m=7$, $q=19/40$, $\lambda=29/25$. In this section let $a(x)$ be the **square** of the certified upper bound for $h$ on $x\bmod3^m$. Write $E x=2x$, $O x=(2x-1)/3$ when $x$ belongs to $B$, and $P y=(3y+1)/2$ on ternary units. The finite check constructs strictly positive weights $w,\psi$ on the $3^m$ unit classes with
$$
 a(x)\big[w(E x)+q^{-1}1_B(x)\max w(O x)\big]<w(x),
 \qquad
 a(y/2)\psi(y/2)+\lambda^{-1}a(P y)\psi(P y)<\psi(y).
$$
The maximum in the first inequality is over the three lifts compatible with the residue of $x$. Values of $O$ on the other classes are omitted. The maximal row ratios are at most $0.989946$ and $0.999702$, respectively. The verifier constructs each integer vector by 160 normalized positive iterations with floors and tests every displayed strict inequality by rational arithmetic. Only positivity and the final inequalities are needed; the iterations propose a reproducible witness. [Exact certificate](controls.py)

For a cylinder $x\bmod3^r$ write $\omega_r(x)$ for its oscillation of $h$. For $r>m$, subtract the two Riccati equations for any pair in the cylinder. The difference of reciprocals supplies a factor $h(x_1)h(x_2)\le a(x)$, giving
$$
 \omega_r(x)\le a(x)\big[\omega_r(E x)+1_B(x)\omega_{r-1}(O x)\big].
$$
Set $S_r=\max_x\omega_r(x)/w(x)$ and evaluate at a maximizing residue. The first strict weight inequality forces $S_r\le q S_{r-1}$: if $S_r>qS_{r-1}$, substituting both maxima into the last display gives $\omega_r(x)<w(x)S_r$. Thus $\max\omega_r=O(q^r)$.

For a complementary summed bound put $d(y)=\psi(y)-a(y/2)\psi(y/2)>0$. Multiply the oscillation inequality by $\psi(x)$ and sum over level-$r$ unit classes. The even map permutes these classes. Also $O$ maps the branching residues at level $r$ bijectively to all unit residues at level $r-1$; the inverse class at level $r$ is $(3y+1)/2$. Moving the even sum to the left and using the second certificate gives
$$
 \sum_x d(x)\omega_r(x)
 \le\sum_y a(P y)\psi(P y)\omega_{r-1}(y)
 \le\lambda\sum_y d(y)\omega_{r-1}(y).
$$
The left and right sums are over levels $r$ and $r-1$, respectively. Hence $\sum_x\omega_r(x)=O(\lambda^r)$, since the fixed finite vector $d$ has a positive minimum.

For $F=-\log h$ the same two oscillation bounds hold up to a constant, as $h\ge1/2$. In the martingale difference decomposition of OP-I3, level $r$ contributes at most $O(kq^r)$ to a sum over $k$ doublings. It also contributes at most $O(\lambda^r)$: subtract complete periods, then bound the remainder by its sum of absolute values over all residue classes. Each level-$r$ difference is bounded by the oscillation of its parent cylinder and each parent has three lifts. Summing the smaller bounds on both sides of $R=\lfloor\log k/\log(\lambda/q)\rfloor$ yields $O(k^\delta)$, $\delta=\log\lambda/\log(\lambda/q)$. Finally $19\cdot29^5<40\cdot25^5$ gives $q\lambda^5<1$ and $\delta<1/6$. Bounded discrepancy at exponent $\beta$ remains open.
