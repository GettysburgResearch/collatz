# The norm of an ordinary Collatz operator on a single component

**PROPOSED theorem with a written proof.** For the shortcut map, every operator generated in norm by the two branch maps, their adjoints and periodic coefficient multipliers has its full operator norm on every weak component. It has that same essential norm on every component, including the basin of 1. The theorem uses the ordinary arithmetic progression theorem of Kenneth M. Monks (2006). It requires no conjecture about convergence, predecessor density or trace onset.

## The algebra and the conclusion

Put $T(2n)=n$ and $T(2n+1)=3n+2$ on the positive integers. Two points are in the same component if their forward orbits intersect. These are the weak components of the directed graph of $T$. Each component is infinite, since it contains the dyadic multiples of each of its elements.

On counting-space $H=\ell^2(\mathbb N_{>0})$, define two partial isometries by
$$
 (V_i u)(x)=1_{x\bmod 2=i}u(Tx),\qquad i\in\{0,1\}.
$$
Each restricted branch is injective. Its adjoint reads its unique predecessor of type $i$ when one exists and returns zero otherwise. Let $\mathcal A$ be the concrete unital C*-algebra generated by $V_0,V_1$ and all multiplication operators by bounded periodic sequences. Every component $C$ reduces every generator, so restriction to $H_C=\ell^2(C)$ defines a unital representation. Write $A_C$ for the restriction and $\|B\|_{\rm ess}$ for distance to the compact operators on the specified Hilbert space.

**Theorem O1.** For every $A\in\mathcal A$ and every component $C$,
$$
               \|A\|=\|A_C\|=\|A_C\|_{\rm ess}.             \tag{1}
$$
In particular, the full and essential spectra of $A_C$ equal the spectrum of $A$ on $H$, including for nonnormal $A$. This applies to the named generated algebra. The algebra of all finite-propagation operators, for example, also contains individual vertex projections and fails (1).

### 1. Remove the cycle obstruction without assuming an orbit classification

Fix a source component $D$ and a finite *-polynomial $P$ in the generators. Let $r$ be the maximum number of branch or adjoint factors in a monomial. The finitely many periodic diagonals in $P$ have a common period $d$, with $d=1$ allowed.

Give $D\times\mathbb Z$ the directed edges
$$
                       (x,j)\longrightarrow(Tx,j+1).
$$
Branch types and periodic diagonals use the first coordinate. Each connected component is a tree. Indeed it has no directed cycle since the second coordinate increases on every arrow. A finite undirected simple cycle in a graph of outdegree one must be directed: its edges require as many outgoing arrows as there are vertices, forcing one along the cycle at each vertex. Loops and opposite parallel arrows cannot occur in the lift. Both ordinary arrows of the cycle $1\to2\to1$ are retained.

For a finitely supported unit vector $u$ on $D$, give $u(x)/\sqrt{2m+1}$ to each $(x,j)$ with $|j|\le m$ and zero elsewhere. Call this unit vector $v_m$. For $m\ge r$ and $|j|\le m-r$, all intermediate clocks in evaluating a word stay in the chosen interval. Thus on each such layer the lift $\widetilde P v_m$ equals $P_Du/\sqrt{2m+1}$ pointwise, with all coefficients and cancellations retained. In particular,
$$
 \|\widetilde P v_m\|\ \ge
   \sqrt{(2m+1-2r)/(2m+1)}\,\|P_Du\|.                         \tag{2}
$$
The lift operator reduces its tree components. On at least one of the finitely many trees meeting the support of $v_m$, the output-to-input norm ratio is at least the right side of (2). Restrict and normalize there to obtain a finite unit vector $v$.

### 2. Realize a finite lifted tree arbitrarily far out in any ordinary component

Choose a root $(a,j_0)$ of this tree. Use graph distance on undirected edges. The support of $v$ is contained in a ball of some radius $R_0$. Choose an integer $R>R_0+2r+1$. All balls used here are finite, since each vertex has at most three neighbors. Each vertex $(x,j)$ of the radius-$R$ ball has a unique reduced path from the root. Compose that path's rational affine maps, from the list
$$
  X/2,\qquad (3X+1)/2,\qquad 2X,\qquad (2X-1)/3,
$$
to obtain $F_{x,j}(X)$. Its value at $a$ is $x$. Its positive slope is $3^s 2^{-(j-j_0)}$, where $s\in\mathbb Z$ is the signed count of odd edges (forward counts positive). Distinct lifted vertices have distinct affine maps. Equality of maps would give equality of the underlying integers at $a$, and equality of the slopes gives equality of clocks by their dyadic valuations. The two coordinates would then coincide.

Choose $b>a$ with $b\equiv a\pmod{d6^{R+1}}$. For each path of length at most $R$, its slope has denominator dividing $6^R$. Therefore $F_{x,j}(b)-x$ is an integer divisible by $6d$. It is nonnegative because the slope is positive. Along every intended path the substituted values consequently have the required parity, ternary residue and periodic label. The outgoing branch is determined by parity. The even predecessor always exists; the odd predecessor exists exactly at vertices congruent to 2 modulo 3. These checks apply also to rejected branch choices. At an interior vertex its entire list of legal labeled neighbors is therefore reproduced. Backtracking uses mutually inverse affine maps. On a tree every edge is on the unique reduced path to one of its endpoints, so these path formulas agree with each edge.

There are only finitely many pairwise equality roots among the distinct affine maps of this ball. Above those roots, the substitution is injective. The copied ball has no unintended identifications, and the neighbor check handles extra potential arrows at every vertex of radius less than $R$. Escaping through the outer boundary cannot affect evaluation of a word on the supported input: outputs are within radius $R_0+r$ and their entire evaluation paths stay within radius $R_0+2r<R$. Consequently transplanting $v$ to the substituted vertices, with identical coefficients and zero elsewhere, gives a unit vector $v_b$ satisfying
$$
                        \|P_C v_b\|=\|\widetilde P v\|          \tag{3}
$$
whenever $b\in C$. More explicitly, every input-supported term in a word ends within the copied $r$-neighborhood of the support by following the adjoint path backward. All output entries there can then be evaluated inside the copied ball, including the zero contributions from inadmissible branches.

Monks's Theorem 1.1 states that every progression $A+B\mathbb N$ with positive step meets every merge component for this same shortcut map. Apply it to arbitrarily distant tails of the single progression for $b$. It supplies arbitrarily large such $b$ in any specified ordinary component $C$. Every one of the finitely many affine maps has positive slope. Hence successive choices may be made so that the whole copied balls are pairwise disjoint and move beyond every finite set. The resulting unit vectors are orthonormal. This is an ordinary construction, distinct from assigning the root a profinite or 2-adic value. [Monks, §1 and Theorem 1.1](https://monks.scranton.edu/content/pubs/SufficiencyRev4.pdf)

### 3. Norm and spectrum

A compact operator on $H_C$ takes the orthonormal sequence $v_b$ to a sequence of norm tending to zero. Equations (2) and (3) yield
$$
           \|P_C\|_{\rm ess}\ge
           \sqrt{(2m+1-2r)/(2m+1)}\,\|P_Du\|.
$$
Let $m\to\infty$, use density of finite vectors, and take the supremum over the source component $D$. This gives $\|P_C\|_{\rm ess}\ge\|P\|$, since $P$ is the direct sum of the component operators. The reverse inequalities in (1) follow from restriction and the quotient norm. Norm approximation proves (1) for every $A\in\mathcal A$. The map from $\mathcal A$ into the Calkin algebra on $H_C$ obtained by restriction followed by quotient is a unital isometric *-homomorphism. Spectral permanence for unital C*-subalgebras gives the spectrum claim. No step takes an uncontrolled limit over test-vector support radii.

## Distance from an actual invariant label

**Theorem O2.** Let $f$ be any bounded complex label satisfying $f(Tn)=f(n)$ for every positive ordinary integer. For the corresponding diagonal multiplier,
$$
       \operatorname{dist}(M_f,\mathcal A)
          =\inf_{z\in\mathbb C}\sup_{n\ge1}|f(n)-z|.              \tag{4}
$$
In particular the distance for a nonconstant binary label is exactly $1/2$. For the indicator of failure to reach 1, the distance is zero if all starts reach 1 and exactly $1/2$ otherwise.

To prove (4), write $f_C$ for the constant value on $C$. For $A\in\mathcal A$ put $\delta=\|M_f-A\|$. By (1) applied to $A-f_C I$,
$$
                  \|A-f_C I\|=\|A_C-f_C I_C\|\le\delta
                  \quad\text{for every }C.
$$
For any unit vector $e\in H$, the one scalar $z=\langle Ae,e\rangle$ (inner product linear in the first variable) satisfies $|z-f_C|\le\delta$ for all $C$. Scalar operators give the reverse inequality.

The equality persists if an arbitrary compact correction on the full space is allowed. Compress a compact $K$ to $H_C$ by the orthogonal projection. Its compression is compact even if $K$ mixes different components. Hence $\|M_f-A-K\|\ge\|f_C I_C-A_C\|_{\rm ess}=\|f_C I-A\|$ for every $C$. The same scalar argument proves (4) with $\mathcal A+\mathcal K(H)$ on the left.

The commutant makes the distinction explicit. Any bounded operator commuting with every periodic diagonal is diagonal: separate two distinct positive integers by a residue class and apply the commutation identity to their matrix coefficient. A diagonal commutes with the branch operators and their adjoints precisely when its coefficients agree across every ordinary edge. Thus $\mathcal A'$ consists exactly of the component-constant diagonals, and $\mathcal A''=\prod_C B(H_C)$ with the bounded product understood. Invariant projections are accessible in the strong operator closure. In the norm-closed algebra the only invariant diagonal multipliers are constants. Singular spectrum, essential spectrum, or an abstract norm identity for an element of this algebra alone has the same value on the known basin and the entire system.

## Polynomial height weights

On $\ell^2(\mathbb N_{>0},n^{-\sigma})$, any real $\sigma$ gives bounded branch operators. The unitary $u(n)\mapsto n^{-\sigma/2}u(n)$ to counting space turns each branch into its old partial isometry times the diagonal coefficient $(Tn/n)^{\sigma/2}$ on the source fiber. The even coefficient is $2^{-\sigma/2}$, and the odd coefficient tends to $(3/2)^{\sigma/2}$. A diagonal tending to zero is compact on counting space, and multiplying it by a bounded operator preserves compactness. Hence after quotient by compacts these generators equal the indicated nonzero constants times the unweighted branches. The same holds on every component. For any given *-polynomial in weighted branches and periodic multipliers, its **essential** norm and essential spectrum agree across all components, with the corresponding rescaled unweighted polynomial giving the common values. This extends by norm continuity to the weighted generated algebra, through its common Calkin image. The compact coefficient correction can affect full weighted norms and spectra.

## Source comparisons and the remaining global question

Monks proves the progression input. Mori's Theorem 4.2.7 gives the irreducibility criterion for two full-step branch operators. His §4.3 constructs an induced return system with two Cuntz isometries, giving a representation of the simple Cuntz algebra $\mathcal O_2$ and an irreducibility criterion in Theorem 4.3.10. For that induced algebra nonzero restricted representations are already faithful by simplicity. The present algebra has a circle of characters, as shown in the [companion structural result](OPERATOR_STRUCTURE.md), so simplicity of the induced algebra cannot justify (1). Mori later studies reducing spaces for discrete maps with branching hypotheses. Our theorem concerns norm and essential norm in a specified shortcut-map algebra with periodic diagonals. The computation of its commutant has a simpler proof because those diagonals distinguish individual integers. Cuntz's ax+b algebra and weak-containment theorems for amenable groupoids provide related settings. The proof above uses only finite clock lifts, ordinary congruences, Monks's theorem and standard Hilbert/C*-algebra facts. No identification with a full affine semigroup algebra, general simplicity theorem, or claim of priority for the underlying operator techniques is required. [Mori, arXiv:2411.08084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08084) · [Mori, arXiv:2508.05713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05713) · [Cuntz, ax+b semigroup](https://ems.press/books/ecr/60/1196)

Bell and Lagarias show in Theorem 1.2 that inverse-orbit generating functions have the unit circle as a natural boundary for every positive target outside $1,2,4,8$, and that these four exceptional targets encode the open conjecture. Neklyudov's Hardy-space fixed-point result imposes square-integrability of the coefficients. The multipliers in (4) are bounded operators without that hypothesis. PR193's symmetric Fejér norm criterion concerns Fourier representations of a label on the additive integers. Its mark for first visiting 5 has a single edge defect. The obstruction here instead compares full ordinary branch representations and applies to all bounded invariant labels.
