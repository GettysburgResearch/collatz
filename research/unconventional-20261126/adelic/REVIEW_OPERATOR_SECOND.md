## Boundaries and Hilbert spaces

On a simple undirected path a backward edge followed by a forward edge would immediately reuse the unique outgoing edge of the intervening vertex. Hence each path is a forward segment followed by a reversed forward segment, either possibly empty. After a finite cut, the prefix and suffix are actual ordinary simple paths at height at least the cut threshold. The reciprocal-moment tail O2 therefore bounds both intrinsic path lengths by the asserted uniform tail. Every high piece of a fixed component is adjacent to the deleted finite set once that set meets the component. Local finiteness gives finitely many pieces at each cutoff and finitely many positive-resistance edges at each deleted set. The latter separate different ends by positive distance. These facts give total boundedness and distinguish the ends in the metric completion. If a Cauchy sequence escapes every finite set, its tail selects nested complementary pieces, which define an end by finite branching. The two parallel edges near 1 affect neither the tail estimates nor this identification.

Finite energy bounds squared endpoint oscillation by path resistance times energy. A form-norm limit of finitely supported functions consequently vanishes at each end, since evaluating at one fixed vertex is bounded by its positive inverse square-root mass. Conversely a real finite-energy function vanishing at the ends has finite superlevel sets in each compactified component. Soft thresholding it at size $\epsilon$ gives a finite-support approximation there. The clipped remainder is 1-Lipschitz as a function of the original values, so the summable original edge energy dominates its edge energy pointwise. The vertex squares obey the analogous domination. Apply separately to real and imaginary parts and then cut off the countable component sum in the full form norm. Every doubling ray has residual resistance $n^{-\theta}/(2^\theta-1)$ at its starting vertex. A common end value therefore forces convergence to that same value in the ordinary height variable uniformly across components, with the displayed energy bound. These arguments justify the exact minimal and maximal domains in W1.

For the two specified ends in the good component, the bridge path consists of $3\cdot2^k$ down to 3, then 5 and 8, then up through the powers of two starting at 16. Its free resistance is exactly the sum of the individual resistances, since holding a unit-current path voltage constant in each side subtree gives a bounded finite-mass function for $s>1$. The geometric-series limit is the stated one. For wired ends the two disjoint doubling tails yield the shorter upper bound by Cauchy–Schwarz against their common end value. The distinction already occurs within the known component.

For $s>1$ each component constant is square integrable. A positive-mass weighted variance identity on one component $C$ gives the gap. On its mean-zero energy Hilbert space evaluation difference has squared norm at most the path metric. Bessel's inequality makes the sum of its squared values over any finite energy-orthonormal family at most $D_*$. Summing the pairwise variance identity thus gives $D_*\mu_s(C)/2$ for the Hilbert–Schmidt norm squared of the inclusion into the physical mass Hilbert space. The positive inverse is $JJ^*$ by the variational identity, so summing these bounds across components is legitimate even if the kernel itself is infinite dimensional. The finite-rank approximation uses individual low vertices and the means over their adjacent high pieces. Every entirely high component already has mean zero on the stated orthogonal complement. A high piece's local energy is bounded by the full energy there and its mass by the $s$-tail of the counting measure. Substituting these into the variance estimate gives exactly the stated squared error and rank.

The original W2 row equivalence omitted the computability of $s$. Under a membership algorithm, effective truncation of the marked mass requires both the weights and an effective tail. The positive component includes 2, so its total mass varies strictly with $s$. Even with the component known, the continuum of possible $s$ cannot all give computable values for this scalar. For fixed computable $s>1$, approximation from below to $s-1$ yields an effective positive lower bound to control the integral tail. Conversely a row oracle and the positive value at vertex 1 give a computable rational separation threshold for deciding which entries vanish. The second pin states the sufficient qualifier explicitly.

## The unit core and its half-lines

The only unit inverse branches at a vertex with two children are at residues 2 and 8 modulo 9. Along doublings the residues cycle through $1,2,4,8,7,5$. Thus a consistently oriented escaping ray, read in either direction, has branching gaps bounded by four. A simple ray eventually has one orientation by the same outgoing-edge argument as W1. Far along it the off-ray child at each branch is off-cycle and its whole descendant tree avoids the other side trees. Infinitely many such subtrees avoid any prescribed finite cut. The OP-H1 lower bound for unit descendants of each off-cycle root gives infinite $s$-mass for $s\le9691/25000$, including the endpoint by partial summation. Every core end consequently has infinite mass in every neighborhood. A nonzero continuous end value would contradict square integrability. Each full component contains a unit because after the next odd departure subsequent states are units.

The unit graph has zero trace at all its ends in this mass range. The other full-graph ends are precisely the open half-lines starting at positive odd multiples $m$ of three. Along the half-line, including its initial edge from $m$ to its core attachment, path resistance is $m^{-\theta}/(1-2^{-\theta})$. The attachment heights $h_m=(3m+1)/2$ are distinct and comparable to $m$. On each doubling chain, a zero boundary value lets one write the value as a sum of successive differences from infinity. Weighting the $j$th term by $2^{j\theta/2}$ gives the convolution tail with $\ell^1$ norm $1/(2^{\theta/2}-1)$. The chains partition the vertices according to their odd part, so Young's inequality controls the full attachment norm. The constant extension on disjoint open half-lines has exactly one energy contribution per line, on its attachment edge. The exact trace quotient and bounded right inverse follow. Subtraction of this extension leaves a function with zero limits on all doubling rays. The residual weighted inclusion has squared singular values $n^{-(s+\theta)}$ and the isolated half-line extension contributes the analogous $m^{-(s+\theta)}$. This verifies the stated positive gap and trace-class range. The classical Krasikov–Lagarias input covers every fixed positive target prime to three, allowing the same argument up to $s=0.84$. [Krasikov–Lagarias, Theorem 6.1](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa109-3-4)

W4 uses a varying unit target in a fixed bad component. The cylinder construction chooses the exponent modulo a fixed ternary order and has bounded multiplicity and a linear upper height bound. Infinite mass transfers to the fixed cylinder. The two different marked-projection values have a fixed nonzero separation, which verifies the stated sufficient condition.

There is a stronger external input for the W3 mass range. The accepted ProofAtlas formal release for Lech Mazur's September 2026 result gives positive lower natural density for predecessors of each fixed unit target in the ordinary **unaccelerated** map. For any shortcut core target $a$, choose $0\le j\le3$ with $2^ja\bmod9\in\{2,8\}$ and put $c=(2^{j+1}a-1)/3$. This is an odd unit shortcut descendant of $a$. An ordinary unaccelerated orbit hitting odd $c$ also hits it in the shortcut clock, since only the intermediate even states of odd departures are dropped. Partial summation and the half-line mass comparison now give infinite unit descendant mass of $a$ for **all $0<s\le1$**. This sharpens the W3 and W4 uses of predecessor mass. The release reports an owning-target rebuild at source `c290ac229b379d2b51026d3e86140496870d6f39`. I checked its statement and reported evidence without rebuilding this external development. ## Exact and approximate unit-circle modes

An acyclic component is a tree, so choosing the base level fixes $t(Tn)=t(n)+1$ consistently everywhere. For $s>1$ the function $\lambda^{t(n)}n^{-s/2}v(n)$ belongs to counting-measure $\ell^2$, with $v(n)$ the product in S1. The uniform reciprocal tail of simple paths and $\log(1+u)\le u$ give a positive common lower bound on $v$, while $v(n)=d(n)v(Tn)$ gives the exact eigenvalue equation. On a connected component propagation of a nonzero eigenfunction for $\lambda\ne0$ is unique in both directions. A periodic component instead forces $|\lambda|^k=(3^p/2^k)^{s/2}<1$ by positive carry. This gives precisely the direct Hilbert sum of the asserted one-dimensional spaces.

For the adjoint, $\nu(n)=n^{-s/2}u(n)$ is absolutely summable. The exact adjoint recurrence gives $\sum|\nu|\le\sum d|\nu|\le\sum|\nu|$. Hence all odd vertices have zero mass and no fiber has cancellation. Positive absolute mass would propagate forward to an odd departure, which is impossible. In particular the forward and adjoint kernels can differ.

The known-basin test vector on all descendants of each $y_k$ satisfies the exact equation away from the root. Its parent lies outside the subtree, and all predecessors of subtree vertices lie in it, so the residual has exactly the one specified coordinate. If $n_k$ has its initial $k$ odd departures ending at $y_k$, the amplitude there simplifies to $(3/2)^{sk/2}y_k^{-s/2}$ by telescoping. This even gives the relative residual bound $(2/3)^{sk/2}$ without the positive constant $a_s$. Each fixed good vertex has a finite forward orbit and belongs to only finitely many tested subtrees. The normalized test sequence is weakly null even if two subtrees overlap. It gives a singular Weyl sequence for the squared operator. The diagonal entries of $C_s^*C_s$ count the mandatory even predecessor and the odd one exactly when $y\equiv2\pmod3$. Infinitely many two-child fibers in the known basin give cokernel vectors for its proper isometry. This verifies the finite-trace obstruction and does not infer anything about the existence of exceptional components.
