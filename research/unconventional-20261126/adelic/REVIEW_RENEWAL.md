### A finite path and its unrestricted low visits

Fix one ordered finite list of **distinct ordinary positive** states, each the shortcut image of the preceding one. For a native window of length $t$, after $j$ agreeing bits the difference of two lifted sources has become $3^p2^{-j}$ times the original difference. The two lifts modulo $2^{j+1}$ consequently have opposite next parity. Thus specifying $t$ bits gives one source class modulo $2^t$. For $t=\lfloor\log_2 Y\rfloor$, at most two starting integers in $[1,Y]$ belong to that class. Distinctness of the given path is what licenses counting source values instead of occurrences.

The high-grade tail starts at $p=\lfloor\beta t\rfloor-1$, with $\beta=\log_3 2>1/2$. Stirling at that fixed-offset cutoff yields $O(2^{tH_2(\beta)}/\sqrt t)$, and the binomial ratios stay strictly below one from there for large $t$. In the complementary tail, the linear coefficient is at most $1/9$. Deleting the even divisions acting on the nonnegative affine offset bounds it by $(3/2)^p-1\le Y^{1-\beta}$. Thus for large $Y$ the **actual** $t$-step endpoint lies below $Y/2$. The fixed index shift $i\mapsto i+t$ is injective on index positions and the ordinary states when the positions all lie on this same simple path. It needs only a start below $Y$; no constraint on intermediate peaks is used. At most $t$ final positions lack a full window. Iteration of the resulting inequality down to a bounded scale gives (H3). Small scales contribute only $O(Y^{\theta/2}\log(2Y))$ after splitting the dyadic sum at $\sqrt Y$.

### A bounded-height path and the explicit anti-concentration constant

If every step of a native window stays in $[X2^{-D},X]$, the absolute value of its log multiplier $Z=\alpha p-j$ is at most $D+1$ on every subinterval. Indeed the ordinary log height change lies in $[-D,D]$ and the positive additive correction is at most $n/(3Y\log 2)<1$. Under the *auxiliary* Bernoulli law only, the single-step mean is zero, its variance is $\sigma^2=\alpha-1>1/2$, and its fourth moment is at most its second. For $K=\lceil4(D+1)^2\rceil$, $E Z_K^2\ge2(D+1)^2$ and $E Z_K^4\le5(E Z_K^2)^2$. It follows that $E[Z_K^2;|Z_K|>D+1]\ge E Z_K^2/2$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the stated lower probability $1/20$, with strict excess in the event and without a continuity assumption. Disjoint complete $K$-blocks are independent for this word-counting measure. The tilt cost per admissible whole word is bounded because its grade differs from $\beta n$ by $O(D+1)$. This gives (H4).

For the whole bounded-height path, a length-$n$ window failing the band test contains a low vertex, and each vertex lies in at most $n+1$ such windows. Good windows have distinct words because $2^n>X$. Combining with (H3), a sufficiently small fixed $D/n^{1/3}>0$ absorbs both the real tilt and the factor $n+1$ to give (H1). Inverting uses $X\ge L$ from distinct positive states. On a primitive cycle remove its repeated terminal before applying the bound. An odd strict record is strictly greater than every earlier odd state since the suffix multiplier exceeds one and the offset is positive. Repetition of any earlier state would imply an earlier occurrence of the terminal state. Every intervening even state is at most the first shortcut image of a preceding odd, hence at most $2q$. These facts validate the stated record consumers. For the finite recurrence (H5), the displayed $A'$ is the exact rounded upper estimate on the low-grade endpoint; the recursion is used only when it drops. Each inequality in (H6) follows from the two endpoint ratios and $1\le$ distortion $\le(1+1/(3Y))^j$. Counting prefixes by Pascal recurrence is an upper bound on the admitted full words; (H7) deliberately does not impose the all-subwindow restriction of (H4).

### The one-sided wall and the extra logarithm

For (O3), use the bounded-time stopped *signed* mean-zero walk. Its overshoot is at most one. Its value at stopping or truncation has mean zero, is at least $-d-1$, and is at least $b$ on the upper-exit event. This gives $P(\mathrm{upper\ exit})\le(d+1)/(b+d+1)$. The quadratic test function is nonnegative even at an overshoot, with conditional mean drop exactly $\sigma^2$ at every active step. Therefore $E(\tau\wedge h)\le(d+1)(b+1)/\sigma^2$. The upper exit and survival without exit dominate survival above the lower barrier; taking $b=\sqrt h$ proves (O3) for both signs.

For (O4), fix the integer bin $k\le Z_t<k+1$ and let $h=\lfloor t/3\rfloor$. The first $h$ bits must respect the floor $-D$. Reversing the last $h$ bits yields an upper barrier $D+k+1$ since $Z_{t-j}\ge-D$. For this *fixed* bin each necessary event depends only on its own block of bits; they are independent. Conditional on those blocks, the $t-2h$ middle increments must end in a prescribed interval of length one. Their lattice spacing is $\alpha>1$, so at most one binomial atom qualifies, uniformly $O(t^{-1/2})$. The resulting bound per bin is $O((D+1)(D+k+2)t^{-3/2})$. Here $k\ge-\lceil b_0\rceil$ and $D\ge b_0+1$ keep the reverse-barrier parameter nonnegative. Multiplying by $e^{-ak}$ and summing gives (O4). No independence is assigned to an ordinary trajectory.

For a high-grade ordinary window from a state at most $X$ with all states at least $Y=X/t^4$, the prefix wall follows from its real height ratio and $t/(3Y\log 2)<1$. The retained total grade gives $Z_t\ge-2\alpha$. The inverse probability of a word is exactly $2^{\theta t}\exp[-aZ_t]$; reversing this sign would invalidate the bin sum, and the written sign is correct. Windows failing the floor contribute at most $(t+1)V(Y)\ll X^\theta t^{1/2-4\theta}\le O(X^\theta t^{-3/2})$ by (H3) and $\theta>1/2$. The low-grade futures are charged once to $V(X/2)$ regardless of the heights in between. The dyadic induction handles the final $O(\log X)$ terms and yields (O1) uniformly in the supplied finite path.

Finally, summing $y^{-\theta}=\theta\int_y^\infty t^{-\theta-1}dt$ over the visits $y\ge M$ and using (O1) leaves a tail bounded by $C\int_{\log(2M)}^\infty(1+\log v)^2v^{-3/2}dv$. This is $O((1+\log\log(2M))^2/\sqrt{\log(2M)})$ and is equivalent to the displayed (O2) for $M\ge2$. The integral remains finite with an extra factor $(\log(2y))^\gamma$ for $\gamma<1/2$. Finite-prefix exhaustion works by monotone convergence and the bound is independent of the chosen path. An infinite repetition of a bounded cycle is outside this step.

### Independent bounded checks

An independently written check enumerated 393,192 word/barrier pairs at clocks 3–16. On all 125,240 eligible pairs it checked the two necessary endpoint-thirds barriers and the single allowable middle grade using exact powers of two and three. On 4,096 actual simple prefixes from starts 1–4096 it checked 20,480 finite low-visit splits, including 121,901 low-grade endpoint inequalities, distinct shifted endpoints and source-class caps. For eight integer band widths $D\in\{1,2,3,4,7,10,16,20\}$ it also evaluated the binomial small-ball probability at 80-digit working precision; the smallest observed value was approximately 0.4400187. The exact uniform constant $1/20$ follows from the fourth-moment argument above, independently of those numerical values. None of these finite checks is an evaluation of the unknown asymptotic constants.

### Scope against an earlier posted theorem

The October 26, 2026 version of the rStation Collatz Paper 28 explicitly states in §8.1 (P2) an estimate $\#(\mathcal O\cap[a,a+m))\le K m^{\theta-\gamma}+\lceil\log_2m\rceil^2$ for a fixed $\gamma>0$, uniformly for an ordinary orbit set, and in (P3) a critical Mellin tail $O(n^{-\gamma})$. Taking $a=1$ includes each of the positive finite simple paths considered here. An asymptotic power saving also dominates (H1), since $X^{-\gamma}\exp(c(\log X)^{1/3})\to0$. If Paper 28's proof is sound, the height and occupation asymptotics therefore have a stronger posted antecedent. The reconstructed proofs above stand independently. The elementary derivations and the finite computable recurrences should be described separately from novelty of the consequences.
