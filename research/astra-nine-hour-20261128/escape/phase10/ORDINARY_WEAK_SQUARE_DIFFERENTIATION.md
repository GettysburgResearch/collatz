# The weak-square endpoint at every prescribed ordinary point

Let \(y\) be any positive odd integer and \(h(y)\) the original canonical trace. Then
\[
   \big\|\rho(y+3^n\,\cdot)-h(y)\big\|_{L^{2,\infty}(\mathbb Z_3)}\longrightarrow0.    \tag{1}
\]
If \(3\nmid y\), every term inside this limit has infinite strong second moment. The [subcritical prescribed-point theorem](ORDINARY_LEBESGUE_POINTS.md) gives the exact fixed-root clock and source law. The endpoint needs more than density of locally constant functions, which fails in the weak-square space for this \(\rho\).

Use the equivalent Banach weak-square norm on a Haar probability space,
\[
         N(f)=\sup_{0<m(E)\le1}m(E)^{-1/2}\int_E|f|\,dm.
\]
For a nonnegative variable with \(t^2m(f>t)\to c\), the decreasing rearrangement satisfies \(s^{1/2}f^*(s)\to\sqrt c\) at zero (including \(c=0\)). Therefore the small-set supremum in this norm tends to \(2\sqrt c\) as the allowed set size tends to zero. The same limit holds for \(N(f1_{f>T})\) as \(T\to\infty\): the rearrangement is restricted to the top \(m(f>T)\) mass, and larger set sizes cannot improve that restricted supremum.

Let \(\mathcal G\) be the compact group of ternary affine Haar isometries \(g(u)=x+v u\), with \(x\in\mathbb Z_3\), \(v\in\mathbb Z_3^\times\). For each fixed unit \(y\), normalized inverse-word weights at depth \(n\) give a probability \(\mu_n\) on \(\mathcal G\) by marking the true source \(x\) and \(v=2^{S_w}\). From the clock-augmented source theorem, \(\mu_n\) converges weakly to Haar probability \(\nu\) on \(\mathcal G\): \(2\) generates the finite unit groups modulo \(3^k\), and the additive residue is independent and uniform at every fixed precision. One could instead work with the closed subgroup generated by the unit \(2\); the independent uniform additive residue is sufficient below. On full ternary Haar,
\[
            \rho(y+3^n u)=\rho_n(y)\int\rho(g u)\,d\mu_n(g)
                     \quad\text{almost everywhere in }u.              \tag{2}
\]

Write \(U_g f(u)=f(gu)\). The high exceedances of \(f=\rho\) have the [angular limit](JOINT_ANGULAR_TAIL.md)
\[
                 t^2 1_{f>t}dm\Rightarrow\kappa\sigma ,
\]
where the tilted ternary law \(\sigma\) is singular to \(m\). For singularity alone it suffices that the entropy of geometric gaps with parameter \(3/4\) is strictly smaller than \(\log3\). For \(\sigma\)-almost every code the first \(r\) letters belong eventually to a set of at most \(e^{(H+\epsilon)r}\) words by the iid information law. Each such word occupies one trit cylinder of mass \(3^{-r}\). These covering masses tend to zero when \(H+\epsilon<\log3\). This proves singularity without an exact-dimensionality input.

## A generic finite tuple is stable even at diverging thresholds

For independent \(g_1,\ldots,g_K\sim\nu\), the measures \((g_i^{-1})_*\sigma\) are pairwise mutually singular almost surely. Indeed each is singular to Haar, and the expectation of any one of them is Haar by its independent uniform translation. Conditioning on any other one proves singularity of each pair by testing a Haar-null carrier. Equivalently, one can first fix the slopes and apply Fubini to translates of a fixed null Borel carrier, avoiding any random-carrier selection.

Fix such a tuple. If \(g_{i,l}\to g_i\) and \(t_l\to\infty\), the single-branch measures \(t_l^2 1_{U_{g_{i,l}}f>c t_l}dm\) converge weakly to \(c^{-2}\kappa(g_i^{-1})_*\sigma\) for every \(c>0\). The tests after these isometries converge uniformly when the tests are continuous. The doubly exceeded set for any two distinct branches has \(o(t_l^{-2})\) mass: any subsequential weak limit of its rescaled measures is bounded by both mutually singular limiting measures. Consequently for \(B_l=K^{-1}\sum_iU_{g_{i,l}}f\),
\[
                  t_l^2 m(B_l>t_l)\longrightarrow\kappa/K.            \tag{3}
\]
For the lower bound take the union of the \(K\) events \(U_{g_{i,l}}f>Kt_l\). For the upper bound, apart from a double exceedance at thresholds proportional to \(t_l\), at least one term must be greater than \((1-\epsilon)Kt_l\). Send \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). Subtracting the constant \(\int f=1\) leaves the same limit for the absolute tail. The fixed-tuple version follows as a special case.

The bounded function
\[
  (g_1,\ldots,g_K)\longmapsto N\big(K^{-1}\sum_iU_{g_i}f-1\big)         \tag{4}
\]
is continuous at every such tuple. Indeed \(B_l\to B=K^{-1}\sum_iU_{g_i}f\) in \(L^1\) by continuity of Haar translations there and approximation by continuous functions. Norm tests on sets whose measure stays bounded below therefore converge uniformly. For a sequence of measures \(s_l\to0\), (3) gives an upper limit \(2\sqrt{\kappa/K}\) for the normalized integral of the largest \(s_l\) mass of \(|B_l-1|\). To see uniformity, apply (3) to any diverging sequence of quantile thresholds: beyond a fixed large threshold and for all sufficiently large \(l\), the tail constant lies within any specified error of \(\kappa/K\), or a sequence violating (3) would exist. The subset of values below that fixed threshold costs \(O(\sqrt{s_l})\). The limiting \(N(B-1)\) itself is at least \(2\sqrt{\kappa/K}\) from its small-set quantiles. These two bounds give upper semicontinuity; the lower bound follows from \(L^1\) convergence on each fixed positive-measure test. The uniform bound for (4) is \(N(f)+1\). The function is measurable, for example from its decreasing-rearrangement formula or as a countable supremum of tests on finite unions of cylinders.

## Haar tuple averages and the ordinary limit

For \(K\) independent Haar maps put \(B_K=K^{-1}\sum_iU_{g_i}f\). Its law as a random function is invariant under right composition by any fixed affine isometry. By (3) and the high-truncation norm formula,
\[
              \lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb E\,N(B_K1_{B_K>T})
                                      =2\sqrt{\kappa/K}.
\]
This uses the deterministic bound \(N(B_K)\le N(f)\). Take \(L\) independent copies of this \(K\)-letter block. For each fixed \(T\), the part of a block clipped by \(1_{B_K\le T}\) has a constant pointwise expectation by Haar invariance (an almost-everywhere version suffices). The \(L\) clipped blocks are independent. The expectation of the squared \(L^2\) norm of their centered average is at most \(T^2/L\) by Fubini and variance addition. Hence its expected \(N\)-norm is at most \(T/\sqrt L\). For the remaining positive parts and their means use the triangle bound by twice their expected \(N\)-norm. First choose \(K\) large, then \(T\) large for this fixed \(K\), then \(L\) large. We obtain arbitrarily large finite tuple sizes \(N_0=KL\) for which
\[
                \mathbb E_{\nu^{N_0}}N(B_{N_0}-1)
                          \quad\text{is arbitrarily small}.            \tag{5}
\]
The two-stage averaging allows the high cutoff to depend on the first block size.

For any such fixed \(N_0\), \(\mu_n^{N_0}\Rightarrow\nu^{N_0}\). The bounded function (4) is continuous almost everywhere for the limiting law, so its expectations converge. Taking the supremum over norm tests after expectation shows
\[
  N\left(\int U_g f\,d\mu_n(g)-1\right)
     \le \mathbb E_{\mu_n^{N_0}}N(B_{N_0}-1).
\]
This inequality also avoids strong measurability assumptions for translations into the full weak-square Banach space. By (5) its upper limit is zero. Since \(\rho_n(y)\to h(y)\), (2) proves (1). Nonunit \(y\) has identically zero field at every positive depth.

The same endpoint holds in each of the three moving regimes in the [ordinary strong-differentiation theorem](ORDINARY_LEBESGUE_POINTS.md). In its prefront and postfront upper proofs use \(N(\sum a_iU_i\rho)\le(\sum a_i)N(\rho)\), (1) at the fixed postfront anchor, and the very same grade-mass bounds. The positive fixed-offset lower estimate already follows from any subcritical norm. A growing precision relative to a moving ordinary root is essential to the postfront conclusion.
