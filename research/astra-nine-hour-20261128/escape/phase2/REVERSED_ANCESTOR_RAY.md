# A divergence model retaining the true CRT witnesses at every target

**PROPOSED obstruction.** Uniform complete-basin sampling, the fixed-target clock Gaussian, the uniform early-clock upper bound, and an absolute arithmetic-progression floor on genuine-depth witnesses can coexist with a positive-density escaping component of a sparse modification of the shortcut map. In particular, every lower-count witness constructed below follows only **unaltered genuine updates** up to and including its first arrival at its claimed target. This isolates a limitation of the proposed combination of ordinary-source and CRT estimates. The model changes the native odd update on an explicit recursively selected infinite set; it gives no counterexample for the genuine map.

Let \(U\) be the genuine positive odd map, \(T\) the genuine shortcut map, and \(d(v)\) the source density of genuine first hits at an odd target. The estimates used as imports are those in [the complete moving-clock law](MOVING_CLOCKS.md), [the early-clock bound](../UNIFORM_EARLY_ARRIVALS.md), and [the companion CRT theorem](../../machinery/ARITHMETIC_PROGRESSION_FLOOR.md). The construction only needs the stated density and counting outcomes of those results, and does not transfer any assertion that every modified trajectory satisfies the native inverse arithmetic.

PR211 already planted a lacunary escaping ray by bounded additive changes at odd multiples of three, optionally attaching a positive-density gate. Its altered successors violate the genuine odd image class. Here the changed successors stay in that ternary image class, and every unit root keeps a quantitatively full untouched genuine branch supporting all the CRT and central-row witnesses. ## One branch in the genuine tree of one

Choose a fixed \(a_0>1\) odd, prime to three, in the genuine tree of one. It may be chosen arbitrarily large, for instance \((4^k-1)/3\) with \(3\nmid k\). Starting at \(a_j\), consider two integer inverse children
\[
 b=(2^e a_j-1)/3,\qquad 3\le e\le14,\qquad b\equiv2\pmod3 .
\]
Since two generates the units modulo nine, the equation \(2^e a_j=7\bmod9\) has one residue class of exponents modulo six. The displayed interval contains two choices. Both children are odd units, and each grows by a factor greater than two from its parent. The two predecessor sets are disjoint, since \(a_j\) is aperiodic and they enter it through different odd children. Their masses sum to at most \(d(a_j)\). Choose either lighter child for \(a_{j+1}\), with a fixed tie rule, and write its exponent as \(e_j\). Then all gates lie in the genuine tree of one, with
\[
   U a_{j+1}=a_j,\quad a_{j+1}>2a_j,\quad
   a_{j+1}\le(2^{14}/3)a_j,\quad d(a_{j+1})\le d(a_j)/2.              \tag{1}
\]
A mathematically defined density choice is sufficient. No algorithm computing the densities is assumed.

Change the shortcut update only at these odd gates, sending \(a_j\) to \(a_{j+1}\). Even updates are unchanged. Each changed odd successor is an odd ternary unit in the exact genuine image class \(2\bmod3\). Its positive multiplier is bounded uniformly in \(j\). Each octave contains a bounded number of exceptions. An original member of \(P(a_0)\) first follows its genuine path to its first gate and then follows the strictly increasing new ray forever. Conversely a source outside \(P(a_0)\) never visits a gate and keeps its genuine path. The new escaping basin therefore contains precisely this positive mass from the formerly convergent component. All other components are untouched. A second gate in the genuine tree with an independent route to one leaves a positive convergent population, also implied by the CRT argument below.

## Every target keeps a full genuine inverse branch

For each odd ternary unit \(y\), choose a nonperiodic unit odd child \(q=(2^e y-1)/3\), \(e\le18\), such that no gate belongs to \(P(q)\). To verify existence, distinct off-cycle children have disjoint predecessor basins. Since the gates are a single linearly ordered **genuine** ancestry chain, two distinct such branches cannot both contain a gate. There is at most one further child on a cycle. Among \(1\le e\le18\) there are at least three unit children, by the elementary calculation modulo nine. Thus one of them qualifies. In particular \(q\le 2^{18}y/3\). A source hitting \(q\) under the genuine map has made no earlier gate visit, since such a gate would itself reach \(q\). The departure from \(q\) is genuine, and its following intermediate physical states are even through their descent to \(y\). The chosen inverse branch consists entirely of unaltered paths until \(y\), even if \(y\) itself is a changed gate.

For \(X\ge C' y\) and odd \(M\le (X/y)^{\delta'}\), apply the companion CRT lower count at \(q\), reducing the absolute exponent and enlarging the onset to absorb \(q/y\le 2^{18}/3\). This gives
\[
 \#\{x\le X:x\ {\rm odd},\ x\equiv u\bmod M,\ (U')^j x=y
               \text{ for some }j\}\gg X/(yM)                         \tag{2}
\]
uniformly in every supplied target and residue. Each witness follows a genuine path. For the exact-depth result, a depth \(n-1\) path to \(q\) extends to a depth \(n\) path to \(y\) with added physical total \(e\). At a central requested total \(s\), the preceding total is \(s-e\). Its difference from \(2(n-1)\) changes by at most 16. Use a fixed slightly smaller central window, or the enlarged fixed central window in the companion proof, so the original floor applies. The change from \(2^{s-e}/3^{n-1}\) to \(2^s/3^n\) costs only \(3\,2^{-e}\ge3\,2^{-18}\). Consequently the same order of exact-total row count and absolute geometric mass floor \(c'/M\) survives at every odd unit \(y\), for all large depths with a slightly reduced modulus exponent. The source itself, including its input residue, is unchanged. This proves the compatibility of the floor with escape on **actual unmodified inverse witnesses**, not just in a finite quotient.

In particular \(P'(y)\) has positive lower density for every fixed odd ternary unit. For an even positive target coprime to three, use the same legal \(q=(2^e y-1)/3\) and the intervening even shortcut steps. Distinct branch basins are again disjoint away from at most one cycle child: after \(y\) all future states are fixed, so a path visiting a different first child would force a cycle through \(y\). Four or more bounded-exponent unit choices easily allow these exclusions.

## Fixed and moving source laws

Put \(P_j=P(a_j)\). The genuine basins are nested decreasing. The sources whose first gate is \(a_j\) are exactly \(P_j\setminus P_{j+1}\). Indeed one that visits \(a_j\) cannot have visited any gate of higher index without also visiting \(a_{j+1}\). A finite genuine hitting path cannot first pass infinitely many gates. The expression consequently covers all of \(P_0\).

For the modified target \(a_k\), its basin is exactly \(P_0\setminus P_{k+1}\), and its first clock on \(P_j\setminus P_{j+1}\), \(j\le k\), is \(\tau_{a_j}+k-j\). The genuine first clock to \(a_j\) on \(P_{j+1}\) is \(\tau_{a_{j+1}}+e_j\). The every-target clock Gaussian on these finite differences therefore gives the same Gaussian with each modified gate's own density.

For a nongate target \(y\), a modified hit must occur strictly before its source's first gate, and its clock is genuine. The removed genuine basin is the union of \(P_j\) over gates whose genuine futures contain \(y\) after their gate visit. If nonempty, this union equals \(P_{j_0}\) with the least such index. Since gates are nonperiodic, if a gate reaches \(y\ne a_j\) then \(y\) cannot reach that gate. Every source removed in this way genuinely hits \(y\) at \(\tau_{a_{j_0}}+\tau_y(a_{j_0})\). If none is removed the timed count is unchanged. Finite subtraction again gives the full fixed-target Gaussian at each modified basin density. The same statements apply to even nongate targets, with their true shortcut clocks.

Minimum tightness follows by ignoring the small upper tail of first-gate indices. The genuine source fraction first entering beyond index \(J\) is bounded by the frequency in \(P_J\), whose density is at most \(2^{-J}\). On the other changed sources the modified minimum is at most \(a_J\), since the new future after entrance increases. For macroscopic shortcut tracking up to \(\mu\log n\), truncate this gate index too. On the finite set of possible first gates the unchanged first clock is \(\mu\log X+O_P(\sqrt{\log X})\) by the true fixed-target theorem. Thus any altered portion up to the macroscopic deadline has length \(O_P(\sqrt{\log X})\), and the new log height changes by at most a fixed constant per step from a bounded entry site. The genuine future of each retained gate is bounded. This transfers Inselmann's density-one macroscopic log estimate at every fixed relative tolerance. The first-gate tail has arbitrarily small limiting probability, as required.

For any growing cutoff \(R\), the modified first odd landing or cemetery is, as a label of the **genuine** odd dynamics, invariant on odd edges above \(R\) except at gates. By (1) the uniform sparse-exception hypothesis of PR213 holds for this set at every \(R\): at most a fixed number of exceptions per octave, dominated by \(C_A Z(1+\log(Z/R))^{-A}\) for \(Z\ge R\ge1\). Hence the same relative landing estimate holds with the model's own measure. For the uniform sampling of each complete modified target basin use the same argument at \(r=1\), with the gates plus the possible additional exception \(y\). This supplies a genuine density law for arbitrary moving targets in this model.

## Quantitative uniform early arrivals still hold

For \(L=\log X\) and \(S=\mu L-z\sqrt L\), let \(B_X(z)=C e^{-c z^2}+C_B(1+z\sqrt L)^{-B}\) be the genuine uniform early-clock estimate for positive odd targets. For a nongate the modified event is a subset of the genuine event. For a gate \(a_k\), the union above only uses \(0\le j\le k\) with \(k-j\le S\), and each term is bounded by the genuine early event at \(a_j\) by time \(S\). There are \(O(1+L)\) such indices. If \(z\ge K\sqrt{\log(2+L)}\), with a sufficiently large absolute \(K\), this many terms are absorbed by reducing the Gaussian constant and using the genuine estimate at polynomial exponent \(B+2\).

For smaller \(1\le z<K\sqrt{\log(2+L)}\), first sum the genuine early bounds for \(j<\lceil z^2\rceil\). The remaining terms all lie in \(P_{\lceil z^2\rceil}\). By the uniform full-basin estimate (2) of the moving-clock note their total source probability is \(O(2^{-z^2}+L^{-1/9})\). In this range the last term is at most \(C e^{-c' z^2}\) for sufficiently small \(c'>0\). The polynomial factors from the finite early sum are harmless after raising \(B\). This proves the same form of uniform early upper bound, all targets and all \(1\le z\le\mu\sqrt L\), with model-dependent constants.

The ray has positive log corrections, but it violates the genuine elementary upper ratio at the changed sites. In fact (1) gives \(2T'(a_j)/a_j>4>10/3\), whereas the genuine map has \(2T(x)/x\le10/3\) at odd \(x\ge3\). Its escaping ray does not obey the native exact \(+1\) product identity or the simple-path logarithmic correction bound. The critical-tail theorem that uses that identity therefore has no such transfer. The quantitative inverse floor (2) is paid by a different bounded-exponent child even at a gate whose main source population now travels from the previous low gate. Hence it cannot identify the share of the last incoming branch from the absolute target-relative normalization alone. An exact arithmetic argument about the dominant old source remains available to distinguish the positive Collatz map from this construction.
