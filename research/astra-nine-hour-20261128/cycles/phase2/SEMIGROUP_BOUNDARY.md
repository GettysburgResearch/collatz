# The complete denominator on a numerical-semigroup boundary

**Proposed written theorem.** Each mixed binary circle has a finite boundary in a numerical-semigroup ideal, with a color in a finite cyclic group when its two counts have a common divisor. Evaluating the boundary recovers its complete reduced Collatz denominator. In the coprime case its normalized coefficients alternate between +1 and −1, and one positive rational evaluation can give the *exact* cancellation. The [explicit families](BOUNDARY_FAMILIES.md) include arbitrarily long words at positive proportional Hamming distance from every mechanical reference at their own slope.

## The coprime clocks

Let a binary circle have length $n$, weight $u$, $v=n-u>0$, $u>0$ and $\gcd(n,u)=1$. Let $U_i$ count the ones before clock $i$. Subtract the minimum of $ui-nU_i$ to form heights $h_i\ge0$. They are all distinct modulo $n$ and obey
$$h_{i+1}=h_i+u-n b_i.$$
Their rays $h_i+n\mathbb N$ are disjoint. Their union $S$ contains zero, is cofinite in $\mathbb N$ and is closed under addition of both $u$ and $v$. Indeed $h_i+u=h_{i+1}+n b_i$ and $h_i+v=h_{i-1}+n(1-b_{i-1})$. In particular all gaps of $S$ are gaps of the ordinary numerical semigroup generated by $u,v$.

Conversely take any cofinite $S\subseteq\mathbb N$ containing zero and closed under $+u,+v$. Its least representative $h_i$ of residue $ui\bmod n$ satisfies $h_i-v\le h_{i+1}\le h_i+u$, by the two closure assumptions. The two admissible values in this residue are precisely the endpoints. This reconstructs the word normalized at its height minimum, with total weight $u$. Thus all normalized semimodules for $\langle u,v\rangle$ occur, and no extra feasibility predicate is implicit. For example the full set gives the mechanical circle, and the semigroup $\langle u,v\rangle$ itself gives the circle with just one run of each symbol.

Put $H_S(z)=\sum_{s\in S}z^s$, $G(z)=\sum_{h\notin S}z^h$, and
$$J(z)=(1-z)H_S(z)=1-(1-z)G(z).                                      \tag{1}$$
Here $G$ sums over nonnegative integers only. For nonempty gap intervals $[l_j,r_j)$ the boundary is $1-\sum_jz^{l_j}+\sum_jz^{r_j}$. Consecutive nonzero coefficients alternate and the first and last are positive. Define the odd and even departure sums $O(z),E(z)$ by summing $z^{h_i}$ over the respective phases. Ray decomposition gives $O+E=(1-z^n)H_S$. Telescoping the single steps gives $(z^{-v}-1)O+(z^u-1)E=0$, hence
$$O=z^v(1-z^u)H_S=z^v[ u ]_z J,\qquad E=[ v ]_z J,\qquad [k]_z=1+z+\cdots+z^{k-1}.     \tag{2}$$
These are polynomial identities over the integers before any modular reduction.

Write $D=2^n-3^u\ne0$, and let $C=\sum_{i:b_i=1}2^i3^{u-1-U_i}$ be the unrotated affine numerator. The actual rational cycle has reduced denominator $d=|D|/\gcd(D,C)$, with the usual sign of its phases determined by $D$. For $|D|=1$ this gives $d=1$. Otherwise work in $R=\mathbb Z/|D|$, where 2 and 3 are units. Choose integers $a,b$ with $au+bn=1$ and put $z_0=2^a3^b$ in $R$. Then $z_0^u=2$, $z_0^n=3$. Moreover $z_0-1$ is a unit because $(z_0-1)[u]_{z_0}=1$. Up to a unit arising from the common height translation and $3^{u-1}$, $O(z_0)$ equals $C$. Thus
$$d=\text{additive order of }J(z_0)\text{ in }R.                     \tag{3}$$
The order of the zero element is one. Equation (3) retains complete prime powers, signed words and every rotation.

One can equivalently work in $A=\mathbb Z[z]/(z^n-z^u-1)$, where $z$ is a unit. Then $A/(z^u-2)\simeq R$. Indeed the quotient imposes $z^u=2$ and $z^n=3$, so $D=0$. The two constants are units there, and $z=2^a3^b$. The evaluation and constant maps are mutual inverses. In particular multiplication by $z^u-2$ in the free rank-$n$ ring $A$ has absolute determinant $|D|$. This particular trinomial uses the physical identity $3-2=1$.

A norm bound is a weaker consequence. For example $\gcd(D,C)$ divides the determinant of multiplication by $J$ on $A$, by applying the adjugate to the evaluation row modulo the gcd. This determinant can vanish, or exceed $|D|$, and one cannot replace (3) with the single norm test. The [bounded arithmetic controls](semigroup_controls.py) exhibit both failures and preserve the signed integral cycle through $-17$.

## All clock types

For $g=\gcd(n,u)$, set $n=gN$, $u=gU$, $V=N-U$. Work on $\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/g$. With $aU+bN=1$, use the shifts
$$X=(U,b),\qquad Y=(N,-a),\qquad Y-X=(V,-a-b).$$
In monomial notation they are $X=z^Ut^b$, $Y=z^Nt^{-a}$, where $t^g=1$. They come from the integral changes of coordinates $z=X^aY^b$, $t=X^NY^{-U}$. The clock points are $iX-U_iY$, followed by a common translation so the minimum first coordinate is zero. Their images in $(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/g)/\mathbb ZY$ are all distinct: the image of $X$ has exact order $Ng=n$, since $NX-UY=(0,1)$. Therefore the rays from the clock points under $+Y$ are disjoint and cofinite above height zero in every color. Their union $S$ is stable under $+X$ and $+(Y-X)$ by the same two step identities. Conversely the Apéry representatives of any such cofinite normalized set, read in $X$ order, have successive displacements $X$ or $X-Y$ by the upper and lower inequalities along each $Y$-ray. This also allows words displayed with repeated period.

In the group ring with the height variable allowed as a formal Laurent series bounded below, let $G$ be the finite colored holes polynomial and let $C_g(t)=1+\cdots+t^{g-1}$. Then
$$H_S=C_g(t)/(1-z)-G,\quad J_X=(1-X)H_S=[U]_z C_g(t)-(1-X)G,\quad O=(Y/X)J_X.       \tag{4}$$
The coefficient of $J_X$ is 1 exactly at $S\setminus(S+X)$. The sum has $u$ terms. The equation for $O$ follows by telescoping and ray decomposition as in (2), with $z^n,z^u$ replaced by $Y,X$. Cancellation of $1-Y$ is valid as a formal power series since $N>0$.

Specialize $X=2,Y=3$ modulo $|D|$, so $z_0=2^a3^b$ and $t_0=2^N3^{-U}$. Because $t_0^g=1$, (4) holds in the complete modulus, and $d$ is precisely the additive order of $J_X(z_0,t_0)$. Equivalently impose $t^g=1$, $Y-X-1=0$ and then $X-2=0$ in the Laurent group ring. The quotient is $\mathbb Z/|D|$ by the same constant-map argument. In particular replacing $D$ with $2^N-3^U$ would lose information when $g>1$.

There is a simple consequence on the *whole* geometric-sum factor. Put $A_0=2^N$, $B_0=3^U$ and $Q=\sum_{r=0}^{g-1}A_0^rB_0^{g-1-r}$. Since $C_g(t_0)=Q/B_0^{g-1}$, it vanishes modulo $Q$ without any coprimality assumption between $Q$ and $A_0-B_0$. Since $1-X=-1$, the sole residue on this complete factor is the bare colored holes polynomial $G(z_0,t_0)$. If all holes have one height and a nonempty proper set $I$ of colors, the surviving unit times the cleared value is the integer $P=\sum_{r\in I} A_0^r B_0^{g-1-r}$ with $0<P<Q$. Thus $Q/\gcd(P,Q)>1$ divides $d$. In particular this entire family has no ordinary cycles. The argument is a positive proper-subsum calculation and needs no verified Collatz floor or logarithm estimate. The equal-clock repeated-atom exclusion in PR191 already overlaps ordinary exclusion in small contracting dictionaries.

## Source scope

The numerical semigroup/partition correspondence is classical. Anderson's simultaneous-core theorem identifies the order ideals of the gap poset for two coprime generators. The elementary bijection above is included to specify each physical bit and its orientation. [Anderson, Discrete Mathematics 248 (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(01)00343-0)

The coprime residue coordinate and the specialized first-difference norm predate this note. In the rank-$u$ order $z^u=2$ of PR188, $(z-1)[u]_z=1$, so its first-difference operator already effects the cancellation in (2), up to a unit and the chosen rotation. PR191 TCP-4 gives the Bezout and torsion coordinates for all gcd types, its cyclotomic norm certificates, and correct shared-prime accounting. The use here is the numerical-semigroup polynomial before specialization, the colored positive holes, the alternative trinomial order and the explicit evaluations in the companion note. No novelty is claimed for the original full-affine divisibility criterion or the residue coordinates.
