# Exact limiting valuations of bounded signed sums

**Source-qualified written theorem.** Let $n\to\infty$ and consider
$$            S=\sum_{j=1}^r c_j2^{a_j}3^{b_j},\qquad
  r\text{ fixed},\quad |a_j|+|b_j|=O(n),\quad c_j\in\mathbb Q^\times,
                               \ h(c_j)=o(n).                         \tag{1}$$
Pass to a subsequence where each $(a_j/n,b_j/n)$ converges and where every group sum used below is either always zero or always nonzero. The latter extraction is finite. A group at one second coordinate $B$ consists of those indices with $b_j/n\to B$.

* If $S\ne0$, then $v_3(S)/n$ converges to the least $B$ whose group sum is nonzero. The corresponding dyadic valuation converges to the least normalized first coordinate of a nonzero first-coordinate group.
* If $S=0$, then for every pair of limiting coordinates $(A,B)$ the **exact rational subsum** on that pair vanishes eventually on the retained subsequence. In particular a nondegenerate vanishing sum, one with no nonempty proper zero subsum, has all exponent vectors within $o(n)$ of each other.

The result allows opposite signs, changing coefficients, and rational denominators. There is no positivity or one-lap separation premise. It treats a fixed number of terms and the fixed places two and three. The analogous all-divisor estimate in [phase four](../phase4/MOVING_SPARSE_SUMS.md) is a different assertion. The Subspace Theorem for open powered paths and for critical backward scripts is used in PR214 and PR210, respectively. Our use here is a fixed-place valuation formula for arbitrary bounded signed charts with small coefficients. ### One prime measuring powers of the other

For a nonzero binary sum $Z=\sum c_j2^{a_j}$ as in (1), $v_3(Z)=o(n)$. The lower bound follows immediately by the ultrametric inequality and the small coefficient heights. For the upper bound group indices with the same limit of $a_j/n$, take one representative exponent in each group and absorb the factors $2^{a_j-a_*}$ into the coefficients. The resulting group coefficients have height $o(n)$ by the fixed term count. Remove zero groups. If one group remains, its ternary valuation is $o(n)$ directly. Otherwise write $z_1,\ldots,z_t$ for the group terms, which have distinct limiting binary exponents.

Suppose $v_3(Z)\ge\eta n$ on an infinite subsequence. At infinity and two use the coordinate forms in the variables $z_j$. At three replace just the first coordinate by $Z=\sum z_j$, keeping the other coordinate forms. These are fixed rational independent linear forms at every place. For each original coordinate, the product of its absolute values at the three places is $\exp(o(n))$, since the binary monomial cancels and its coefficient has height $o(n)$. Replacing $z_1$ by $Z$ at three multiplies the total by at most $3^{-\eta n+o(n)}$, as $v_3(z_1)=o(n)$.

To work with primitive integer vectors, clear denominators by a power of two times the product of finitely many small coefficient denominators. The power of two leaves the product unchanged, and the positive factor supported away from two and three has logarithm $o(n)$. Dividing the resulting integer vector by its common gcd cannot increase the product. Its primitive supremum norm is at most $\exp(Cn)$ for a fixed $C$, and tends to infinity because some coordinate ratio has exponential real magnitude. The rational $p$-adic Subspace Theorem gives a fixed proper rational linear relation among the $z_j$ on an infinite subsequence. Such a relation is impossible: among its nonzero terms the one with largest limiting binary exponent dominates all the others at infinity by an exponential factor. This proves the one-prime result. Swapping two and three proves the reverse assertion. [Schlickewei, *The quantitative subspace theorem for number fields*](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1992__82_3_245_0/) · [Evertse, rational normalization, Theorem 1.1](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~evertsejh/dio2011-padicsubspace.pdf)

In each $B$-group in (1), factor out one $3^{b_*}$ and absorb all $3^{b_j-b_*}$ into its small coefficients. If its sum is nonzero, the preceding result shows its valuation is $b_*+o(n)$. The least nonzero group's valuation is strictly below every later nonzero group's, so the ultrametric inequality is an equality there. This proves the first assertion. If $S=0$, a least nonzero $B$-group would contradict the same strict inequality, hence each $B$-group vanishes. Within one such group, factor out $3^{b_*}$ and collect the binary exponent groups. Real exponential domination among distinct limiting binary exponents now forces every collected coefficient to vanish. This proves the second assertion.

### A restriction on every bounded signed formula at an ordinary minimum

Suppose a signed formula of type (1) represents the raw affine numerator at the minimum $m$ of a hypothetical growing sequence of ordinary cycles. Then
$$                            S=m\,2^n-m\,3^u.                        \tag{2}$$
Here $m=n^{O(1)}$ by the effective two-logarithm bound, so its height is $o(n)$. Pass to the above subsequence, with $u/n\to p=\log_3 2$. The sum of terms at the limiting coordinate $(1,0)$ is exactly $m2^n$, the sum at $(0,p)$ is exactly $-m3^u$, and **every other exponent-pair group cancels exactly**. This follows by subtracting the two displayed terms and applying the zero-sum part. Thus any bounded signed formula whose terms miss either required corner cannot represent the numerator of such cycles. The actual physical sum has $u$ terms, and no bounded representation missing a corner is forced here.

In particular if a fixed polynomial identity $P(2^n,3^u,m)=0$ held along infinitely many distinct ordinary cycles for some nonzero $P\in\mathbb Q[X,Y,Z]$, expanding its coefficients in the third variable would give a contradiction. The minimum tends to infinity, so each nonzero coefficient polynomial evaluated at $m$ is eventually nonzero and has height $O(\log n)$. The distinct monomials $X^iY^j$ have distinct limiting vectors $(i,jp)$ in (1). Hence the triples would be Zariski dense despite the explicit full closure (2), which involves the additional native numerator coordinate. This only tests polynomial certificates using the two clock powers and the minimum.
