# Binary and ternary Apéry sets of the same clock semigroup

Fix positive exponents \(b_0,\ldots,b_{q-1}\), write \(a=\sum b_i\), and suppose \(D=2^a-3^q>0\). Set \(B_i=2^{b_i}\), \(e_i=\sum_{j<i}b_j\),
\[
                  w_i=3^{q-1-i}2^{e_i},\qquad
                  S=\langle w_0,\dots,w_{q-1},2^a\rangle\subseteq\mathbb N.
\]
The semigroup is numerical because its generators include \(3^{q-1}\) and \(2^a\). It also contains \(3^q\). Use \(\operatorname{Ap}(S,s)=\{n\in S:n-s\notin S\}\). Then
\[
 \operatorname{Ap}(S,2^a)=\left\{\sum_i r_iw_i:0\le r_i<B_i\right\},\qquad
 \operatorname{Ap}(S,3^q)=\left\{\sum_i t_iw_i:0\le t_i<3\right\}.     \tag{1}
\]
Each sum within either box has a unique digit string, including as a residue modulo its designated modulus. These are instances of the usual transport of a digit through a telescopic sequence. Our application uses the difference \(D\) of the two moduli and the same-class extremum of the actual rounding law. The semigroup here is generated by exponential-size integer weights. It differs from the linear two-generator semigroup attached to physical rank defects in the [earlier Apéry dictionary](../phase2/SEMIGROUP_BOUNDARY.md).

Indeed \(B_iw_i=3w_{i+1}\) for \(i<q-1\), \(B_{q-1}w_{q-1}=2^a\), and \(3w_0=3^q\). Carrying in increasing index reduces any nonnegative sum to binary digits and a nonnegative multiple of \(2^a\). The first differing digit proves distinct residues modulo \(2^a\) by its exact dyadic valuation. There are exactly \(2^a\) strings, giving (1) at this modulus. Reverse the carriage using threes, beginning by replacing any generator \(2^a\) by \(B_{q-1} w_{q-1}\). The last differing ternary digit proves distinct residues modulo \(3^q\). This gives the second identity.

Let \(C_B(s)\) and \(C_3(s)\) count the two Apéry sets at all nonnegative integers at most \(s\) in a fixed class modulo \(D\), with \(s\) itself in the class. There is the exact prefix interlacing identity
\[
                      C_B(s)-C_3(s)=\mathbf1_S(s-3^q).                 \tag{2}
\]
Extend \(\mathbf1_S\) by zero at negative integers. At a single integer \(n\), the binary and ternary indicator difference is \(\mathbf1_S(n-3^q)-\mathbf1_S(n-2^a)\), by (1). Sum this expression over the progression ending at \(s\) to telescope by \(D\). In particular the number of binary digit strings in *each* class modulo \(D\) exceeds its ternary count by exactly one, without any prime restriction on \(D\). Equivalently the two digit polynomials \(P_B(X),P_3(X)\), summing one monomial at each value in their boxes, satisfy \(P_B(X)(1-X^{3^q})=P_3(X)(1-X^{2^a})\).

### Arithmetic meaning for the greatest integer threshold

Following one full scheduled orbit \(B_i x_{i+1}=3x_i+r_i\) gives \(D x_0=\sum_i w_i r_i\). Conversely any sum on the first side of (1) divisible by \(D\) recovers the nonnegative integer \(x_0\) and all intermediate exact divisions: reduce the equality modulo \(B_0\), then peel the updated first edge and continue cyclically. Hence the complete list of integer fixed points of the scalar ceiling schedule is
\[
             \{x\ge0:Dx\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,2^a)\}.                 \tag{3}
\]
For the mechanical \(b_i\) of the [staircase inversion](STAIRCASE_INVERSION.md), its largest element is exactly the first grid resolution whose clock falls below \(a/q\). The alternating prefix law gives an exact accounting of the remaining representations, but does not presently localize this extremal residue near the mean of the digit box.

For another description in established terminology, take the cyclic matrix \((Mx)_{i+1}=B_i x_{i+1}-3x_i\). For \(q>1\) it has positive diagonal and nonpositive off-diagonal entries. Positivity of its inverse follows explicitly by solving the positive one-lap affine sum at any origin, since \(D>0\). It is a nonsingular \(M\)-matrix of determinant \(D\). Let \(z\) be the greatest fixed vector, \(r=Mz\) its stable digit vector, \(h_{i+1}=B_i-1\). Then \(h-r\) is \(z\)-superstable in the conventional chip-firing sense: no nonzero nonnegative integer vector \(v\) satisfies \(Mv\le h-r\). Indeed such a vector would give \(M(z+v)\le h\), which says \(T(z+v)\ge z+v\) by the integer ceiling rule. Increasing iteration stays below a sufficiently large supersolution and would reach a fixed vector strictly above \(z\). The complement duality of Guzmán and Klivans identifies \(r\) with the critical configuration representing the zero class. For \(q=1\) the cyclic diagonal combines its two entries; (1)–(3) still hold directly, whereas the displayed chip capacity is not the one-dimensional matrix's diagonal minus one. [Guzmán and Klivans, *Chip-firing and energy minimization on M-matrices*, Theorem 4.14](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1635)

On a genuine positive odd cycle with this exponent word one has the specific stable zero-class vector \(r_i=1\) at every edge. The two Apéry boxes then share its physical weight \(D x_0=\sum w_i\). The prefix identity alone allows this intersection and supplies no exclusion of an ordinary cycle.
