# Extra ternary digits of a finite word and the ordinary binary tail

Let the odd future start with the iid geometric gap code of parameter θ. Use the finite counted-fusion classes, total physical clock S_n and reversed n-letter affine offset Y_n of [the population theorem](../phase10/INTEGER_FUSION_POPULATIONS.md). Write Z_n=Y_n mod 3ⁿ. The r further trits below refer to Y_n itself with no newly sampled letters. All total-variation distances use the supremum-over-events convention.

**Fair source.** At θ=1/2, for a numerical C and all n,r≥1,
\[
 \left\|\mathcal L(S_n,Y_n\bmod 3^{n+r})-
        \operatorname{HaarLift}_{n,n+r}\mathcal L(S_n,Z_n)\right\|_{\rm TV}
       \le \min\{1,C\sqrt{(1+r)/(1+n)}\}.                         \tag{1}
\]
The lift fills each of the r higher trits uniformly, retaining the same lower trits and total. In particular every sublinear number of actual, still unrevealed trits of the finite prefix mixes in this joint sense. The averaging over exact totals in (1) is essential. It does not give the same error conditional on an arbitrary individual total.

Take x odd dyadic Haar and give it an independent uniform mark i modulo m=3ʳ. In the finite decorated n-class, a counted shift h carries (x,i) to (x+h,i+h). The conditional distribution in a finite class is uniform since each alternative has an equally probable gap word, the same total and identical unread dyadic future. Its conditional mark law is, up to a permutation, the upper-r-trit law of Y_n given S_n,Z_n. The permutation may depend on the class. Indeed numerators D_v=2^{S_n}Y_v satisfy h=(D_w−D_v)/3ⁿ, so after fixing the lower trits, multiplication by the unit 2^{-S_n}, a sign and a translation permute the m residues. The common unread tail and the orbit's initial mark only specify this relabeling. Therefore the left side of (1) equals the distance between the joint law of the decorated-class label and its mark and the independent product of that label's law with uniform marks.

Choose a second independent uniform mark u and take the unique even representative 0≤h<2m of u−i mod m. Then (x+h,u) has the same odd-Haar-times-uniform law as (x,i), and u is independent of the original decorated class label. If x,x+h have counted-fused by physical time n, they also have counted-fused by S_n≥n; hence the two decorated class labels coincide. Conditional on h, the dyadic source is still odd Haar. For unrestricted Haar and fixed positive h, Weaver 124, Lemma 2 with canonical state (0,h) gives a failure probability at time n of at most C√[(1+log(2+h))/(1+n)]. Restricting the source to odd Haar costs at most a factor of two. For h=0 success is automatic. Taking the coupling error proves (1). This uses the prior fixed-pair estimate of Leen in its scalar form recorded by Weaver. Weaver–Wren 48, §8, already gives stronger clock-retaining mixing up to a small positive linear precision excess depending on the requested power. Formula (1) is an alternative short proof from actual dyadic-pair synchronization; no priority is claimed for fair extra-trit mixing. [Weaver 124, Lemma 2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/124) · [Weaver–Wren 48, §8](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/48)

**Digit tail of the original dyadic integer.** Write its ordinary binary digits from least significant upward. For biased θ≠1/2 there is a single conull Borel set S on which two odd points are integer counted-fused precisely when their ordinary binary digit strings eventually coincide. To see this, fix one even integer h. On the complement of its counted-fusion cylinders the two translated source measures are mutually singular by the exact binary affine classification. Choose disjoint carriers there, thus deleting from the original space a null set so that x and x+h cannot both remain outside the fusion domain. Intersect over the countably many h and remove the countably many ordinary integer digit strings. Two such remaining dyadic strings have ordinary integer difference exactly when they agree from some position onward: binary addition of a fixed finite integer has only finite carry/borrow unless a tail is constant. Consequently the completed counted-fusion invariant field is the completed remote ordinary-digit tail field. In detail, invariant subsets of the restricted tail relation can be extended by saturation to invariant Borel sets in the full tail relation: its countably many finite-prefix replacements have Borel images and restrict back to the original set. Such saturated Borel sets are measurable from every tail. This statement uses no globally measure-preserving translation of the biased law. At θ=1/2 Weaver 124, Theorem 1 gives almost sure fair counted fusion over every fixed finite interval of translates, and a countable intersection gives the analogous fair result. [Exact binary translation decomposition](../phase8/BIASED_BINARY_FUSION.md) · [Weaver 124, Theorem 1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/124)

**A monotone entropy array at every bias.** Put
\[
 e(n,k)=H(D_{n+k}(Y_n)\mid S_n,Y_n\bmod 3^{n+k-1}),\qquad k\ge1,
\]
where D_j denotes the j-th ternary digit. The word can be read with its iid inverse order. Conditioning on its first exponent at length n+1 leaves an independent n-letter word after multiplication by 3 times a unit and translation. Conditioning instead on its last exponent removes the known final summand 3ⁿ2^{-S_{n+1}}, revealing the prefix total as well. Conditioning lowers entropy and these affine changes preserve the appropriate conditional digit entropies, giving
\[
                  e(n+1,k)\ge \max\{e(n,k),e(n,k+1)\}.             \tag{2}
\]
It follows that ℓ_k=lim_n e(n,k) exists, that ℓ_k decreases to ℓ∞, and that e(n,k)≤ℓ_k simultaneously at every depth n. The limits control the whole large-k array. At fair bias (1) gives ℓ_k=log 3 for every k. In the abundance range H_geom>log 3, g=(log 2)/θ−log 3>0, Leen's entropy row gives
\[
                \frac{(H_{\rm geom}-\log3)\log3}{g}
                      \le \ell_\infty\le\log3.                       \tag{3}
\]
Indeed for every c>1+g/log 3 the sum through k≤(c−1)n is at least (H_geom−log 3)n−o(n); its upper limit divided by (c−1)n is at most ℓ∞ by (2) and Cesàro convergence.

This yields a conditional sharpening of Leen's absolute-continuity test. For any fixed ε>0, use G_ε(u)=min(G(u),ℓ∞+ε), where G is the increasing concave entropy envelope in Leen's logarithmic averaging lemma. It remains increasing, concave and nonnegative. All but a bounded number of entries of each row obey this additional envelope by (2). The omitted entries cost O_ε(1) per row, which is absorbed in the theorem's o(n) error. Thus Δ>∫₁ᶜG_ε(log(t/(t−1)))dt suffices for finite-relative-entropy absolute continuity, for c>1+g/log 3. A strict bound ℓ∞<log 3 at a specified parameter would strictly lower the original integral, because G approaches log 3 as its argument tends to infinity. Its value at biased parameters has not been evaluated here. [Leen, tilted Syracuse laws, §3](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23268832)
