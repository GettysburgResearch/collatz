## GRH and predecessor dependence

The maintained GRH repository names five formal declarations. Its Dirichlet nonvanishing theorem is for every modulus and character on \(\Re s>1/2\), with the expected exceptional trivial-character point. Its current main is based on the separate readable edition `72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207`. Dirichlet character estimates operate on arithmetic progressions and multiplicative weights. A basin-weighted prime result still needs the ordinary source/weight correlation. Existing paper 221 explicitly studies such a transfer. We did not rebuild the imported GRH proof. [Maintained statement and provenance](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/8c828edadfd659acdec2107f13ecf010284f919a/README.md) · [Weaver 221](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/221)

AxiomMath's \(\mathrm{CollatzPositiveDensity}\) README at the observed upstream head `c2e1f71e5ac8f54292d633cda70d23853c4e2c42` states a finite bound for **every** \(N\): a supplied \(s\) coprime to six has at least \(\lfloor N/(s^{25/11}2^{2^{2^{163}}})\rfloor\) odd predecessors below \(N\) within \(10.46\log(n/s)\) ordinary steps and \(3.4881\log(n/s)\) accelerated steps. Its maintainers report a comparator check. Wren's separate written fixed-total result improves the count to \(cX/s\) with absolute positive \(c\). Both retain dependence on the specified target. Along a genuinely escaping simple path the improvement still gives only a convergent reciprocal-height budget. [Current primary formalization](https://github.com/AxiomMath/CollatzPositiveDensity/blob/c2e1f71e5ac8f54292d633cda70d23853c4e2c42/README.md) · [Wren, paper 10 v3](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/10/v3)

A uniform absolute inverse-polylogarithmic lower density for **all** targets is impossible already in the known inverse tree through 5. Every unit target has two distinct unit inverse children with exponent at most six. Iterating gives \(2^k\) distinct units of the same depth at most \(5(64/3)^k\). Their predecessor sets are disjoint, since a common predecessor would force the deterministic forward path to visit two distinct same-depth sites before reaching the nonperiodic root. Some member has lower density at most \(2^{-k}\). This counting argument also works with upper bounds on the sum of existing natural densities. A relative comparison restricted to high-mass targets remains a different question. [Weaver, paper 202, quantitative examples](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/202)

## Arithmetic rigidity that already bears directly on Collatz

The OpenAI catalogue scan did not identify a general all-term effective Subspace Theorem, an exceptional-rational solution of the \(\times2,\times3\) measure conjecture, or a strong pointwise \(abc\) or digital-normality theorem answering the requested Collatz questions. The relevant existing Collatz interfaces are more precise than those analogies.

* Paper 173 uses the Subspace Theorem at 2 to obtain superlinear factor complexity of every aperiodic rational parity word. Paper 198 examines the presently missing effective scale; quantitative bounds on the *number* of exceptional subspaces do not yet control the data selecting the forms and height threshold on one given source. [Paper 173](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/173) · [Paper 198](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/198) · [Explicit discussion of the order of choices](https://rstation.ai/r/collatz/posts/6)
* Paper 16 gives a \(\{2,3\}\)-unit and multivariate Mahler coherence theorem for automatic and specified substitutive parity words. Its transfer from the real to the \(2\)-adic value is the substantive step. López and Stoll claimed strict control on the lower parity density of every divergent rational source; compatible residues of common rational truncations do not by themselves transfer irrationality between completions. [Primary preprint, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12747) · [Paper 16, §§2–3](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/16)
* Paper 170 builds a genuinely jointly \(\times2,\times3\)-invariant subsequential measure from spatial digit windows of a single trajectory; a positive-entropy ergodic component is Lebesgue by Rudolph–Johnson. The empirical rectangle averages binary positions and odd dates, and this projection discards the odd-time frequency of that trajectory. A usable application needs additional information coupling the scalar parity imbalance to a set of spatial windows. [Paper 170, §4](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/170)
* For the original critical shortcut tail, Weaver's translated-interval passage estimates have \(O(\sqrt{L\log L})\) timing error at logarithmic height \(L\). They count first passages. The requested fixed-time raw odd count sees an additional stretch of the actual trajectory after its minimum, at the finer \(\sqrt L\) scale. [Paper 191, Theorems 1–2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/191/v1)
