# Real dimensions of prescribed-speed ordinary inverse rays

Let \(y\) be any supplied positive odd ternary unit. Consider infinite ordinary unit inverse rays whose every finite prefix first hits \(y\) at its last forward step. In inverse order let \(S_j\) be the cumulative gap sum, and let \(R_\infty\) be the limit of the increasing real corrections. For \(0.21<\theta<0.550024\), the marks of rays satisfying \(S_j/j\to a=1/\theta\) have Hausdorff dimension at least
\[
 \frac{H_\theta-\log3}{a\log2-\log3},\qquad
 H_\theta=-\log\theta-(a-1)\log(1-\theta).                            \tag{1}
\]
The proof uses the earlier supplied-root fixed-total row theorem, with its stated primary inputs. At \(\theta=1/2\) the lower bound is one. Full real dimension of the natural mean-two marks is already obtained in the prior fixed-root work. The new range here prescribes the entire limiting speed at each supplied ordinary root. If \(y\) is periodic, graft one legal unit inverse edge outside its unique cycle predecessor. There are two unit choices among the first six exponents. No descendant of the graft can pass \(y\) before the designated final hit, since a return to the graft would put it on the same cycle. An aperiodic \(y\) already has the first-hit property for every inverse path. Such an initial affine coordinate change preserves dimension and limiting speed. We may therefore construct rays in the full inverse tree of an off-cycle root.

Write \(b=H_\theta-\log3>0\) and \(g=a\log2-\log3>0\). For \(0<\epsilon<b\), choose a fixed sufficiently large block depth \(N\). Prescribe the total in block \(k\ge1\) by
\[
         s_k=\lfloor kaN\rfloor-\lfloor(k-1)aN\rfloor.
\]
There are at most two values, both \(aN+O(1)\). At **every** ordinary unit reached, retain \(M=\lfloor e^{(b-\epsilon)N}\rfloor\) distinct legal unit-ended words of that total and depth, each with its block correction at most one. The fixed-total lower bound provides this simultaneously, since it applies at the infimum over supplied roots. Each contraction \(\lambda_k=3^N/2^{s_k}\) is at most \(1/2\) for large \(N\). Hence all complete tails have real offset at most two. Each individual gap lies in \([1,\lceil aN\rceil]\), so the balanced block-boundary speed extends to every time along every ray.

At block level \(k\) every word has the same contraction \(L_k=\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_k\). Its mark center is \(y-L_k x\) with \(x\) its actual positive odd endpoint. Different words at that level have different endpoints, since the physical forward map prescribes the whole path. Thus the centers are spaced by at least \(2L_k\), and the full mark image from one prefix lies between its center and its center plus \(2L_k\). Put equal mass \(1/M\) on each retained child. Any interval of length at most \(L_k\) receives mass from at most three level-\(k\) prefixes, hence has mass at most \(3M^{-k}\). The lower and upper bounds on the ratio of consecutive \(L_k\) are positive constants for this fixed \(N\), and \(-\log L_k=kgN+O(1)\). The mass-distribution bound gives dimension at least \(\log M/(gN)\). Let \(N\) increase and \(\epsilon\) decrease to obtain (1).

The same construction supplies positive interval sums **using only genuine infinite rays of this speed**, with a finite summand count depending on \(\theta\). Fix one admissible large \(N\) with \(M\ge4\), and at each vertex retain just the words with smallest and largest centers. Their interval hulls are disjoint with gap at least \((2M-4)\lambda_k\), because the original centers were \(2\lambda_k\)-spaced and their tail images have width at most \(2\lambda_k\). The resulting binary tree has all unscaled hull lengths between \(d=(2M-4)\min_k\lambda_k>0\) and two. Its child hulls have relative length at least \(\rho=(\min_k\lambda_k)d/2>0\). An optional initial off-cycle graft scales a root by a factor between \(3/64\) and \(3/2\), or by one when absent. Thus the initial hull lengths for arbitrary root lists have a common positive lower bound and finite upper bound. Taking the minimum of their uniform ratio and \(\rho\), a sufficiently large finite number of summands makes the other hulls cover the gap when splitting a largest hull. The largest-hull argument from the preceding note applies without an infinite-skip option. Compactness of this finitely branching tree and uniform contraction ensure that every limiting coordinate comes from an infinite ray of the prescribed speed.
