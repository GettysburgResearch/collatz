# Positive-temperature pressure improves at every single new trit

For \(t>0\), let \(P_W(t)\) be the supremum of \(B-tA\) over width-\(W\) stationary inverse policies, where \(A\) is the mean gap and \(B\) the mean conditional action entropy per odd edge. Include all positive legal gaps. Finite cutoff approximations give the same supremum when optimized over the cutoff. For every \(W\ge2\),
\[
                         P_{W+1}(t)>P_W(t).                            \tag{1}
\]
Thus finite positive-temperature optimization strictly benefits from each extra observed trit, including in parameter ranges where the infinite pressure is already known. The zero-temperature proof in this pass yields strict improvement after an explicitly bounded number of trits. It uses a different inequality because a minimizing hard selector may ignore any nonminimal action. [Infinite-window pressure](../phase5/UNIFORM_INVERSE_PRESSURE.md) · [Zero-temperature finite-window strictness](../phase9/STRICT_WINDOW_IMPROVEMENT.md)

Put \(q=e^{-t}\), \(N=2\cdot3^{W-1}\). Let \(V\) be a finite-window soft Bellman bias and \(P=P_W(t)\), so
\[
 P+V(s)=\log\sum_{e\ge1\ {\rm legal}}q^e\exp\{\bar V(I_e(s)\bmod3^{W-1})\}. \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\bar V\) averages the three coarse terminal lifts. For existence, group together the congruent actions \(e=d+Nk\), with \(1\le d\le N\). They have the same next-state kernel, and the maximum action entropy minus cost over this group equals \(\log(q^d/(1-q^N))\) per group selection, plus the entropy of the group selection itself. This is an exact finite-state control with a compact simplex of randomized actions, bounded continuous reward and a communicating finite underlying action graph. The usual vanishing-discount construction gives (2): after subtracting a reference value, the discounted biases have a common span bound because any supplied state can hit any desired state in geometrically many bounded-cost attempts, by routing to its lower class and accepting the desired lift of probability one third. Pass to a convergent subsequence, apply continuity, and verify the constant equals the optimal stationary objective by integrating (2)'s inequality and its Gibbs equality. The optimal probabilities of all original legal gaps are positive, with geometric tails in each group. Finite cutoffs approximate this objective by truncating the geometric choices within each group while retaining its group mass and hence its stationary transition law.

At a coarse state \(s\), reveal its currently fresh top trit, giving one of the three fine lifts \(s_j=s+j3^W\) in width \(W+1\). For each exponent the successor has deterministic *coarse* label \(u_{e,j}=I_e(s_j)\bmod3^W\), and a uniformly fresh fine top trit. One may therefore choose the Gibbs action with probabilities proportional to \(q^e\exp V(u_{e,j})\) on that actually observed fine state. Its expected objective plus terminal coarse bias at \(s\), averaging the uniform observed trit, is
\[
 H(s)=\tfrac13\sum_j\log\sum_{e\ {\rm legal}}q^e\exp V(u_{e,j})
                   \ \ge\ P+V(s) .                                    \tag{3}
\]
This is convexity of log-sum-exp and the coarse identity (2). It can be checked on finitely many action groups modulo \(3N\), pulling out the common factor \(1/(1-q^{3N})\). Equality in (3) forces the three vectors \((V(u_{e,j}))_e\) to differ only by scalar constants as \(j\) changes: log-sum-exp is strictly convex modulo constants for all-positive coordinate weights, by its variance Hessian (or strict Hölder).

Fix any lower unit target class \(u\bmod3^{W-1}\) and select a legal \(e\) reaching it from \(s\). The three action groups \(e,e+N,e+2N\bmod3N\) are distinct. Since \(2^N\equiv1+d3^W\pmod{3^{W+1}}\) for a ternary unit \(d\), changing from \(e\) to \(e+N\) adds a nonzero constant mod three to the top trit of \(u_{e,j}\), independently of \(j\). Changing \(j\) also cycles through all three target lifts, by the factor \(2^e\). The three entries of these action groups are therefore permutations of the same target triple as the observed \(j\) changes; their row sums agree. If the vectors in (3) differ only by constants, these constants must vanish by restricting to those three entries. In particular the three values of \(V\) above \(u\) coincide. Every coarse source can reach every lower target, so equality at even one source requires \(V\) to be constant on *every* top triple.

The geometric action weights exclude such a bias. Write \(f(s)=\exp V(s)\). Put \(g(u)=\exp\bar V(u)\) on lower units, extended by zero on the multiples of three, and let \(L=\exp P\). Shifting the action index in (2) gives the exact recurrence
\[
 L[f(s)-q f(2s)]= q\,1_{2s\equiv1\ (3)}g((2s-1)/3\bmod3^{W-1}).      \tag{4}
\]
When \(2s\equiv1\ (3)\) but the quotient is a nonunit, the right side is zero by definition. If \(f\) ignores its last trit, the left side does too. As \(s\) ranges over the three lifts of any fixed source class (of depth at least two during the induction), the arguments on the right side run over the full three lifts at the next lower depth. Thus \(g\) is constant on those triples. Since \(V\) already ignores its top trit, \(g\) is exactly the descended \(f\) on units. This forces \(f\) to ignore one further trit. Repetition would make \(f\) depend only on \(s\bmod3\). Now take the three unit classes \(s\bmod9\) with \(2s\equiv1\bmod3\). The left side of (4) would be the same, whereas their arguments on the right run over all three trits, one with value zero and two strictly positive. This contradiction proves that (3) is strict at every source, with a positive minimum margin at fixed width and temperature.

Under the resulting actual fine policy, any stationary distribution is uniform in the fine top trit conditional on its coarse state: every inverse transition produces such a fresh uniform lift. Integrate (3) and telescope the coarse potential. The fine stationary objective is strictly above \(P_W(t)\). If a finite cap is desired, approximate the fine Gibbs policy within each of the finitely many congruence groups, preserving group masses and the entire transition kernel; its geometric tails make the objective continuous in this truncation. This proves (1). It gives no uniform lower bound on the increment as \(W\) grows and does not identify the limiting pressure outside the proved equality range.
