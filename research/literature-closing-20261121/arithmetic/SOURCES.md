# Primary sources for the arithmetic attempt

Checked on 2026-11-21. Only the original papers below are used as mathematical
inputs. Contemporary search results containing purported Collatz solutions or
new broad transcendence claims were not imported as theorems.

## BL96 — Bernstein and Lagarias

Daniel J. Bernstein and Jeffrey C. Lagarias, **The 3x+1 Conjugacy Map**,
Canadian Journal of Mathematics 48 (1996), 1154–1169.

- Author-hosted full paper:
  <https://websites.umich.edu/lagarias/doc/bernstein.pdf>
- Publisher record:
  <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-mathematics/article/3x-1-conjugacy-map/6975BB4A8C46CF6842217043AAF9EC13>
- Used: §1, formulas (1.5) and (1.6); the two-way finite congruence property of
  the parity coding; the ordinary/rational/2-adic distinction on pp. 2–3.
- Exact limitation: the paper proves that the inverse parity encoding
  \(\Phi\) maps rational 2-adic integers into rational 2-adic integers.
  Equality of the two rational sets is explicitly the **Periodicity
  Conjecture**. Its reverse implication cannot be used as a proved rationality
  transfer. Periodicity alone would also leave the classification of positive
  integer cycles to be addressed.

## MY04 — Monks and Yazinski

Kenneth G. Monks and Jonathan Yazinski, **The autoconjugacy of the 3x+1
function**, Discrete Mathematics 275 (2004), 219–236; author manuscript dated
July 10, 2002.

- Author-hosted full manuscript:
  <https://monks.scranton.edu/files/pubs/AutoConjV13.pdf>
- Publisher record:
  <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X03001250>
- Checked: Lemma 3.1 (an odd denominator is preserved), Lemma 4.2 and
  Theorem 2.7 (density restrictions on rational trajectories), and the
  abstract's conditional rationality result for the autoconjugacy.
- Scope: these results do not assert rationality preservation by the
  autoconjugacy or inverse parity map. Fixed odd denominator invariance is
  elementary and does not turn a 2-adic limit into an ordinary integer.

## Dub09 — Dubickas

Artūras Dubickas, **On integer sequences generated by linear maps**, Glasgow
Mathematical Journal 51 (2009), 243–252,
DOI [10.1017/S0017089508004655](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0017089508004655).

- Publisher full paper:
  <https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C40C0C07FEC20797475BB2899C436C9A/S0017089508004655a.pdf/on_integer_sequences_generated_by_linear_maps.pdf>
- Publisher record:
  <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/glasgow-mathematical-journal/article/on-integer-sequences-generated-by-linear-maps/C40C0C07FEC20797475BB2899C436C9A>
- Used: Theorem 5 on p. 246 and its proof in §5, pp. 249–250. For an ordinary
  positive shortcut trajectory tending to infinity, the proof gives
  \(\liminf p(L)/L\ge\log2/\log(3/2)\).
- Exact hypothesis: a divergent positive trajectory; no assertion that such
  a trajectory exists. Theorem 3 and Corollary 4 earlier in the paper concern
  the different everywhere-expanding map \(x\mapsto\lceil3x/2\rceil\).
  They must not be substituted for Theorem 5. The proof in §5 retains the
  actual ordinary source and obtains a lower complexity bound, not an upper
  bound or eventual periodicity.

## AB07 — Adamczewski and Bugeaud

Boris Adamczewski and Yann Bugeaud, **On the complexity of algebraic numbers
I. Expansions in integer bases**, Annals of Mathematics 165 (2007), 547–565.

- Publisher full paper:
  <https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v165-n2-p04.pdf>
- Publisher record:
  <https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2007/165-2/p04>
- Used: Theorem E, p. 555, supplies the precise normalized Subspace-Theorem
  inequality. The dimension, finite place set and full-rank algebraic linear
  forms are fixed, and the saving exponent is positive. No such saving is
  inferred from an inverse-logarithmic band.
- Checked: Theorem 1B, p. 561, and Theorem 6, p. 562, concern the **Hensel
  digits of the number itself**. The latter requires non-eventual periodicity
  and a fixed repetition exponent greater than one, with bounded
  preperiod-to-period ratio and unbounded period lengths. It does not apply
  directly to Collatz parity words. The real noninteger-base Theorem 5 also
  has a Pisot-or-Salem hypothesis; \(3/2\) is neither.

## Sch76 — Schlickewei

Hans Peter Schlickewei, **On products of special linear forms with algebraic
coefficients**, Acta Arithmetica 31 (1976), 389–398,
DOI [10.4064/aa-31-4-389-398](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-31-4-389-398).

- Journal archive full paper:
  <https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa31/aa3148.pdf>
- Checked: Theorem 1.1 and the local-form framework of §2. These retain all
  the local factors, fixed algebraic coefficients and a negative fixed power
  of the ordinary size. The arithmetic calculation here uses the clearer
  general normalization in AB07, Theorem E; it does not import an additional
  finiteness conclusion from Sch76.
