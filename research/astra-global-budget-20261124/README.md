## 1. An actual global account of the weighted duration

With binary length \(b(n)\), charge an edge \(u,v\) the toll
\(1/\max(b(u),b(v))\). The earlier bound paid for one visit to
\([X,3X)\). The new argument keeps the original path across all changes
of scale. A balanced parity word is one whose equal-length factors differ
in odd count by at most one. A balanced unabsorbed prefix has length
\(O(b(x))\), and its total toll is \(O(\log(2b(x)))\). Powers of two
attain this latter order. No balance assumption is made in the following
decomposition of an arbitrary path.

Cut at each first parity imbalance. Let \(L_j\) be the block lengths
and \(B_j\) the bit lengths at their boundaries. At every finite clock
\(N\) at or before the first hit of 1,
\[
 D(x,N)\le25\left[\log\frac{B_0}{B_s}
       +\sum_{j<s}\log\frac{B_{j+1}+L_j}{B_j}\right].                 \tag{1}
\]
Each summand in the remaining sum is nonnegative. It vanishes on a block
consisting entirely of bit-length drops. The bound includes arbitrary
returns to previously visited scales. **No bound on this remaining sum
in terms of the original source has been proved.**

We directly tested an integer reserve that is bounded throughout every
individual source's lifetime: maximize the affine carry over all words
with the same raw length, odd count and terminal residue. Its improvement
is nondecreasing and at most \(x-1\). But it cannot pay a late return
by the proposed mechanism. If all smaller starts converge and \(A\)
is their largest score \(2P_t-t\), a still-unabsorbed source can match
one of their scores only before
\[
                              t<8(b(x)+A).                           \tag{2}
\]
Beyond that clock a smaller comparator with equal raw and odd clocks is
ineligible. Even fully convergent paths can have zero improvement for
their whole preterminal history. The original size allowance and
comparisons at different clocks remain available, but are not paid here.
