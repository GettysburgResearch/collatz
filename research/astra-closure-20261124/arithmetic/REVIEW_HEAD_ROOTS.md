**Conditioning in (4).** At an arbitrary cap $H$, the fiber argument bounds the mass of one root by a count divided by the number of timely children. If those children occupy fraction $p$ of the original interval, the resulting product deficit bound is $O((H+1)^5/(pM))$. The version without $1/p$ follows at PR197's cap and barrier, where $p=1-O_A(d^{-A})$, or whenever $p$ has a fixed positive lower bound. The original text's first statement of (4) needed that scope. The later arbitrary-selection statement gives the correct normalization. Equations (5)–(6) use the PR197 population and the selected mass explicitly.

There is an explicit obstruction to dropping this hypothesis. Set $H=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$. Let $X$ be the smallest multiple of $81\cdot2^H$ at least $7R$, and choose $M=X/2^H$ with the non-strict first passage. This satisfies $M\asymp\sqrt R$, $M\le R^{.98}$ and $M/d^{14.3}\to\infty$. Both $X$ and $X/81$ reach the threshold by $H$ using only even inputs until their respective crossings. For any original child, one odd step by a proposed crossing $t\le H$ would give $T^t(x)\ge 3\cdot7R/2^H>M$. A crossing with $t<H$ would already give $T^t(x)\ge14R/2^H>M$. Thus each timely child is divisible by $2^H$ and lies in $[7R,X]$, which contains at most 82 multiples of $2^H$. The conditional mutual information is at most $\log 82$ although $\log M-5\log(H+1)\to\infty$. The strict passage has the same example with $M=X/2^H+1/2$. This is an arithmetic counterexample for the unqualified cap, independent of eventual convergence.

**High barrier.** For each fixed remainder, a low-bit prefix fixes whether the next input parities agree on the two arms. If they differ, the sign of the count increment is fair. Sampling the agreeing bits and mixed signs from separate independent streams gives the ballot lower bound without conditioning on the number of mixed steps. Distinct clocks give distinct powers of two in the high-quotient slopes. At equal clocks a positive discrepancy in the counts including the initial four gives distinct powers of three. Thus the cost of actual off-clock collisions over all good residue classes is at most $(b+1)^2 2^b$. For $b=\lfloor\lambda d\rfloor$ with fixed $\lambda<1$ this is $o(R/\sqrt d)$. The separate binomial exceptions on the two arms have the same order bound since $81$ is odd. Both paths cross by $b$, so disjoint prefixes give simultaneous disagreement at every higher barrier. The upper bound uses a uniform grid of $2^{b'}$ parents for each of 81 remainders while both arms remain strictly above the barrier. PR146 supplies exactly a common-clock fusion on this grid, which suffices for identical subsequent first roots.

**Clock signs and uniqueness.** Let $E_x,E_m$ be the products of the corrections $1+1/(3n)$ at odd inputs before the two crossings. For $x=81m+r$ the exact identity is

$$
\frac vw=3^{4+p_x(t)-p_m(s)}2^{s-t}
              \left(1+\frac r{81m}\right)\frac{E_x}{E_m}.
$$

Every pre-crossing input is at least $M$ and $1\le E_x,E_m\le\exp(H/(3M))$. The initial source ratio contributes $O(1/R)$ after the factor $81$ has been extracted. The two first roots lie between $M/2$ and $M$. Comparing two candidate exponent pairs therefore gives a nonzero integral linear combination of $\log 2,\log 3$ of height $O(H+1)$, or a nonzero integral multiple of $\log 2$. Rhin's power lower bound with exponent $13.3$ separates the first alternatives, including either sign, once the doubled tolerance is $o((H+1)^{-13.3})$. This follows from $H\ll d$ and $M/d^{14.3}\to\infty$. The finitely many bounded heights use the irrationality of $\log_23$. At $v=w$ the comparison with the formal zero pair remains valid even if the zero pair has not been seen among the sources. The signs give $s=t$ and $p_m(s)=4+p_x(t)$. **Physical stopping in (5).** A centered count for *every prefix* of the length-$l_i$ parity block has binary-grid exceptional probability at most $2(l_i+1)d^{-2C_A^2}$. PR197's noninitial entry bound pulls this back to an absolute original-source count $O(Rd^3(l_i+1)d^{-2C_A^2})$ per stage. The complete grid fits below $Y_i$ by its choice of $D$, and there are $O(\log d)$ stages. Mapping back from each parent to at most 81 children preserves that exceptional order after increasing $C_A$. Successive partial stage lengths partition each *actual* stopped prefix, including the final incomplete stage. Their centered errors sum to $O_A(\sqrt{d\log d})$ since the $\log Y_i$ decrease geometrically. Applying the product identity with $1-\log_2(3)/2>0$ yields the asserted bounds on $s-t$ and $4+p_x-p_m$.

Each complete $b$-step window before crossing has multiplier at most $2^{-cb}$ by PR197's odd-count test and by the actual pre-crossing correction. In a fixed residue class of input times modulo $b$, summing reciprocals backwards gives at most $1/[M(1-2^{-cb})]$. There are at most $b$ classes. This proves the logarithmic correction $O_A((\log d)/M)$. For a fixed root $v$ there are $O_A(\sqrt{d\log d})$ choices of $a$, a bounded number of $k$ per $a$ since $v/w\in[1/2,2]$, and $O_A(\log d)$ integer $w$ per allowed interval. This yields the asserted row bound.

**Original multiplicities in (6).** For fixed $w$, the parent range and the centered clock estimate allow $O_A(\sqrt{d\log d})$ values of $s$. At each $s$ the bounded ratio of the largest and smallest parent allows $O(1)$ odd counts. At fixed $(w,s,p_m)$ the ratio of any two eligible parents is at most $\exp(O_A((\log d)/M))$. The parent interval therefore contributes $O_A(1+R(\log d)/M)$ integers per fiber. Each has at most 81 children. Division by the actual selected population $\ge pR$ gives maximum marginal atom $O_A(\sqrt d(\log d)^{3/2}/(pM))$. The child arm has the same bound with multiplicity one. Integrating the uniform row bound against a probability marginal incurs no additional $1/p$. This proves the product-mass bound in (6). Entropy of the second marginal minus conditional row entropy gives the stated mutual information on the selected law. Redrawing the unconditional joint on an exceptional event of probability $\tau$ costs at most $\tau$ in variation and changes its product marginals by at most $2\tau$.

In particular (6) establishes separation of the physical joint law from the product of its own marginals. It still permits substantial weight on pairs whose two trajectories remain in different components. Bounding that weight strongly enough to sum over scales is the unproved inference for global convergence.
