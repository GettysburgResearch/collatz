The constructed final upcrossings all have a later return below the ceiling. Consequently the result leaves the permanently departing population uncontrolled.

1. **The all-future ceiling test.** Write $B=M/2^h$ and $t=\lfloor .4\log_2 B\rfloor$. During the initial $t$ clocks every positive start in $[.8B,1.1B]$ has height at least $.8B/2^t$. Therefore the exact multiplicative correction to $y3^{p_i}2^{-i}$, uniformly in the prefix, is $1+o(1)$, independently of $h$. At fixed binary depth the fair-parity word gives the nonnegative mean-one martingale $3^{p_i}2^{-i}$; Ville's inequality bounds the bad full-grid fraction by $1.2\cdot2^{-h}$. Endpoint error on the proportional interval is $O(2^t/B)$. The binomial test $p_t\le .55t$ gives exponent $1-.4(1-.55\log_2 3)<.98$, so the first entrance to $B^{.98}$ has already occurred. The actual-return theorem holds between that first entrance and the first passage to fixed $K$. Its ceiling $B^{.98\cdot1.004}$ is below $M$. On the convergent population, every possible $K$ landing has a bounded future, and finitely many such roots have a common bound. Sending $B\to\infty$ before $K\to\infty$ proves the lower estimate. The interval mean of the basin indicator tends to $\delta$ by differences of natural-density prefixes, since its width is a fixed positive multiple of $B$.

2. **A single growing progression with its proper normalization.** A counted fusion before $t$ gives a common actual future. Ceiling compliance is supplied separately on both physical prefixes. For $0\le b<Q$, take the union of the nonfusing binary classes for $y,y+b$ and the two binary bad maximal-multiplier tests (one shifted by $b$). Its grid fraction is bounded by $C\sqrt{\log(2Q)/(t+1)}+2.4\,2^{-h}$, uniformly in the shift. The small intervals for possible shifts remain inside $[.8B,1.1B]$ since $Q=o(B)$. With $F(y)=Q\mathbf1_{y\equiv a\pmod Q}$, CRT gives mean exactly that binary exceptional fraction for $F(y+b)$ times the exception on a full $Q2^t$-period. A nonnegative remainder costs no more than one complete period. Shifting the endpoints of $I$ by $b<Q$ costs at most $2Q$, since each of the two omitted intervals contains at most one supported residue. Finally $Q^{-1}\sum_{b<Q}F(y+b)=1$ exactly. This verifies (3) without assuming unconditional ceiling invariance or multiplying its relative error by $Q$. The separately checked input is the offset-uniform counted-fusion inequality on the complete binary grid. [Paper 13 v1, equation (1) and Proposition 1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/13)

3. **The excursion and its future mark.** For $t_0=6u+3$, the $k$ intermediate ascent values are $4\cdot 3^i2^{k-i}t_0-1$, $0\le i\le k$. They are positive, odd, and increasing up to $q$, so each of these $k$ odd gaps is genuinely one. The next three states are $r=(3q+1)/2$, $2w$ and $w=(9/8)(q+1)-1/2$. The numerical interval on $q$ makes the first two strictly above $M$ and $w<M$ for sufficiently large $M$. Thus the sole upcrossing on this excursion is $q\to r$. Imposing $w=2^h y$ with $f_M(y)=1$ ensures there is no further upcrossing, while $w<M$ proves this departure is not permanent. The progression $w=Qu+(Q-1)/2$ with $Q=3^{k+3}$ gives one $y$-residue modulo the same odd modulus, bijective to the allowed $t_0$. The target $z$ is an odd unit below the chosen $L$, as required for the incoming-atom theorem.

A bounded number of positive unit inverse steps, followed in reverse by their literal forward path, preserves the first odd landing at $L$.
