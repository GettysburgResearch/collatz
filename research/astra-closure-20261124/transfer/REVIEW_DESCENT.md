## Checks on the claims

1. **The floor is an integer.** For an edge $3m+1=2^ez$ with $e\ge1$, the difference $3(m+1)-2(z+1)=(2^e-2)z$ is nonnegative. Composing gives the length bound. At a target $y\equiv-1\pmod{3^d}$ the inverse-one state at reverse clock $h\le d$ equals $2^h(y+1)/3^h-1$. These are positive odd integers, including the final source. At $h=d$, powers-of-two divisibility on the source gives $2^d\le m+1\le F$ when $m<F$ and $F$ is integer. Thus $(3/2)^d(m+1)\le F^{\log_2 3}$. This last implication should retain the integer-floor convention: a real threshold would provide $m+1\le\lceil F\rceil$. In the original-floor application $F$ is an ordinary integer. For the critical-arm statement $r\ge d$ gives the needed $2^d(x-13)\le z$; strict comparison with $3^dx$ gives $m+1>x$, hence $m\ge x$. A moving minimum can equal the original floor without weakening the exclusion of $m<F$.

2. **Arbitrary late arrivals are controlled in target space.** Adding each complete orbit to previous ones, the new states form an injective path, a finite prefix ending at the old union, or a finite preperiod and a new cycle. Delete the last $l$ states of the path or preperiod when necessary. The remaining states, together with all new cycle states, have distinct $l$-step images on the new pieces. This proves (2) even for seeds on cycles, or inside earlier orbits. At finite $j$, the parity upper tail costs at most $(X+2^j)e^{-c_1j}$ source points; the rest have outputs at most $X(3^t/2)^j+(3/2)^j$ when $1/2<t<\log_32$. The expression $\log_32$ here denotes $\log(2)/\log(3)$. The last additive term is from the exact shortcut recursion. Taking $X\to\infty$ first gives the exponential image estimate (3). Images of $[1,KX]$ at clock $J$ are seeds for **all** later beneficiaries; (2) bounds those beneficiaries by the image of $[1,X]$ plus $J$ times the number of seeds. Odd relative density introduces exactly a factor two in this tail bound.

3. **Finite ternary cylinders and the physical limit.** For inverse clock total $S$ and odd length $h$, the integer equation is $3^hz=2^Sq-C_w$. There is exactly one residue of $q$ modulo $3^h$, since $2^S$ is a unit. Reduction recursively from the final edge certifies each intermediate integral, positive, odd predecessor. A negative additive intercept in $z=(2^Sq-C_w)/3^h$ admits the homogeneous equality $2^S/3^h=K$. For finitely many words with $2^S/3^h>K$, only finitely many ordinary $q$ can meet $z\le Kq$. Odd numbers are equidistributed modulo $3^h$. Therefore the physical density at each fixed deadline equals the Haar measure of the **union** of cylinders, without adding overlaps. The bound just checked controls the whole physical tail uniformly in the cutoff after its limsup. Monotone convergence of the Haar unions now identifies both physical upper and lower limits. This order is valid for countably many allowed prefixes as well.

4. **Renewal and binary normalization.** Conditional on an ordinary odd start, a full forward gap word of total $G$ and positive letters has dyadic cylinder probability $2^{-G}$, including the final requirement that its endpoint is odd. Under the auxiliary geometric law, $2^{-s}\exp(W_h)=3^{-h}$ on a free extension of total $s$. I get $r=\sqrt3\sum_{e\ge1}2^{-3e/2}=\sqrt3/(2\sqrt2-1)<1$. Starting at the first entry to a unit interval, any later visit at lag $n$ requires the displacement to be at most one, whose probability is at most $e^{1/2}r^n$. This gives the printed absolute occupation constant, followed by $1/(1-e^{-1})$ from the half-open strip sum. The empty extension is included. The adaptive low-total consequences give a positive failure proportion for all sufficiently large $b$ at fixed $K$. No comparable assertion at every small $b$ is needed.

5. **Two independent arrival clocks, same original source.** With $y=\lambda q+c$, $c\ge0$, every competitor $z\le Kq$ has $z\le (K/\lambda)y$. For any fixed first-arm word the map $q\mapsto y$ is injective. The number of odd admissible $y\le\lambda X+c$ has the factor $\lambda X/2$, so the homogeneous scale $K/\lambda$ cancels, and dividing by the odd-source count $X/2$ introduces no further factor. Positive compositions of $t\ge1$ number $2^{t-1}$. The empty route gives exactly $1+\sum_{t=1}^J2^{t-1}=2^J$. The limit is first in $X$ with fixed $J$. A growing-deadline finite estimate is not being silently used.

## A quantitative combination for original two-arm sources

The finite-word argument admits a uniform strengthening which matters because the stopped head barrier and the candidate prefix family both vary. Let $K>0$ be fixed and $L\ge2$. Uniformly in every finite or countable family $\mathcal W$, put $u(\mathcal W)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal W}2^{-|a|}$. Then for some absolute $\delta>0$,
$$
 |V_K(\mathcal W)\cap[1,L]|\ll_K L[u(\mathcal W)+L^{-\delta}].                    \tag{7}
$$
One may enlarge $K$ to the fixed integer $B=\lceil K\rceil$, as only an upper bound is needed. Choose $J=\lfloor(\log_2 L)/10\rfloor$. The finite version of the image estimate in item 2 is $|T^J([1,N])|\le C(Ne^{-cJ}+2^J)$, also when $N\asymp_K L$. The complete-orbit estimate therefore bounds all physical hits after $J$ by $O_K((J+1)(Le^{-cJ}+2^J))$. At shorter clocks there are at most $2^J$ full gap words in total. For $2^S>B3^h$ an allowed target satisfies $(2^S-B3^h)q\le C_w$, and $0\le C_w\le 2^S3^h\le6^J$. **All** such exceptional targets therefore lie in $[1,6^J]$, regardless of the number of words. For the other words their individual legality classes contain at most $L/3^h+1$ target integers. The sum of $3^{-h}$ over eligible words is at most $C B u(\mathcal W)$ by the same renewal computation (5), which already bounds this multiplicity sum before taking any union. Hence the short-clock terms cost at most $C B L u(\mathcal W)+2^J+6^J$. All error terms are $O_K(L^{1-\delta})$ for sufficiently small fixed $\delta>0$. This proves (7), including families changing with $L$.

At the polylogarithmic head floor $M=d^D$, $d=\log_2R$, consider either actual shortcut landing $v$ and its odd part $q$. There are $O_K(M[u(\mathcal W)+M^{-\delta}])$ possible integers $v\le M$ with $q\in V_K(\mathcal W)$, by summing (7) at $L=M/2^a$ over initial halving depths $a$. The geometric sums handle rounding and the last bounded range; decrease $\delta$ to lie in $(0,1)$ if needed. By [the actual-root marginal estimate (6)](HEAD_ROOTS.md), the fraction of all original head children for which **either** arm has such an odd landing is therefore
$$
 O_{A,K}\left(d^{-A}+\sqrt d(\log d)^{3/2}[u(\mathcal W)+M^{-\delta}]\right).   \tag{8}
$$
Here $A>1$ is fixed and $D$ is sufficiently large for the regular two-arm first-passage estimate. The exceptional term covers undefined or late roots as well. This is an absolute original-source bound, valid also after intersecting with the rare nonfused cohort.

Choose $D$ large enough that $D\delta>2$ and take the single word of $b=\lceil2\log_2d\rceil$ ones, or any family of total binary mass $\le d^{-2}$. The right side is summable over $R=81^j$. Thus a small prefix family cannot give same-scale inverse repayment on more than a summably small proportion of the actual stopped heads, even with unlimited continuation, nonuniform physical multiplicities, and either arm available. **Remaining implication:** if discordance forced this kind of repayment on either actual landing outside another summable exception, it would prove the head criterion. Existing constructions give selected witnesses in actual components, without this implication for discordant roots. Estimate (8) bounds successful repayment events. It does not by itself bound discordance.

**Direction of the remaining comparison.** A hypothetical escaping odd ray itself does eventually supply rare visible targets. Fix a tail-minimum seed $s\ge5$ on it, so $U^i(s)\ge s$ for all $i$. The exact product on each initial $b$-step segment gives
$$
 2^{E_b(s)}\le (3+1/s)^b.
$$
Choose the family of all length-$b$ words whose total is at most $b\log_2(3+1/s)$; this mean is strictly below two, so its binary mass is exponentially small in $b$. Every later ray point $y=U^i(s)$ with $i\ge b$ lies in $V_1$ of that family, using the *same* source $s\le y$. Also $i\gg_s \log y$ for large $y$ by the one-step growth ceiling. Hence $b$ may grow as an arbitrarily large fixed multiple of $\log\log y$. If a fixed bad unit $v$ feeds this ray, then for $M\ge v$ every member of its ancestor set has the same final upward exit at level $M$ on the common ray, with a single exit point. On the odd map that exit point belongs to $(M,2M]$ and to the preceding rare family for large $M$. Fixed-target positivity gives its final-exit atom positive original-source lower density on ordinary initial cutoffs. In contrast (8) bounds the *first downward* landing on the corresponding visible population. The first-landing theorems provide no domination of the final-exit law, whose point mass would have to be explained to exclude such a ray. An uncontrolled replacement of one stopping direction by the other would erase the central obstruction.
