## 1. Finiteness of dependent edges over all primitive bases

Starting from $x+1=2^k a$ with $a$ odd, the shortcut orbit has peak $3^k a-1=2^l y$ and $k,l\ge1$. Thus $2^{k+l}y=3^k x+3^k-2^k$. Positive dependent $x,y>1$ have a common primitive odd integer base $b$ by taking the primitive vector of prime valuations. If $x=b^s,y=b^t$, the carry is divisible by $b^{\min(s,t)}$. Even $s$ gives $k=1$ and hence carry one. For odd $s$, the odd alternating quotient gives $k=v_2(b+1)$. The bound $3^k\le(2^k-1)^2\le b^2$ for $k\ge2$ gives $\min(s,t)=1$. When $s=1$, $2y\le(3/2)^k(b+1)-1<(b+1)^2<2b^2$ for $b\ge3$, which gives $t=1$ as well. Thus every dependent transition terminates at $b$.

Set $U=2^k,W=3^k,c=(W-U)/b$ and $a=(b+1)/U$ as in the proof. From $W<U^2$ and $b\ge U-1$ follows $1\le c<U$. Then $Uc\le(b+1)c=W-U+c<W$, and $W+c=U(ac+1)$. The distance of $W/U$ to an integer is at most $c/U<(3/4)^k$. Mahler's original Theorem 2, p. 122, assumes coprime integers $u>v\ge2$ and gives only finitely many positive exponents at any fixed positive exponential accuracy. His own Waring application on the same page makes exactly the choice $u=3,v=2,\epsilon=\log(4/3)$. Its use here supplies finiteness of $k$; the subsequent logical steps are indispensable. The residue of $-W$ modulo $U$ picks exactly one possible $c\in[1,U-1]$ at each $k$, hence at most one $b$. For each remaining $b$, the independently checked coset theorem below with $h=1$ bounds all starts in $\langle b\rangle$ with end in that group. This proves finiteness of distinct transitions even as the base varies, without any uniform bound for the starting exponent being supplied by Mahler. [Mahler, original paper](https://carmamaths.org/resources/mahler/docs/135.pdf)

I also checked the Waring side condition. Writing $W=Uq+r$ gives $c=U-r$ and $q=ac$. If $a=1$, then $b=2^k-1$ would divide $3^k-1$; this is impossible modulo three for even $k$. For odd $k\ge3$, $b\equiv7\pmod{12}$ makes the Jacobi symbol $(3/b)=-1$, contradicting the square root produced by the odd exponent. Therefore $q+r>U$. This violates Habsieger's displayed fractional-part inequality (1.1). His §1 identifies the imported Kubina–Wunderlich checked range through $471600000$. No finite census was repeated for this review. [Habsieger, printed p. 299](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/82203)

## 2. Fixed numbers of mountains with unbounded individual runs

The telescoping weights in (4) follow by multiplying the $i$th single-edge equation by $2^{A_i}3^{P-P_i-k_i}$. The dyadic valuations of the signed internal terms are, in increasing order, $A_i,A_i+k_i$, followed by $A_{i+1}=A_i+k_i+l_i$. Positivity of both run lengths excludes ties. A nonempty signed subset has a unique lowest valuation, so its sum is nonzero.

Let $M=3^P x$ and $T=8h$. If every **starting valley** in a prefix of $j\le h$ mountains exceeds $T$, the peak relation gives $v_i p_i<u_i(x_i+1)\le(1+1/T)u_i x_i$ and the next scaled valley equals that scaled peak. Iteration bounds every such scaled valley and peak by $(1+1/T)^hM<2M$. Both the peak and its starting valley exceed $T$; thus each internal coefficient is less than $2M/T$. The sum of their absolute values is less than $4hM/T=M/2$. An internal-only subset cannot vanish by dyadic separation. A zero subset containing the initial endpoint must contain the final endpoint by size, and its complement then cannot be a nonempty internal-only subset. A zero subset containing just the final endpoint is excluded by applying the size argument to its complement. These checks do not impose a lower bound on the final valley.

After dividing by the **internal** term $u_0=3^P$ and transferring that term to the other side (with the fixed signs), the equation has $2j+1$ nonzero left coordinates and right side one. The normalized initial coordinate can be $g=x/a_0$ itself. The $2j$ unrestricted run lengths give one joint monomial homomorphism from $\mathbb Z^{2j}$, not independent prime pools for every output coordinate. Multiplying in the source and target group coordinates contributes at most $r_0+r_1$ more to the joint rank. The coset representatives, signs and rational factors independent of the run lengths become fixed coefficients. Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt, Theorem 1.1, uses a subgroup of the product torus of finite rank $R$, arbitrary fixed nonzero coefficients, and no nonempty vanishing left subsum. The bound is $\exp((6n)^{3n}(R+1))$. We may embed the rational groups in $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. With $n=2j+1$ and $R\le r_0+r_1+2j$ this is exactly the stated $E_j$. One tuple fixes the source; repetitions in the clocks do not increase the number of sources. [ESS, printed pp. 807–808](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0409604)

Starts $x\le T$ contribute at most $T$ distinct integers. For $x>T$, if the first small valley is $x_j$ with $1\le j<h$, fix its integer value ($\le T$ options) and apply the same nondegenerate estimate to the prefix, with the terminal group trivial. This costs at most $TE_j(r_0+r_1)$ per $j$, independently of what happens after that visit. If only $x_h$ is small, the full $h$ argument already covers it. This verifies the uniform count with no bound on either run length and no assumption about a changing prime support.

## 3. The gcd at every actual edge

The carry identity makes $g=\gcd(x,y)$ divide $3^k-2^k$. Set $u=x/g$, $v=y/g$, $c=(3^k-2^k)/g$. The tuple $(3^ku,c,-2^{k+l}v)$ is an integer zero sum with no proper zero subsum by its signs and positivity. The integer $c$ is coprime to both two and three. Any other common prime of the tuple would divide both $u,v$, contradicting their coprimality. Thus the tuple is relatively prime in the exact normalization of Evertse's 1984 Corollary 1 on printed p. 227. That corollary bounds the product of the outside-prime parts with their **full multiplicities**, for a fixed finite prime set, by finiteness of exceptions below height to the power $1-\epsilon$. Taking the minimum positive ratio over the finite exceptions gives the uniform positive coefficient in the paper. This is not an application of a radical bound. [Evertse, original Corollary 1](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1984__53_2_225_0.pdf)

For the fixed prime set $\{2,3\}$ the outside product is at most $uvc$, and height is at least $3^ku$. Therefore $vc\ge C_\epsilon 3^k(3^ku)^{-\epsilon}$. The identity $vc=y(3^k-2^k)/g^2$ gives $g^2\le C_\epsilon^{-1}y(3^kx)^\epsilon$. From $2^k\le x+1$ and the peak formula, $y<(x+1)^{\log_2 3}$. Letting $\epsilon$ be arbitrarily small after taking $x$ large validates the two uniform power-scale bounds. This controls loss of prime factors between the entries of each mountain and permits acquisition of new ones.
