# A complete-denominator bound for rotations that exchange two powers

**PROPOSED, with source-qualified Diophantine inputs.** Consider a sequence
of primitive positive ordinary shortcut Collatz cycles of lengths L tending
to infinity. Suppose two distinct rotations of each parity word have the
forms P U^a V^b S and P V^b U^a S. The words P and S may be empty, the
words U and V are nonempty, and all words and positive exponents may vary
with the cycle. Then

    liminf (|U|+|V|)/L >= (ln 2)/(3 ln 3).

In particular every fixed pair U,V is excluded asymptotically, even with
arbitrary common flanks. There is no effective length threshold here. When
both powered blocks have odd densities different from the whole cycle's,
the right side improves to ln 2 / ln 3. If at least one density differs,
the right side is at least ln 2 / (2 ln 3).

Fourier Wren derives the powered-block brackets and two-block commutator in paper 57. [Prior results and scope comparison](PRIOR_WORK.md)

## Exact difference

For a forward word W put B_W=2^|W|, A_W=3^ones(W), and let C_W be its
affine numerator, so the shortcut branches send x to (A_W x+C_W)/B_W.
Then C_XY=A_Y C_X+B_X C_Y. Put D=B_full-A_full. This number is positive,
coprime to six, and divides both ordinary phase numerators. Cancelling the
common factor B_P A_S, one finds

    D | K G_U(a) G_V(b),
    K = C_U (A_V-B_V) - C_V (A_U-B_U),
    G_U(a) = (B_U^a-A_U^a)/(B_U-A_U).

Indeed C_(U^a)=C_U G_U(a), and the equal total slopes and lengths cancel
the common flank contributions. The two phase values are distinct by
primitivity, so K is nonzero. The all-ones word maximizes C at each
length: changing any even branch to odd increases the affine constant
weakly, and for the all-ones word C=3^|W|-2^|W|. Consequently
|K|<=2 times 3^(|U|+|V|). Write N_U=|B_U^a-A_U^a| and define N_V
likewise. We have

    D <= 2 times 3^(|U|+|V|) gcd(D,N_U N_V).                 (1)

The positive logarithmic form L ln 2 - q ln 3 is at least L^(-13.3) by
the Rhin bound used in [the cycle-height packet](../cyclic-shift-distance/README.md).
Thus log D = L ln 2 + O(log L) uniformly for growing cycles; this follows
from D=2^L(1-exp(-(L ln 2-q ln 3))).

## Two sources of common divisors

Write gamma=2^L/3^q and eta=B_U^a/A_U^a. Both lie in the fixed group of
rational units generated by 2 and 3. When the densities differ, the
2- and 3-exponent vectors are not proportional, so the units are
multiplicatively independent. Corollary 1, equation (1.3), of Corvaja
and Zannier gives log gcd(D,N_U)=o(L) uniformly. Indeed the absolute
values outside 2,3 and infinity contribute exactly minus the logarithm
of this gcd to the sum of negative logarithms. All remaining terms are
nonpositive, both heights are O(L), and the height of gamma tends to
infinity. The same reasoning applies to V. [Corvaja and Zannier,
*A lower bound for the height of a rational function at S-unit points*,
Corollary 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0311030)

For an equal-density block write (L,q)=t(l,s) with gcd(l,s)=1, so
(a|U|,a ones(U))=u(l,s) for a positive u<t. Put
Delta_j=2^(lj)-3^(sj). These are positive and obey
gcd(Delta_i,Delta_j)=Delta_gcd(i,j), by the Euclidean algorithm and
coprimality of 2^l and 3^s. Thus gcd(D,N_U)=Delta_g, g=gcd(t,u) a
proper divisor of t. In particular its logarithm is at most L ln 2/2.

If both densities agree, write g and h for the two proper divisors.
Prime by prime,

    gcd(D,N_U N_V) <= t lcm(Delta_g,Delta_h).                 (2)

Indeed for an odd prime p dividing D and at least one block difference,
choose the larger valuation of the two truncated factors gcd(D,N_U)
and gcd(D,N_V). If this is v_p(Delta_g), lifting the exponent in
(2^(lg))^(t/g)-(3^(sg))^(t/g) gives v_p(D)=v_p(Delta_g)+v_p(t/g).
The other choice is symmetric, and 2 does not divide D. This proves
the stronger divisibility by the right hand side of (2).

For k<=t we have log Delta_k = lk ln 2 + O(log L) uniformly by the same
logarithmic-form lower bound applied to (lk,sk). Strong divisibility
gives log lcm(Delta_g,Delta_h)=(g+h-gcd(g,h))l ln 2+O(log L).
The expression g+h-gcd(g,h) is the size of the union of the subgroups
of orders g,h in Z/tZ. Enlarge each to a maximal proper subgroup.
Identical maximal subgroups give a union of size at most t/2. Distinct
ones, of prime indices p<q', give proportion 1/p+1/q'-1/(pq'), at
most 2/3 (indices 2 and 3). Thus (2) costs at most (2/3)L ln 2+O(log L).

Finally gcd(D,N_U N_V)<=gcd(D,N_U) gcd(D,N_V) suffices when at least
one block has different density. Insert the three resulting estimates
in (1), divide by L and take lower limits. The proof uses complete
cycle divisibility and never certifies an ordinary cycle from a word.
It does not exclude unrestricted primitive rotations.

## Finiteness for two arbitrary powers without flanks

**PROPOSED.** Only finitely many primitive positive ordinary cycles have a
rotation of the form U^a V^b with a,b>=2. Both root words and both
exponents may vary without bound. In particular the theorem addresses
both squares and higher powers. It gives no effective maximum period.

Rotate at the block cut to get V^b U^a. If these rotations coincide then
the original parity has a proper period, impossible for a primitive
ordinary cycle. If the densities of U and V differ, neither equals their
weighted mean q/L. The different-density bound already proved requires
a limiting lower root-length proportion ln 2/ln 3>1/2. Yet the proportion
is at most 1/2.

If the densities agree, continue with (L,q)=t(l,s). Write the two base
lengths u,v in physical steps, alpha=q/L, and the powered indices
i=au/l, j=bv/l, so i+j=t. Then g=gcd(t,i)=gcd(t,j). Write x=g/t.
In a primitive positive cycle exp(L ln 2-q ln 3)<=q+1, by the correction
product in the cycle-height packet. Thus alpha tends to
theta=ln 2/ln 3>1/2 as L grows. For any word W of length w and alpha w
ones,

    C_W <= 2^(w-alpha w) (3^(alpha w)-2^(alpha w))
         <= 2^((2-alpha)w).

The maximum occurs with the ones at the end: exchanging 10 to 01 raises
the numerator, and the all-ones suffix has the specified numerator.
The second bound uses 3^(alpha w)<2^w. Both base differences B_W-A_W
are positive and at most 2^w. Consequently

    log_2 |K| <= 1 + u+v + (1-alpha)max(u,v).                 (3)

If x<1/2 then x<=1/3. Each powered length is at least xL, as both i,j
are multiples of g. Thus u+v<=L/2 and max(u,v)<=(1-x)L/2 since both
exponents are at least two. Equations (1) and (2), using |K| itself
in (1), force

    1-x <= 1/2 + (1-alpha)(1-x)/2 + O(log L/L).

But the left side minus the main right side is at least
(1+alpha)/3-1/2, bounded away from zero because theta>1/2.

If x=1/2, the two powered lengths are both L/2. Now
D_+=2^(L/2)+3^(q/2) divides D and is coprime to both powered differences,
which equal 2^(L/2)-3^(q/2). This uses coprimality of the summands and
their differing parity. Thus D_+ divides K. The base indices are
t/(2a),t/(2b), so K is a multiple of Delta_h with
h=t/(2 lcm(a,b)). This divisor of the powered difference is coprime to
D_+, hence D_+ divides the nonzero integer K/Delta_h. From (3) we get

    1 <= 1/a+1/b-1/lcm(a,b)+(1-alpha)/min(a,b)+O(log L/L).

The first three terms are at most 2/3: they are the proportion occupied
by two proper subgroups of indices a,b in a cyclic group of order
lcm(a,b). The fourth term is at most (1-alpha)/2. Their sum remains
strictly below one as L grows. Both equal-density cases contradict
unbounded L. Finally, each fixed length has finitely many words and at
most one cycle for any word, so bounded length implies finiteness.
