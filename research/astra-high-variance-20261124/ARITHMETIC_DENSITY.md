# Positive inverse density can coexist with uniform synchronization and escape

The following explicit integer map tests a proposed implication about the residual return argument. It has a positive-density inverse basin at every vertex and admits a stronger translated-interval synchronization bound than the bound used in the residual construction. Its escaping forward orbit shows that these properties alone cannot force recurrence. The example is a different map, so any inference using the specific Collatz affine equations remains a separate question.

On positive integers set \(V(2^j)=2^{j+1}\), and otherwise \(V(n)=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Every vertex eventually reaches the escaping sequence \(1,2,4,\ldots\) or a suffix of it.

* **Inverse density.** For a nonpower \(q\) and every \(h\ge0\), each of the \(2^h\) integers in \([q2^h,(q+1)2^h)\) reaches \(q\) in exactly \(h\) steps. Its intervening binary prefixes extend the nonpower prefix for \(q\), hence remain nonpowers. Choosing the largest \(h\) with \((q+1)2^h\le X\) gives an inverse count larger than \(X/(2(q+1))\) for large \(X\). For \(q\) a power of two, use its nonpower child \(2q+1\). Every inverse basin therefore has lower natural density at least \(1/(4q+4)\).
* **Synchronous levels.** If an integer of octave \(d\) first reaches \(2^b\) after \(r\) steps, then \(d=b+r\). Its synchronous level relative to 1 is \(t=b-r\), increasing by one under each application of \(V\). All source values at level \(t\) are at least \(2^{|t|}\). In particular the minimum at each level gives a transversal critically summable at *every* positive exponent. The \(2^h\) nonpower-rooted ancestors above share one synchronous level.
* **Uniform synchronization.** For an arbitrary interval \(I\) of \(L\) positive integers take the common horizon \(h=\lceil\log_2(2L)\rceil\). Points which have yet to hit a power by that time yield at most two images \(\lfloor n/2^h\rfloor\). If the first power hit occurs at time \(j\le h\), it is \(2^b=\lfloor n/2^j\rfloor\). The latter floor varies over an interval of at most \(L+1\) integers, which contains at most \(O(\log(2L))\) powers. Each \(j,b\) gives only the final image \(2^{b+h-j}\). Hence \(|V^h(I)|=O(\log^2(2L))\), uniformly in the translation.

Thus even a lower density of order \(1/q\) at every inverse root and a polylogarithmic number of synchronized images can coexist with an escaping backbone. A positive result for the ordinary Collatz component needs control over the distinct synchronized *levels* supplied by the backward tree, or another arithmetic restriction. Exponential multiplicity at one inverse depth gives no such bound.
