# Returning below the original least nonconverger

**Universal target remains open.** Assume \(m>1\) is the globally least positive odd source whose shortcut trajectory does not hit one. Its component contains no positive odd integer below \(m\). This applies whether the forward orbit escapes or eventually reaches another cycle.

PR193 at d35bdfd9d0c75effda50bbb8040c136482431e24 proves that the forward trajectory of every positive odd ternary unit reaches 1 or \(U(B)\), the odd images of critical marks; its selected anchors progress on the same ordinary orbit. A general odd start becomes a ternary unit after one odd step. The forward point supplied is \(y=U(x)\) for a critical mark \(x\). Its arbitrarily large inverse lift is not required to occur on the same forward trajectory. [Pinned proof, sections 1–4](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/d35bdfd9d0c75effda50bbb8040c136482431e24/research/astra-global-synthesis-20261124/section/proof.md).

The least ordinary odd predecessor of \(y\) is \(p(y)=(2^a y-1)/3\), with \(a=1\) for \(y\equiv2\bmod3\) and \(a=2\) for \(y\equiv1\bmod3\). Since \(y\ge m\), the latter case gives \(p(y)\ge m\). The former gives \(p(y)<m\) **exactly when** \(y<(3m+1)/2\). A forward visit satisfying both the ternary guard and this original absolute bound would prove descent. Neither follows from the phase steering. More generally the missing incidence is an actual forward endpoint hitting the finite forward hull of smaller ordinary sources. Nothing requires the first such endpoint to have a one-step predecessor below \(m\).

There can be exactly zero gain relative to the underlying predecessor. If \(q\) has gap one or two and \(U(q)\in U(B)\), its critical lift has the form \(x=4^j q+(4^j-1)/3\), since allowable predecessor exponents differ by two. Every quarter deletion just undoes this lift, ending at \(q\). If \(q\) is a minimum of its whole synchronous level, the smallest-mark-at-common-clock projection of this \(x\) also equals \(q\): quarter deletion preserves the mark, \(U(q)=U(x)\), and any smaller synchronously merging mark would violate level minimality. Thus the critical lift's large octave drop has already been spent upon returning to \(q\). The statement is conditional on the indicated level; it asserts no ordinary escaping example.

## A two-sided finite-horizon test at this same floor

Let \(H,K\ge0\) and choose any odd \(m>1\) with \(m\equiv-1\bmod 2^{H+1}\) and \(m\equiv1\bmod3^K\). At every actual forward endpoint \(U^i(m)\), \(0\le i\le H\), **every positive odd predecessor at any depth \(j\le K\) is at least \(m\)**. All competing inverse exponents are unrestricted. In particular the whole inverse tree to that depth misses all smaller starts simultaneously at all of the displayed forward endpoints.

Indeed those \(i\) forward gaps are all one. Fix an arbitrary actual inverse word with positive exponents \(b_1,\ldots,b_j\), in backward order from this endpoint. Form its affine evaluation at a parameter \(t\) by starting with \(X_0(t)=(3/2)^i(t+1)-1\) and iterating \(X_l(t)=(2^{b_l}X_{l-1}(t)-1)/3\). At \(t=m\) these are the given ordinary integers. At \(t=1\) there is no claim that its initial *forward* word is the true itinerary from one. Put \(D_l=\sum_{s=1}^l b_s\). The exact difference is
\[
 X_l(m)-X_l(1)=(3/2)^i\,2^{D_l}3^{-l}(m-1).
\]
For all \(l\le K\) the evaluated \(X_l(1)\) therefore has only a possible power of two in its denominator. For \(i\ge1\) this denominator divides \(2^{\max(i-1-D_l,0)}\); the same holds with denominator one for \(i=0\).

All these evaluated values are at least 1. Before the denominator clears, \(l\le D_l<i-1\); comparing stepwise with exponent one gives \(X_l(1)\ge2(3/2)^{i-l}-1>1\). The comparison holds inductively because the previous lower bound is positive. At the first clearing step the previous value is at least one, hence the new value is positive and integral. After that it stays positive and integral. If the starting denominator was already one, \(X_0(1)\ge1\) starts this same induction.

Writing \(\lambda=(3/2)^i2^{D_j}/3^j\), each inverse step in the coordinate \(X+1\) multiplies by \(2^{b_l}/3\) and subtracts \((2^{b_l}-2)/3\ge0\). Consequently \(2\le X_j(1)+1\le2\lambda\). The exact difference identity now yields \(X_j(m)=X_j(1)+\lambda(m-1)\ge m\), as claimed.

For \(H=K=t\) the Chinese remainder theorem supplies a residue modulo \(2\cdot6^t\). Taking a representative between that modulus and twice it gives \(\log m=t\log6+O(1)\). One can instead insist on a mark: take an octave \(d=\lceil\log_2(2\cdot6^t)\rceil+2\lceil\log_2(t+2)\rceil\). Its marked interval has length exceeding \(2\cdot6^t\) for large \(t\), so contains the class. Then \(\log m=t\log6+O(\log t)\). These are actual ordinary starting values, with a potentially different \(m\) for each horizon. Their convergence status is not used. This finite obstruction gives no infinite exceptional trajectory and no impossibility of a later return below the floor.

## The variable correction at slope \(60/83\)

On a positive ordinary infinite L/P source, write \(Y_i=(60q_i+B_i)/83\). For odd integer targets the simpler coordinate is \(D_i=(q_i-Y_i)/2=(23q_i-B_i)/166\). The chronological target gap pair may use either split of 3 or any of the four splits of 5. An L source has carry 7 and its target carries are 5,7. A P source has carry 5 and its target carries are 5,7,11,19. Subtracting the two endpoint equations shows exactly
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 &\text{permitted initial residue of }D& D'\\ \hline
 L&0,7\bmod8&\lceil9D/8\rceil\\
 P&0,25,11,15\bmod32&\lfloor9D/32\rfloor
\end{array}
\]
Indeed \(8D'=9D+\epsilon\) for \(\epsilon=0,1\), and \(32D'=9D-\epsilon\) for \(\epsilon=0,1,3,7\). This supplies an exact integer test at every step, independent of source magnitude. A viable \(0<D_0<q_0/2\) gives a positive odd \(Y_0=q_0-2D_0<q_0\). Subsequent odd target endpoints are integers; the certified affine word and its unique dyadic residue force its physical parity bits, and ordinary forward iteration therefore preserves positivity. Every positive \(D_i\) stays positive, since \(9D_i-7>0\). The aligned boundary values remain strictly smaller; no assertion about unequal-clock intersection is needed.

The original infinite L/P source cannot hit 1: its future odd-gap word would then be all 2s, incompatible with either block. Hence every odd boundary has \(q_i\ge3\). Over \(k\) blocks with total time \(A\), the source equations give \(2^A q_k\le(9+7/3)^k q_0\). Since \(9+7/3<16\), the chronological excess \(4k-A\) of odd over even shortcut inputs tends to infinity. The target uses the same total odd and raw clocks, whereas any positive trajectory reaching 1 would thereafter alternate between 1 and 2 with bounded cumulative excess. Thus this \(Y_0\) would be a strictly smaller ordinary nonconverger. The missing point is the existence of such a positive viable integer \(D_0\) for the initial source in question. The choice \(D_0=0\) always survives and only reproduces the source.

There is a small exact obstruction to a uniformly bounded correction covering both labels from one state. Write \(Z=(5-B)/2\) for odd \(B\). The stated transitions become \(8Z'=9Z-83h\) for \(L\), \(h\in\{0,1\}\), and \(32Z'=9Z-83h\) for \(P\), \(h\in\{0,1,3,7\}\). For an indefinitely legal bounded all-L input, the partial map on integer states is deterministic and must eventually repeat. Reversing a periodic L cycle in \(Z=(8/9)Z'+83h/9\) shows \(0\le Z\le83\) on the whole cycle. On such a cycle the residue modulo 83 is multiplied by \(9/8\equiv53\). This multiplier has order 82: direct modular squaring gives \(53^{41}\equiv-1\pmod{83}\), while \(53\ne-1\) and \(82=2\cdot41\). A nonzero residue cycle would therefore visit residue 1, necessarily the integer 1 in the forced interval; it permits neither L transition. Thus the only bounded all-L cycles are the fixed points 0 and 83. Their only integer L-predecessors are themselves by the reverse congruence modulo nine. Since \(Z=83\) has no admissible P transition, the full bounded both-label core consists exactly of \(Z=0\), or \(B=5\), which requires \(83\mid q+7\) for an integral target. This applies to an alphabet-universal controller; an all-L infinite word is not realized by a positive ordinary source. On an individual unguarded path a uniformly bounded controller cannot accommodate arbitrarily long L runs, by the same finite-state argument and the invariant nonzero residue of \(Z\) modulo 83. No all-path classification of the existential bounded core or of the unbounded integer controllers is claimed.
