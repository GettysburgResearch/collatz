## Two different universal quantifiers

ACS-001 states a necessary condition on EVERY hypothetical least ordinary
counterexample. Smaller ORIGINAL companions converge by its defining minimality.
Their raw clocks are not reset, and their source labels are not replaced when
coordinates swap. Hence they cannot appear indefinitely at core-avoiding
checkpoints. The actual finite escape remains uncontrolled.

ACS-003 classifies EVERY finite collection of nonconstant positive-slope affine
source templates. It constructs parameters in a subprogression. It does NOT
prove success at EVERY fixed parameter. Positivity is obtained by moving to a
sufficiently large representative of the constructed class, never by calling a
negative number a positive witness.

The all-positive-input source-order goal still requires a merger with m<N for
the given N. A smaller companion at a later, larger orbit state is insufficient
unless it is lifted with a valid original-source inequality.

## Useful controls and rejected strengthenings

- Infinite return words with changing constructed sources do not contradict
  ACS-001. Their smaller companions and stopping times may change as well.
- `(2,1)` never synchronously merges, although its two affine templates have
  successful cylinders in every neighborhood of that parameter.
- `(3003,999)` retains its opposite raw core phases and no equal-clock meeting.
  A different companion from #124 is a different theorem.
- Incompatible slope cores rule out an infinite parameter set for FIXED words;
  they do not rule out isolated coincidences or variable words. The pair
  `5t+1,t+1` at t=0 is an immediate example.
- Choosing y's parity in ACS-002 is a compiler operation on a parameter cylinder,
  not a dynamical control available at an already fixed y.
- The open dense union of success cylinders is not known here to contain every
  ordinary integer or to have measure one. No survivor-counting or fractal-dimension
  claim is inferred.
- Constant source families with slope zero are outside the classification.
  No assertion about arbitrary fixed positive tuples is hidden in that exclusion.
