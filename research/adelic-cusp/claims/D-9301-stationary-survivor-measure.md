## Statement

Put

\[
\rho=\frac{64}{81},\qquad d=\frac{17}{81},\qquad
J_\varepsilon(x)=\rho x+d\varepsilon
\quad(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}).
\]

Let

\[
\Omega=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}
\]

with the product topology and fair Bernoulli probability `nu`. Define

\[
\pi_2(\varepsilon)
 =\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}d\varepsilon_t\rho^t.
\]

Then:

1. the series converges in `Z_2`, uniformly in `epsilon`;
2. `pi_2: Omega -> Z_2` is continuous and injective;
3. its image
   \[
   V=\pi_2(\Omega)
   \]
   is the unique nonempty compact set satisfying
   \[
   V=J_0(V)\cup J_1(V);
   \]
4. the pushforward measure
   \[
   \mu=(\pi_2)_*\nu
   \]
   satisfies the exact self-similarity equation
   \[
   \boxed{
   \mu=\frac12(J_0)_*\mu+\frac12(J_1)_*\mu;
   }
   \]
5. for every sequence `epsilon`, reduction modulo `64` recovers its first digit:
   \[
   \pi_2(\varepsilon)\equiv\varepsilon_0\pmod{64}.
   \]

Under the issue-#4 notation, `V` is the survivor attractor `V_infinity`.

## Definitions

The 2-adic valuation is normalized by `v_2(2)=1`. A series in `Q_2` converges when its terms tend to zero 2-adically. The product topology on `Omega` is generated by finite-prefix cylinders.

The phrase *stationary survivor measure* means that `mu` is one fixed probability measure; depth enters later only through the character at which its Fourier transform is evaluated.

## Motivation

The issue-#4 Fourier objects were presented as a depth-indexed family of finite measures on changing cyclic groups. That description is exact but hides a stationary source. Establishing one fixed measure is the first prerequisite for any legitimate attempt to import renewal, harmonic-analysis, martingale, or adelic-dynamics methods.

This statement does not assert that `V` contains an ordinary positive integer. It only freezes the infinite 2-adic object and its probability law.

## Proof or construction

For every `t >= 0`,

\[
v_2(d\rho^t)
 =v_2\!\left(\frac{17}{81}\right)
  +t\,v_2\!\left(\frac{64}{81}\right)
 =6t.
\]

Thus the `t`-th summand has 2-adic absolute value at most `2^(-6t)`, uniformly over the digit choice. The series therefore converges in `Z_2`, uniformly in `epsilon`. Uniform convergence of the finite-prefix maps proves continuity of `pi_2`.

To prove injectivity, let `epsilon` and `epsilon'` first differ at position `j`. Then

\[
\pi_2(\varepsilon)-\pi_2(\varepsilon')
 =d\rho^j
 \left(
   \varepsilon_j-\varepsilon'_j
   +\sum_{r\ge1}(\varepsilon_{j+r}-\varepsilon'_{j+r})\rho^r
 \right).
\]

The first term inside parentheses is `+1` or `-1`; every later term is divisible by `64` in `Z_2`. Hence the parenthesized factor is an odd 2-adic unit. The difference has valuation exactly `6j`, so it is nonzero. Therefore `pi_2` is injective.

Since `Omega` is compact and `Z_2` is Hausdorff, `V=pi_2(Omega)` is compact. Splitting off the first digit gives

\[
\pi_2(\varepsilon)
 =d\varepsilon_0+\rho\,\pi_2(\sigma\varepsilon)
 =J_{\varepsilon_0}(\pi_2(\sigma\varepsilon)),
\]

where `sigma` is the left shift. This proves

\[
V=J_0(V)\cup J_1(V).
\]

For uniqueness, let `W` be another nonempty compact set with the same invariance. Every `x in W` has, for each `K`, a representation

\[
x=\sum_{t<K}d\varepsilon_t\rho^t+\rho^Kx_K,
\qquad x_K\in W.
\]

Compactness bounds `|x_K|_2`, while `|rho|_2^K=2^{-6K}` tends to zero. Choosing a compatible infinite digit path and taking `K -> infinity` gives `x in V`. Thus `W subseteq V`. The reverse inclusion follows by the same finite-prefix approximation, so `W=V`.

For the measure equation, the first Bernoulli digit is fair and independent of the shifted tail. Therefore, for every bounded continuous test function `f`,

\[
\int f\,d\mu
 =\frac12\int f(J_0(x))\,d\mu(x)
  +\frac12\int f(J_1(x))\,d\mu(x),
\]

which is exactly the displayed self-similarity identity.

Finally, `81 ≡ 17 (mod 64)` and `17^{-1} ≡ 49 (mod 64)`, so

\[
d=17\cdot81^{-1}\equiv17\cdot49\equiv1\pmod{64}.
\]

All terms with `t >= 1` are divisible by `64`. Hence

\[
\pi_2(\varepsilon)\equiv d\varepsilon_0
\equiv\varepsilon_0\pmod{64}.
\]

This also shows directly that the two first-level pieces are disjoint. QED.

## Dependency audit

The proof uses only:

- completeness of `Z_2`;
- compactness of the binary product space;
- elementary valuation arithmetic;
- the definitions of `rho`, `d`, and `J_epsilon`.

The identification with the branch object `V_infinity` is a notation crosswalk: the same maps and digit order occur in issue #4. No issue-#4 theorem is used to prove the construction above.

## Gap audit

- A point of `V` is generally a 2-adic integer, not an ordinary nonnegative integer.
- Injectivity in `Z_2` does not imply any favorable real-size property of the same digit sequence.
- The word “stationary” refers to the measure, not to an invariant probability under multiplication by `81/64` alone.
- No external real self-similar Fourier theorem is invoked.
- The uniqueness argument uses compactness of `W`; it is not a claim about arbitrary unbounded invariant sets in `Q_2`.

## Adversarial tests

- All-zero digits give `pi_2=0`.
- All-one digits give
  \[
  \pi_2=\frac{d}{1-\rho}=1
  \]
  as a rational identity, so the two trivial points are present.
- If two sequences first differ at `j=0`, their images differ by a 2-adic unit; if they first differ at `j=1`, their difference has valuation exactly `6`. These are the first two cases of the injectivity proof.
- Modulo `64`, the image has only the digits `0` and `1`, as required.

## Remaining uncertainty

The proof is elementary and complete-looking. A reviewer should check the issue-#4 digit-order convention before treating the final notation crosswalk as exact; reversing inner and outer IFS composition would reverse the symbolic indexing, though not the existence of the stationary measure.
