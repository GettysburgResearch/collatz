## Paired inverse paths and the missing original sources

The latest PR197 projection preserves the two marked futures at the same original source. This is a substantial improvement over independently sampling endpoints. It does not assert a quantitative lower density for the exact disparate-component fiber of one fixed failure. Our [paired-prefix theorem](transfer/README.md) imposes an unconditional quantitative restriction on every such fiber: at clock h, its source height is smaller than `2^h` by an exponential in `sqrt(h)` when its unequal endpoint marks are fixed.

We also constructed an actual twelve-step inverse branch of the pair. It retains both components and the original residue on a specified class. Repeating that one branch at a fixed source is limited by an exact ternary valuation. The investigation of bounded-clock families is in the same transfer note. The unsupported step would be an unbounded family with enough original-source growth in the two specified components. A high-density census of other records cannot supply that growth.

The new [joint-target classification](renewal/README.md) makes the distinction precise. Under the pinned coalescence and predecessor-density inputs, two fixed odd unit targets have positive paired hitting density exactly when one lies on the forward trajectory of the other. An actual occupied fiber can have density zero even when its two targets reach one: k=3 gives targets 29 and 263. Therefore neither existence of a pair nor positive marginal basin densities supplies the needed joint lower bound.

## Routing produces quantitative common landings

The ordinary six-step cylinder `k=64v+12` coalesces at `243v+47`. An elementary fixed-total inverse route reaches this cylinder from every odd unit target while preserving the original parameter and controlling the clock. Varying its odd depth and using the irrational rotation `log_2(3)/d` localizes the original parameters in any fixed ratio interval, with any fixed common-clock residue modulo d.

Weaver's paper 12, Theorem 14 under its written selected-block and averaged joint-mixing inputs, supplies a linear scalar first-landing cohort. Routing it proves the source-qualified linear paired count. To separate the new construction from those analytic dependencies, we built a monotone inverse tree directly. Its finite four-generation weight exceeds one at every ternary unit class and is checked by Lean. A stopped-tree argument supplies the unconditional power count `(X/M)^(2/5)`. Both routes have actual first-landing guarantees, even for periodic targets.

These cohorts have equal futures by construction. They do not intersect a fiber whose marked futures are disjoint. The new distinction concerns the terminal event being counted, not the magnitude of a discarded error term.

## Arithmetic machinery applied to both complete denominators

Corvaja–Zannier's general polynomial gcd theorem was checked at its primary statement, including the proper translated subtori. It is stronger than merely comparing the two raw divisors used in the preceding packet. Positive Nielsen moves preserve an affine commutator that is a unit outside two and three in both coordinates. This eliminates the two long block carries in favor of a fixed pair of seed polynomials.

At an irrational limiting density, unimodular clock vectors exclude every fixed exceptional translate, yielding an exact symbolic cyclotomic loss fraction for both complete denominators. A separate argument treats the primitive clock on every exceptional translate by irreducibility and positivity. It proves a positive fraction at all densities, and the full leading exponents for coprime seed counts. The whole-seed restriction is explicit: the polynomial and exceptional set may depend on the seed. PR195's fixed-field unit machinery on discrepancy masks does not remove this dependence for unrestricted words.

The [pointwise seed bound](arithmetic/README.md) applies to a changing seed along one fixed escape. Mechanical blocks have at most m+1 factors of length m. Repeated parity windows force a dyadic congruence between distinct small ordinary states, so each of the two used blocks has length O(log(n+1)). This gives a linear lower bound on the seed length. The cycle case has an O(log L) block bound after the primary two-log estimate.

The attempted converse is unavailable. Even a hypothetical escaping component has a fixed ancestor with parity prefix 0011; all its later prefixes resist every nontrivial positive Nielsen desubstitution. A specially chosen later mark is a different possibility, for which no complexity upper bound was obtained. General free-group automorphisms can introduce inverse letters and cancellation and therefore do not preserve the physical-word hypotheses.

## Other deep interfaces considered

The latest adaptive-dictionary theorem in paper 15 gives lower complexity requirements at a fixed ordinary escape; it supplies no opposing upper bound. The capped inverse kernel in paper 127 has Haar stationarity and continuous-test convergence. Ordinary component indicators are not continuous in the ternary topology, so that statement does not transport a fixed component into another. The uniform sparse-image estimates in paper 28 still allow a single retained source. These statements were read as potential closing inputs; none was strengthened beyond its hypotheses.

The existing PR186 map already distinguishes the missing commuting action, invariant joining and regularity assumptions in proposed Furstenberg, rigidity and dynamical Mordell–Lang routes. This continuation does not relabel those missing hypotheses as consequences of ordinary integrality.

Full convergence is still open. The new statements narrow and quantify the actual pointwise problem, with complete finite proofs where claimed, rather than silently assuming the universal transfer that the previous Lean reduction isolates.

There is genuine overlap in conditional common landings. In PR203's bounded-offset inverse words, every outward prefix has \(0<\beta_j\le F_*\), and its last summand is \(1/(3G_j)\). Therefore every intermediate state exceeds \((q-F_*)/(3F_*)\). Taking a nonperiodic direct predecessor q of a sufficiently far above M keeps the path above the odd floor. Combining its depth bank with our explicit merged residue gives a conditional common first-landing construction, with additional compatible source digits. Its printed uniform statement restricts \(q\le X^\lambda\); the broader ratio formulation would require rerunning its estimates with \(\log(X/M)\). We do not claim that the conditional paired count is inaccessible to that method. Our unconditional power count uses neither analytic floor, and the explicit fixed-total two-gap argument and shrinking-window estimates remain separate.

Both prefix-density constructions preserve one target, or work after several arms have already merged. Neither gives lower mass for two prescribed disjoint futures. The antichains cannot be combined across arbitrary scales without checking comparability, and the many synchronized blocks can have different common roots and clocks.

There is a precise obstruction to inserting our elementary tree directly into PR200's FS. Its variable words have all odd gaps at least two, so no consecutive odd inputs. At length e and odd count p there are at most \(\binom{e-p+1}{p}\) such words. At FS's balance \(p/e\to1/2\), this is \(\exp(o(e))\), whereas its required multiplicity is \(\exp((\log2-\tfrac12\log3+o(1))e)\). A prescribed word gives at most one ordinary source at a fixed target. Bounded end pieces do not remove this exponential deficit. The analytic scalar landing input allows unrestricted words, but does not retain FS's exact clock, bounded offset and ratio-five relative-state menu.

Mere ternary integrality of a menu is also insufficient for FS. With \(k=0,D=-3,B=1/2\), its positive unit companion at \(y\equiv1\bmod3\) is \(5y-3\), but its required ordinary real offset would be \(5\beta-3<0\). This does not contradict the common-suffix theorem. It means that FS still needs a physically attainable menu and enough prefixes at the single supplied target.

Finally, PR203's companion diagnostic explains why a bounded actual dyadic coordinate is not a bound on the global algebraic height. Under hypothetical algebraicity of degree d>1, the other dyadic embeddings of the tail have valuation \(-(i+P_i)+O(1)\), and the global height grows at least \((d-1)(i+P_i)\log2/d-O(1)\). The ordinary source has not supplied the extra control required by a Northcott or Subspace Theorem closure. No algebraicity assertion is made.
