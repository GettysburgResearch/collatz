# The primitive clock survives even on every exceptional translate

**PROPOSED written theorem.** Keep the fixed seed, two-letter positive Nielsen dictionary and two reduced denominators of [the full fraction theorem](DENOMINATORS.md). For *every* sequence with expanding length $L\to\infty$, without a hypothesis on $p/L$,
$$
 \log d\ \ge\ \tfrac1g\log M-o(L),\qquad
 \log d_*\ \ge\ \tfrac1g\log M_*-o(L), \qquad g=\gcd(a,b).       \tag{5}
$$
In particular when the seed counts are coprime both denominators have their entire leading exponents, even when the density tends to a rational number or does not converge. The sole asymptotic dependencies are the same Corvaja–Zannier theorem on *fixed* polynomials and Lagarias's two-log bound. Constants may depend on the seed.

Write $W=X^{a/g}Y^{b/g}$ and $F_0=W-1$. The polynomial $F_0$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ by the primitive monomial change. Since $S_1(1,1)=b>0$, it is coprime to $S_1$. At every prime away from six, (2) of the companion note gives
$$
 \min(v_\ell(F_0(U,V)),v_\ell(C))
 \ \le\ \min(v_\ell(F_0(U,V)),v_\ell(S_1(U,V))).
$$
Indeed $F_0\mid F$ in the polynomial ring and the coefficient $K$ of $S_1$ is a unit. Thus it suffices to bound the outside-$S$ numerical gcd of $F_0,S_1$ by $\exp(o(L))$. The gcd theorem gives this off the exceptional translates for each fixed $\epsilon$. We handle those translates directly. [Corvaja and Zannier, Theorem 1, proof of (2.30)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0311030)

Use a primitive equation $U^rV^s=\lambda$ for a one-dimensional translate. If it contains any dictionary point, $\lambda$ is a positive rational $S$-unit. Its two valuations $(v_2(\lambda),v_3(\lambda))$ are relatively prime: they are the image of the primitive vector $(r,s)$ under the unimodular exponent matrix of this actual pair of slopes. In particular $\lambda\ne1$. Choose a complementary unimodular monomial coordinate $T=U^hV^j$. The variables $U,V$ are Laurent monomials in $(\lambda,T)$. The primitive character $W=\lambda^uT^v$ has $\gcd(u,v)=1$. If $v=0$, $F_0(U,V)$ is a fixed nonzero rational and its gcd is bounded. Otherwise the Laurent polynomial $\lambda^uT^v-1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ up to units. To see this, reduce it to $T^{|v|}-c$ for $c=\lambda^{u}$ or $\lambda^{-u}$. All complex roots have the same absolute value $c^{1/|v|}$. A monic rational factor of degree $j_0$ has rational nonzero constant coefficient of absolute value $c^{j_0/|v|}$. Taking its valuations at two and three yields $|v|\mid j_0v_2(c)$ and $|v|\mid j_0v_3(c)$. Since the two valuations of $\lambda$ are relatively prime and $\gcd(u,v)=1$, this forces $|v|\mid j_0$. This also covers $u=0$, when $|v|=1$.

This binomial has a positive real root. At that root both $U,V$ are positive, and the sum of the $b$ suffix monomials defining $S_1(U,V)$ is strictly positive. Irreducibility therefore makes $F_0,S_1$ coprime after restriction to the translate. Bézout in $\mathbb Q[T^{\pm1}]$, followed by clearing rational coefficient denominators, expresses a fixed nonzero integer as a Laurent-polynomial combination with coefficients in $\mathbb Z[1/6]$. At every rational $S$-unit $T$ the outside-$S$ numerical gcd divides this integer. Each exceptional translate gives its own constant; their number is finite at each $\epsilon$. Isolated points give bounded gcd as well. Taking $L\to\infty$ and then $\epsilon\to0$ proves the required $o(L)$ bound, since $h(U),h(V)=O(L)$.

The numerator of $F_0(U,V)$ is a difference of the two coprime prime powers at the primitive clocks. Outside two and three its logarithmic size is $(\log M)/g+O(\log(2L))$, or $(\log M_*)/g+O(\log(2L))$ for the companion. Here the odd count $p$ is positive since the used dictionary includes both original letters by unimodularity. The two-log lower bound applies even if the smaller exponent stays bounded. The raw phase denominator, after cancellation of $C$, contains at least the uncancelled part of this factor, prime power by prime power. This proves (5). [Lagarias, Lemma 2.2(1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0512006)

**Why the argument singles out the primitive clock.** The higher cyclotomic factors can acquire new common roots on specialization. For example the primitive seed $011100110010$ has $(a,b)=(6,6)$. On the curve $XY=-1$, evaluation of $S_1$ gives $-1+3X-2X^2$, which is nonzero as a Laurent polynomial. The factor $XY+1$ is thus absent from its symbolic gcd. Nevertheless on the translate $X=1/2$ its specialization vanishes at $Y=-2$, so this factor *does* divide the specialized seed sum. The actual dictionaries $A=0,B=0^k1$ have $X=1/2,Y=3/2^{k+1}$ in the physical coordinate. By (2), since $X-1=-1/2$ is a unit, the entire evaluated factor divides the numerical cancellation outside $S$. Its cleared numerator is $3+2^{k+2}$. In the companion coordinate for the same dictionary $X=3/2$, for which the same restricted polynomial gives $-1$. This example concerns the specified factor; no claim of equality or inequality of the sums of all exceptional losses is needed.

Even (5) requires a fixed seed as $L$ grows. The arbitrarily long irreducible words in the main note preclude an unrestricted reduction of the positive-input word language to finitely many seeds.
