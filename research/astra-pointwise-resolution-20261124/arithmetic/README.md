# How an ordinary orbit constrains two substituted blocks

**PROPOSED written theorems.** Use the shortcut branches $x/2,(3x+1)/2$, recording one bit per step. Start with dictionary $A=0,B=1$. One may swap the two members or replace one by its concatenation with the other in either order. These are the elementary positive Nielsen moves used here. An expansion of a seed word $w$ reads $A$ or $B$ for each seed bit. A *used* member actually occurs in the expansion.

1. On a fixed escaping positive integer orbit from $n$, every used member of every such expansion of a prefix of length $L$ has length at most $C\log(n+1)$. Consequently **every seed for the prefix has length at least $L/[C\log(n+1)]$**. The constant is uniform in the seed, dictionary, directive and depth.
2. On any primitive positive integer cycle of period $L$ and minimum $m$, every used member in a circular expansion has length at most $C\log(m+1)$. Its seed length is at least $L/[C\log(2L)]$ after changing the absolute constant. This includes a minimum inside a member.
3. For a fixed seed containing both symbols, [the complete denominator theorem](DENOMINATORS.md) gives the *same explicit fraction* of the leading logarithmic denominator in the physical coordinate and its dyadic companion, along every such expanding sequence with irrational limiting odd density. The [exceptional-translate argument](EXCEPTIONAL_TRANSLATES.md) gives a positive fraction at every density, and the full leading exponents whenever the two seed counts are coprime. Conclusions 1–2 apply to varying seeds and require no limiting density.

## A mechanical factor on an ordinary orbit

A periodic rational mechanical binary sequence has differences $b_i=\lfloor(i+1)t+u\rfloor-\lfloor it+u\rfloor$ with rational $0\le t\le1$. Include constant sequences and all translates. The periodic repetitions of each dictionary member are mechanical. To verify this, view the dictionary construction as a composition of elementary substitutions on individual letters. The class of directives includes their reverse orders. For a nonconstant mechanical sequence the positions of its successive ones are
$$
 p_j=\lceil(j-u)/t\rceil-1,\qquad j\in\mathbb Z.
$$
Inserting a zero immediately before or after every one changes the ordered positions to $p_j+j+c$ for an integer $c$, depending on time origin and side. These are again ceiling positions, with spacing parameter $1/t+1$. Conversely such ceiling positions describe a mechanical sequence by the same formula. Swapping letters preserves the family. The complement is a ceiling-difference coding at slope $1-t$, and for a rational slope the finitely many fractional parts allow one fixed intercept change turning its ceiling differences into floor differences. This handles insertion of a one next to a zero by conjugation. Constant inputs give constant or alternating outputs. Start with the periodic repetitions of a single letter. A member and any consecutive repetitions therefore have at most $k+1$ distinct length-$k$ factors. Indeed each of the $k$ prefix sums $\lfloor jt+v\rfloor$, $1\le j\le k,\ 0\le v<1$, changes at most once as $v$ ranges over the intercepts. The bound includes segments starting partway through a member.

Consider a length-$N$ segment of such a periodic sequence actually followed from the positive integer $y$, and assume the **$N$ inputs** on this segment are distinct. If $N\ge2k+1$ and $k\ge1$, the $k+2$ starts at offsets $0,\ldots,k+1$ have complete length-$k$ words in the segment. Two words agree. Their distinct ordinary states differ by a nonzero multiple of $2^k$, by subtracting the common physical affine equations. Since $T^j(y)+1\le(3/2)^j(y+1)$,
$$
 (4/3)^k\le \tfrac32(y+1).
$$
With $k=\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor$ this proves
$$
 N\le 2+2\log\big(\tfrac32(y+1)\big)/\log(4/3) .                 \tag{1}
$$
The small cases $N\le2$ satisfy the same bound. This applies the known physical parity-complexity obstruction at the start of each complete mechanical run. ## Applying the bound at a fixed source

On an escape all states are distinct. The initial run of copies of the first member is one segment in (1), of total length $O(\log(n+1))$. If the other member occurs, its first copy starts immediately there, at a state with $\log(y+1)=O(\log(n+1))$ by the same height bound. Equation (1) bounds the length of that whole member too. This proves conclusion 1. The proof applies to any injective finite prefix and to any two blocks with periodic mechanical repetitions, even if they do not share a directive.

For a primitive cycle rotate to a minimum. If just one member occurs, all $L$ bits starting at the minimum are mechanical. Otherwise, start partway through a run of the current member. Follow its remaining suffix and copies until the first switch. Then follow the entire run of the other member until the switch back. Each segment is mechanical, of length at most $L$, with distinct inputs by primitivity. Applying (1) successively bounds their lengths by $O(\log(m+1))$. The state at the switch back still has logarithmic height $O(\log(m+1))$. One entire copy of the original member from there has this same length bound, even if it passes the original mark. Finally, the cycle product gives $g=L\log 2-p\log 3=\sum_{e_i=1}\log(1+1/(3x_i))\le p/(3m)$. The lower bound $g\gg L^{-13.3}$ gives $m\ll L^{14.3}$. Summing over the seed letters proves conclusion 2. [Lagarias, Lemma 2.2(1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0512006)

## The missing pointwise implication

One positive source known to converge has arbitrarily long prefixes with no shortening at all under these moves. The shortcut orbit of $3$ begins $3,5,8,4,2,1,2,\ldots$. Every prefix of length at least four contains both $11$ and $00$. In the image of any outermost nonpermutation elementary substitution, one letter is always immediately adjacent on a prescribed side to the other within its image. Consecutive occurrences of that protected letter are impossible. Outer swaps merely rename the letters. A word having both doublets has no nontrivial desubstitution, even up to rotation when both doublets are circular. For the displayed source the doublets already occur internally with its original mark.

Every prescribed finite word containing both doublets also occurs from a positive source that hits 1. Choose its unique residue modulo $2^L$. On positive lifts of that residue, the physical endpoint runs through a tail of an arithmetic progression of difference $3^p$. The integers $(4^j-1)/3$ exhaust every residue modulo $3^p$ repeatedly, since $4$ has order $3^p$ modulo $3^{p+1}$, and each hits 1. This provides changing positive sources for primitive words $0^{L-p}1^p$ with arbitrarily large $L$ and with $p/L$ approaching the critical ordinary density from below. Closing these words at the same positive source would require more than their finite legality.

The same obstruction holds inside any hypothetical escaping component. Take a ternary-unit state $n$ on the escape; the first odd step gives such a state if necessary. Choose large $k$ with $2^{k+4}n\equiv20\bmod9$, possible because $2$ generates the units modulo $9$, and set $m=(2^{k+4}n-20)/9>0$. Then $m\equiv12\bmod16$, its first four bits are $0011$, and $T^4m=(9m+20)/16=2^k n$. Thus $T^{k+4}m=n$. The single positive ancestor $m$ escapes in the very same component, and its minimum seed length is exactly $L$ for **every** prefix of length $L\ge4$. This rules out deriving a prefix shortening merely from finite computability and a shared ordinary future. Choosing a distinguished later starting mark using the future remains a separate possibility. The Subspace Theorem for the fixed seed polynomials gives no upper bound on this changing seed length. Adamczewski and Bugeaud's Theorems 1 and 1B give lower complexity bounds for the ordinary base-$b$ or Hensel digits of an algebraic irrational; neither identifies the observed parity bits with such an expansion of the fixed integer. [Adamczewski and Bugeaud, §§2, 6](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2007/165-2/p04)

On a failed merger whose left arm reaches 1, conclusions 1–2 apply to the nonconvergent right arm in the appropriate case. Neither supplies the shared state. The unrestricted parity language allows the long seeds required by both bounds. Whether physical integrality forces a successful triadic companion or excludes all positive integer cycles remains open.
