# Positive smooth summands along one ordinary orbit

**Status.** Written theorem for a fixed ordinary positive source, conditional on the cited published unit-equation theorem. The argument has not been formalized in Lean.

For positive accelerated odd Collatz iterates write \(2^{a_i}q_{i+1}=3q_i+1\). Fix a finite prime set \(P\) containing 2 and 3. Write \(h_P(x)\) for the least number of positive **integer** \(P\)-units summing to \(x\). This exists since 1 is allowed. Put \(\alpha=\log_2 3\).

**Theorem.** If the orbit is not eventually periodic, the empirical distributions of \(h_P(q_i)\), \(0\le i<N\), have no tight subsequence as \(N\to\infty\). Consequently every fixed \(K\) satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty} N^{-1}\#\{i<N:h_P(q_i)>K\}>0.
\]
For every nonnegative \(f\) with \(f(k)\to\infty\), the prefix means of \(f(h_P(q_i))\) tend to infinity. Conversely any eventually periodic positive orbit has bounded \(h_P\).

On any subsequence with \(\log q_N/N\to\gamma>0\), the lower density along **that subsequence** in the displayed assertion is at least \(\gamma/\log(3/2)\), for every fixed \(K\). This is a quantitative bound on the proportion of high-complexity **individual states**. It applies even when \(P\) contains every prime divisor of the initial source.

## Compactification and the clock

Choose an ordered minimal presentation of each \(q_i\) (e.g. lexicographically first nondecreasing list) and pad it with zeros. Mark date \(i\) by the gap \(a_i\), length \(h_i\), and padded sequence of summands. Each coordinate takes values in the compact metrizable one-point compactification of the nonnegative integers. The mark space and its two-sided sequence space are compact metrizable countable products. Extend the original sequence at negative times by arbitrary fixed marks. Subsequence limits of the empirical distributions of its first \(N\) shifts exist and are shift invariant by telescoping continuous test functions. They are supported on the tail orbit closure, since deleting finitely many shifts changes no limit.

Nonperiodicity implies injectivity and \(q_i\to\infty\). The exact clock gives
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{i<N}a_i=\alpha-\frac{\log q_N-\log q_0}{N\log 2}
       +\frac1N\sum_{i<N}\log_2(1+1/(3q_i)).
\]
The last term tends to zero by Cesàro averaging. For a limit law \(\mu\), truncation of \(a_0\) gives \(\int a_0\,d\mu\le\liminf N^{-1}\sum a_i\) along its convergent subsequence. Thus all gaps are finite almost surely. Tight lengths would likewise give finite lengths at all dates almost surely. Without tightness put \(G=\{h_0<\infty\}\) and let \(S\) be its two-sided shift saturation. Almost every sequence in \(S\) visits \(G\) infinitely often in both directions by recurrence.

## Connecting two finite-length rows through an arbitrary intervening word

At a finite-length row define \(x\) as the sum of its *finite* summands (padding contributes nothing). This is a measurable nonnegative integer. Let dates \(i<j\) both have finite lengths in a tail-limit sequence with finite gaps. Approximate it by actual shifts tending to infinity. For all sufficiently large shifts the endpoint lengths and the intervening gap word are constant, as those coordinates are finite and discrete. Put \(k=j-i\), \(B=\sum_{t=i}^{j-1}a_t\), \(B_s=\sum_{t=i}^{i+s-1}a_t\), and
\[
 C=\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}3^{k-1-s}2^{B_s}.
\]
Thus \(C\) is a fixed positive odd integer for this comparison. The actual endpoint summands satisfy
\[
 3^k\sum u_t+C=2^B\sum v_t.
\]
Partition the signed terms, omitting padding, into minimal nonempty zero-sum blocks. Each block has finitely many possible projective ratios. Divide by any one term and apply Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt, Theorem 1.1, to the remaining nondegenerate unit equation in a finite product of copies of the rational multiplicative group generated by \(P\), \(-1\), and the prime divisors of \(C\). There are finitely many block indices and sizes for this comparison. Two-term blocks have a fixed ratio. Pass to a further subsequence with fixed partition and ratios. Each block consists wholly of bounded or wholly of divergent terms. The block containing \(C\) is bounded. Adding precisely the bounded blocks proves \(2^B x_j=3^k x_i+C\).

In particular \(x_i\) is odd by reduction modulo 2, so it is positive. On a recurrent sequence in \(S\) this holds at **every** finite-length visit, including \(j\). To verify the intervening gaps, apply the rational affine steps \(z_{s+1}=(3z_s+1)/2^{a_{i+s}}\) from \(z_0=x_i\). Their final value is \(x_j\). At every step \(z_s\) has denominator a power of two by forward iteration and denominator dividing a power of three by backward iteration from the integral \(x_j\). Hence it is integral. Each state before the last is odd by its next equation, and the last is odd by the next visit. All states are positive by forward iteration. The displayed gaps are therefore the exact valuations of ordinary accelerated Collatz steps.

Filling between consecutive visits defines a shift-equivariant measurable positive odd integer state \(X\) at every date of almost every sequence in \(S\). Equivariance follows from the unique forward path starting from the last visit. Hitting times and finite forward evaluations are measurable. If \(\mu(S)>0\), normalize the invariant measure on \(S\). Recurrence on each level set \(\{X_0=x\}\), and determinism of the ordinary map, force almost every \(X_0\) to be periodic. On any positive period, telescoping the clock gives mean gap \(\alpha+\operatorname{mean}\log_2(1+1/(3X_i))>\alpha\). The stationary pushforward on countably many cyclic states is a mixture of uniform cycle measures. Consequently
\[
 \int a_0\,d\mu>\alpha\mu(S)+1-\mu(S) \quad\text{if }\mu(S)>0.
\]
When \(\mu(S)=0\) the weak inequality holds. Strictness requires no uniform cycle excess.

## Deductions

A tight subsequence would give \(\mu(S)=1\) while the clock gives \(\int a_0\,d\mu\le\alpha\). This proves the first assertion. Zero lower density above some fixed \(K\) would itself supply a tight subsequence. A bounded subsequence of means of any coercive nonnegative \(f\) would give tightness by Markov's inequality.

On a positive-rate subsequence choose a further subsequence realizing the lower limit of the proportion with \(h_i>K\), and take a limit law there. The clock gives \(\int a_0\,d\mu\le\alpha-\gamma/\log 2\). Since \(\{h_0\le K\}\) is clopen and contained in \(S\), its limiting proportion is at most \(1-\gamma/\log(3/2)\). This proves the quantitative claim, including its maximal-rate endpoint.

## Comparison with the signed-carry and birth estimates

FS3 restricts visits to a fixed multiplicative coset. Sums of even two smooth integers can contain primes outside that group. The theorem here restricts every fixed additive length without restricting the prime factors of the sums. Positivity and integrality give a nonnegative integral ordinary shadow. FS5 concerns signed rational-unit length of a different integer, the affine carry across an entire window. The estimates do not identify those two complexities.

PR194's run partition bounds a number of binary carry paths at prescribed canonical word and carry budget. Each nonzero binary carry can occur at a different time. A linear budget or an entropy bound does not furnish tight smooth-summand presentations of the states. A contradiction with the theorem above would follow from any fixed \(P\) and uniformly tight distributions of positive integer summand lengths along one prefix subsequence of an escaping source. On a subsequence of escape rate \(\gamma>0\), even a single fixed \(K\) with upper density of lengths \(>K\) strictly below \(\gamma/\log(3/2)\) suffices. No such upper bound is proved. Eventual periodicity would still allow nontrivial cycles.
