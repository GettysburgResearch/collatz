# Independent reconstruction of the odd-cycle width bound

I checked the written theorem in `arithmetic/README.md` at SHA-256 `170717f6947bcef2af2f1c4dfa317be42b11034132e205bcc60ce401ec8d276b`. My reconstruction supports its stated asymptotic bound. The interval count and its combination with a lower bound for a linear form are written arguments. The author's local Lean terminal-uniqueness lemma addresses the congruence step.

1. Fix $2<K<4$ before sending the odd minimum $m$ to infinity. For an odd-only cycle set $\alpha=\log_2 3$, $\beta=\log_2K$, and $k=\lceil\log_3(Km)\rceil$. Telescoping for the cumulative valuation $S_i$ gives $S_i=i\alpha+\log_2(x_0/m)-\log_2(x_i/m)+e_i$ with $0\le e_i\le k/m$. This holds for every one of the $p$ rotations of the same ordinary cycle, including when $k>p$.
2. Fix a bin width $\eta>0$ with $\beta+2\eta<2$. For the start bin with left endpoint $b$, every $S_i$ lies in the **same** closed interval $[i\alpha+b-\beta,i\alpha+b+2\eta]$ for every start in that bin once $k/m\le\eta$. For length $w=\beta+2\eta\in(1,2)$ there are two integers exactly when the fractional left endpoint belongs to the closed circle arc $[2-w,1]$ with zero included. Single grid coincidences at either end cannot change its limiting frequency. Irrational rotation gives frequency $w-1$ of such positions. One can compare each length-$q$ block to a translated $q$-point grid using $|\alpha-a/q|<q^{-2}$. The phase error inside the block is bounded by $1/q$, independent of the initial block index. The arc count error is at most four.
3. Each start bin permits at most $2^{(w-1+o(1))k}$ cumulative clock tuples. Counting choices independently is valid as an upper bound. The bins are finite and fixed while $m$ grows. Each tuple determines its valuation word because $S_0=0$. In the affine identity $2^{S_k}x_k=3^kx_0+C$, the integer $C$ is determined by that word. Coprimality gives one residue of the **terminal** odd state mod $3^k$. The entire band has length $(K-1)m<3^k$, so one tuple supplies at most one ordinary terminal. Advancing a primitive odd cycle $k$ steps permutes all its phases, so it supplies $p$ distinct terminals. After taking the limsup with $\eta$ fixed, sending $\eta$ to zero gives the exponent $\log_3(K/2)$.
4. The full lap gives $0<A\log2-p\log3\le p/(3m)$, where $p\le A\le2p$ follows termwise from positive valuations and from $3+1/x_j\le4$ in the product. A homogeneous lower bound $c\max(A,p)^{-\kappa}$ implies $m\ll p^{1+\kappa}$. For each **strict** $K<2\cdot3^{1/(1+\kappa)}$, there is room between the exponents, so the odd minima of cycles with width at most $K$ are bounded. Finite minimum possibilities give finitely many cycles because one minimum determines its orbit. The argument supplies no all-cycle cutoff from an ineffective lower bound and allows wider cycles.

I independently read Wu–Wang Theorem 1 at the publisher: setting the constant coefficient to zero gives exponent $4.1163051+\varepsilon$. I also read Chudnovsky's November Corollary 1.2, which explicitly states exponent $n+\varepsilon$ in the homogeneous case and cites the October preprint for $n=2$. Taking the multiplicatively independent rationals 2 and 3 gives $2+\varepsilon$ and hence the source-qualified threshold $2\sqrt[3]3$. This uses a bound for a linear form; an irrationality-measure exponent for a quotient would require conversion. I did not independently build the outside formalizations or prove their number-theoretic inputs. [Wu–Wang, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2014.03.007) · [Chudnovsky, Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.30820v1)

## Forward band exit and elapsed shortcut steps

I also checked the added forward-exit theorem in the final `arithmetic/README.md` at SHA-256 `b0856510fab45e1c99dc0b3583c81b8172c409ac2a995753eb0a2978aa576b21`. Fix $2<K$ strictly below the applicable cycle-width threshold. For a sufficiently large odd $n$, a repeat before leaving $[n,Kn]$ would make a cycle whose odd minimum is at least $n$ and whose ratio is at most $K$. Hence all the pre-exit odd states are distinct. After omitting the first $k=\lceil\log_3(Kn)\rceil$, each remaining pre-exit state is the terminal of a complete in-band window. The same tuple count bounds this population uniformly in the path. This forces an exit in the stated number of odd steps. If $j$ is the first exit time, the initial states $x_0,\ldots,x_{j-1}$ of those steps are all at least $n$, and the terminal $x_j$ is a positive integer. Consequently $2^{S_j}=n\prod_{i<j}(3+1/x_i)/x_j\le n4^j$, so the elapsed shortcut clock is at most $2j+\log_2 n$. The additive logarithm is absorbed by the positive power of $n$. A large valuation on the final exit step and a downward exit to an odd endpoint below $n$ both satisfy this inequality. The first exit can be upward.
