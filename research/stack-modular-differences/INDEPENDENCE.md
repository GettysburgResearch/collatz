# Algebraic independence of modular differences and of explicit binary survivor values

**PROPOSED.** Any three distinct members of the native mask family in [the construction](PROOF.md) generate a field of transcendence degree at least two over $\mathbb Q$. Any two differences of native values whose corresponding differences of mask indicators are not proportional are algebraically independent. The field over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ generated by *all* differences in the family has transcendence degree exactly two. In particular, if a member of the family is algebraic, then any two distinct other members are algebraically independent.

For two explicit survivor codes, take the indicators of the perfect squares and of the squares of even integers, using nonnegative roots. Their 2-adic survivor values are algebraically independent. Every survivor value in the [bilateral quadratic image theorem](SURVIVOR_IMAGES.md) is algebraic over the field generated by these two values and $\overline{\mathbb Q}$.

## Specialization preserves polynomial relations

Let $f_1,\ldots,f_s$ be the finitely many unary theta forms under consideration, with algebraic Fourier coefficients and periodic weights, in a common modular variable. The same reasoning applies if the rational powers of the variable occurring in a Fourier expansion have been cleared by passing to a common root. Formally each $f_i$ is algebraic over $K=\overline{\mathbb Q}(E_4,E_6)$ by the full-level norm argument in [the modular proof](PROOF.md). At a fixed nonzero algebraic $q_0$ of 2-adic absolute value less than one, specialization is injective on $\overline{\mathbb Q}[E_4,E_6]$ by Nesterenko's theorem. It is therefore injective on the ring generated also by the $f_i$: if $b$ in that ring is nonzero, its minimal polynomial over $K$ has nonzero constant coefficient. Clearing denominators gives a polynomial for $b$ with nonzero constant coefficient in $\overline{\mathbb Q}[E_4,E_6]$. That coefficient remains nonzero after specialization, so $b(q_0)\ne0$. All identities here are between convergent series or their polynomial combinations.

Two nonproportional forms $f,g$ of the same positive weight $1/2$ in this family are algebraically independent as formal series. On a common finite-index subgroup their multipliers agree after restriction, or one can first replace the forms by equal powers of positive even integral weight with trivial character. Their equal powers are still nonproportional since a nonconstant meromorphic ratio cannot have a constant positive integral power. A relation between integral-weight forms splits into homogeneous components: apply the transformations with lower row $(Nm,1)$ for unbounded integers $m$ at a common congruence level $N$. The distinct weight powers of $Nm\tau+1$ give a polynomial with infinitely many roots for each fixed $\tau$ in the upper half-plane. A nonzero homogeneous relation would then force the ratio of the forms to take only finitely many constant values, a contradiction. By specialization their 2-adic values are algebraically independent.

## Consequences for the codes

For two different difference indicators the formal theta series are proportional exactly when their even periodic root weights are proportional: one recovers the weights from the coefficients at positive squares. Three distinct binary mask vectors $a,b,c$ cannot be collinear, since if $b-a$ and $c-a$ are proportional their nonzero supports coincide, and on that support both $b$ and $c$ equal $1-a$. The two differences of native values therefore give the lower transcendence degree. The norm construction gives the matching upper bound for the field of all differences, since each difference is algebraic over the same field $\overline{\mathbb Q}(E_4(T^9),E_6(T^9))$.

The values for square and even-square support are the nonzero rational normalizations of $(1+\theta(q))/2$ and $(1+\theta(q^4))/2$ at $q=T$, where $\theta(q)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}q^{n^2}$. These same-weight forms are nonproportional, as seen at the constant term and at exponent one. For any one bilateral quadratic image, write all its rational powers and the two square series in a common modular variable $q=T^{1/D}$. The three theta forms then have values algebraic over the same transcendence-degree-two field by the norm. Because the two square values are algebraically independent, the third value must be algebraic over them and the algebraic numbers.
