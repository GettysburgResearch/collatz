# Real and finite-place transcendence at rational arguments with nonunit numerator

**Proposed.** Fix coprime integers $2\le Q<P$. Let $(d_n)$ be an integer digit word with a morphic presentation of exponential emitted length, whose distinct digit values represent distinct residues modulo $Q$. At the real place and at each prime dividing $Q$ the convergent value
$$
 f(Q/P)=\sum_{n\ge0}d_n (Q/P)^n
$$
is transcendental if $(d_n)$ is not eventually periodic, and rational otherwise. In particular every nonperiodic binary word in this class gives an individual transcendental value at every such positive rational argument in each of these completions. The proof is a proposed corollary and archimedean adaptation of the [exponential morphic specialization](EXPONENTIAL_MORPHIC.md).

Use the [nonerasing reduction](MORPHIC_NORMALIZATION.md) and weights $z_b=(Q/P)^{|\tau(b)|}$, so the initial sum is an explicitly rational row of the prefix generating functions. The exponent vectors for all primes are scalar multiples of the same positive length vector. Consequently the reduced coordinates have the form $y_{t,i}=(Q/P)^{r_{t,i}}$ (take compatible positive roots), where $r_{t,i}\asymp U_{t,i}\to\infty$ in each of the layers of the exponential proof. They contract at both the real place and each $p\mid Q$.

The finite-place proof applies as written at all these primes. To begin instead at the real embedding, we record the two analytic changes. On a small complex polydisk about a late positive sample $y_j$, every transformed coordinate obeys
$$
 \log|(S^t y)_i|=\log|y_{j+t,i}|+\sum_k(B^t)_{ik}\log|y_k/y_{j,k}|.
$$
Here $B$ may have negative entries. Its $i$th row has norm $O(U_{t,i})$: in $B^t=V^{-1}D^{et}V$ the $i$th coordinate of $V^{-1}$ on $W$ is a fixed linear combination of original rows of its own and slower layers, by the block triangular construction. The row sums of the nonnegative $D^{et}$ on these same rows are $O(U_{t,i})$. Meanwhile the first term is at most $-c_j U_{t,i}$ for some fixed $c_j>0$. Choosing all relative radii sufficiently small therefore bounds the modulus of every transformed coordinate by $\exp(-c_jU_{t,i}/2)$ for large $t$. Shrink the polydisk if necessary at finitely many initial iterates. The iterated matrices after denominator clearing cost $\exp(O(D_0 t))$ also in complex sup norm (include a fixed matrix-dimension factor per multiplication). In the auxiliary fast-tail estimate, absolute coefficient convergence on a fixed origin polydisk of radius $R<1$ controls the summed Taylor coefficients just as their Gauss norms did at the finite place.

For division by the nonzero analytic germ $F_*$, choose a smaller polydisk centered at $y_j$ on whose distinguished boundary $|F_*|$ has a positive lower bound. Indeed expand at the center and choose a positive weight vector with rationally independent entries. A unique monomial of least weighted order has nonzero coefficient; there are only finitely many exponents below any fixed weighted order. Taking the boundary radii to be a sufficiently small common parameter to those respective powers makes the leading term larger than the sum of the remaining terms, by absolute convergence on a larger polydisk. Bound the cleared auxiliary function there after division, then apply the maximum modulus principle in each variable to bound its central value. All dimension, fixed-field height and Liouville bounds are unchanged, giving the same linear independence over the algebraic numbers at the late real sample.

If $f(Q/P)$ is algebraic at any one of the stated places, transport its defining row to that completion's late independent basis. The resulting algebraic row is zero. At every one of the completions, and for every conjugation of the reduced parameter constants, the corresponding evaluated prefix monomials still give the original rational weights term by term. Thus, as in the [inverse-parity descent](TRANSCENDENTAL_PARITY.md), every conjugate of the assumed algebraic value equals that same value in the initial completion, and the same rational number $r$ occurs in all the completions.

For each $n$ put $R_n=(P/Q)^n(r-\sum_{j<n}d_j(Q/P)^j)$. These rationals are uniformly bounded at infinity by the real tail, and are integral at all primes dividing $Q$ by their convergent local tails. At other primes their denominators divide the fixed denominator of $r$ by the recurrence $R_{n+1}=P(R_n-d_n)/Q$. Only finitely many $R_n$ can occur. Since $R_n=d_n+(Q/P)R_{n+1}$ and $R_{n+1}$ is integral at the primes of $Q$, the residues of $R_n$ modulo $Q$ determine $d_n$ uniquely. This gives a deterministic map on the finite tail-state set and makes the word eventually periodic. For an eventually periodic word the original series sums rationally in all indicated completions.
