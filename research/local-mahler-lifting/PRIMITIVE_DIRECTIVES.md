# Primitive substitution directives and rational-base paths

**Proposed.** Let $P>Q\ge2$ be coprime integers. An unbounded ordinary integer trajectory $Qx_{n+1}=Px_n+d_n$ cannot have an error word obtained by a letter-to-letter integer coding of a fixed point of a primitive substitution whose incidence characteristic polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ and whose Perron root exceeds one. The conclusion also permits a finite prefix or deleting a finite prefix. It follows from the multivariable finite-place argument below and Brechler's formal regularity proposition. Paper 16 already addresses this substitution class for shortcut-Collatz parity. The fixed-affine chart here is arbitrary. [Prior Collatz result](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/16/v1) · [Brechler, Proposition 4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24877)

### A multidimensional finite-place specialization

Let $T$ be the monomial map for a nonnegative nonsingular primitive integer matrix acting on $a$ coordinates. Let $\lambda>1$ be its Perron root. Suppose $F=A(z)F(Tz)$ is an invertible rational system over a number field and its component series converge on the open unit polydisk at a chosen finite place. Suppose their components are independent over $\overline{\mathbb Q}(z)$. Let $\alpha$ be an algebraic point inside that polydisk with nonzero coordinates. Assume every fixed nonzero polynomial over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ is eventually nonzero along $(T^j\alpha)$ and all iterates are regular for $A$. Then the components of $F(\alpha)$ are algebraic-linearly independent at that place.

Here are the changes to the [one-variable proof](PROOF.md), including how the bounds handle rational matrix denominators in several variables. Write $z_j=T^j\alpha$ and use $B_j=A(z_0)\cdots A(z_{j-1})$. The assumed eventual nonvanishing makes the relation ideal a proper ideal over $K(z_1,\ldots,z_a)$, with all nonzero rational-function denominators eventually allowed. Its invertible relation matrix $\phi$, its translates (1), and the fixed number field are obtained by the identical radical ideal argument. A primitive element for the finite function-field extension of the entries of $\phi$ is separable in characteristic zero. Its discriminant, denominators of entries and norm of $\det\phi$ exclude a finite union of proper algebraic loci. Choose $\xi=z_{j_0}$ outside them and choose a simple algebraic branch there. The implicit-function theorem gives analytic germs on a small polydisk. Formal regularity over $\overline{\mathbb Q}(z)$ is Brechler's Proposition 4.3: $T$ belongs to his class $\mathcal F$, since the matrix is nonsingular and all coordinate images under sufficiently large iterates have degree tending to infinity. In the germ realization independence is preserved by regularity and the analytic identity principle; the latter follows one coordinate at a time on a polydisk. Thus for a putative relation $\tau F(\alpha)=0$ the germ $F_*(z)=\tau C\phi(z)F(z)$ is again nonzero, where $C=B_{j_0}\phi(\xi)^{-1}$.

Use auxiliary polynomials of total matrix degree $D$ and degree at most $H$ in each $z_i$. With the same $r_D$ and $c_D$ reasoning, the quotient dimension is between $r_D(H+1)^a$ and $r_D(H+c_D+1)^a$. The bound $r_{2D}\le C_mr_D$ is unaffected. For $D$ sufficiently large and $H$ sufficiently large after $D$, take
$$
 s=\left\lfloor H\,(D/(4^a C_m))^{1/a}\right\rfloor .
$$
Truncation to total $z$-degree below $s$ has codomain dimension at most $C_mr_D(s+c_{2D}+1)^a$, smaller than the $(D+1)r_D(H+1)^a$ available coefficients. Use the same complement, twist, and polynomials $P_b$. Then the truncated auxiliary series vanishes identically after substituting $\Theta_t=C\phi(z)A_t(z)$ and $T^t z$. These identities hold as meromorphic germs. The multiindex $z$-degree allows $s/H\to\infty$ with $D$.

On a sufficiently small fixed closed polydisk $\mathcal D$ around $\xi$, every coordinate has the fixed norm $|\xi_i|_p<1$. Perron–Frobenius gives $\max_i\|(T^t z)_i\|_{\mathcal D}\le\exp(-c\lambda^t)$ with fixed $c>0$: the logarithms of these norms are given by the nonnegative matrix powers applied to the strictly negative log-radius vector. Write $A=N/g$ with a polynomial matrix and a scalar polynomial denominator. We may take $g(z_i)\ne0$ throughout the sample trajectory after increasing $j_0$; here $g(z_i)$ denotes its evaluation on the point $z_i$. Define $g_t(z)=\prod_{i<t}g(T^i z)$. The matrices $g_t\Theta_t$ and the scalars $g_t$ have polydisk norm at most $\exp(O(t+1))$, because their factors are polynomials evaluated in the unit polydisk. This clearing avoids any requirement of a simultaneous pole-free neighborhood for all iterated denominators.

Write $\mathcal E$ and $E$ for the two auxiliary functions of the one-variable proof and $b_0$ for the first nonzero coefficient-polynomial index. Since all matrix degrees are at most $2D$, the function $g_t^{2D}\mathcal E(\Theta_t,T^t z)$ is analytic on $\mathcal D$. Its summands are cleared powers of $g_t$, entries of $g_t\Theta_t$, and the coefficient tails of total degree at least $s$. For a fixed radius $R<1$ all the finitely many untwisted coefficient series are bounded on the radius-$R$ polydisk. Their tails at $T^t z$ consequently cost at most $\exp(-cs\lambda^t+O_{D,H}(1))$. It follows that the Gauss norm after clearing is at most $\exp(-cs\lambda^t+O(Dt)+O_{D,H}(1))$. Divide by the fixed positive norm $\|F_*\|^{b_0}$ using polydisk Gauss-norm multiplicativity. At $\xi$ this bounds
$$
 \left|g_t(\xi)^{2D}P_{b_0}(B_{j_0+t},z_{j_0+t})\right|_p .
$$
The displayed algebraic numbers lie in the fixed number field and are nonzero infinitely often. Their heights are $O((D+H)\lambda^t)+O_{D,H}(1)$ with implied main constant independent of $D,H$: monomial degrees are $O(\lambda^i)$ for primitive $T$, giving the same bound on each $h(z_i)$, then sum these bounds over the matrix and denominator factors. Local Liouville contradicts $s/H\asymp D^{1/a}$ for sufficiently large $D,H$. This proves the finite-place specialization.

### The substitution word

Let the substitution be $\sigma$ on $a$ letters, with incidence matrix $M_{ij}=|\sigma(j)|_i$. Write $w=\sigma(w)$ and $c_n$ for the column of prefix letter counts through $n-1$. Form the vector of series
$$
G_b(z)=\sum_{n:w_n=b} z^{c_n}.
$$
Each converges on the open unit polydisk because $\sum_i(c_n)_i=n$. Substituting every occurrence of every letter gives $G(z)=L(z)G(Tz)$, with $(Tz)_j=\prod_i z_i^{M_{ij}}$. The $b,j$ entry of the polynomial matrix $L$ sums $z^{c}$ over prefixes of $\sigma(j)$ whose following letter is $b$, with $c$ their count vectors. Include the constant $1$ and select an independent rational-function basis from this enlarged vector as in the [automatic chart proof](AUTOMATIC_CHARTS.md). Coefficient-field descent for independence is the same in several variables.

At the diagonal point $\alpha=(Q/P,\ldots,Q/P)$ the coordinates of $T^j\alpha$ equal $(Q/P)^{l_{j,b}}$ with $l_j=(M^t)^j\mathbf 1$. The vector $l_j/\lambda^j$ tends to a positive right Perron eigenvector of $M^t$ times a positive constant. Its coordinates are rationally independent: a nonzero rational row annihilating it would produce, by right multiplication with successive powers of $M^t$, a nonzero invariant rational row space in its proper annihilator, contradicting irreducibility of the characteristic polynomial. Distinct monomials thus have asymptotically separated real evaluations, with ratios tending to zero or infinity exponentially in $\lambda^j$. In any nonzero polynomial a unique monomial eventually dominates in ordinary absolute value, proving eventual nonvanishing. In particular the rational basis and its system are regular at all sufficiently late iterates.

At a prime $p\mid Q$ the integer recurrence gives the row relation $Px_0+\sum_n d_n(Q/P)^n=0$. Transport this row through the polynomial matrices to a late regular point and then through the rational basis. The finite-place theorem forces the resulting row to be zero identically, so the same relation holds in the real embedding. The real tail again gives $|x_n|\le\max_j|d_j|/(P-Q)$. An arbitrary finite steering prefix only changes the starting integer. For an error word obtained by deleting a finite prefix of the coded fixed word, its series is a rational monomial multiple of the fixed-word series minus a rational finite sum at the diagonal point; transport that rational row in precisely the same way.
