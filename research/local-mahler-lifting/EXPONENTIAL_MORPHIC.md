# Exponential substitution presentations cannot encode divergent rational Collatz parity

**Proposed.** Let $w=\sigma^\infty(a)$ be a fixed word generated from a prolongable letter of a nonerasing substitution. Suppose $|\sigma^j(a)|$ grows exponentially, meaning $\limsup |\sigma^j(a)|^{1/j}>1$. Any letter coding of this word as shortcut-Collatz parity forces eventual periodicity on each positive rational source in $\mathbb Z_2$. Any letter coding as the errors $d_n=Qx_{n+1}-Px_n$ of an integer chart with coprime $P>Q\ge2$ forces boundedness. Fixed finite prefixes and deletions are allowed. In particular, if a divergent orbit had a morphic parity presentation of this nonerasing form, the presentation would have polynomial seed growth. Equal growth rates of the letters, primitivity, and restrictions on Jordan blocks are unnecessary.

The proof extends [the primitive construction](ALL_PRIMITIVE.md) by separating letters of different growth rates and counting auxiliary coefficients only in the fastest variables. Paper 16 proves automatic and primitive parity exclusion and specified nonprimitive cases; its v3 lists further nonprimitive cases as open. We give a separate finite-place argument. [Paper 16 v3](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/16/v3)

## Absolute convergence for the two applications

Write $c_n$ for the letter count vector of the first $n$ letters and $G_b(z)=\sum_{w_n=b} z^{c_n}$. In an expanding integer chart take $z_b=Q/P$ and a prime $p\mid Q$. In the parity application take $z_b=2/3^{e_b}$ and $p=2$. In both cases the $z_b$ are positive rationals, the distinguished valuations $v_p(z_b)$ are the same positive number, and the sums converge at $p$. In the first application they converge over the reals as well.

For the second application suppose a positive rational nonperiodic orbit with fixed odd denominator $d$ realizes the coding. Write $M_n=2^n3^{-s_n}$ for its parity coefficient, $s_n=\sum_{i<n}e_i$. The orbitwise reciprocal estimate for positive integers extends to such an orbit: apply the two-lane argument to its distinct positive odd numerators, iterating $a\mapsto(3a+d)/2^{v_2(3a+d)}$. Each fixed length-$j$ valuation word of total $A$ still selects one odd residue modulo $2^{A+1}$ (multiply the ordinary residue by the odd unit $d$), and its positive additive remainder after division is at most $d((3/2)^j-1)$. Thus the high-valuation distinct endpoints in that proof number at most $(\eta^j X+d(3/2)^j+1)/2$, with $\eta=3/2^{5/3}$, and the low-valuation count is unchanged. The same $j=\lfloor(3/5)\log_2 X\rfloor$ yields $O_d(X^{39/40})$ odd numerators up to $X$. Dyadic summation gives $\sum_{e_n=1}1/x_n<\infty$. Hence $M_nx_n=x_0\prod_{i<n,e_i=1}(1+1/(3x_i))$ is bounded. The exact real telescope then bounds $\sum_{e_n=1}M_n=3\lim_n(M_nx_n-x_0)$. In an even run the $M_n$ double each step, so their sum before the next odd is bounded by that odd's $M_n$. There are infinitely many odds. Consequently $\sum_n M_n<\infty$ and all $G_b(z)$ converge absolutely over the reals. [Orbitwise two-lane count and exact constants](../astra-three-routes/pass2/ORBIT_MELLIN.md)

For either application every fixed substitution iterate of the point has the same real absolute convergence: its prefix weights are the original weights sampled at the increasing endpoints $|\sigma^j(w_{<n})|$. At $p$ all iterated weights also have norm less than one.

## Length levels and sufficiently many fastest letters

Discard letters unreachable from $a$. Write $A=M^t$ for the transpose incidence matrix. After passage to a fixed common power and a sufficiently late start, every unbounded length $|\sigma^j(b)|$ is asymptotic to $C_b j^{h_b}\lambda_b^j$ with $C_b>0$, $h_b\ge0$, $\lambda_b\ge1$ (use the indices of the chosen subsequence). Call these asymptotic orders the length levels, ordered lexicographically by $(\lambda_b,h_b)$. Write $U_j=j^h\lambda^j$ for the largest level; accessibility implies $\lambda>1$ and the seed has this level. Bounded letters have eventually periodic substituted words and can be frozen by a common power. Here and below further positive powers change the exponential base in $U_j$ accordingly.

For the length assertion, put the nonnegative incidence matrix in component upper triangular form and take a power killing all irreducible periods. The nonnilpotent diagonal blocks are then primitive on their cyclic classes. In a directed component path, generating functions for length counts contain the product of the block resolvents $(I-xB)^{-1}$ and the nonnegative transition matrices. A primitive block of radius $r$ has a simple pole at $1/r$ with strictly positive Perron residue in the expansion in $(1-rx)^{-1}$, with all other poles of strictly larger modulus. Smaller-radius blocks give nonnegative resolvents there, positive between reachable vertices. Thus the leading order on a path is $j^{k-1}r^j$, where $r$ is its largest block radius and $k$ the number of blocks of this radius on that path. Reachability makes its coefficient positive. Summing the finitely many paths proves the length assertion, with no cancellation of the largest order.

Moreover for a constant $C$ the first $n$ letters of the fixed word contain at least $n/(C(1+\log n)^h)$ occurrences of letters at the fastest level. Indeed choose a path from the seed realizing the maximal pair $(\lambda,h)$. The first radius-$\lambda$ block on it contains a letter $b$ itself at this maximal level. The number of copies of $b$ in the seed's $j$th substituted word is $\gg\lambda^j$ for all large $j$ on the chosen power, by its fixed access path and the primitive block powers. For $|\sigma^j(a)|\le n<|\sigma^{j+1}(a)|$ use the whole $j$th prefix, and $n\ll (1+j)^h\lambda^j$. The same argument works when the seed was first shifted by a fixed number of substitutions: alternatively work throughout with the fixed point of the common power. All sufficiently large prefixes are covered.

## A layered integral parametrization

Apply the [prime-exponent construction](ALL_PRIMITIVE.md) to the finitely many prime vectors $u_l=(v_l(z_b))_b$. Take the initial shift killing nilpotent components, and the common iterate $D$ removing root-of-unity ratios including $1$ and freezing bounded letter words. Define $c_l$ by projection to the generalized eigenvalue-$1$ summand and $W=\operatorname{span}\{(D^{j+1}-D^j)u_l\}$. The previous algebraic conclusions $D^ju_l-c_l\in W$, $(D-I)c_l\in W$ and invertibility of $D|_W$ hold verbatim. In every row belonging to a bounded letter, all these differences vanish, because its substituted words are frozen. The distinguished prime vector before the initial shift is a positive multiple of $\mathbf1$, so $D^ju_p-c_p$ has in every unbounded row the positive order of that letter's length. Every other prime-vector row is $O(|\sigma^j(b)|)$, by nonnegativity of the incidence matrix and bounded initial weights.

We choose a rational lattice basis matrix $V$ for $W$ up to finite index as follows. On all growing rows, take the leading normalized limit of the distinguished vector divided by the fastest length order. Its support consists exactly of the fastest letters, and its entries there are strictly positive. The limit belongs to $W_{\mathbb R}$. In the rational face subspace of $W$ consisting of vectors zero outside that support, choose independent positive rays close to this limit and surrounding it in their relative interior; here positivity means strictly positive on its support. These columns are the fast variables. Project $W$ to the remaining growing rows (the preceding kernel has just been filled), and repeat on the next length level, until the projection is zero. At each step lift the new columns to $W$ and add sufficiently large positive combinations of earlier columns to make their entries on all preceding rows nonnegative. The resulting columns are nonnegative integers, fast columns vanish on slower rows, and every growing letter has a positive entry in some column.

For precision about integrality, the successive kernels of these projections define a rational flag, and their intersections with $\Lambda=W\cap\mathbb Z^a$ are saturated. Take an integral basis of $\Lambda$ adapted to the flag. In each quotient choose approximating columns in the corresponding basis residue classes modulo $b=|\det(D|_\Lambda)|$, as in the primitive proof. A bounded-error approximation of large multiples of the desired rays suffices. Lift while preserving these classes and add multiples of $b$ times preceding positive columns on the faster supports. The resulting finite index is $1$ modulo $b$. A further power $D^e$ therefore preserves the generated sublattice. Its matrix $B$ in the chosen basis is an invertible-over-$\mathbb Q$ integer matrix; negative entries are permitted.

Use $D^{en}u_l=c_l+Vt_{l,n}$, sample coordinates $y_{n,i}=\prod_l l^{(t_{l,n})_i}$, substitution $Z_b(y)=\prod_l l^{(c_l)_b}\prod_i y_i^{V_{bi}}$, and the resulting twisted Laurent monomial map $S(y)_i=\gamma_i\prod_j y_j^{B_{ij}}$ from the primitive proof. The argument with S-unit equations and Skolem–Mahler–Lech is unchanged: every fixed nonzero polynomial is eventually nonzero along $y_n$. The coordinates in each layer satisfy
$$
 v_p(y_{n,i})\asymp U_{n,i},\qquad h(y_{n,i})=O(U_{n,i}),
$$
where $U_{n,i}$ is the corresponding length level. Indeed solve the block triangular basis from slowest to fastest. A layer is uniquely determined by the rows of its level after subtracting the strictly lower-order contributions of slower variables. For the distinguished prime its normalized limiting vector is in the interior of the chosen cone, hence all leading coordinates are positive; for every prime the rowwise length majorant gives the height estimate. All reduced coordinates therefore tend to zero at $p$. Fast coordinates decay as $\exp(-cU_n)$ and all slower coordinates have height $o(U_n)$.

## Formal series, regularity, and truncated dimensions

Set $H_b(y)=G_b(Z(y))$ using the explicit prefix sum, and adjoin $1$. Its term at $n$ has slow and fast exponents $V^t c_n\ge0$. The total fast degree is at least the number of fastest letters in the prefix, because the fast columns are integral and positive there. Thus bounded fast degree meets only finitely many $n$. The coefficient of the term is an S-unit of logarithmic height bounded by a fixed polynomial in $\log(2+n)$. To see this, for each neutral vector $c_l$ its images under successive original matrix powers have polynomial norm, since after the chosen power it belongs to the generalized eigenvalue-$1$ summand. A prefix inside a depth-$j$ substituted seed block decomposes, by descending its prefix tree, into at most $\max_b |\sigma(b)|$ complete blocks at each level. Each contributes $O((1+j)^C)$ to $|c_l^t c_n|$. The depth needed is $O(\log(2+n))$ by exponential seed growth. It follows that the prefix sums defining all $H_b$ converge on the entire open unit polydisk at every place: the fast degree grows at least $n/(C(1+\log n)^h)$ and dominates the coefficient logarithms. Their coefficients are S-integral in a fixed field, also when like monomials are combined. The [regularity lemma](ALL_PRIMITIVE.md) applies.

Splitting the fixed word by the chosen substitution power gives the polynomial matrix identity in the original variables. After $Z(y)$ it is valid as an analytic identity on small fixed-modulus polydisks about late samples: $Z(Sy)=T^L Z(y)$, the coordinates of the original point there have $p$-adic norm less than one, and each reduced iterate about the sample stays inside the open unit polydisk at $p$. Laurent monomials are analytic on a sufficiently small closed polydisk avoiding its coordinate hyperplanes. One may now select a rational-function basis from the entries of $H$. The selected square matrix is invertible: any left null row would give an algebraic relation among that basis as germs, contrary to independence and the analytic identity principle. Its rational iterates, and the relation ideal and auxiliary tuples in the [finite-place proof](PRIMITIVE_DIRECTIVES.md), require only rational substitutions and the analytic identities on these sample neighborhoods.

Here is the dimension change needed for the unequal rates. Let $d_f\ge1$ and $d_s\ge0$ be the numbers of fast and slow coordinates. For total matrix degree $D_0$, use degrees at most $H_f$ in each fast variable and $H_s$ in each slow variable. The quotient dimensions modulo the relation ideal are between
$$
 r_{D_0}(H_f+1)^{d_f}(H_s+1)^{d_s}
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 r_{D_0}(H_f+c_{D_0}+1)^{d_f}(H_s+c_{D_0}+1)^{d_s},
$$
by the same common-denominator basis argument. The constant twist preserves the boxes in the parameter variables. Truncate now only in total fast degree $<s$. Every entry of the truncated $H$ is a polynomial in the slow variables, as shown above. For the powers up to $D_0$ in the auxiliary construction, let $C(s,D_0)$ bound all such slow degrees. The truncated target modulo the twisted relation ideal has dimension at most
$$
 r_{2D_0}(s+c_{2D_0}+1)^{d_f}
       (H_s+C(s,D_0)+c_{2D_0}+1)^{d_s}.
$$
Take $D_0$ large, $H_f$ large after $D_0$, $s=\lfloor c H_f D_0^{1/d_f}\rfloor$ with a sufficiently small fixed $c>0$, and finally $H_s$ sufficiently large after all these choices. Since $r_{2D_0}\le C_mr_{D_0}$, the $(D_0+1)$ independent complement slots exceed the target dimension. This yields the same nonzero auxiliary family with the fast truncation vanishing on the relation matrix orbit. The size of $H_s$ is unrestricted because it is chosen before the sampling index tends to infinity.

Fix a small $p$-adic polydisk around a late sample with constant modulus in each coordinate. Every $(S^t y)_i$ has the same norm as at its sample center, even for negative Laurent exponents. The fastest norms are at most $\exp(-c' U_t)$; all slower norms eventually fall below any fixed radius $R<1$. The fast tail of the auxiliary series on this polydisk is accordingly bounded by $\exp(-c's U_t+O_{D_0,H_f,H_s}(1))$ before the matrix factors. Clear the iterated rational matrix denominators exactly as in the multivariable proof; the polynomial factors remain uniformly bounded on these sample polydisks, costing $\exp(O(D_0t))$ in the upper bound. At the algebraic evaluation the resulting nonzero numbers in the fixed field have heights
$$
 O((D_0+H_f)U_t)+H_s\,o(U_t)+O_{D_0,H_f,H_s}(1).
$$
Indeed the matrix and denominator products have height $O(\sum_{j\le t} U_j)=O(U_t)$ by exponential growth, each fast argument has height $O(U_t)$, and each slow argument has height $o(U_t)$. Dividing the local norm by the positive fixed norm of the putative relation germ and using Liouville gives the same contradiction as before, since $s/H_f\gg D_0^{1/d_f}$. Thus the selected formal basis is algebraic-linearly independent at late $p$-adic samples.

Transport the rational row from the starting recurrence to such a sample. Its algebraic coefficients must all be zero. For the real transfer the reduced sample coordinates need not lie in the unit polydisk. Nevertheless the explicit series for $H_b$ there converge absolutely: each term is exactly the original positive prefix weight at a fixed substitution iterate. Polynomial relations defining the rational basis may therefore be evaluated there after clearing denominators: each coordinate series and every finite polynomial multiple converges absolutely, so grouping by monomials preserves the sum. All original and reduced basis denominators can be avoided by taking a sufficiently late sample. The initial relation consequently holds over the reals. For the integer chart this implies bounded states by the real tail identity. For shortcut parity the relation would equate the positive number $x_0$ to a nonpositive sum, a contradiction. Finite prefixes and shifts are handled by their rational affine identities as before.
