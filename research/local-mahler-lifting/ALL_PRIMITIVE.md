# Comparison across completions for every primitive substitution

**Proposed.** Let $w=\sigma(w)$ be a fixed point of a primitive nonerasing substitution of Perron root $\lambda>1$ on a finite alphabet. Write $h=(h_b)$ for its normalized vector of letter frequencies and $c_n$ for its prefix count vector before position $n$. Fix positive rational weights $z_b$. At any place $v$ of $\mathbb Q$ satisfying $\sum_b h_b\log|z_b|_v<0$, form the convergent sums
$$
 G_b(z)=\sum_{n:w_n=b}z^{c_n}.
$$
Every rational linear relation among $1$ and these values at a qualifying finite place holds at every qualifying place. In particular the rational relation spaces agree for any two such finite places. This is a new proof candidate based on the finite-place specialization argument.

* An unbounded integer chart $Qx_{n+1}=Px_n+d_n$, $P>Q\ge2$ coprime, cannot have an error word obtained from any primitive substitution fixed point by a letter coding. This includes every positive ray in the rational-base representation tree with transition $Qm=Pn+a$, $0\le a<P$.
* A positive rational $x\in\mathbb Z_2$ with a primitive-substitution-coded shortcut-Collatz parity word has an eventually periodic orbit. This is already Theorem 1d of rStation paper 16. The argument gives a separate deduction using the regularity of globally convergent series and an integral change of monomial coordinates. [Paper 16 v3](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/16/v3) · [Rational-base representation tree](https://doi.org/10.23638/DMTCS-20-1-10)

There is no restriction on the characteristic polynomial, zero eigenvalues, or sizes of torsion Jordan blocks. A finite prefix or shift is allowed in both consequences.

## A regularity lemma for the reduced generating functions

Suppose $H_1,\ldots,H_m$ are multivariate power series with S-integral coefficients in a fixed number field, converging on the whole open unit polydisk at every place and embedding. Then $\overline{\mathbb Q}(y,H_1,\ldots,H_m)$ is regular over $\overline{\mathbb Q}(y)$. We give the deduction from Bell–Chen–Nguyen–Zannier used in Brechler's regularity proof, since our coordinate substitutions can produce constant multipliers in a monomial map. [Brechler, Theorem 4.1 and proof of Proposition 4.3, citing Bell–Chen–Nguyen–Zannier, Corollary 1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24877)

Take an element algebraic over $\overline{\mathbb Q}(y)$ in the generated field and multiply it by a nonzero polynomial so that the result $g$ is integral over $\overline{\mathbb Q}[y]$. It is a quotient $U/V$ of polynomials in $y,H_i$ with number-field coefficients. The formal power series ring is integrally closed, so $g$ is a formal series. Its coefficients are S-integral after a finite enlargement of the field and of $S$: choose the first nonzero monomial $y^b$ of $V$ in graded lex order, invert its coefficient, and compare the coefficients of $y^{a+b}$ in $gV=U$ in increasing order of $a$. All other terms of $V$ contributing there multiply earlier coefficients of $g$.

At any finite place and any closed origin polydisk of radius $R<1$ in the value group of the completed algebraic closure, $g$ belongs to the fraction field of the Tate algebra and is integral over it. That algebra is integrally closed, so $g$ belongs to it. At an infinite place $g$ is a meromorphic function integral over the holomorphic functions on the open polydisk, hence holomorphic there by integral closedness of local holomorphic rings. Cauchy or Gauss coefficient bounds for every $R<1$ give $\log^+|[y^a]g|_v=o(|a|)$ at each place. Only finitely many places and embeddings enter the coefficient heights. Thus those heights are $o(|a|)$, and the cited theorem for algebraic S-integral power series gives $g\in\overline{\mathbb Q}(y)$. This proves regularity.

## Reducing the restrictions on a monomial orbit

Let $a$ be the alphabet size and $M_{ij}=|\sigma(j)|_i$, $A=M^t$. Its action on a weight vector is $(Tz)_b=\prod_a z_a^{A_{ba}}$. We use all finitely many primes $p$ in the weights, and put $u_p=(v_p(z_b))_b\in\mathbb Z^{a}$. Then $T^n z$ has prime exponent vectors $A^n u_p$. At a qualifying place the negative log-radius vector $l_v=(-\log|z_b|_v)_b$ has positive Perron projection, since $h^t l_v>0$. Consequently $\lambda^{-n}A^nl_v$ tends to a positive multiple of the positive Perron right eigenvector $r$ of $A$. All powers are evaluated at positive rational numbers; coherent fractional powers below can be taken in a fixed number field using positive real roots and an embedding at each finite place.

First take a fixed number of iterations to kill all nilpotent components, renaming the resulting $u_p$ as the starting vectors. Replace $A$ by a fixed power $D$ such that among its distinct nonzero eigenvalues, together with $1$, no two have a root-of-unity ratio. Such a power kills all root-of-unity ratios of the original eigenvalues. Work on the rational stable image, on which $D$ is invertible. Let $c_p$ be the projection of $u_p$ onto the generalized eigenspace of eigenvalue $1$, using the invariant rational complementary summand. Let
$$
 W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{D^{n+1}u_p-D^n u_p:n\ge0,\ p\}.
$$
It is invariant and $D$ restricts invertibly to it. All $D^n u_p-c_p$ belong to $W$. Indeed on the complementary summand $D-I$ is invertible with inverse polynomial in $D$, and the spectral projection is polynomial in $D$; apply these polynomials to $(D-I)u_p$. Also $(D-I)c_p\in W$. Each $c_p$ has $h^t c_p=0$ since $h$ is a left Perron eigenvector and the corresponding eigenvalue exceeds one. The real span of $W$ contains $r$: some $u_p$ has nonzero Perron projection, by the assumption on $l_v$, and normalized successive differences of its iterates tend to a nonzero multiple of $r$.

Put $d=\dim W\ge1$ and $\Lambda=W\cap\mathbb Z^{a}$. Choose positive integral columns $v_1,\ldots,v_d$ spanning a simplicial cone with $r$ in its relative interior in $W$, whose generated sublattice has index coprime to $|\det(D|_\Lambda)|$. To see this, take $d$ positive independent real rays around $r$ (a small transverse simplex suffices). In a basis $e_i$ of $\Lambda$, approximate large multiples of their representatives by lattice vectors $v_i\equiv e_i\pmod{b\Lambda}$ with $b=|\det(D|_\Lambda)|$. The errors are bounded independently of the chosen scaling, so the positivity and interior conditions persist. The determinant of the chosen columns in the lattice basis is $1$ modulo $b$. Let $V$ denote this column matrix. With $c$ its sublattice index, $D$ acts invertibly modulo $c\Lambda$, so some positive power is the identity there. Consequently that power preserves $V\mathbb Z^d$, since $c\Lambda\subset V\mathbb Z^d$. For a sufficiently large multiple $e$ of this exponent the matrix $B$ given by
$$
 D^e V=V B
$$
is strictly positive integral and nonsingular. Indeed each $D^e v_i$, after positive normalization, approaches $r$ in the interior of the cone. Its Perron eigenvalue is denoted by $\rho>1$.

Write $D^{en}u_p=c_p+V t_{p,n}$. The vectors $t_{p,n}$ have bounded common denominator, and $t_{p,n+1}=Bt_{p,n}+a_p$ with fixed rational $a_p$ since $(D^e-I)c_p\in W$. Define the reduced point and substitution of variables by
$$
 (y_n)_i=\prod_p p^{(t_{p,n})_i},\qquad
 Z_b(y)=\prod_p p^{(c_p)_b}\prod_{i=1}^d y_i^{V_{bi}} .
$$
Thus $Z(y_n)$ is the original rational sampled point. The reduced map is $S(y)_i=\gamma_i\prod_j y_j^{B_{ij}}$, with $\gamma_i=\prod_p p^{(a_p)_i}$. It satisfies $Z(Sy)=T^L Z(y)$ with $L$ the total iteration exponent corresponding to $D^e$ (the prior nilpotent-removal step affects only the starting point). At every qualifying place, $-\rho^{-n}\log|(y_n)_i|_v$ converges to a positive real number for each $i$. This follows by applying the inverse coordinate isomorphism $V^{-1}:W\to\mathbb Q^d$ to the Perron-asymptotic log-radius vectors: the subtracted $c_p$ are fixed and $V^{-1}r$ has strictly positive coordinates. Also all coordinate heights of $y_n$ are $O(\rho^n)$.

Every nonzero polynomial over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ is eventually nonzero on $(y_n)$. Otherwise the S-unit equation theorem, applied after passing to a fixed minimal vanishing subsum, makes the ratio of two distinct monomials constant on an infinite subsequence. The points are in a fixed finitely generated multiplicative group, using the bounded common denominator of the $t_{p,n}$. For a nonzero integer row $b$ the quantities $b t_{p,n}$ must therefore be simultaneously constant on that subsequence by unique prime factorization after clearing this common denominator. Extend $bV^{-1}$ to a rational row on the full rational ambient space. Each of the sequences obtained by applying it to $D^j u_p-c_p$ and subtracting the observed constant is an exponential polynomial, with nonzero characteristic roots among $1$ and the eigenvalues of $D$. By Skolem–Mahler–Lech, infinitely many zeros entail zeros on an arithmetic progression. Distinct characteristic roots still remain distinct upon taking the progression step, so independence of polynomial-exponential sequences makes all polynomial coefficients zero. The sequence consequently vanishes for every $j\ge0$. The row then annihilates every generator of $W$, contrary to $b\ne0$. [Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt, *Linear equations in variables which lie in a multiplicative group*](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/155-3/p04)

## Transferring values

Define $H_b(y)=G_b(Z(y))$ as a formal series by the prefix sum. The exponent of $y$ in its $n$th term is $V^tc_n$ and every coordinate increases by at least one with each new letter. Its coefficient is $\prod_p p^{c_p^t c_n}$, an S-unit in a fixed number field of logarithmic height $o(n)$: primitivity gives $c_n/n\to h$ and $c_p^t h=0$. Thus all the $H_b$ converge on the entire open unit polydisk at every place. The regularity lemma applies.

As in the [irreducible special case](PRIMITIVE_DIRECTIVES.md), splitting the fixed word by substituted letters gives a polynomial matrix system for the $G_b$, augmented by $1$. Iterating it $L$ times and inserting $Z(y)$ gives $H(y)=M_*(y)H(Sy)$, where $M_*$ is polynomial because $V$ is positive. Select a basis over $K(y)$ from its entries, with $K$ a fixed number field containing the constants of the parametrization. Field-basis expansion gives independence over $\overline{\mathbb Q}(y)$. The selected vector has an invertible rational system with transformation $S$: substituting the basis into the selected rows gives a square matrix whose left kernel would contradict independence.

At any late sampled point of a qualifying finite place, each basis component is analytic and all iterates avoid the poles of the basis reduction, the square matrix and its inverse. The finite-place argument in [the multivariable specialization](PRIMITIVE_DIRECTIVES.md) works for this twisted monomial map. Explicitly, the relation ideal uses rational substitution $P(YA_t(y),S^t y)$, the same rank and truncation count gives $s/H\gg D_0^{1/d}$ for auxiliary matrix degree $D_0$, and the regularity lemma supplies independence from the algebraic relation matrix germs. On a small fixed polydisk around a late point $y_j$ each coordinate has fixed modulus there, so $\|(S^t y)_i\|=|(y_{j+t})_i|\le\exp(-c\rho^t)$. The polynomial numerators and common scalar denominator of the system are bounded uniformly after those substitutions. Clearing all denominators before taking Gauss norms costs $\exp(O(D_0 t))$. The global lower bound has exponent $O((D_0+H)\rho^t)$, since all sample heights are $O(\rho^t)$. Thus exactly the same fixed-field Liouville contradiction proves linear independence at the late sample.

For any rational linear relation at the original finite place, transport it through the original polynomial matrices to a late sample, then through the rational basis change for $H$. The resulting algebraic row must be zero by that independence. This is an algebraic identity in the chosen number field. At any other qualifying place all original prefix sums converge by $c_n/n\to h$, as do those at each fixed iterate since $h$ is the left Perron eigenvector. The same finite substitutions therefore transport the zero row back to the original relation there. This proves the comparison in the asserted direction.

## The ordinary charts

For fixed $P/Q$, take every weight equal to $Q/P$, and a prime dividing $Q$ as the starting place. The two places qualify. The [real tail identity](AUTOMATIC_CHARTS.md) bounds the integer states by $\max|d_n|/(P-Q)$ whenever the finite-place relation transfers.

For shortcut parity let $e_b\in\{0,1\}$ denote the coded letters, set $z_b=2/3^{e_b}$, and let $\theta=\sum h_b e_b$. All weights are 2-adically contracting. A nonperiodic positive rational orbit in $\mathbb Z_2$ has fixed bounded odd denominator; its distinct states $x_n$ tend to positive infinity. Telescoping logs in $x_{n+1}=3^{e_n}x_n(1+e_n/(3x_n))/2$ thus gives $\log x_n/n\to\theta\log3-\log2\ge0$. The frequency $\theta$ is algebraic by Perron–Frobenius, whereas $\log_3 2$ is transcendental by Gelfond–Schneider and multiplicative independence. Hence the real place also qualifies. The inverse-parity identity is $x_0+\frac13\sum_b e_b G_b(z)=0$ at 2, by telescoping and bounded odd denominators. It cannot hold over the reals, where $x_0>0$ and every term of the sum is nonnegative. This proves the second consequence. A deleted fixed prefix changes each relevant specialization by a finite rational combination and a nonzero rational monomial factor. A prepended prefix may be removed by starting from its terminal state.

By Durand’s theorem, every uniformly recurrent morphic word is primitive substitutive. Thus both chart exclusions apply to every uniformly recurrent morphic directive, even if its given presentation uses a nonprimitive substitution or an additional morphism. [Durand, Theorem 3](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129054113500032)
