# From wild multipliers to ordinary orbit certificates

**Status.** The elementary statements below are **PROPOSED, with the stated
Lean proofs and finite-certificate checks**. The Collatz conjecture remains
open. The multiplier-pullback arithmetic and the new eight-step diamond were
checked by Lean 4.34.1, using `Std` only and no additional axioms. Two further
modules import the pinned PR184 `OffsetDescent` proof, check all 446 certificate
rows in the kernel, and prove their infinite-progression soundness. The guided
certificate covers 446 infinite progressions within one classical search
branch; its other 130 progressions are explicitly unresolved.

We use the shortcut map

\[
T(n)=\begin{cases}n/2&n\equiv0\pmod2,\\(3n+1)/2&n\equiv1\pmod2.\end{cases}
\]

## 1. What the literature supplies

Applegate and Lagarias, *The 3x+1 Semigroup*, J. Number Theory 117 (2006),
146–159, Theorem 1.1, characterize the multiplicative semigroup generated by
`2` and `(2k+1)/(3k+2)`. Their Lemma 2.1 obtains a factor at most `76/79` outside
`−1 mod4096` using ordinary steps together with multipliers from
`{5,7,11,13,23,29,43}`. Lemma 2.2 treats the remaining tower using
`m=(2^j+1)/3` and the identity

\[
T^j(mx)=x+(x+1)/2^j
\]

under its stated congruence assumptions. Multiplication by these numbers is an
allowed operation in their semigroup problem; it is not an ordinary Collatz
orbit step.

Primary full text: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0411140>, version 2,
4 May 2005. The two illustrative rows below are from its Table 1.

Yolcu, Aaronson and Heule, *An Automated Approach to the Collatz Conjecture*,
J. Automated Reasoning 67, 15 (2023), Theorem 3.17, give an exact mixed
binary–ternary rewriting formulation. Their administrative carries preserve
the represented integer; their dynamic rules perform actual shortcut steps.
That distinction motivates the physical lifting checks here.

Primary full text: <https://emreyolcu.com/research/rewriting-collatz.pdf>.
DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10817-022-09658-8>.

## 2. Exact multiplier pullback and its ternary precision cost

Let a legal parity word have length `h`, odd count `p`, and affine numerator

\[
2^hT^h(n)=3^p n+B.
\tag{1}
\]

If `p>0`, then `3` does not divide `B`. Indeed, write the odd positions as
`0≤j₁<⋯<j_p<h`. The usual branch composition gives

\[
B=\sum_{i=1}^p 2^{j_i}3^{p-i};
\]

only the last term survives modulo `3`. Therefore `gcd(3^p,B)=1`. For `p=0`
the corresponding statement is `gcd(1,0)=1`.

Suppose we try to pull multiplication by a positive integer `m` backward
through this same word, using an affine ordinary source `q=mn+c`, `c≥0`.
The required identity is

\[
3^p(mn+c)+B=m(3^p n+B).
\]

It is equivalent to

\[
3^pc=(m-1)B.
\tag{2}
\]

The coprimality just proved gives the **exact criterion**

\[
\boxed{\text{An integral correction exists}\iff 3^p\mid m-1.}
\tag{3}
\]

When it exists it is unique, with `c=(m−1)B/3^p`. Moreover,

\[
q-n=\frac{m-1}{3^p}(3^pn+B)
    =2^h\frac{m-1}{3^p}T^h(n).
\]

Thus `q≡n mod2^h`: the same physical parity word is automatically legal, not
merely the same formal affine expression. Equation (1) then proves

\[
\boxed{T^h(q)=mT^h(n).}
\tag{4}
\]

The file `PullbackArithmetic.lean` proves the correction criterion, uniqueness,
affine identity and binary congruence at the general coprime-integer level.
The displayed word formula supplies its Collatz-specific hypothesis. The file
does not encode all parity words or claim universal component preservation.

### Two source-pinned examples

The table row starting at `27` performs its first odd step and then multiplies
`41` by `13`. The prefix has `(p,B)=(1,1)`, so the ordinary pullback is

\[
q=13n+4,\qquad T(q)=13T(n)
\]

on the legal odd cylinder. At `n=27`, `q=355` and `T(q)=533`.

The row starting at `103` performs two odd steps before multiplying `233` by
`13`. Here `(p,B)=(2,5)`, and (2) would require

\[
9c=60,\qquad c=20/3.
\]

No ordinary integer correction exists with that same prefix. This failure
holds for the whole source cylinder, not just the displayed integer `103`.
Both arithmetic examples are checked in `PullbackArithmetic.lean`.

Even (4) is not a merger with the original source when `m` is arbitrary: its
right-hand endpoint is multiplied by `m`. No basin closure under a wild
multiplier is assumed.

### An unconditional physical special case

When `m=2^a` and `3^p` divides `2^a−1`, the endpoint in (4) has exactly the
known factor `2^a`. Appending `a` actual even steps gives

\[
\boxed{T^{h+a}\!\left(2^a n+\frac{2^a-1}{3^p}B\right)=T^h(n).}
\tag{5}
\]

This is a genuine all-parameter common-endpoint lift. It is generally an
upward source construction, and by itself does not give the needed smaller
partner. For `p=1`, `a=2`, and the one-odd-step word, it recovers the familiar
`4n+1` odd-predecessor relation.

## 3. A new physical diamond with an unbounded odd-run application

Direct affine synthesis found the following two exact ordinary paths, for
every `v≥0`:

\[
\boxed{
 T^8(256v+184)=T^8(768v+554)=27v+20.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Their parity words are respectively `00011100` and `01000001`. Every parity
and integer division in (6) is checked in `WildLift.lean`, theorem
`WildLift.bridge_eight`. In terms of `A=256v+184`, it says

\[
T^8(A)=T^8(3A+2).
\]

For any `k≥1`, positive odd `u`, and

\[
3^{k-1}u\equiv185\pmod{256},
\]

put

\[
n=2^ku-1,\qquad x=2^{k-1}u-1=(n-1)/2.
\]

The exact consecutive-odd formulas give

\[
T^k(n)=3^ku-1,
\qquad T^{k-1}(x)=3^{k-1}u-1.
\]

Their endpoints have the shape `3A+2` and `A` from (6). Hence

\[
\boxed{0<x<n,\qquad T^{k+7}(x)=T^{k+8}(n).}
\tag{7}
\]

The congruence ensures `3^{k-1}u≥185`, so `x` is positive even when `k=1`.
For every `k` there are infinitely many positive odd `u` satisfying the
congruence, because powers of `3` are invertible modulo `256`.

Crucially, `3^ku−1≡42 mod256`, so its 2-adic valuation is **exactly one**.
This family is outside the earlier two-step diamond's post-run valuation
`≥2` condition. It handles arbitrarily long initial odd runs with a partner
less than half the source. The generic deletion and further short–long–short
compositions are developed in the companion symbolic packet; (6) is the new
eight-step input supplied here.

This excludes the family from being a least positive counterexample. It does
not independently assert convergence of every member without the usual
all-smaller-sources induction hypothesis.

## 4. A physically certified replacement on one classical branch

The first semigroup-assisted table row, `n≡27 mod128`, was used as a search
seed. The new search uses only actual forward steps and actual inverse
Collatz branches. It refines this branch into the `576` disjoint progressions

\[
n=n_0+73728t,\qquad
0<n_0<73728,\quad n_0\equiv27\pmod{128},\quad t\ge0.
\]

Here `73728=2^13·3^2`. The certificate contains `446` successful rows. Every
row gives positive integers `x₀<n₀`, `0<S<73728` and physical words of lengths
`13` and at most `16`, such that for **every** `t≥0`,

\[
x=x_0+St<n_0+73728t=n,
\qquad T^{a}(x)=T^{13}(n).
\tag{8}
\]

Of the 576 progressions, `192` already have the elementary smaller predecessor
`(2n−1)/3`, and `207` have direct descent by clock `13` from their least positive
representative, which transports to the whole progression. Their union has
`330` classes. The certificate contains these and **116 additional classes**.
This comparison is recomputed by the verifier. It is not a claim of priority
over every merger elsewhere in the repository.

For example, an additional class beyond those two baselines is

\[
n=283+73728t,
\quad x=239+62208t,
\quad T^{8}(x)=T^{13}(n).
\]

### Why the finite certificate proves infinite progressions

If a source `n₀` follows a word of length `b` with `p` odd steps, the elementary
parity-cylinder identity is

\[
T^b(n_0+2^b z)=T^b(n_0)+3^p z.
\]

The verifier checks `2^13 |73728`, `2^a|S`, equality of the base endpoints,
and equality of their slopes,

\[
3^p\frac{73728}{2^{13}}=3^q\frac{S}{2^a}.
\]

These conditions prove (8), while the two strict inequalities prove descent
for all `t≥0`. There are no omitted small exceptions in these 446 rows.

## 5. The attempted completion and the remaining step

The intended route was to replace the semigroup proof's nonphysical
multipliers by legal ordinary mergers while retaining its contraction. The
pullback criterion isolates the exact ternary precision cost, and (6)–(8)
give genuine smaller-source replacements. They do not cover the complement.

Even the particular diamond `A↔3A+2` cannot close every input synchronously:
`A=8` reaches `1` in three shortcut steps, while `3A+2=26` reaches it in eight.
They thereafter occupy opposite phases of the two-cycle, and their earlier
paths do not meet at equal clocks. Other partners and clocks are necessary.

A finite directed cycle of pure ternary-scaling diamonds cannot repair this.
Pad the finitely many diamonds to a common clock `H` and common modulus
`2^M`, `M≥H`. A positive-exponent cycle would imply

\[
T^H(v-1)=T^H(3^R v-1),\qquad 3^Rv\equiv v\pmod{2^M},\quad R>0.
\]

The congruence fixes the same first `H` parities at both ends. Their output
difference is then `3^p(3^R−1)v/2^H`, which is positive for ordinary `v>0`, a
contradiction. An unbounded erasure proof therefore needs growing precision,
growing clocks, or another kind of physical rewrite; a finite residue cycle
does not supply it.

The full remaining task is an all-source physical reduction with a proved
terminating selector. No such selector, fixed-floor survivor estimate, or
full Collatz proof is asserted in this packet.
