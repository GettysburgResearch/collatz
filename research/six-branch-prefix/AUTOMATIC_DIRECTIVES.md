# Automatic directives in expanding integer charts

Let P>Q≥2 be coprime integers, choose a prime p dividing Q, and suppose an integer trajectory has a finite set of integer digit errors

    Q x_(n+1)=P x_n+d_n.

Write α=Q/P and f(z)=Σ d_n z^n. If f(α) has the same rational value in the real and p-adic completions, all states are bounded. In particular no positive integer trajectory in the six-branch chart can have such a directive. Indeed the recurrence telescopes to

    α^n x_n=x_0+(1/P)Σ_(j<n)d_j α^j.

Its left side tends p-adically to zero. If f(α)=−P x_0 also in the real embedding, then x_n=−(1/P)Σ_(j≥0)d_(n+j) α^j is uniformly bounded. In the six-branch chart all digits and x_0 are positive, so its real sum could not equal −P x_0 in the first place.

## Unconditional scalar case

**PROPOSED.** Suppose the digit series satisfies a scalar Mahler equation

    f(z)=A(z)+B(z) f(z^k),       k≥2,  A,B ∈ Q[z].

Then any such integer trajectory is bounded. This includes every coding of a fixed point of a constant-length substitution on two states, as well as the binary automatic sequences generated by a two-state most-significant-digit automaton. For binary state indicators the state transition for each appended digit r is one of 0, 1, x or 1−x as a function of the preceding binary state x. Summing the resulting relations over residue classes gives rational A with possible factor 1−z^k in its denominator, and polynomial B. The same proof applies to rational A,B without poles along the evaluation sequence (in either completion). An arbitrary output coding is a rational affine change of a two-valued indicator, up to an additive rational series.

Here is a direct p-adic auxiliary-polynomial proof. If B vanishes at some α^{k^j}, unrolling up to that zero gives the same rational value in both embeddings. If f is a rational formal series, its real and p-adic evaluations agree wherever both bounded-digit series converge: clear its polynomial denominator, cancel any rational removable factor, and use the same formal identity in both completions. Otherwise Fatou's theorem on bounded integer coefficients gives transcendence over Q(z). Assume f(α) rational in Q_p. All f(α^{k^j}) are rational by the scalar recurrence and their logarithmic heights are O(k^j): at each step the fixed rational coefficients have height O(k^j) and iteration sums a geometric series.

For fixed D choose a nonzero polynomial R(z,Y)∈Z[z,Y] of degree at most D in each variable whose formal specialization R(z,f(z)) vanishes at zero to order at least (D+1)^2−1. This solves fewer homogeneous rational equations than unknowns. The resulting series is nonzero by formal transcendence. Its coefficients are p-adically bounded. Hence R(α^{k^j},f(α^{k^j})) is nonzero for sufficiently large j, with negative log p-adic absolute value at least ((D+1)^2−1) k^j log(1/|α|_p)−O_D(1). Its rational logarithmic height is at most C D k^j+O_R(1), with C independent of D. The numerator of a nonzero rational cannot have p-adic valuation larger than allowed by that height. First choose D large, then j large, to contradict rationality. Thus only the preceding cases can occur. This argument actually works with algebraic starting values in a fixed number field by the same height inequality at a fixed place, with a field-dependent constant.

## Any finite automaton, using a prior local theorem

**SOURCE-QUALIFIED.** If Proposition 3 of rStation paper 16 v1 is accepted as written, the boundedness conclusion holds for every k-automatic directive, without a two-state or scalar restriction. That proposition is a proposed p-adic linear-independence lifting result. We use it as an external input here; the scalar proof above is independent of it. [Paper 16 v1, sections 4–8](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/16/v1)

For clarity, the specialization needed is only one-variable. Include 1 and the series of all elements of the finite k-kernel of the digit sequence in a vector G(z). Splitting n by residues modulo k gives G(z)=M(z)G(z^k) with a polynomial matrix over Q (use the identity 1=1 for the constant function). Pick a Q(z)-basis F of the span from the entries of G. The same entries remain independent over the algebraic-constant rational-function field by expanding any finite coefficient extension in a Q-basis. Write G=RF. Combining the selected rows with M(z)R(z^k) gives F(z)=A(z)F(z^k) with A rational and invertible: a nonzero left kernel would be a relation among F. For sufficiently large m, every evaluation of A, its inverse, and R along α^{k^{m+j}} is free of poles. Transport the relation P x_0+f(α)=0 from Q_p through the polynomial matrix product up to m and then through R, obtaining a rational linear relation among F(α^{k^m}).

Proposition 3 applies with its slow variety a point, fast parameter X=α^{k^m}, and a dummy independent fast parameter Z=p/l for a prime l not dividing PQ. Both are positive real, p-adically less than one, and multiplicatively independent; the functions and system ignore Z. Independence over the enlarged rational-function field follows by clearing denominators and comparing Z coefficients. All selected series have bounded p-adic coefficients. Consequently the transported row is identically zero. The same row, obtained through rational matrices and the same polynomial identities, evaluates to zero in the real embedding. The boundedness observation applies.

In particular this proposed transfer would also exclude automaticity of the error digits of every positive integer ceiling orbit x_(n+1)=ceil(Px_n/Q), because these digits are nonnegative and the states increase. For P/Q=3/2 this is the minimal-word setting in Stephan's work, which separately proves that automaticity would force transcendence of the associated real orbit constant. The present cross-completion deduction relies on the indicated p-adic proposition for arbitrary automata. [Stephan, *Transcendence criteria for the minimal word of the rational base 3/2*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19007)

Automaticity is a condition on the ordered error letters here. The earlier [recognizable-set obstruction](README.md) concerns sets of integer state values. Neither condition is known for a hypothetical unrestricted six-branch survivor.
