# Detecting ordinary cycles by a single unconditioned affine trace

Write \(\alpha=\log_3 2\). For \(n\ge2\), choose \(1\le u\le n\) with \(D=2^n-3^u>0\). Both maps \(F_0(x)=x/2\) and \(F_1(x)=(3x+1)/2\) permute the residue classes modulo \(D\). Let \(P_{\rho,D}\) be the transition matrix choosing these two maps with probabilities \(1-\rho\) and \(\rho\). Define \(N_{n,u}\) to count the rooted binary words of length \(n\) and weight \(u\) which give an ordinary positive integer cycle, allowing repetitions. A root is a distinct binary word, so a word of least period \(d\mid n\) gives \(d\) roots.

**Complete-modulus trace theorem.** Fix \(C>0\). For every \(0\le\rho\le1\), and uniformly in \(n,u\),
\[
 \operatorname{tr} P_{\rho,D}^n
   =D\rho^u(1-\rho)^{n-u}N_{n,u}+E_{n,u}(\rho),\qquad
 0\le E_{n,u}(\rho)\le \exp(o(n)).                                      \tag{1}
\]
For every \(1\le t\le Cn\), the total contribution to \(\operatorname{tr}P_{\rho,D}^t\) of all word weights \(0\le v\le t\) with \(nv\ne ut\) is at most \(\exp(o_C(n))\). The implied subexponential rate is ineffective. If \(\gcd(n,u)=1\), the entire trace is subexponential at every such time not divisible by \(n\). The same statements hold with the Markov matrix on \(D^e\) for each fixed positive integer \(e\), with the same leading coefficient \(D\) in (1).

**Proof.** A length-\(t\), weight-\(v\) word with ones at zero-based indices \(i_1<\cdots<i_v\) gives
\[
 F_w(x)=(3^v x+C_w)/2^t,\qquad C_w=\sum_{j=1}^v 2^{i_j}3^{v-j}.
\]
Its number of modular fixed points is zero or \(\gcd(D^e,|2^t-3^v|)\) according as that divisor fails to divide or divides \(C_w\). At \((t,v)=(n,u)\) the divisor is exactly \(D\). Since \(C_w>0\), the unique rational fixed point \(C_w/D\) is positive. All its iterates have denominator dividing \(D\): at every rotation the corresponding carry is integral, and the rotated fixed point is the affine image of the preceding fixed point. Equivalently the fixed-point congruences modulo powers of two force the prescribed word's ordinary parities. Thus \(D\mid C_w\) is precisely an ordinary cycle.

For the competing types use Corvaja and Zannier, Corollary 1, equation (1.3). The rational \(S\)-units \(a=2^n3^{-u}\) and \(b=2^t3^{-v}\), with \(S=\{2,3,\infty\}\), are multiplicatively independent exactly when \(nv\ne ut\). Their heights are \(O_C(n)\). At every prime outside \(S\), the positive gcd contribution for \(a-1,b-1\) equals the common prime valuation of \(D,2^t-3^v\), times the logarithm of that prime. The first integer is prime to six, so these contributions sum to the logarithm of the full integer gcd. Dropping the other nonnegative contributions from the bound in (1.3) yields
\[
 \sup_{1\le t\le Cn,\;nv\ne ut}\log\gcd(D,|2^t-3^v|)=o_C(n).             \tag{2}
\]
This corollary already permits all independent pairs apart from a finite set for each error tolerance. No moving exceptional translate remains. The bound on the \(D^e\) gcd follows since it divides \(\gcd(D,|2^t-3^v|)^e\). Summing over words uses probabilities of total mass at most one. If \(\gcd(n,u)=1\), any resonance \(nv=ut\) requires \(n\mid t\). [Corvaja and Zannier, pp. 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0311030)

**Two exponential rates on the critical slice.** Put \(u_n=\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor\), \(D_n=2^n-3^{u_n}\), and \(P_n=P_{\alpha,D_n}\). Set \(h=H_2(\alpha)\) in bits. Then
\[
 \frac{\log_2(1+\operatorname{tr}P_n^n)}{n}
      =(1-h)\,\boldsymbol1_{N_{n,u_n}>0}+o(1).                            \tag{3}
\]
To see the size of the signal, let \(\Lambda=n\log2-u_n\log3>0\). The linear-form estimate of Bondareva, Luchin and Salikhov gives \(\Lambda\gg n^{-4.116201}\). Hence \(D_n=2^n(1-e^{-\Lambda})=2^{n-O(\log n)}\). For an ordinary cycle let \(s\) be its least phase. The exact clock identity gives \(\Lambda=\sum_{\mathrm{odd\ steps}}\log(1+1/(3x))\le u_n/(3s)\), also when the cycle is presented repeatedly. There are \(O(n^{5.116201})\) possible integer minima, each identifying at most one cycle. Each gives at most \(n\) rooted words, so \(N_{n,u_n}=O(n^{6.116201})\). Finally \(\alpha^{u_n}(1-\alpha)^{n-u_n}=2^{-hn+O(1)}\). Equations (1) and (3) follow with no bounded-width or word-complexity hypothesis. [Linear-form source and application](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/5ac2b4a7ab9fee19d2e1b1c2e4aadecd08413128/research/astra-diagonal-frontier-20261127/arithmetic/WIDTH_THREE.md)

There are infinitely many distinct positive ordinary cycles if and only if the limsup in (3) is \(1-h\). Indeed, the complete-denominator census at denominator one bounds the sum of the positive least-period clock gaps over all actual cycles, so all but finitely many have gap less than \(\log3\) and hence land on the critical slice. Conversely, each fixed cycle of least-period gap \(\Lambda_0>0\) has only finitely many repeated presentations of gap less than \(\log3\). Thus a subexponential trace bound along the whole slice implies finiteness of the ordinary cycles. Excluding each nontrivial ordinary cycle still needs bounds at finite periods, including any exceptional off-slice periods. **Fourier location of the surviving term.** On the character with frequency \(j\bmod D^e\), each word acts by a unit complex phase and sends its frequency to \(3^v2^{-t}j\). Its conductor is \(d=D^e/\gcd(D^e,j)\). The diagonal character matrix entry vanishes unless \(d\mid2^t-3^v\). Thus for any fixed \(\delta>0\), all conductors \(d\ge e^{\delta n}\) have identically zero diagonal contributions from every off-slope word with \(t\le Cn\) once \(n\) is large, by (2). At time \(n\) the critical words return only conductors dividing \(D\), even in a fixed higher-power cover. There the affine phase at frequency \(j\bmod D\) is \(\exp(2\pi i j C_w2^{-n}/D)\). Its sum over the frequencies is exactly \(D\boldsymbol1_{D\mid C_w}\). All estimates above leave this exact carried sum at its single resonant clock undetermined. In particular, the quiet neighboring powers by themselves give no bound at that power. The gcd method previously applied to fixed symbol dilation rays in PR 211; here its inputs range over all independently sloped clocks of comparable size. **An increasing time window.** Set \(\rho=u/n\). There is an ineffective sequence \(L_n\to\infty\) on which the total off-slope contribution remains \(\exp(o(n))\), uniformly over \(1\le t\le nL_n\) and all admissible \(u\). To prove this, write \(t=kn+r\), \(0\le r<n\), and \(w=v-ku\). Since \(2^n3^{-u}\equiv1\pmod D\), off-slope gcds are unchanged when \(2^t3^{-v}-1\) is replaced by \(2^r3^{-w}-1\), with denominators interpreted as units modulo \(D\). Also \(nw-ur=nv-ut\). Equation (2) extends with the same proof to \(0\le r<n,\ |w|\le Bn\) for every fixed \(B\), since the rational height bound also allows signed exponents. By slow diagonalization choose \(B_n\to\infty\) so that this gcd is uniformly \(\exp(o(n))\) whenever \(|w|\le B_n n\). Take \(L_n=B_n^2/4\). Under the word law, \(v\) is binomial with mean \(\rho t\), and \(\mathbb E w=\rho r\in[0,n]\). Hoeffding's inequality gives a probability at most \(2\exp[-2(B_n-1)^2n^2/t]\) for the complement of the good interval. Even after multiplication by \(D\le2^n\), this tail tends to zero uniformly. For a fixed cover \(D^e\), use \(L_n=B_n^2/(4e)\). Thus coprime clock vectors have subexponential full trace at every nonmultiple of \(n\) throughout an increasing multiple of their period.

These neighboring moments alone allow the spike in (3). For example choose a dimension \(d=n\lfloor2^n/n\rfloor\), let \(K\) permute its coordinates in blocks of length \(n\), let \(J\) have every entry \(1/d\), and put \(M=rK+(1-r)J\) for \(r=2^{-h}\). This strictly positive doubly stochastic matrix has the uniform stationary law uniquely and satisfies \(\operatorname{tr}M^t=1+r^t(\operatorname{tr}K^t-1)\). All nonmultiples of \(n\) have trace at most one, while time \(n\) has rate \(1-h\). This example concerns general stochastic matrices. The arithmetic condition remaining for the two affine maps is the exact resonant character sum above.

**Why the complete determinant matters even for these affine generators.** There is a sequence of genuine primitive positive rational words of length \(n\) and nearcritical weight \(u\), of bounded physical amplitude, whose exact reduced denominators satisfy \(q>1\) and \(\log q=o(n)\). Use the nearcritical diagonal in PR 212's independent-clock construction. Put \(d=D/q=2^{n-o(n)}\). On this proper modulus the constructed word is an identity for the very same permutations \(F_0,F_1\). The normalized trace there has exactly the active exponential rate \(1-h\): the word supplies \(d\alpha^u(1-\alpha)^{n-u}\), and the off-slope contribution is at most \(\exp(o(n))\) by (2). For the upper bound, a resonant word is an identity mod \(d\) exactly when its own reduced denominator divides \(q\). For each such denominator \(b\), its positive rational minimum has numerator \(O(b n^{5.116201})\) by the same clock estimate as above, with at most \(n\) rooted words for each minimum. Summing over \(b\mid q\) gives \(\exp(o(n))\) resonant words. The specified PR 212 word fails to be identity mod \(D\), by its exact \(q>1\). The presence of other ordinary words at those clocks is unknown. The construction has unbounded \(\gcd(n,u)\); it supplies no coprime-clock example. Thus even within the two actual affine generators and even with primitive physical words, changing the modulus by a factor of subexponential size can produce the full active trace exponent.
