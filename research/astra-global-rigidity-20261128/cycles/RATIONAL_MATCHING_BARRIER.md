# Exact rational cycles with uniformly fragmented native rotations

There are primitive positive rational cycles with growing least odd period \(p\), complete reduced denominator \(q>1\) satisfying \(\log q=o(p)\), polynomially bounded real minimum \(m\ge p^{2-o(1)}\), bounded real maximum-to-minimum ratio, and all native exponents in \(\{1,2\}\), on which
\[
          \max_{0<r<p}\ \max\{L:\text{some two blocks at lag }r
                              \text{ match for }L\text{ native steps}\}=o(p).
\]
Thus every fixed number of matching runs at any proper lag covers only \(o(p)\) positions in these actual orbits. This coexists with far stronger uniform fragmentation than the necessary positive residual mass for an ordinary cycle. It identifies a limitation on combining that mass condition with a polynomial **real** minimum and nearcriticality alone.

Use the strong nearcritical diagonal of PR212's independent-clock construction. Its shortcut clock is \(h=Nn\), odd clock \(p=Nb\), with coprime upper convergents \(n/b\to\log_2 3\), and \(N=h^{o(1)}\). It proves \(\log q=o(h)=o(p)\), odd width at most \(2+h^{-1+o(1)}\), \(m\ge h^{2-o(1)}\), and absolute determinant small enough that \(0<h-p\log_2 3<h^{-1+o(1)}\). Markers selected by phase give exponents one or two. Primitivity follows because any repetition multiplicity divides \(\gcd(h,p)=N\) and therefore any smaller period would be a cell shift, which fails on at least one independent prime marker. The exact denominator formula contains every nontrivial cyclotomic cell factor without cancellation and gives \(q>1\). Separately \(m\ll p^{B+1}\) follows from the usual two-log bound and positive lap product; here \(h=O(p)\). All physical odd numerators thus satisfy \(\log \max y_i\le\log q+O(\log p)=o(p)\).

If two blocks at any proper lag agree for \(L\) native steps, subtraction of their complete affine maps gives \(2^{A_L}\mid y_u-y_{u+r}\), where \(A_L\ge L\) is the common halving total. The source difference is nonzero by primitivity, so \(2^L\le |y_u-y_{u+r}|\le\max_i y_i=\exp(o(p))\), uniformly in the lag and phases. This proves the assertion.

The candidates have width close to two and already fail the separate ordinary-cycle width inequalities. This comparison concerns the discriminatory power of fragmentation and the stated height/denominator premises. It does not assert that the rational cycles meet every known necessary ordinary-cycle condition.
