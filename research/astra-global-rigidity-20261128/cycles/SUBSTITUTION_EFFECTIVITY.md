# Effective onset for the primitive uniform substitution bound

The fixed-degree constant in the uniform-subshift measure is computable from the primitive aperiodic binary substitution and the degree. The following is an algorithmic qualification of that theorem. It need not give a practical cutoff.

The finite factors of the primitive language can be computed exactly. Start with the internal adjacent pairs in the two images. Close under the rule sending a legal pair \(ab\) to the crossing pair formed by the last letter of \(\sigma(a)\) and first of \(\sigma(b)\). Every added pair occurs in an iterate, and every pair occurring in an iterate arises either inside one image or across an earlier pair. Both letters recur by primitivity. For \(k^t\ge J\), all length-\(J\) factors are precisely those in the words \(\sigma^t(ab)\) over these legal pairs. Deep occurrences cross at most one block boundary, and every listed word occurs in deeper iterates.

For the auxiliary degree \(m\), the height constants independent of \(m\) in (5), (8) and (9) come from explicit degrees and coefficient norms of finitely many rational functions. Use the coefficient \(1\)-norm bound for polynomial evaluation and the elementary height inequalities for sums, products and quotients. Thus one can choose \(m\) effectively with \(m^2\log2>2d(c_2+1)m\). Use exact rational linear algebra over \(\mathbb Q\) or \(\mathbb Q(Y)\) for every legal \(m^2\)-letter prefix, clearing all denominators.

Increase \(J\ge m^2+1\). For each legal length-\(J\) word use its assigned polynomial, and compute all coefficients of its substituted power series of degrees less than \(J\). Stop when each such finite word has a nonzero coefficient in this range. This search terminates by the compactness and fixed-weight or formal transcendence argument in the main proof. The resulting finite nonzero leading coefficients have explicit degrees, heights and multiplicities. In the varying-weight case, remove the finitely many rational roots of the resulting integer polynomials from the tail of \(3^{-\delta^j}\); this is effective by rational root bounds and the known growth of \(|\delta|^j\). The same coefficient norm and height inequalities bound each remaining leading valuation by \(c_m(1+|\delta|^j)\). Fix the onset at which this is strictly below \(k^j\) and the slow term from (9) falls below the chosen linear margin in \(k^j\). For the fixed weights, compute the relevant valuations exactly. Each subsequent inequality for the choice of \(N_j=k^j\) and \(V\) in (8)–(9) then involves only the recorded constants. Increasing the bound to absorb small \(j\) is effective.

This gives a computable finite matching horizon uniformly for all intercepts and fixed degree without enumerating algebraic approximants. The numerical rate and feasible sizes of the auxiliary matrices depend on the substitution. No effective constants for the separate general finite-complexity theorem follow from this argument.
