# An all-interval power bound for the full original first-hit correction

**Source-qualified written deduction.** The proof retains the supplied certified-stage counts, tagged-source transport, natural first-passage measures and canonical normalizations. Their analytic and formal dependencies were read at the cited sources; those external developments were not rebuilt in this pass.

The checkpoint12 concurrent refresh also credits PR212's complete [raw landing moment](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/e55fb7cac646013f143b563014ae97b105bc7547/research/astra-global-20261128/escape/RAW_LANDING_MOMENTS.md) and [all-scale fibre-density](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/e55fb7cac646013f143b563014ae97b105bc7547/research/astra-global-20261128/escape/ALL_SCALE_FIBRE_DENSITY.md) results. Their overlapping untrimmed conclusions and sharper small-order logarithmic rate are compared explicitly in the [companion moment note](UNTRIMMED_LOG_MOMENTS_AND_CLOCKS.md).

For a positive odd unit $y$, let $B_y(n)$ be the sum of $\log(1+1/(3v))$ over odd departures strictly before the first visit to $y$, on the true odd predecessor basin of $y$. Let $\lambda_y$ be its **unnormalized** relative natural source measure, of total mass $d(y)$. Leading even halvings do not change the mark, so the all-positive source normalization agrees. At a periodic target take its first hit. The existence of these measures is the original first-passage input. Put $g=1-H_2(\log_3 2)+1/351$, with $H_2$ binary entropy in bits, as in Weaver 102/v1.

For every $0<c<g$ there is $C_c<\infty$ such that
\[
 \boxed{\quad\sup_{y\ {\rm positive\ odd\ unit}}\ \sup_{|I|\le r}
                   \lambda_y(I)\le C_c r^c,\qquad 0<r\le1/2.\quad}       \tag{1}
\]
The supremum ranges over all real intervals and the entire first-hit measure. The constants are independent of $y$, the location of $I$, and the source cutoff taken to define the natural measure. The proof does not assume Collatz convergence. This supplies a power bound for an individual worst interval. The sharp fixed-discard quantile and collision estimates, and the distinct fixed-root dimension results, are prior. [PR215, fixed-discard physical energy and typical cell information, 121054cf](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/121054cf6569859585ede5c2d6e024eb2d97bbc5/research/astra-nine-hour-20261128/escape/phase6/CRITICAL_QUANTILE_COVER.md) · [PR212, uniform qualitative spatial concentration, (7), 0172f151](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/0172f15109b8749c14fed272e733c97a5a0f7d24/research/astra-global-20261128/escape/SPATIAL_TRACE_TAIL.md) · [Weaver 175/v1, natural terminal laws and their fixed-root dimensions](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/175)

On any grid of mesh $r$, let $m_b=\lambda_y([b,b+r))$ denote all its bin masses. A simultaneous full moment bound is
\[
 \sum_b m_b^p\ll_{p,\epsilon}r^{\min(p-1,pg)-\epsilon},\qquad
                   p>1,\quad \epsilon>0,\quad y\text{ any positive odd unit}.             \tag{2}
\]
All statements concern the complete untrimmed measure. The estimates use the first *uncertified* actual starting labels from Weaver's descent proof, with both their cardinalities and the number of physical sources landing there. An uncertified label may later reach the target after unrestricted time.

## What is retained at the first uncertified stage

Use the shortcut map $T$, with $T(2n)=n$, $T(2n+1)=3n+2$. Work first with ordinary positive starts up to $R$ (an upper count which includes all odd starts). Select a fixed sufficiently large lower binary rank $q_0$. Follow Weaver 102/v1's completed good stages while the current rank is at least $q_0$; each has a lower planned rank, and its endpoint is the actual first passage to that planned height. Stop on reaching rank below $q_0$ or encountering the first uncertified stage's initial site $w$. Include the initial start if itself uncertified. Let $K_R=1+\lceil\log_2 R\rceil$. The following consequences, with an absolute finite $C$, are proved here from that paper's precise inputs. [Weaver 102/v1 §§2–3, (5)–(10), (13), (18)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/102)

1. The elapsed shortcut time to this stop is $O(K_R)$. Indeed it contains only completed good stages, whose total time is at most $cK_R/(1-r_0)$ by that paper's (10), where $r_0=999999/1000000$, $c=15/14$. The empty prefix has time zero. Thus the stop is defined at a finite date even when the subsequent ordinary orbit diverges.
2. Classify first uncertified labels in bands indexed by $q$. For initial bad sources use their starting rank $q$; for later bad endpoints use their last planned threshold rank $q$, so $2^{q-1}<w\le2^q$. The number of possible bad labels in the group is $O((q+1)2^{(1-g)q})$ by (6) for $B_q$ and $LB_q$. The finitely many boundary ranks may instead be included in the finite low set.
3. The number of starts through $R$ ending in the bad group $q$ is $O(R K_R^C2^{-gq})$. To see this for later endpoints, work in a physical source shell $[2^K,2^{K+1})$, including any last partial shell by enlarging it. A possible planned rank $q$ is at most $K-1$, and the elapsed time is $O(K+1)$ by (10). At any fixed time and endpoint the tagged-source bound (9) at height $2^q$ applies with scaled loss $D_q=(q+2)/r'$, by (10), where $r'=r_0/c$. For large $q$, $D_q/2^q\le1/3$. Thus there are $O((q+1)2^{K-q})$ possible starts per endpoint and time. Multiplying by the count in item 2 and the clock allowance gives $O((K+1)(q+1)^2 2^K2^{-gq})$. This is exactly the individual summand from (18), before summing over planned ranks. An initially bad shell has $O((K+1)2^K2^{-gK})$ labels directly from (6). Summing over all shells uses $\sum_{K\le\log_2 R}2^K=O(R)$ and proves the stated bound. At finitely small $q$, the trivial $R$ bound suffices.

For each start that reaches $y$, truncate this stop earlier if necessary at its first hit on $y$. There is then one additional possible stopped vertex, namely $y$, with arbitrary multiplicity. Combine this with the bounded low labels in one extra group. All remaining groups have the same or smaller label counts and source counts. In all groups the continued mark $B_y(w)$ is deterministic for each stopped label $w$ actually used. For even labels its definition simply uses the remaining odd departures of the shortcut path.

## Mark counting and a full finite-source moment

Set $R=r^{-M}$ with fixed $M>\max(100,2/\eta,2/g)$, where $\eta=1-H_2(\log_3 2)+7/2500$ is the singleton and failure exponent of Weaver 102/v1 (3); harmless rounding is allowed. On a retained path starting at $z\le R$ let $t$ be its shortcut date at the stopped $w$ and $j$ its number of odd departures through that date. Always $j\le t\ll K_R$. The ordinary log-correction identity is
\[
         B_y(z)=B_y(w)+\log w+t\log2-j\log3-\log z.                    \tag{3}
\]
Thus for a fixed triple $(w,t,j)$ at most $C(rR+1)$ actual integers $z\le R$ have marks in any given interval of width at most $3r$. Their log heights are in a translated interval of width $3r$ and their heights are bounded by $R$. This uses neither a phase independence assumption nor a duration bound for the future after $w$.

Form finite subprobabilities by assigning mass $1/R$ to each successful odd $z\le R$ in its group of stopped labels. Write $m_{q,b}$ for the mass in the $b$-th grid bin from a bad group $q$, and $m_{0,b}^*$ for the extra finite-label group. With $q$ from a bounded lower index through $K_R$, the three stage bounds and (3) give
\[
 \sum_b m_{q,b}\ll K_R^C 2^{-gq},\qquad
 \sup_b m_{q,b}\ll K_R^C\min\{2^{-gq},(r+R^{-1})2^{(1-g)q}\}.         \tag{4}
\]
For the finite group the bounds are $1$ and $O(K_R^C(r+R^{-1}))$, uniformly in the target including the exceptional label $y$. These bounds hold as well if all input odd labels are assigned smaller nonnegative weights, or if any interval of length at most $3r$ is used in the supremum. Summing the two sides of the minimum before and after $2^q\asymp1/r$ gives total maximum bin mass $O(K_R^{C'}r^g)$.

For $p>1$ apply $\sum_b m_{q,b}^p\le (\sup_b m_{q,b})^{p-1}\sum_bm_{q,b}$. Below $2^q=1/r$ this is at most $K_R^{C_p}r^{p-1}2^{q[(p-1)-pg]}$; above that crossover it is at most $K_R^{C_p}2^{-pgq}$. The extra group's bound is $K_R^{C_p}r^{p-1}$. Minkowski's inequality over $O(K_R)$ groups gives a combined finite-source $p$-th moment $O_p(K_R^{C'_p}r^{\min(p-1,pg)})$. This step retains both absolute mass and worst-bin control from the *same* exceptional group.

## Transfer of the entire original law

If $y>R$, the far sources whose first hit at $y$ occurs *before* their first odd landing through $R$ have total limiting upper mass $O(R^{-\eta})$. Indeed every such source has exactly the same first-$R$ landing label as $y$, including failure if $y$ never enters. Apply the uniform singleton/failure bound (3) of Weaver 102/v1 once to this single deterministic label. If $y\le R$ this early event is empty. Every remaining successful source $n>R$ first lands through $R$ at some odd $z$ before or at its first hit at $y$. All prior departed odd states are distinct and exceed $R$. The uniform simple-path dyadic packing estimate $O(Y^{49/50})$ sites in $[Y,2Y]$ bounds the sum of their logarithmic corrections by $C R^{-1/50}$, uniformly in the path, by dyadic summation. Thus the original mark differs from the later deterministic mark $B_y(z)$ by at most $r$ once $r$ is sufficiently small. [Uniform simple-path reciprocal packing, PR212 at 3131111](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/31311116eac06e87320c84e11de6c105292a6f78/research/astra-infinite-descent-20261127/escape/HIGH_TARGET_ROWS.md)

The limiting odd first-landing subprobability $\mu_R$ obeys $\mu_R(E)\le F_R(|E|/R+R^{-g})$, uniformly for all sets $E$ of labels, where $F_R=C\exp(C\sqrt{\log R})$. Its excess above the pointwise envelope $F_R/R$ has *total mass* at most $F_R R^{-g}$: apply the set inequality once to the set of all labels with $\mu_R(z)>F_R/R$. Equivalently split its finite-label measure as $\mu_R^0+\mu_R^1$, with $\mu_R^0(z)=\min(\mu_R(z),F_R/R)$ and $\|\mu_R^1\|\le F_R R^{-g}$. This is the clipping used in Weaver's entropy argument. No additive error is charged separately for every fine bin. [Weaver 102/v1, Theorem 1, and §5 before (25)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/102)

Push these two submeasures, restricted to $z$ reaching $y$, by $B_y(z)$. The deterministic binned pushforward of $\mu_R^0$ satisfies the preceding finite-source estimates multiplied in norm by $F_R$, since its individual label weights are at most $F_R/R$. For $\mu_R^1$ all of its $\ell^p$ bin norms are at most its total mass. For the original mark law, the mass in any bin is at most the sum of the deterministic pushforward masses in that bin and its two neighbors, since every prior added correction is between zero and $r$. One can take the far-source limit first at this fixed $R$ for the finite label sets defining these neighbors. The exceptional before-$R$ hits contribute a positive measure of total mass $O(R^{-\eta})$, added once in norm: along any subsequence of far-source measures take a further weakly convergent subsequence of the two event restrictions on the compact first-hit mark support. Their total weak limit is $\lambda_y$, and the exceptional mass bound survives. To avoid any reliance on weak convergence at an interval endpoint, continuously majorize a closed test bin by a slightly enlarged interval and use at most five neighboring deterministic bins, or use the corresponding weak-limit domination on the enlarged open grid bins. The multiplicity is an absolute constant. Therefore the original worst-bin and all-bin $\ell^p$ estimates follow, with $F_R R^{-g}+O(R^{-\eta})$ added once in norm. In (2) this residual is negligible since $M\min(g,\eta)>2$; $F_R K_R^{C_p}=r^{-o(1)}$. An original arbitrary interval of width $r$ is covered by at most two of the grid bins. This proves (1)–(2).

## Physical unit-bin consequence

Let $\mu_y^{\rm phys}$ be the normalized canonical law of the absolute real intercept on a nonperiodic odd target $y$. The first-hit measure $\lambda_y$, weighted by $e^{-B}$ and transformed by $B\mapsto y(1-e^{-B})$, equals a fixed positive constant times $(h(y)/y)\mu_y^{\rm phys}$. Every first visit has $e^{-B}$ bounded above and below by positive absolute constants by distinct-path packing. The preimage, within the support, of any physical interval of width one is consequently a log-mark interval of length at most $C/y$. Using (1), for each positive $c<g$,
\[
    \sup_k\frac{h(y)}{y}\mu_y^{\rm phys}([k,k+1))\ll_c y^{-c}.        \tag{6}
\]
For instance apply (1) with $r=C/y$. On a fixed escaping component ray $h(y)/y\to H_C>0$, so its full normalized physical measure has $\sup_k\mu_y^{\rm phys}([k,k+1))\ll_C y^{-c}$ and the stronger complete square-energy bound
\[
 y\sum_k\mu_y^{\rm phys}([k,k+1))^2\ll_{C,\epsilon} y^{1-2g+\epsilon}. \tag{7}
\]
For this use (2) with $p=2$ and change variables on the support. The inverse images of the unit bins are disjoint intervals of lengths between $1/y$ and $C/y$, except for the two boundary pieces. A grid of mesh $1/y$ covers each by $O(1)$ cells and each cell occurs in $O(1)$ such covers, so the $\ell^2$ bound survives the variable bin widths. The survival weight is bounded. This includes every bin, including any rare heavy spikes; it leaves a gap to the sharp $\sqrt{\log y}$ order for fixed-discard energy. At a fixed nonperiodic $y$, the same bounded change of variables gives a positive uniform Frostman exponent for its full physical fibre at arbitrarily fine absolute scales. ## Periodic roots with their full stationary lap mixture

Use the relative complementary physical mark $b=1-R_{\rm phys}/y$. At a periodic target of primitive lap multiplier $\omega=\prod_{\rm lap}3v/(3v+1)<1$, write $\tau_y=1-\omega$; set $\tau_y=1$ at nonperiodic $y$. The stationary full measure, after first weighting the first-hit mark by $e^{-B}$ and mapping to $b=e^{-B}$, is the mixture of its $b\mapsto\omega^k b$ images with probabilities $(1-\omega)\omega^k$, $k\ge0$, when normalized by the mass at first hit. Equivalently $(\tau_y h(y)/y)\mu_y^{\rm rel}$ is a fixed constant times this geometric mixture of the **unnormalized weighted** first-hit laws. This is the primitive-lap decomposition, with no omission of paths circling the target. [PR212, marked harmonic row normalization and lap factor](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/0172f15109b8749c14fed272e733c97a5a0f7d24/research/astra-global-20261128/escape/SPATIAL_TRACE_TAIL.md) · [Weaver 19/v2, (F9)–(F12)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/19)

The first-hit $b$ coordinate lies in $[c_*,1]$ at all targets by the simple-path product floor, so $B\mapsto e^{-B}$ has derivative bounded above and below on its entire support. Transfer (1) and (2) to any grid of relative $b$-mesh $r$ by bounded overlap, after multiplying by the bounded weight. At lap $k$ the pulled-back mesh is $r/\omega^k$. A first-hit worst-bin or $\ell^p$ estimate in norm $O(r^a)$ with $a<1$ bounds that lap by $C(r/\omega^k)^a$, also valid for $r/\omega^k>1/2$ by bounded total mass. Minkowski and $(1-\omega)\sum_{k\ge0}\omega^{k(1-a)}\le1/(1-a)$ preserve the bound uniformly even if $\omega$ is arbitrarily near one. Thus (1)–(2) on relative bins hold for $(\tau_y h(y)/y)\mu_y^{\rm rel}$ over every positive odd unit, and at relative scale $r=1/y$ give the corresponding lap-corrected estimates for physical unit bins.

## Complete escaping flux with separate translations on all components

For an escaping odd component $C$, let $B_\infty(n)=\sum_{\rm all\ odd\ departures}\log(1+1/(3v))$ on $C$, and let $\Lambda_C$ be its unnormalized natural source measure, of mass $p_C$. Distinct escape paths have $0\le B_\infty\le K_*$ uniformly by simple-path packing. These laws exist: along a fixed ray choose increasing targets $a_i$ whose predecessor basins exhaust $C$ in density by the finite minimum-seed argument. On its whole basin, $B_\infty(n)=B_{a_i}(n)+B_\infty(a_i)$. Thus the restricted law is the translated natural first-hit law; the error from the rest of $C$ is at most $p_C-d(a_i)\to0$. The component densities sum to one over all components by the same finite minimum tightness. In particular finite truncations of the escaping components lose arbitrarily small original natural upper mass. This last step is needed before treating a countable union. These arguments also give existence after any chosen separate real translations $t_C$, including unbounded translations across different components. [Fixed-seed density exhaustion](TRUE_BASIN_MASS_ACQUISITION.md)

Let $\Lambda=\sum_{C\ {\rm escaping}}(B\mapsto B+t_C)_*\Lambda_C$. Uniformly over **every choice of the translations**, its maximum bin and its untrimmed $p$-th bin moment satisfy (1)–(2). The same holds for the flux measure with each $\Lambda_C$ first weighted by $\kappa e^{-B}$, $\kappa=\log(4/3)/2$, of total mass $\sum_C H_C$, since this weight is bounded. To prove the assertion directly, discard the escaping sources that miss the first $R$-landing. They have total mass $O(R^{-\eta})$ from the one failure label. After that landing at $z$, the omitted earlier mark is at most $r$. In the finite-source counting proof now use only escaping $z$ and stop at the low set or first bad label $w$, without a target truncation. Replace $B_y(w)$ by the deterministic value $B_\infty(w)+t_{C(w)}$ in (3). The exact equation and every count in (4) are unchanged, independently of the size and number of the translations. Finite-component truncation or tightness of the translated countable measure justifies the weak-limit comparisons for the exceptional mass. Consequently
\[
       \sum_{C\ {\rm escaping}}\sup_{|I|\le r}\Lambda_C(I)
                       \ll_c r^c\qquad(0<c<g).                        \tag{8}
\]
For a finite list, translate one near-maximizing interval per component into a common interval and apply (1). Then increase the finite list and remove the approximation errors. This is a bound for all components' full surviving source law, allowing their separate heavy intervals to be aligned adversarially. A positive mass on a singular, full-dimensional fixed-component terminal law remains compatible with every estimate here.
