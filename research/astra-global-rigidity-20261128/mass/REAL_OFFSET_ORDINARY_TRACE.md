# The full ordinary trace with its real affine offset retained

Let $U$ be the positive odd Collatz map, $D=\log(4/3)$, $\kappa=D/2$, and $h(y)$ the canonical stationary integer trace. At a positive odd unit let $\mu_y$ be its full stationary real fibre in the *absolute* affine intercept $R\in[0,y]$. At multiples of three give the marked trace zero. Let independent positive $G_i$ have law $2^{-e}$ at $e\ge1$ and put
\[
  (R_*,Z_*)=\left(\sum_{i\ge1}3^{i-1}2^{-\sum_{a\le i}G_a}
                                      \right)_{\mathbb R,\mathbb Z_3} .
\]
The same series is evaluated in the two completions. Let $I_Y=[Y,\lambda Y]\cap(2\mathbb N+1)$ for fixed $\lambda>1$.

**Theorem.** For every fixed ternary cylinder $C$ and every continuous compactly supported $f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R$,
\[
 \boxed{\quad
  \sum_{y\in I_Y\cap C}\frac{h(y)}y\int f(R)\,d\mu_y(R)
  \longrightarrow \frac{\log\lambda}{2}
              \mathbb E[1_{Z_*\in C}f(R_*)].\quad}                      \tag{1}
\]
This uses the full untruncated trace at **all** positive odd sites in the interval, including periodic sites and all weighted visits on any cycle. It is vague convergence of locally finite measures in the absolute real coordinate. Uniform natural sampling gives the same joint stationary limit after replacing the harmonic root weights by $2h(y)/((\lambda-1)Y)$. Continuous physical height tests of $y/Y$ factor with the corresponding natural or harmonic interval law.

Equation (1) also holds for continuous $f$ with
\[
      \sum_{k\ge0}2^k\sup_{2^k\le r\le2^{k+1}}|f(r)|<\infty.          \tag{2}
\]
In particular, every fixed $f(r)=(1+r)^{-p}$ with $p>1$ and every fixed exponential damping are allowed. An escape component has its positive limiting relative trace at real offsets of order its growing root height. Tightness of the untruncated first moment in the absolute real coordinate would be an additional assertion.

The source-qualified inputs are the ordinary weighted first-hit densities and full-lap decomposition, the canonical inverse-path identification of the real integer fibre, the elementary uniform reciprocal tail on simple ordinary paths, and Weaver's smooth stopped-set comparison. ## A uniformly bounded marked occupation

For a source $x$ put $y_j=U^jx$,
\[
    b_j=\prod_{i<j}\frac{3y_i}{3y_i+1},\qquad R_j=y_j(1-b_j).
\]
The latter is the true forward real intercept: $y_j=3^j2^{-S_j}x+R_j$, where $S_j$ is the total of its valuations. It depends only on its finite chronological word. Fix $B\ge1$. On any actual trajectory the number of visits with $y_j\in I_Y$ and $R_j\le B$ is $O_\lambda(1+B)$ for $Y>2B$. Indeed each previous visit in $I_Y$ decreases the survival by a factor at most $1-1/(3\lambda Y+1)$. At a counted visit $b_j\ge1-B/Y$, so the number of all preceding interval visits is at most $(3\lambda Y+1)(-\log(1-B/Y))$. Also, after any departure from $z\le L=\lfloor Y/(6(B+1))\rfloor$ no such visit is possible, because then $R_j\ge y_j/(3z+1)>B$ for large $Y$. Both facts include repeated visits.

For all $B,Y\ge1$ the *weighted* count of these visits is at most $C_\lambda(1+B)$. For $B\ge Y/2$ this follows from
\[
     \sum_{j:y_j\in I_Y} b_j
      \le (3\lambda Y+1)\sum_j b_j(1-b_{j+1}/b_j)\le3\lambda Y+1.
                                                                         \tag{3}
\]
Increasing the constant covers bounded heights. This bound already proves that (2) suffices once (1) is proved for compact tests.

For a fixed $Y$ and each of its finitely many sites, weighted occupation of the *whole* true trajectory from a far smooth odd source $\mathbf U_X$ has the limiting identity
\[
 \lim_{X\to\infty}\mathbb E\sum_{j:y_j\in I_Y\cap C} b_j f(R_j)
      = \kappa^{-1}\sum_{y\in I_Y\cap C}\frac{h(y)}y\int f\,d\mu_y .
                                                                         \tag{4}
\]
Here the smooth law is proportional to $\rho(\log_2(n/X))$ as in Weaver (11); partial summation transfers the supplied genuine natural weighted densities to this law. We spell out the mark identification. For a fixed target $y$, the weighted first-visit sources ending with a specified first-visit reverse prefix from $y$ to a nonperiodic $u$ have limiting mass $b_k(u)\beta_u$ at prefix length $k$, by the exact product and the weighted density of the basin of $u$. A prefix with a full reverse lap is excluded from first visits. On the remaining prefixes at a periodic target, any prefix longer than the primitive lap ends at a nonperiodic $u$. Sources of fixed bounded length have zero relative density, since up to height $X$ the uniform simple-path survival lower bound $b_k\ge c_0>0$ and $3^k2^{-S_k}x=b_k y$ give $S_k=O_{k,y}(1+\log X)$ for $x\le X$, and only polynomially many words in $\log X$ occur. The resulting cylinder masses, after multiplying by $\kappa$, are the canonical measure of nonlap paths multiplied by $h(y)/y$. The canonical cylinder telescope and the full-lap split give total first-visit mass $\kappa\beta_y$ in this normalization. Cylinder convergence on a fixed countable partition is in total variation by equality of the limiting total masses. At nonunits the first-visit basin is the singleton.

For the mark, choose any cutoff $M$. Among positive odd vertices $u\le M$ that reach $y$, their first-visit depths have a finite maximum. For longer simple first-arrival words all the still earlier departed vertices lie above $M$ once the reverse prefix exceeds that maximum. Uniform simple-path reciprocal packing bounds the residual mark error by $C y M^{-1/50}$: extending a survival product by earlier vertices changes $R$ by at most $y\sum_{u>M}1/(3u)$. This applies to the nonlap canonical paths by finite prefixes as well. At fixed $y$, let $M\to\infty$ after the finite-prefix limit. Finally, on a periodic target successive actual laps multiply the weight by its true $\omega<1$ and send $r$ to $y-\omega(y-r)$. The resulting uniformly absolutely convergent geometric series is the full stationary fibre at that target. This proves (4).

## Resetting the absolute mark above the observed height

Set $N=\log Y$, $\zeta=1/1000$, $Q=Y^{1+\zeta}$ and $\gamma=1.001$. Real thresholds mean their integer floors in a first-landing test. On a far source that lands at or below $Q$, discard its history before the first such landing. With limiting probability $1-o(1)$, uniformly in the subsequent path, the change in $R_j$ at **every subsequent visit** to $I_Y$ is $o(1)$. The change in the weight is also $o(1)$ there.

To check this, use the finite ladder $Q_i=Q^{\gamma^i}$ from $Q_0=Q$ up to the first $Q_m\ge Y^{100}$; $m$ is bounded independently of $Y$. Before the first landing at $Q_m$ all departures exceed $Q_m$. If the landing occurs the preceding segment is simple. Its total reciprocal cost is $O(Q_m^{-1/50})$. The cost at root height at most $\lambda Y$ is consequently $o(1)$ in absolute intercept.

For each stage from first landing at $Q_{i+1}$ to first landing at $Q_i$, the probability in the far-source limit that the upper landing succeeds and the subsequent stage takes more than $K_i=\lfloor(\log Q_i)/2\rfloor$ steps is $o(1)$. Indeed this is a bounded test of the first upper landing label, defined as zero at failure. Compare it to a local smooth source of scale $X=Q_{i+1}^{\xi}$ with fixed $\gamma<\xi<\gamma^2$. Weaver's (20) along the ladder starting at $x=X^{1/\gamma}>Q_{i+1}$ has total error $O_A((\log Q_i)^{-A})$ by (24). The true limiting first-landing distribution exists for these finite labels, so the far ladder comparison determines its value. At the reference source a bad stage entails failure to reach $Q_i$ within $K_i$ steps from the source. Its first $K_i$ positive geometric gaps couple to iid gaps with an $O(Q_i^{-c})$ exception by binary residue counting, as detailed in [the ordinary trace calculation](POWER_CAPPED_ORDINARY_TRACE.md). If it misses $Q_i$ throughout, $V_{K_i}=S_{K_i}\log2-K_i\log3\le(\gamma^3-1)\log Q_i+O(1)$ on the coupling because the source is at most $4Q_i^{\gamma^3}$ and the affinity correction is at most $K_i/(3Q_i)$. The negative half exponential moment $\mathbb E e^{-V_1/2}<19/20$ bounds the probability by $O(Q_i^{-c'})$, since $(\gamma^3-1)/2<1/40$. On all good stages there are $O(\log Y)$ total departures from the first $Q_m$ landing to the first $Q$ landing, each greater than $Q$ until the latter landing. Thus $Y$ times their reciprocal sum tends to zero. Skipped rungs have zero duration. The finite union of the stage exceptions costs $o(1)$ in far-source probability. This proves the reset on every source that contributes. A source failing to land at $Q$ never visits $I_Y$.

If $b$ is the discarded survival before the reset, then $0\le R_j-R'_j\le \lambda Y(1-b)$ at a subsequent interval visit, with $R'_j$ measured from the landing. For a fixed support $[0,B]$, the preceding occupation bound at $B+1$ and uniform continuity of $f$ make the cost of the reset $o(1)$ in expectation, including its exceptional event. On the selected visits $b_j=1+O(B/Y)$, so the weights may be replaced by one at cost $o(1)$. The whole far observable has now become a bounded test of the first $Q$ landing, counting its actual future with the reset affine mark, zero at failure.

## Evaluation at an ordinary local source

Fix $\gamma<\xi_1<\xi_2<\gamma^2$. Transfer this bounded test at $Q$ to $\mathbf U_X$, $X=Q^\xi$, uniformly in $\xi\in[\xi_1,\xi_2]$. Use (20) and (24) exactly as above. There the history from the local source to its first $Q$ landing costs $O((\log Y)/Q)$ in cumulative reciprocal loss outside probability $O(Y^{-c})$: its duration is at most $K=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ by the same gap/negative-tilt calculation. Consequently we may restore the true mark from this local source at cost $o(1)$. Its first visit at or below $L=\lfloor Y/(6(B+1))\rfloor$ also occurs by $K$ outside probability $O_B(Y^{-c})$, since its log initial height exceeds $\log L$ by at most $((1+\zeta)\gamma^2-1)N+O_B(1)$, whose coefficient is less than $1/100$. On hitting $L$, all eligible visits have finished. By their uniform count, the contribution of later local times or exceptional trajectories is $o(1)$.

On the first $K$ steps jointly couple the genuine gaps to independent $G_1,\dots,G_K$ and the source height to $Xv$, with $v$ bounded above and away from zero and independent of the iid gaps. More explicitly $v$ has density proportional to $\rho(\log_2v)$, and one rounds $Xv$ to the closest member of an independently uniform odd class mod $2^{b+1}$, $b=\lfloor0.9\log_2Y\rfloor$. The discrete smooth source and this rounded law differ in variation by $O(2^b/X)$. A random binary extension of the class produces the independent geometric gaps. Their total overflow past $b$ has probability $O(Y^{-1/50})$. On the agreement event the relative source error is $O(2^b/X)$. These claims follow directly from the residue cells and the compact Lipschitz weight; see [the physical coupling](POWER_CAPPED_ORDINARY_TRACE.md). Counting at most $K+1$ times absorbs all exceptional probabilities.

In the coupled word set $V_j=S_j\log2-j\log3$ and let $R_j^*$ be its actual forward affine offset, computed from the last gaps backwards. For $j\ge k$ the ternary class through level $k$ of the genuine $y_j$ is forced by the last $k$ gaps. Write $Z_{j,k}^*$ for this class. The function $f(R_j^*)$ restricts the offset to $[0,B]$. Hence the positive physical output $e^{-V_j}\mathbf U_X+R_j^*$ belongs to $I_Y$ exactly up to an $o(1)$ widening or narrowing of the logarithmic window for $e^{-V_j}Xv$. For nonnegative $f$ this gives upper and lower bounds by the two enlarged or diminished windows; a signed $f$ follows by positive and negative parts. The finitely many $j<k$ have negligible chance to bridge the order-$N$ initial logarithmic separation, by a positive exponential moment.

Average $\log X$ uniformly from $\xi_1\log Q$ to $\xi_2\log Q$ and set $a=\xi_1\log Q-\log Y$, $c=\xi_2\log Q-\log Y$. For each fixed word and $v$, the acceptable log-scale interval for this visit has length $\log\lambda+o(1)$ whenever $V_j\in[a+C_0,c-C_0]$, and is empty outside $[a-C_0,c+C_0]$, where $C_0$ absorbs the bounded $\log v$ and window width. The lengths are divided by $c-a\asymp N$. The indices $j$ with mean $Dj$ in $[a+\epsilon N,c-\epsilon N]$ contribute their full length except an $O_\epsilon(e^{-c_\epsilon N})$ probability per index. Those outside $[a-\epsilon N,c+\epsilon N]$, $j\le K$, contribute at most the same error per index. Both claims use either exponential tilt for a deviation exceeding $\epsilon N-O(1)$, uniformly up to $K$. The remaining edge strips have $O(\epsilon N+1)$ indices. The bulk lies strictly between $0$ and $K$ for all small fixed $\epsilon>0$, since $0<a/N<c/N<1/100<D/2$.

For any bulk $j$, the pair $(R_j^*,Z_{j,k}^*)$ has precisely the law of the first $j$ terms of the iid stationary affine series and its first $k$ ternary digits. This uses reversal of the order of $j$ iid letters. As $j\to\infty$ its bounded $1_C f$ mean tends to $\mathbb E[1_{Z_*\in C}f(R_*)]$. The last assertion is uniform across these bulk indices as their minimum tends to infinity. No independence between that mark and $V_j$ is needed, since the discarded probability is absolute and $f$ bounded. The bulk has $(c-a)/D+O(\epsilon N+1)$ indices. Letting $Y\to\infty$ and then $\epsilon\downarrow0$ gives the limiting expected occupation $(\log\lambda)/D$ times the stationary marked mean. In (4) multiply by $\kappa$ to prove (1). Testing subintervals and using the uniform local marked bound yields the stated natural and spatial forms.

Every actual escaping component still contributes normalized unmarked stationary mass $h(r_i)/r_i\to H_C>0$ along its own high ray. The corrected relative real fibre converges there to the bilateral survival law, which is supported in $(0,1)$ up to a null endpoint by its atomlessness. Thus its absolute affine intercept goes to infinity in probability. Compact real tests in (1) retain the whole ordinary graph while their tested mass on such sites vanishes per site. Equations (1)–(3) give no uniform integrable bound for the constant test $f=1$.
