# Exact ordinary sampling at the strict future gate

Let $T$ be the shortcut Collatz map, $U$ its odd map, $Q_t$ the odd departure count, $b_t=2Q_t-t$, $g=-\min_{t\ge0}b_t$, and $\mathcal P=\{n:b_t(n)>0\text{ for every }t>0\}$. Write $D=\log(4/3)$. For every arbitrary union $A$ of full components, and for the predecessor basin of any fixed odd unit, write $d$ for its ordinary natural density. Then
\[
 \lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt{\log X}}X
       \#\{X\le n<2X:n\in A\cap\mathcal P\}
                  =\frac{\sqrt D}{2\sqrt\pi}\,d.                       \tag{1}
\]
Thus uniform sampling from the actual decidable gate within each large octave recovers the ordinary distribution on the countably many graph components, in total variation. Equation (1) also applies to every fixed first-hit predecessor basin. The proof uses the source-qualified ordinary half-normal law in [True gate source law](TRUE_GATE_SOURCE_LAW.md). The extra analytic step retains one path minimum in the ternary mixing argument. No test is performed on an independently resampled future of the observed integer.

## Pair-boundary minimum in the full ternary columns

For $N$ independent outward geometric letters $e_i$ with mass $2^{-e}$ at $e\ge1$, use $S_i=\sum_{h\le i}e_h$, $F_N=\sum_{i\le N}3^{i-1}2^{-S_i}$, $V_i=S_i-2i$, and for $N$ even retain the mark
\[
 H_N=\min_{0\le 2i\le N}V_{2i}.
\]
The minimum over all $i$ differs from this by at most one, because $V_{i+1}-V_i\ge-1$. For every $B$ there are $C_B,\delta_B>0$ such that for even $N,q,m$ with $m\le q$, $0\le q-N\le\delta_B N$ and $m\le N$,
\[
 \sum_{s,h}\left\|\mu_{s,h}^{[q]}-L_m^q\pi_m\mu_{s,h}^{[q]}\right\|_1
                 \le C_B m^{-B},                                      \tag{2}
\]
where $\mu_{s,h}^{[q]}(z)=\Pr(F_N=z\bmod3^q,S_N=s,H_N=h)$ and $L_m^q\pi_m$ is normalized averaging on the residue classes modulo $3^m$. The norm is counting-measure $\ell^1$ on mass vectors.

Here are details of the extension of Weaver–Wren (63)–(69). Their primitive Fourier bound (63) also holds with a multiplier of modulus at most one depending arbitrarily on the full list of adjacent pair totals and its last singleton: this multiplier is fixed under the very conditioning used to take the absolute conditional characteristic function bounded by their (60). If a suffix has even length $n$ greater than conductor depth $a$, condition on the letters after its first $a'=2\lfloor a/2\rfloor$ letters. All resulting tests of pair totals remain tests of the first $a'/2$ pairs. The residual affine phase from the fixed tail depends on those first letters only through $S_{a'}$. Apply the same primitive bound with the bounded deficit $a-a'\le1$. The argument when $n\le a$ uses the allowed small fractional conductor deficit. [Weaver–Wren, Sect. 8, (58)–(69)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/48)

First consider the loss of just two trits $q\to q-2$. For large even $q$ split at the even $r=2\lfloor17q/40\rfloor$. In estimating one slice at total $s\le4q$ it suffices to treat the predicate $H_N\ge h$, then difference consecutive $h$. At fixed prefix total $d$ this event separates into the prefix predicate $H_r\ge h$ and the suffix predicate $H'_{N-r}\ge h-(d-2r)$. The former restricts the nonnegative prefix column $f_d$ pointwise to at most the original $f_d$. The latter, jointly with any exact suffix total, is a bounded pair-total test. For a frequency of valuation 0 or 1, the suffix conductor $a=q-r-v_3(\xi)$ is comparable to $q$. Its positive deficit is at most $q-N$, so its primitive transform at each exact suffix total is $O_K(q^{-K})$ for arbitrarily prescribed $K$ after decreasing $\delta_B$. Write the full transform as $\sum_d \widehat f^{\rm res}_d(\xi)\widehat g^{\rm res}_{s-d,d}(2^{-d}\xi)$. Use weighted Cauchy–Schwarz in $d$, then sum in these frequencies. The original prefix estimate
\[
 (\sum_{d\ge d_0}u^{-d})\,3^q\sum_{d\ge d_0,z}u^d f_d(z)^2\ll q^2,
 \qquad u=1+(4q)^{-1},\quad d_0=\lceil q\log_2 3\rceil,
\]
holds at every $r\le q$. Plancherel on the restricted prefix therefore bounds the retained high-frequency squared sum by $O_K(q^{2-2K})$. Spatial Cauchy–Schwarz on the mass vector cancels the Fourier group size. Discarding $d<d_0$ costs $O(e^{-cq})$ since $2r/q\to1.7>\log_2 3$. There are $O(q^2)$ eligible pairs $(s,h)$ since $-N\le H_N\le0$. The omitted $s>4q$ costs $O(e^{-cq})$ in summed norm, and differencing thresholds costs a factor two. This proves the loss of two trits with any prescribed negative power. [Weaver 9, Proposition 13, (38)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/9)

Project successively in even depths from $q$ to $m$. At depths $v\ge N$ use the same $N$, with deficit $v-N\le\delta_BN$. At $v<N$, the projected residue depends only on the first even $v$ letters. The remaining independent tail sends $(S_v,H_v)$ to $(S_N,H_N)$ by adding its total and taking the minimum of $H_v$ and $V_v+H'_{N-v}$. This Markov kernel is independent of the spatial coordinate, contracts summed $\ell^1$, and commutes with spatial averaging. Hence the bound at even $N'=v,q'=v$ controls that stage. Summing $O_K(v^{3-K})$ at all intervening even depths proves (2). All constants for a fixed power are uniform in the retained mark values.

## The actual output mark on short physical intervals

If $u\to\infty$ and $0<a<c$ are fixed, (2) of the preceding true-gate note gives, on $[au,cu]$, the joint law of $1_A$ and $g(y)/\sqrt{2\log u/D}$ on the event $A$: its limiting mass is $d$ and its conditional limit is $|Z|$ for a standard Gaussian $Z$. This follows first on fixed positive-mass basins and on the full space, then by component exhaustion. Fixed proportional intervals follow also from the octave statement by dyadic summation to a cumulative limit and subtraction. The same joint limit holds in each fixed residue class, with the class's ordinary proportion. Indeed for fixed shift $d_0$ the two starts meet by $h=\lfloor(\log u)^{1/4}\rfloor$ except on proportion $o(1)$ by the counted actual merger, using complete binary periods of length $2^h$. Before a meeting each grade moves by at most $h$. The two future deficits therefore differ by at most $2h$ since their subsequent paths coincide. Their component labels agree, and a discrepancy of fixed-target labels requires an early target visit, costing $O(2^h/u)$. This proves fixed-shift invariance in probability of the normalized joint tests, hence equal limits on the finitely many residue classes. The limiting continuous half-normal distribution gives uniform convergence over all threshold values. [Counted actual coalescence and fixed-target exception](ORDINARY_BASIN_WINDOWS.md)

Write $\rho_m(y)=3^m\Pr(F_m=y\bmod3^m)$. For even $m\to\infty$ with $3^m=o(u)$ and $A$ as above, uniformly for real $v$ and fixed $0<a<c$,
\[
 \frac1u\sum_{au\le y<cu,\ y\text{ odd}}\rho_m(y)1_A(y)
          1_{g(y)/\sqrt{2\log u/D}<v}
       =\tfrac12(c-a)d\Pr(|Z|<v)+o(1).                               \tag{3}
\]
For fixed $m_0$, fixed-residue equidistribution proves this for $\rho_{m_0}$, whose mean is one on the odd residue representatives. The Haar $L^1$ distance between $\rho_m$ and $\rho_{m_0}$ is uniformly small as $m_0\to\infty$ by the marginal case of the mixing estimate. Covering the physical interval by complete periods bounds the average absolute error by a constant times this distance. First pass to the large-interval limit, then increase $m_0$. The same argument without either test controls both endpoint strips even when their location varies, by first bounding them inside strips of fixed small relative width and then decreasing that width.

## Physical transfer with the initial strict ballot constraint

Write $L=\log X$. Take even $b\sim L/100$ and even $k\sim\eta b$ with $0<\eta<\min(1/2,\delta_B)$ fixed for (2), say $B=4$. Put $j=b+k$, and $m\sim b^{1/4}$ even. Sample independent initial $k$ **forward** gaps conditioned to have positive surplus after each gap. Write $l$ for their total, $z=2k-l$, and $p_l$ for the unconditioned joint probability of this event and total $l$. The ballot formula gives exactly
\[
 p_l=\pi_k(l)(2k-l)_+/k,\qquad \pi_k(l)=2^{-l}{l-1\choose k-1}.
\]
Consequently $p=\sum_l p_l\sim1/\sqrt{\pi k}$ and under weights $p_l/p$, $z/\sqrt{2k}$ tends to the Rayleigh law with density $r e^{-r^2/2}$ on $r>0$. Both facts follow by the ordinary finite-variance central limit theorem and uniform integrability of the normalized second moments of geometric sums.

For the next $b$ gaps use outward order, starting at the actual output integer $y$, with total $s$, $w=s-2b$ and pair minimum $H_b$. The actual smallest surplus during this middle block is between $z-w+H_b-1$ and $z-w+H_b$. Indeed the full odd-grade path in forward order has values $z-w+V_i$ read from $i=b$ to $i=0$, and the minimum inside each complete shortcut gap is reached at one of its endpoints. Its terminal surplus is $z-w$. The infinite continuation criterion is exactly $g(y)<z-w$. The full affine residue at depth $j$ is $F_b+3^b2^{-s}F'_k$, with $F'_k$ from the reversed initial positive-ballot word. Apply (2) with $N=b,q=j$ to each translate at fixed $s$ and fixed initial word, then average over the latter words. The translate is zero modulo $3^m$. Summed absolute mixing cost over $s,H_b,l$ is $O(p m^{-B})$ on a complete period. The bound remains valid against the two middle-threshold tests (shifted by one) and all admissible physical tests of $y$.

Set $Y=X3^j2^{-s-l}$. The exact physical lift for each word is $y=3^j2^{-s-l}n+F_j^{\mathrm{full}}$ with $0\le F_j^{\mathrm{full}}\le(3/2)^j-1$. Each positive odd representative in the eligible physical band is a true start after the inverse lift when it meets this lower endpoint condition. All compatible predecessors in the bands used here are positive; at every intermediate height the lifted large source stays above $X2^{-3j}$ whenever $s+l\le3j$. Thus $A$ transports across these gates including fixed-target first hits. At fixed $s,l$ the ordinary count divided by $X$ equals the sum of the normalized density column over the actual endpoint crop divided by $Y$. For $s+l\le3j$, $X^{.9}\le Y\le X^{1.1}$ and $3^j=o(Y)$. Covering the outer crop by periods bounds the mixing error in the normalized ordinary count by $O(p m^{-B})$. More precisely, bracket the actual crop by ordinary odd $y$ in $[(1+\epsilon)Y,(2-\epsilon)Y]$ and $[(1-\epsilon)Y,(2+\epsilon)Y]$ at any fixed $\epsilon>0$, then take $\epsilon\downarrow0$ after the main limit. A true strictly positive path always has $s+l<2j$, so no larger clocks can contribute.

One can restrict to $|w|\le M\sqrt b$ and $0<z\le M\sqrt k$ and send $M\to\infty$ at the end with error $o_M(1)pX$. For the discarded true paths, read the first $s+l$ shortcut bits completing the $j$ gaps, and the next odd endpoint bit, modulo $2^{3j+1}$ using the preceding clock cap. Ignoring the future test then costs at most the fair initial-ballot probability times the two independent-block tail bounds (using the ballot-weighted initial law), plus $O(2^{3j+1}/X)$. In either central crop all examined physical clocks satisfy the stated cap for large $X$.

At this point the normalized lift to depth $m$ still correlates the outward initial letters with the true $s$ and $H_b$. The following bridge calculation removes the correlation at the required scale. Given those first $m$ letters, discard total $S_m>4m$ at Haar mass $O(e^{-cm})$. At a retained word the endpoint $S_m-2m$ and minimum over its whole prefix have magnitude at most $4m=o(\sqrt b)$. The remaining $b-m$ letters have exact total $s-S_m$. On $|w|\le M\sqrt b$, uniformly in every retained first word, the geometric local central limit theorem gives
\[
 \pi_{b-m}(s-S_m)=\pi_b(s)+o_M(b^{-1/2}).
\]
Given that exact remaining total, the outward centered path scaled by $\sqrt{2b}$, with the initial $m$ letters included, converges to a standard Brownian bridge from zero to $w/\sqrt{2b}$, uniformly in this compact family of terminal values. Concretely, along any sequence where $w/\sqrt{2b}\to v$, the exact-total bridge finite dimensional laws follow by multiplying geometric sum atoms and applying their uniform local Gaussian limit. On the first half the conditional/unconditional path density is uniformly bounded, since the remaining half has atoms $O(b^{-1/2})$ and the prescribed full atom is $\gg_M b^{-1/2}$. The reversed second half has the same bound; unconditioned functional tightness gives tightness on both halves. This proves the claimed endpoint bridge convergence. Adding the uniform $o(\sqrt b)$ outward prefix and using the even-date minimum changes no limit. The minimum of the limiting bridge has a continuous distribution, including at the lesser endpoint, as follows from Brownian reflection. Compactness gives uniform convergence of its distribution functions in both threshold and endpoint. Thus imposing the middle lower or upper positivity test at this $s,l$ multiplies $\pi_b(s)$ by its deterministic Brownian bridge probability
\[
 K_b(s,z)=\Pr\{z-w+\sqrt{2b}\min_{0\le t\le1} B^{(w/\sqrt{2b})}_t>0\},
\]
up to $o_M(b^{-1/2})$ in absolute joint mass, uniformly in every retained prefix. Here the bridge starts at zero and ends at its displayed superscript. The omitted prefix costs at most $O(b^{-1/2})$ times its Haar density column because the remaining total atom is always $O(b^{-1/2})$. It follows after summing the order $\sqrt b$ central clocks that one may replace the projected middle column by $\pi_b(s)K_b(s,z)\rho_m(y)$ at cost $o_M(1)p$ in the physical count. Indeed the uniform pointwise error is bounded by $o_M(b^{-1/2})\rho_m(y)$ plus $O(b^{-1/2})\rho_m^{\mathrm{bad}}(y)$; both ordinary averages are controlled by complete periods. This step preserves the actual $s,l$ in the physical band and in the remaining threshold.

Use (3) at $u=Y$, with the physical future test $g(y)<z-w$. On the central clocks $\log Y=L-Dj+O_M(\sqrt L)$. Its normalized cost can therefore be evaluated using an independent half-normal of variance parameter $2(L/D-j)$, uniformly in both clocks by continuity. The odd output factor is $1/2$. Sending $\epsilon\downarrow0$ and then $M\to\infty$, the final central expression for the gate fraction, up to $o(p)$, is the fair ballot probability $p$ times $d/2$ times the following limiting probability. Start the middle block with the Rayleigh height in units $\sqrt{2k}$. Run independent variance-two Brownian motion for $b$ units, keeping the path positive. Require its terminal height to exceed an independent $\sqrt{2(L/D-j)}|Z|$. Here probabilities are understood after dividing time by $L$ and space by $\sqrt L$, so the ratios $k/L,b/L$ have fixed positive limits. To see the Brownian middle law in forward order, the normal mixture over $w$ of the outward bridges is Brownian motion; reversing its increments and negating them preserves the Brownian law.

For $N=\lfloor L/D\rfloor$, a fresh fair geometric walk has strict survival through $N$ odd gaps with probability $p_N\sim1/\sqrt{\pi N}$. Conditional on strict survival through $k$, its height is asymptotically Rayleigh as above. The independent rest of this walk converges after scaling to Brownian motion on its two remaining intervals of lengths $b,N-j$. The negative minimum in the last interval has half-normal law by Brownian reflection. All threshold boundaries have probability zero. Therefore the limiting probability just computed is precisely $\lim p_N/p=\lim\sqrt{k/N}$. Substitution gives (1). Pointwise convergence for the countably many positive-mass component labels upgrades to total variation by truncating to finitely many labels of total mass arbitrarily near one. A fixed escaping orbit would still have a positive excess of forward gate dates. Equation (1) concerns sampling original integers and does not supply an upper on those time dates.
