# Narrow prime progressions in the predecessors of a fixed ordinary target

**SOURCE-QUALIFIED written theorem.** Take the shortcut map $T(2u)=u$, $T(2u+1)=3u+2$. Fix an integer $v\ge1$ coprime to three, $k\ge3$, and $\epsilon>0$. Every sufficiently large $[X,2X)$ contains at least $c_{v,k}X/\log X$ pairwise disjoint $k$-term arithmetic progressions of primes with the following properties. Disjointness concerns the prime entries. Subsequent trajectories may intersect.

* Their positive common differences are at most $C_{v,k,\epsilon}(\log X)^{k2^{k-3}+\epsilon}$.
* In each progression, **every integer between its extreme primes** has the same shortcut value and cumulative odd count at a common clock $t\le C_{v,k,\epsilon}(1+\log\log X)^8$. All these integers have one common first-visit clock to $v$, strictly after $t$, and one common total odd count through that first visit.
* For any fixed $0<\gamma<\log(2/\sqrt3)$ the common endpoint at $t$ can simultaneously be required to be below $p\exp(-\gamma t)$, where $p$ is the first prime in the progression. In particular every prime in the progression has a strictly smaller ordinary orbit point.

Here $X$ tends to infinity with $v$ fixed. The theorem supplies actual integer visitors to the target. Whether it has a visitor below the target $v$ remains a separate question.

## Inputs and proof

1. The prime-ancestor bound gives at least $a_v X/\log X$ primes in the octave that visit $v$, for some $a_v>0$ at all large $X$. Its precise external inputs can be taken as the zero-frequency specialization of Paper 221 and the fixed-target octave lower bound proved there from Mazur. Alternatively, Paper 233, equation (1), $D=1$, supplies the transfer under ordinary RH. For the former option the Dirichlet-GRH statement used in Paper 221 has independent proof-checking records. We have not rebuilt its large Lean archives. [Paper 221, “Integer orbit labels” and “On the primes”](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/221) · [Paper 233, §3](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/233) · [Sutherland’s checks](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) · [short proof checks](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/shortgrh/).
2. Put $B=k2^{k-3}+\epsilon$, $H=\lceil(\log X)^{B+1}\rceil$ and $t=\lceil K(1+\log\log X)^8\rceil$. Weaver's block estimate leaves at most $C(1+\log H)^4/\sqrt{t+1}$ of the $2^t$ base residues for which the $H$ consecutive starts fail counted fusion by $t$. On a binary residue with prescribed path and odd count $s$, adding $2^t u$ to a start adds $3^su$ to its endpoint. Hence the counted-fusion event is exact on residues, including across carries in $p+j$. For $2^t=X^{o(1)}$, the Brun–Titchmarsh inequality bounds the number of prime anchors in each odd base residue by $C'X/(2^t\log X)$. Even classes contribute none. Choosing $K$ large enough relative to the fixed data makes bad block anchors remove at most $a_v X/(4\log X)$ primes. [Weaver, Paper 124 v1, Theorem 1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/124/v1).
3. Choose $1/2<\theta<\log_3 2$ with $\log2-\theta\log3>\gamma$. The binary-residue parity bijection puts $O_\theta(\exp(-c_\theta t))$ of all residues in the event that the anchor has more than $\theta t$ odd departures. The same Brun–Titchmarsh bound removes $o(X/\log X)$ primes. Each actual state through time $t$ from $p\ge X$ is at least $X/2^t$. Multiplying the exact odd corrections $1+1/(3T^ip)$ gives

   $$T^t(p)/p\le (3^{\theta}/2)^t\exp\bigl(t2^t/(3X)\bigr)<e^{-\gamma t}.$$

   All $H$ inputs share this endpoint. They stay above $v$ until $t$ since $X/2^t\to\infty$. As the anchor visits $v$, their common continuation gives identical first-hit clocks and odd counts.
4. At least $a_v X/(2\log X)$ eligible primes remain. For any subset still containing at least $a_v X/(4\log X)$ of them, Shao's Theorem 1.1 applies at $2X$ with a fixed positive density parameter. It gives a nonconstant $k$-term progression of difference at most $C(\log X)^B$. Its span is $<H$ for sufficiently large $X$, hence the block certificate of its first prime applies to every intervening integer. Delete its $k$ prime entries and repeat. This yields at least $a_v X/(4k\log X)-1$ disjoint progressions. [Shao, *Narrower arithmetic progressions in the primes*, arXiv:2611.23488v1, 17 November 2026, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.23488v1).

Paper 209, Theorems A–B, studies bounded inverse depth at varying targets. Paper 221’s ternary sums have three separately marked summands. Their stated conclusions differ from the common first-hit and short-progression assertion above. This comparison covers these sources only. ## Pointwise closure test

A hypothetical least nonconvergent source has a positive ternary-unit target on its own forward orbit. Remove initial factors of two and, if the resulting odd value is divisible by three, take one odd shortcut. Every visitor to that target would also miss 1. The theorem produces a positive proportion, relative to primes, packed into disjoint narrow prime progressions of physical coalescence and early strict descent, entirely inside this one failed component. Their smaller common endpoints obey $T^t(p)\ge X/2^t\to\infty$. Closing descent would need a bound on the minimum of that component or an independently certified convergent member among these particular progressions. The construction also applies to the disjoint visit sets of 5 and 85, both contained in the known component of 1.
