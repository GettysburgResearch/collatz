# Smaller Mersenne sources at protected prime exponents

**SOURCE-QUALIFIED written result.** Write $T(2x)=x$, $T(2x+1)=3x+2$ and let $Q_t(x)$ count odd inputs before shortcut date $t$. Set $N_r=2^r-1$ and $C_r=3^r-1$. The first $r$ steps from $N_r$ are odd and end at $C_r$. Fix a large $R$, let

$$K=\lfloor0.49\log_3R\rfloor,\qquad h=\lfloor0.48\log_2 R\rfloor,$$

and choose integers $J\ge5$, $1\le D<R/2$ with $J=o(\log R)$. Consider the primes $p\in[R,2R)$ satisfying

$$p\equiv1\pmod{3^{K-1}},\qquad p\equiv-1\pmod{2^{J-2}}. \tag{1}$$

When referring to an implied bound for a fraction, a bound above one is vacuous. The statement has three parts.

1. For **every** selected prime, every smaller positive integer $b<N_p$ that visits an upper site $T^iN_p$, $0\le i\le p+J$, at any incoming clock $\ell$, must satisfy $Q_\ell(b)>Q_i(N_p)+K$.
2. With the exception of a relative fraction at most $C D(J+1)/\sqrt h$, the single ordinary integer $N_p$ has *all* of the smaller integers $N_{p-1},\ldots,N_{p-D}$ as physical merger witnesses. At the respective shortcut clocks $p+J+h$ and $p-d+J+h$, each pair has the same endpoint and the same total odd count. Every nonempty original prefix through that endpoint is strictly above $N_p$. The common odd count means the corresponding full-map clocks differ by exactly $d$ as well.
3. For $s=\lceil(J-5)/2\rceil>0$, the fraction even having a counted meeting with $N_{p-1}$ by **any** common post-crest clock $j\le J+h$ is at most $C\exp(-s^2/(2h))$. This upper bound concerns equal post-crest clocks and equal total odd counts. A different clock choice or budget has its own quantifiers.

The constants in the relative counting use Dirichlet GRH, the classical prime number theorem in an arithmetic progression on GRH, Brun–Titchmarsh, and the cited conditional-initial-state Collatz estimate. The same integer family comes with an unconditional exact finite-word arithmetic description. In particular $J=\lfloor(\log R)^{1/4}\rfloor$, $D=\lfloor(\log R)^{1/8}\rfloor$ give a relative exceptional fraction $O((\log R)^{-1/8})$. The deepest explicitly named witness satisfies $(N_{p-D}+1)/(N_p+1)=2^{-D}\to0$. Both original and witnesses are positive **ordinary** integers fixed once $p$ is selected. No convergence assumption for them is used.

## Actual words and protection through the specified clock

Put $P=3^{K-1}$. Since $p$ is odd and $P\mid p-1$, Euler's theorem modulo $3^K$ gives $2^p\equiv2\bmod3^K$. Thus $N_p\equiv1\bmod3^K$. The all-odd ramp has the PR146/211 all-clock protection through $i=p$. For $H\ge3$, the equalities $v_2(3^{2m}-1)=3+v_2(m)$ show that the order of three modulo $2^H$ is $2^{H-2}$. From (1), $3^p\equiv3^{-1}\bmod2^J$. Hence, on $J$ post-crest steps, $C_p$ follows the virtual signed rational $3^{-1}-1=-2/3$: it takes an even step to $-1/3$, an odd step to zero, and then only even steps. This use of a rational in $\mathbb Z_2$ determines the word of the original **integer**, not its size or its eventual fate.

For each $2\le i\le J$ that post-crest word is $EOE^{i-2}$. It is maximal in the equal-offset sense of PR211. Here is a direct verification. In the shifted variable $Y=x+1$, a word of length $i$ and weight $a$ has numerator $3^aY+B$. Appending $E$ adds $2^i$ to $B$, and appending $O$ triples $B$. The displayed word has $B=2^i-1$. Any equal-offset word must start with $E$, since a first $O$ leaves $B$ even at every later date. After the first $E$, the defect $2^j-1-B$ is zero. Appending $E$ preserves it. Appending $O$ replaces it by three times itself plus $2-2^j$. At $j=1$ the last term is zero, and at every $j\ge2$ it is strictly negative. Thus only the all-even word and the displayed word can share the offset. The one-letter prefix $E$ is maximal too.

The PR211 protected-barrier theorem now applies at each prefix since $p\ge 21J+9K+3$ for large $R$. Its hypotheses allow **every** positive smaller visitor at **every** incoming clock. The resulting cost is strictly greater than the upper arm's full odd count plus $K$. This is an application of the existing theorem and its $EO^rE^s$ family, not a new post-crest protection mechanism. ## Relative prime sampling with fresh binary suffixes

For each $d=1,\ldots,D$, compare the neighbors with lower exponent $r=p-d$ and upper exponent $r+1$. Their crests are $x=C_r$ and $3x+2=C_{r+1}$, and the upper source has one extra odd departure at that date. Counted absorption compares the *full* odd counts, including this lead.

For $L=J+h$ write $M=2^{L-2}$. Among exponent residues modulo $M$ satisfying the second congruence of (1), the map $p\mapsto C_{p-d}\bmod2^L$ is a bijection onto precisely one prescribed residue class modulo $2^J$, with its $h$ higher bits free. Indeed the order of 3 is $2^{L-2}$ and $3^{2^{J-2}}$ generates the subgroup of all units $1\bmod2^J$ (by order and size). Multiplying by the odd prefactor $3^{p_0-d}$ and subtracting one preserve the claim. This is uniform in $d$. Signed exponents in this modular identity use the unit inverse of 3.

Under this *conditional* low-$J$ law, after reading the $J$ initial bits the canonical pair has count gap at most $J+1$ and normalized offset at most $C(3/2)^J$. These follow from the initial gap one and the one-step deterministic offset estimate in Weaver's Lemma 2. Its remaining-time estimate, uniformly over every complete prefix, leaves failure at most $C'(J+1)/\sqrt{h}$. The lower-slope base after the prefix is an odd-affine function of the unread bits, so it supplies exactly the conditional law needed there. This costs no factor $2^J$. Union over the $D$ adjacent edges. If all agree by the same post-crest date $L$, transitivity gives the endpoint and full odd-count equalities for every $p-d$ against *that fixed* $p$. The cost is linear in $D$, because every comparison used initial gap one. For clarity, the exponent measure in the last paragraph was uniform on finite residue classes. Here is the transfer to the stated primes. The denominator counts one reduced class modulo $q=3^{K-1}2^{J-2}=R^{0.49+o(1)}$. Standard GRH arithmetic-progression estimates give a count asymptotic to $(\operatorname{Li}(2R)-\operatorname{Li}(R))/\varphi(q)$; the coarser error $O(\sqrt R\log^2(qR))$ already suffices, with relative error $O(q\log^3 R/\sqrt R)=R^{-0.01+o(1)}$. Every remaining class modulo $M$ combines by CRT with the ternary condition to one reduced class modulo $q'=3^{K-1}M=R^{0.97+o(1)}$. For all large $R$, $q'\le R^{0.98}$, and Brun–Titchmarsh on the octave bounds the number of primes in each one by $2R/(\varphi(q')\log(R/q'))\le100R/(\varphi(q')\log R)$. Since $\varphi(q')/\varphi(q)=2^h$, a fraction $\beta$ of the $2^h$ lifted classes contributes at most $C\beta$ of the primes in (1). This gives assertion 2 and likewise transfers an exponentially small *upper* bound without a separately accumulated error for every lifted class. For the height conclusion every initial ramp state after time zero exceeds $N_p$. Every following state through $L$ is at least $(3^p-1)/2^L>N_p$ for large $R$. ## Why the quadratic clock is genuine

For $d=1$ the lower virtual crest is $3^{-2}-1=-8/9$. Its actual first-$J$ word agrees with the virtual path
$$-8/9\to-4/9\to-2/9\to-1/9\to1/3\to1\to2\to1\to\cdots.$$
The upper virtual crest visits zero and stays there. As the conditional prefixes preserve their words, at date $J$ the lower arm has accumulated $2+\lceil(J-5)/2\rceil$ post-crest odds, and the upper one has accumulated one. Including the upper's original one-odd lead makes the signed lower lead exactly $s=\lceil(J-5)/2\rceil$. When $s>0$, every counted absorption subsequently requires eliminating this gap. Conditional on the complete prefix, its signed evolution before hitting zero is a martingale with increments in $\{-1,0,1\}$: on equal parities the increment is zero, and on opposite parities the fresh anchor bit gives the two signs with equal probability. The maximal Azuma inequality gives $\Pr(\text{hit zero by }h)\le\exp(-s^2/(2h))$. An earlier absorption would have persisted, contradicting $s>0$. The same CRT prime bound proves assertion 3. The earlier quadratic obstruction from two unequal virtual cycle rates in Paper 21 §9 is expressly credited. The new application here combines it with a growing, independent ternary barrier and a supplied prime exponent.

## Testing a covering argument

The virtual phase excludes *all* shorter crests through each chosen precision $J$ even for $s=0$. Fix $1\le d<p$. If $C_p$ and $C_{p-d}$ were counted-matched at $j\le J$, the equal total odds would force $Q_j(C_{p-d})=Q_j(C_p)+d$. Their words through $j$ agree with those from $z_0=3^{-1}-1$ and $z_d=3^{-1-d}-1$. The real source corrections are $3^p-3^{-1}$ and $3^{-d}(3^p-3^{-1})$ respectively, whose endpoint images coincide by the count equality. Thus the two virtual endpoints would coincide. For $j=0,1$ there is no count catch-up: both start even. For $j\ge2$ the upper endpoint is zero. The lower $z_d$ also starts even, and after its first halving lies in $(-1/2,0)$. While negative, subsequent even steps only move it towards zero. After one odd step it exceeds $-1/4$, and its next odd step is strictly positive. It cannot reach zero with just one odd step, since its reduced denominator $3^{d+1}$ requires at least $d+1\ge2$ odd steps to cancel. Positive states stay positive. This rules out the purported match.

For any finite proposed post-crest clock limit one can prescribe a larger $J$ and use (1), still with arbitrarily large primes and ternary protection. A universal bound on those clocks, even allowing an arbitrary shorter Mersenne at each source, therefore fails. Paper 230 §7 already proves finiteness of each matched crest class and arbitrarily large exceptional exponents without restricting to primes. The proof here supplies an explicit bad binary tail and simultaneous prime and ternary restrictions, plus a quantitative later recovery for most primes in the same restriction. The existence of a common future with an arbitrary smaller integer at independent unrestricted clocks remains open for all supplied sources.
