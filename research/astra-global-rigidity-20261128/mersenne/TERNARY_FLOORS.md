# An all-clock dichotomy around every positive integer ternary anchor

Let $T$ be the shortcut map. Call $n$ directly visited from below if some ordinary $0<x<n$ has $T^h x=n$ at any clock. Fix **any** supplied positive integer $a$. On the actual inverse graph from $a$ allow $E(v)=2v$ and integral $O(v)=(2v-1)/3$. A word of native length $h$ and $q$ odds has inverse slope $2^h/3^q$. For $K\ge1$, let $d_K(a)$ be the limiting fraction of positive $n\equiv a\pmod{3^K}$ visited from below, optionally restricted to any one fixed binary cylinder. The fraction exists, has the same value on every such binary cylinder, and satisfies this dichotomy.

1. If **a finite word at $a$ has slope less than one**, then every sufficiently large ordinary $n\equiv a\pmod{3^q}$ is visited from below, explicitly by that same word. Thus $d_K(a)=1$ for every $K\ge q$.
2. If **every finite word at $a$ has slope at least one**, then $\sum_{K\ge1}d_K(a)<\infty$. In particular, the fraction with any smaller direct ancestor tends to zero down the canonical ternary neighborhoods of $a$. This includes any $a$ which itself has no smaller direct ancestor. The theorem tests actual words at the supplied anchor. It does not require the forward orbit of $a$ to terminate.

At $a=1$ there is the explicit finite bound

$$\sum_{K\ge1}d_K(1)\le\frac{1}{1-(3/4)^2}\sum_{w\text{ positive odd}}w^{-2}=\frac{2\pi^2}{7}. \tag{1}$$

For any fixed odd binary cylinder, the same limiting conditional fractions hold among primes if $3\nmid a$, using the prime transfer cited below. Conditioning on exact depth $v_3(n-a)=K$ in the second alternative gives an upper bound $\tfrac32d_K(a)$ and hence the same summability. Vanishing density here covers **all** inverse histories and unlimited budgets; it exceeds what is obtained by counting a designated terminating-seed construction alone.

## Squared gain and finite-to-ordinary transfer

For a Haar-uniform ternary target, a prescribed reverse word of $q$ odds is legal on exactly one class modulo $3^q$, of measure $3^{-q}$. (When $q=0$ this means the whole space.) Its gain is $G=3^q/2^h$. Stop words at their first crossing $G>g$, $g\ge1$. These form a prefix-free family. In the binary word tree independently weight an odd letter by $3/4$ and an even letter by $1/4$. The word mass is $3^q4^{-h}$. Since $3^{-q}=G^{-2}3^q4^{-h}$, the probability of *any* legal crossing is at most $g^{-2}$. Independence of legal word events in the actual ternary target is unnecessary. This is Weaver's squared-gain prefix estimate. [Paper 178, §2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/178) · [Earlier postpeak application at PR211](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/4b44cded44af5bb7c3ce67fb16a70c898ad505d5/research/astra-reciprocal-rigidity-20261127/descent/postpeak/allclock/POSTPEAK_VISITORS.md).

For each fixed incoming deadline $V$ there are finitely many inverse words. On each word's legality class its resulting source is $gn-c$, where $g$ is the inverse slope and $c\ge0$ depends only on the word. Thus at sufficiently large $n$, it visits from below exactly when $g<1$. The only equal-slope word is empty and its source is $n$. Positivity and parity hold for every sufficiently large member of the legality class. Consequently this truncated fraction stabilizes to its ternary Haar probability on every fixed ternary and binary source cylinder, by CRT.

To remove $V$, pick the **least positive ancestor** of a target missed by the truncation but visited from below. Its first arrival requires more than $V$ steps and stays above or at that ancestor. Weaver's all-physical target estimate, Paper 177 §8 (3), bounds the total upper natural density of such targets by $O(\rho^V)$ with $\rho<1$. Intersect with the fixed source cylinder and divide by its fixed positive density, then let $V\to\infty$. This proves existence and equality to the probability of a ternary crossing; no assertion about naive natural density of the integer points of an arbitrary Haar-measurable set is used. The order is fixed $a,K,$ fixed binary cylinder, source scale tending to infinity, then $V\to\infty$. [Paper 177, §8 (3)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/177).

For primes, Paper 209 Theorem E compares the *actual* first meeting $H(n)$ truncated at a fixed outgoing clock, on all chosen reduced classes modulo $2^t3^{\lfloor(\log_2X)/4\rfloor}$. Choose a fixed $t$ at least the binary precision and take its output $k=0$ (any direct smaller visitor). The error is $o(X/\log X)$ and the conditional denominator has order $X/\log X$ for fixed $K,t$. The same comparison after summing all outputs gives the denominator. Thus the prime limiting fraction agrees with $d_K(a)$ unconditionally in the range used here; Paper 209 uses the Thorner–Zaman result for powerful moduli. [Paper 209 v1, Theorem E and Corollary E.1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/209).

## Proof of the dichotomy

If a legal word of slope $g<1$ at $a$ ends at $b$, then $b=ga-c\le ga<a$. At $n=a+3^qu$ it yields the integer $b+2^h u$. All reverse steps are legal, because the running difference retains a factor three before each odd inverse. For positive $u$ its source is positive and strictly below $n$. This proves the first case.

In the second case any gain crossing uses more than $K$ odds on the ball $a+3^K\mathbb Z_3$: all words using at most that many odds are legal already at $a$. Stop a crossing candidate just after its $K$th odd. At $a$ its endpoint is a positive odd integer $w$, and its length $L$ is determined by $w$ and $K$. In fact an odd $w$ has a unique accelerated forward trajectory; after its $K$th odd there can be multiple successive even endpoints at which it passes $a$, but among them $a$ can occur at most once. From the ternary target $a+3^K u$, the cut point is precisely $w+2^L u$, Haar-uniform when $u$ is Haar. To make a full contraction relative to the original target the rest must cross gain $g=2^L/3^K\ge1$. A union bound gives

$$d_K(a)\le\sum_{(w,L):\,T^Lw=a,\ Q_L(w)=K,\ w\ {\rm odd}}\left(\frac{3^K}{2^L}\right)^2. \tag{2}$$

For an actual positive forward word $2^L a=3^K w\prod_{z\text{ odd departure}}(1+1/(3z))$. In particular $2^L/3^K\ge w/a$. A given odd $w$ first reaches $a$ at a unique physical time if it ever reaches it. If $a$ is nonperiodic there is no second arrival, so summing (2) in $K$ costs at most $a^2\sum_{w\text{ odd}}w^{-2}$. If $a$ is on a physical cycle with native period $c$ and odd count $j>0$, its successive visits multiply $2^L/3^K$ by $\lambda=2^c/3^j>1$. Positivity of the telescoping product on the cycle gives this strict inequality even for a hypothetical nontrivial cycle. Summing the geometric series costs at most the same bound times $(1-\lambda^{-2})^{-1}$. At $a=1$, $c=2,j=1$ prove (1). This proof also shows that any supplied anchor with no smaller direct ancestor is in the second case, since a legal slope below one would give such an ancestor at the anchor itself.

A classification of the limiting density at every positive-integer ternary anchor does not decide which alternative holds for an arbitrary supplied anchor in finite time: a finite contracting inverse word is a positive witness, whereas an unlimited search can continue indefinitely. The second alternative is compatible with Collatz convergence; integer one is an instance. Weaver’s Paper 177 §7 already proves the qualitative vanishing of gain-crossing probability on $1+3^K\mathbb Z_3$, using this same positive reference tree and dominated convergence. Its §8 supplies physical transfer. Formula (1) strengthens that near-one conclusion to summability with an explicit constant, while the dichotomy treats every fixed positive integer anchor. Weaver’s Paper 65 proves vanishing coverage of a specified terminating-seed compiler in a different rational-cycle word. Formula (2) combines that all-time transfer with the complete positive inverse tree and sums across **all** canonical depths of the supplied anchor.
