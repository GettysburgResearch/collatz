# Protected prime representatives of every supplied Collatz component

**SOURCE-QUALIFIED written theorem.** Use the shortcut $T(2v)=v$, $T(2v+1)=3v+2$ and $R(x)\iff(\exists k)\,T^kx=1$. Define the finite test $F(x)$ by requiring this hit within five times the binary bit length of $x$. Fix a supplied ordinary integer $s>0$. If $3\nmid s$, set $y=s$. Otherwise remove powers of two from $s$ and take one odd shortcut. Its value is a positive unit modulo three. Take this value as $y$. In both cases $s$ has a finite track to $y$, so $R(s)=R(y)$.

Choose any integers $K\ge1,J\ge2,H\ge21J+9K+3$ and any real $0<\eta<1/10$. In every sufficiently large octave $[X,2X)$ there are $\gg_{s,K,J,H,\eta}X^2/(\log X)^3$ three-term arithmetic progressions of actual primes $l<m<u$, with all of these properties.

* The left prime visits 5 and the right prime visits 85; both pass $F$. The middle prime visits the supplied $y$. Consequently $R(m)=R(s)$. The fixed terminal paths from the two outer targets enter 8 on different edges.
* The spacing $d=m-l=u-m$ satisfies $0<d<\eta m$. All three primes belong to the same reduced class modulo $2^{H+J}3^K$.
* From every one of the three primes, the first $H$ departures are odd, followed by $EOE^{J-2}$. Through all these $H+J$ initial steps, every smaller positive visitor to any site at any incoming native clock has used more than the upper path's odd count plus $K$.
* For each fixed $0<\epsilon<1$, the primes can additionally be chosen so that no two of the three physical paths intersect within their respective first $(1-\epsilon)\log_2 X$ shortcuts, even allowing different clocks. This last assertion concerns the two supplied arms with both clocks bounded. It does not exclude a smaller visitor outside the arithmetic progression.

The ternary restriction protects the initial sites. The later track from $m$ to $y$ is an actual path in the component of $s$. In particular one prime below $m$ and one above it are separately certified to reach 1. A proof that $m$ shares a future with one of them would prove $R(s)$.

## Three prescribed targets on a prime arithmetic progression

Work first with arbitrary fixed targets $g_l,g_m,g_u$ prime to three and any reduced residue $A\bmod M$. Write $\lambda(n)=\log n$ on primes and zero elsewhere. For the visit indicator $b_i$ let $\rho_i=X^{-1}\sum_{X\le n<2X}b_i(n)$. Paper 221 proves that each $\rho_i$ stays bounded below and that
$$ \sup_\alpha X^{-1}\left|\sum_{X\le n<2X}\lambda(n)(b_i(n)-\rho_i)e(\alpha n)\right|=o(1). $$
It also proves, on prime weights, that the mass of starts reaching 1 later than five shortcut steps per binary digit is $o(X)$. One can apply its uniform-in-deadline comparison with the single smaller cutoff $5\lfloor\log_2X\rfloor$ and Leen's first-hit law; the difference between that cutoff and the bit-length cutoff on the octave is bounded. Thus, when $g_i$ reaches 1, requiring $F$ changes any one normalized Fourier coefficient by $o(1)$. These are imported written results using the independently verified Dirichlet GRH statement. [Paper 221, *On the primes* and *Prescribing the three prime visits*](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/221) · [Fixed-target predecessor density](https://www.proofatlas.ai/formalizations/positive-lower-density-collatz-predecessors/) · [Sutherland's Dirichlet GRH verification](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/).

Fix three disjoint proportional subintervals of the octave centered at $cX,(c+\gamma)X,(c+2\gamma)X$, with positive widths much smaller than $\gamma X$ and $0<3\gamma<\eta$. The same $o(1)$ centered Fourier bound holds after restriction to the relevant interval and the class $A\bmod M$. A fixed congruence is a sum of $M$ characters. For an interval, approximate its scaled indicator above and below by continuous functions outside strips of total length at most $\delta X$ and then by trigonometric polynomials. The prime number theorem bounds the strips' normalized prime weight by $O(\delta)+o(1)$. Take $X\to\infty$ first for fixed approximation, then $\delta\to0$.

The count with positive logarithmic weights has Fourier integral at frequencies $\alpha,-2\alpha,\alpha$. The Green–Tao restriction bound used in Paper 221 controls every one of the three normalized transforms in $L^{5/2}(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z,X\,d\alpha)$ by a constant, both for prime weights and weights dominated by them. Doubling the frequency preserves Haar measure. Telescoping the three centered replacements in the integral therefore costs $o(X^2)$: for any three normalized factors of this type, one bounded by $\delta$, their product is bounded by $\sqrt{\delta}\max_i|f_i|^{5/2}$.

After the replacements the count is $\rho_l\rho_m\rho_u$ times the unmarked weighted prime count plus $o(X^2)$. For the unmarked count apply the complexity-one case of Green–Tao Corollary 1.7 to $A+Mv,A+Mw,A+M(2w-v)$ in the indicated positive-area box, keeping its last coordinate in the third interval. No two linear parts are proportional. At a prime dividing $M$ all values are units. At other primes one can take $v=w$ avoiding zero in $A+Mv$; for all sufficiently large primes the local factor is $1+O(\ell^{-2})$ by the three pairwise independent linear zero conditions. The singular product is positive. Removing proper prime powers costs $O(X^{3/2}\log^3X)$. The unmarked count is therefore $\gg_{M,\eta}X^2$. Each logarithmic weight is $\log X+O(1)$, so the unweighted marked proportion is $\rho_l\rho_m\rho_u+o(1)$. The choice $(5,y,85)$ with deadlines at the two outside coordinates gives the first property. The Fourier proof is adapted directly from Paper 221's colored ternary Goldbach result, including its linear frequency substitutions in §*When a prime summand certifies the sum*. Here the tested midpoint is prime and belongs to the component of every specified ordinary $s$ in turn. [Green and Tao, *Linear equations in primes*, Corollary 1.7](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2010.171.1753).

## Protection and independent clocks

Choose $M=2^{H+J}3^K$ and solve
$$
 A\equiv2^H 3^{-(H+1)}-1\pmod{2^{H+J}},\qquad
 A\equiv1\pmod{3^K}.
$$
The first right side multiplies an inverse of $3^{H+1}$ modulo $2^J$. The resulting class is reduced. For any prime $P$ in it, write $P=2^H v-1$, with $v$ odd. Its crest $3^Hv-1$ is congruent to $3^{-1}-1\bmod2^J$. The signed rational $-2/3$ has word $EOE^{J-2}$; thus the actual positive prime has that prefix after its all-odd rise. Its prefixes are maximal in their signed-offset fibre. PR146's ternary unit shield protects the ascent and PR211's protected-barrier theorem protects the postcrest prefixes at $H\ge21J+9K+3$. These theorems cover every physical smaller visitor and every incoming clock. [PR146, original shield](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437/research/upper-bit-mergers/ADJACENT_DESCENT.md) · [PR211, maximal-word extension](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/4b44cded44af5bb7c3ce67fb16a70c898ad505d5/research/astra-reciprocal-rigidity-20261127/descent/postpeak/POSTPEAK.md).

For each pair of positions in an arithmetic progression, those two coordinates determine the third. For fixed $x$ in the octave and shortcut clocks $i,j\le t$, there are at most $2^j$ positive $z$ with $T^jz=T^i x$: there are only two inverse branches at a step. Across three pairs the number of arithmetic progressions with such a meeting is $O(X(t+1)^2 2^t)$, even counting composite coordinates. At $t=\lfloor(1-\epsilon)\log_2 X\rfloor$ this is $o(X^2/\log^3X)$ and can be deleted from the marked count.

## Decidable fixed-source selection and the global criterion

For the sourcewise version, schedule every positive $s$ infinitely often, for example $s_j=1+v_2(j)$, $j\ge1$. Choose computable $K_j,J_j$ tending to infinity, $H_j\ge21J_j+9K_j+3$ and $\eta_j\downarrow0$. At stage $j$ require the next triple to exceed both the previous maximum prime and $2^{2^j}$; this last threshold can be replaced by any prescribed computable increasing function. Dovetail candidate triples and clock bounds. Primality, arithmetic progression, the class, relative width, the two finite deadlines, the track from $s_j$ to $y_j$, and the middle prime's visit to $y_j$ all admit positive finite certificates. One may also require the three pairwise nonintersection certificates up to $\lfloor(\log_2 l)/2\rfloor$. Each search halts by applying the theorem at this one fixed stage. Strictly increasing prime blocks make membership decidable by running stages until the bound has passed the query. There are $O(\log\log X)$ selected triples below $X$ for the given default threshold.

Universal convergence is equivalent to eventual validity of the implication “if $l$ and $u$ converge, then $m$ converges” on this one computable list. The outside premises have separate finite certificates at selection. If some $s$ misses 1, its infinitely many assigned middle primes miss 1 while both outside primes have passed their deadlines. No oracle for convergence is used to construct the sparse list. This is a three-prime, equal-spacing version of the sparse sufficient-set idea; the recurrence at each supplied component, the two distinct certified exterior visits and the initial all-clock protection are part of the conclusion. [Earlier sparse tripling-implication construction, Paper 227](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/227/v1) · [Semiprime finite-deadline mismatch criterion, Paper 233](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/233/v1).

There is also a single finite count in each octave. In any one of the fixed protected classes and interval windows above, consider all unmarked prime arithmetic progressions. Of these, count the triples with $F(l)=F(u)=1$ and $F(m)=0$. Universal convergence is equivalent to the normalized count tending to zero. Indeed if every source converges, the prime weight failing $F$ is $o(X)$ by the deadline theorem. Its transform has supremum $o(1)$ and the same restriction argument removes all such progressions up to relative $o(1)$. If a supplied $s$ misses 1, the positive fraction of progressions visiting $(5,y,85)$ with fast outsides all fail this middle test. With Leen's stated natural density $h$ of the convergence basin, the Fourier calculation gives the more precise normalized limit $h^2(1-h)$. One uses the finite-deadline approximation to that basin separately in each coordinate. This last limit is an instance of the colored linear configuration calculation; it supplies a decidable aggregate test, without an estimate forcing it to vanish.

The missing implication is a transfer of convergence from the two exterior prime trajectories to the intervening prime trajectory. All three arithmetic coordinates, the class and the source $s$ are ordinary and fixed during each test. The density calculation describes exactly what a failure would contribute.

## A protected first interval followed by actual smaller visitors

There is a stronger mathematical existence result in which all three primes have a genuine gap before any smaller source can visit, followed by a common visit from many individually specified smaller integers. Fix $s$ and $\eta$ as above and choose $J\ge2,D\ge1$. There is a finite $L>J$ depending on $J,D$. For every sufficiently large $H$ depending on $J,D,L$, there are $\gg_{s,\eta,H,J,D}X^2/\log^3X$ prime progressions in every large octave with the same designated visits $(5,y(s),85)$ and the same two fast exterior paths, such that each of their primes $P$ satisfies the following.

1. Its first $H+J$ departures are $O^H EOE^{J-2}$. **No smaller positive integer reaches any site through that date at any incoming clock or odd count.**
2. Put $P_d=(P+1)/2^d-1$, $1\le d\le D$. Each is a positive integer strictly below $P$. At their respective native clocks $H+L$ and $H-d+L$, $P$ and $P_d$ have the same endpoint and the same accumulated odd count. Every positive-date upper state up to that joint endpoint exceeds $P$. For the middle prime the later common future reaches $y(s)$, so all these explicit smaller ordinary sources belong to the supplied component.
3. These progressions lie in the unit ternary class $P\equiv1\bmod3$. The ternary class in this strengthened result comes from unrestricted smaller-visitor survival. A prescribed deeper class $1\bmod3^K$ has the separate odd-budget conclusion in the main theorem.

**Choosing the later release without using extra ternary digits.** Write $P=2^H v-1$. The crests of the $D+1$ sources are $3^{H-d}v-1$ for $0\le d\le D$. Use as pivot the variable $b=3^{H-D}v$. As it ranges over odd units modulo $2^L$, the upper crest's first $J$ steps are fixed by $3^D b\equiv3^{-1}\bmod2^J$. All higher binary bits of $b$ are still free. For each adjacent pair of crests the upper is three times the lower plus two and starts with one more accumulated odd. As in [the prime-exponent proof](PRIME_MERSENNE.md), Weaver's conditional pair estimate after $J$ fixed bits leaves failure probability $O((J+1)/\sqrt{L-J})$ for each edge, uniformly in the offset from the pivot (its slope as a function of $b$ is odd). Choose $L$ so the sum over the $D$ edges is less than one. Some one binary extension therefore matches all crests, including their effective exponents, by $L$. It can be found by finite enumeration. The chosen pivot class and $L$ are independent of $H$. The resulting condition on $P$ is one binary class modulo $2^{H+L}$. Take $H\ge600$, $J\le H/100$, $H>D$, and $(3/2)^H>2^{L+1}$. After the upper ascent all $L$ further values exceed $P$ by the elementary halving lower bound; its ascent is strictly increasing. For large $X$, $P>y(s)$, so a first visit by $P$ to this fixed target comes after the meeting. The original smaller integer $P_d$ then visits that target too. This constructs a smaller visitor to the selected prime. Its size can still far exceed the originally supplied $s$.

**The earlier unrestricted exclusion survives this chosen binary tail.** Let $B=H+J$, $B'=H+L$, and let $W$ be the original word of length $B$. On its binary cylinder, write its affine endpoint as $Y(N)$. PR211's all-clock classification gives ordinary relative survivor density exceeding $1/3$ for this maximal word. We need its proof as well as the bound. At a fixed reverse deadline $V$, testing for a smaller visitor to $Y(N)$ eventually depends only on $N\bmod3^V$. Indeed the finitely many reverse candidates have fixed affine coefficients on their ternary legality classes; equality of coefficients is addressed by the maximal-word cone. A smaller visitor through any earlier site would continue to $Y(N)$. Write $S_V$ for the eventually stable ternary survivor set for this finite test. The sets decrease, and put $S=\cap_VS_V$ in $\mathbb Z_3$. The PR211 long-arrival estimate bounds the upper natural density on the whole source cylinder of starts where this capped test misses a true smaller visitor by $C_{H,W}\rho^V$, $\rho<1$. It follows that $\mu(S)>1/3$ for normalized ternary Haar measure. Restricting the exceptional integers further to the *specific* release cylinder modulo $2^{B'}$ cannot increase their absolute number. Its conditional loss therefore also tends to zero with $V$, for this fixed cylinder. For each finite test, CRT makes its ternary law on that cylinder precisely the original one. Finally $S$ has positive measure on $1\bmod3$. Multiples of three occupy only $1/3$, while every source prime to three in the $2\bmod3$ class has the smaller odd predecessor $(2N-1)/3$ already at its initial site. [PR211, *All-clock protection after a maximal postpeak word*, including *Passing back to complete physical orbits*](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/4b44cded44af5bb7c3ce67fb16a70c898ad505d5/research/astra-reciprocal-rigidity-20261127/descent/postpeak/allclock/POSTPEAK_VISITORS.md).

**Transfer of the all-time test to primes.** Fix $V$ before sending $X$ to infinity. Use the prime transfer theorem of Paper 209 at the fixed outgoing cutoff $t=B'$. Its $R=\lfloor(\log_2X)/4\rfloor$ tends to infinity. The release class and $S_V$ on ternary units give a union of reduced classes modulo $2^t3^R$ once $R\ge V$. For each output $k$ of the truncated *actual* earliest meeting $H_t$, Theorem E gives prime counts in that union equal to $(1+o(1))3/\log X$ times the corresponding ordinary counts plus $o_{t,V}(X/\log X)$. Sum the finitely many $k\le B$ to count capped survivors that have a true meeting by $B$. The finite-test approximation thus differs from actual unrestricted survivor status on prime log weight at most $O_{H,W,B'}(\rho^V X)+o_{H,W,B',V}(X)$ on this chosen fixed class. This uses the theorem with its reverse-clock cap already removed. A fixed finite periodic set of exceptional integers alone would give insufficient information about exceptional primes. [Paper 209 v1, Theorem E and Lemmas 5.1–5.3](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/209).

At finite $V$ insert the survivor restrictions on all three primes in the main proof. They are finite character sums, so Paper 221's orbit-label replacement still applies to each coordinate. Replacing each finite test by actual survivor status has an error tending to zero as $V\to\infty$ after $X\to\infty$. Indeed the centered weight difference has normalized Fourier supremum at most its normalized prime $L^1$ error; the remaining two weights are bounded in the restriction norm. The error in units of $X^2$ is at most $O(\rho^{V/2})+o_V(1)$ with fixed-word constants.

For completeness, the unmarked weighted AP count satisfying the three survivor restrictions has positive lower limit in those same units. Normalize by the APs in the *fixed* binary release class and ternary class $1\bmod3$. For finite ternary precision $V$, the pair $(l,m)$ is asymptotically uniform over the square of ternary lifts of that class, with $u=2m-l$. For each pair of residue lifts apply the linear-forms theorem above: after parametrizing the two variables in their lifted classes the local factors at 2 and 3 are unchanged (the three prescribed coordinates are units), and at a prime $\ell>3$ the change of variables is invertible modulo $\ell$. Its area is divided by $3^{2(V-1)}$. This proves the uniform finite law. By decreasing convergence the limit of the normalized unmarked counts for $S_V$ is the Haar probability of a three-term progression in $S\cap(1+3\mathbb Z_3)$. This probability is positive: in a fine ternary subcylinder where $S$ has relative density strictly above $2/3$, choose independent Haar $l,m$. Then $u=2m-l$ is again uniform on the same cylinder; a union bound gives positive probability of three memberships. Such a cylinder exists by martingale convergence of finite-digit conditional expectations. This argument takes all precision limits inside the one fixed baseline class. The earlier pairwise bounded-clock exclusion still discards only $o(X^2/\log^3 X)$ prime triples.

The stronger claim establishes existence with an unrestricted negative condition. The decidable selection in the preceding section uses finite positive tracks; it can also insist on the explicit later $P_d$ tracks and on exclusion up to any prescribed finite incoming cutoff. It gives no algorithm deciding unrestricted absence of every smaller visitor for one candidate prime.

## Finite midpoint controls

In three direct arithmetic progressions the common class uses $H=56,J=2,K=1$ and the additional modulus $5^{200}$. All coordinates have 541 or 542 binary digits. Since $5^{200}>\sqrt P$ divides each $P-1$, one modular Pocklington witness for the known prime factor 5 proves each coordinate prime. The verifier then follows each ordinary integer step, checks the stated first visits, and exhausts all pairs of early incoming clocks through at least 270 shortcuts. The unrestricted early exclusion in the preceding section is a separate asymptotic result and is not asserted for these finite examples.
