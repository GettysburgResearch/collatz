# Almost complete protection at good ternary depths, even at unrestricted cost

Set $a=3/2$. For a large integer $R$, all but $O(R^{0.96})$ integers $K$ in **every translated interval of $R$ positive depths** satisfy the conclusions below. All logarithms without subscripts are natural. The exceptional depths are independent of the prescribed source, binary cylinder, ascent length and visitor clock.

* Conditional on $N\equiv1\pmod{3^K}$ in **any** fixed odd binary cylinder beginning with $H$ odd departures, the fraction of actual starts for which any smaller integer visits any of the first $H+1$ sites, at any incoming clock or budget, is $O(R^{-0.10})$. The bound is uniform in $H$ after taking the individual source-density limits. One can also prescribe any fixed subsequent binary word.
* The same bound applies to the union over every fixed finite collection of integral scaled sites $Y+1=2^\delta a^i(N+1)$ with $0\le\delta\le R^{1/3}$. Each target is required to be integral on the prescribed binary cylinder; $i$ need not be bounded by $R$. The ternary exceptional-event bound itself is common to all these sites.
* In the fixed source classes at $K=41,141,357$ the unscaled full-ascent failure fraction is at most $4(2/3)^8$, $4(2/3)^{29}$, and $4(2/3)^{44}$, respectively. The third is below $7.2\cdot10^{-8}$, uniform in the length of the prescribed ascent. These are fractions over actual ordinary starts. The isolated prime-exponent examples of the companion note really have visitors at their stated exact prices and so lie among the permitted exceptions.

The pointwise theorem that each good depth forces every visitor to spend $i+K+\lfloor0.25\log R\rfloor+1$ odds already appears in [the ramp paper](RAMP_ANCESTORS.md). The present loss estimate removes the visitor budget entirely after discarding a small relative fraction of ordinary inputs. The all-clock fraction on an unconstrained pure-odd binary cylinder was already proved larger than $0.510269713938$ in Weaver 178 §7. [Paper 178 v1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/178).

## A common crossing event over many upper dates

For a finite inverse competitor whose full slope relative to $N$ is less than one, the scaled unit barrier first excludes budgets up to $i+K$ for actual large members of a fixed comparison cylinder. Equivalently, a strictly contracting word of that budget, if ternarily legal on this class, would produce genuine smaller visitors on arbitrarily large ordinary lifts, contradicting that barrier. This applies to any positive power-of-two scale under the stated integrality condition. [PR211 scaled unit barrier](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/4b44cded44af5bb7c3ce67fb16a70c898ad505d5/research/astra-reciprocal-rigidity-20261127/descent/postpeak/POSTPEAK.md).

Stop after $i+K$ inverse odds, with $r$ evens so far. Run the same word from $2^{\delta+1}a^i-1$. The rational becomes an ordinary positive integer after its first $i$ reverse letters and remains positive as in the ramp proof. Write $w$ for its final positive odd value, $t=K+r+\delta$ and $g=2^t/3^K$. The true cut value at $N=1+3^K u$ is **exactly**

$$w+2^t u,\qquad w+1\le 2g. \tag{1}$$

All dependence on $i$ and on the separate scale disappears from this actual cut value. Whatever the remainder's length and number of odds, a full strict contraction requires it to cross a gain greater than $g$. With $u$ Haar-uniform, $w+2^tu$ is Haar-uniform. At fixed $(w,t)$ its crossing probability is at most $g^{-2}$ for $g\ge1$, by the prefix-free squared-gain argument. [Kraft proof and physical transfer](TERNARY_FLOORS.md).

For any threshold $A\ge1$, pairs with $g\ge A$ consequently cost at most

$$\sum_{w\text{ positive odd}}\sum_{t:\ 2^t/3^K\ge\max(A,(w+1)/2)}(2^t/3^K)^{-2}\le\frac43\sum_{n\ge1}\min(A^{-2},n^{-2})\le\frac4A. \tag{2}$$

Here $w=2n-1$, the inner progression is geometric, and splitting at $\lfloor A\rfloor$ gives the final bound (also for $1\le A<2$). No mutual independence of tails is used. The three finite bounds follow from the finite screens in the ramp paper: if an unscaled virtual prefix had $g<a^{m_0}$ then (1) and its even budget satisfy the same tests used there at $m_0=8,29,44$. Any prefix backtracking its first $i$ odd inverses to $N$ would have $g\ge2^{L_K}/3^K>a^{m_0}$, by the computed minima. Every remaining case failed those necessary screens.

It remains to exclude *virtual prefixes* with $g<A=R^{0.10}$. This is the structural count inside the preceding ramp proof, adapted to all $\delta\le R^{1/3}$. After the first $i$ reverse letters let $d$ count evens and $z=T^\tau(w)$, where $\tau=K+r=t-\delta$ and $Q_\tau(w)=K+d$. The same normalized carry gives

$$2^{\delta+1}3^d-2^d\le z\le2^{\delta+1}3^d-1.$$

Thus $1/2\le g<A$, $\log P_\tau(w)\le\log(2A)$, and for $z\ge Y$,

$$t-K\log_23=\log_2(w/2)+\log_2P_\tau(w)+O(Y^{-(1-\log_32)}).$$

The relative error uses $2^{-\delta}(2/3)^d\ll z^{-(1-\log_32)}$. The physical-date localization becomes $|\tau-K\log_23|\ll\log A+R^{1/3}$. With $Y=R^{0.42}$, $\Delta=R^{-0.15}$, the small-$z$ count per $w$ is now $O((\log R)Y\log Y\,(R^{1/3}+\log R))=R^{0.754+o(1)}$. The monotone-product bins have cardinality $B=O((\log R)/\Delta)$; their projected total $K$-interval length is $O(R+(R^{1/3}+\log R)B)=O(R)$. The same Erdős–Turán estimate therefore gives $O(R^{0.85+o(1)})$ depths per $w$. There are fewer than $2A$ possible roots, so $O(R^{0.95+o(1)})=O(R^{0.96})$ depths admit any such cheap virtual prefix. Taking a union with the previously defined exceptional depth set preserves that bound. This reasoning constrains the prefix before the tail and never assumes the tail is itself cheap.

At any fixed physical deadline a smaller visitor eventually needs strict slope contraction. The equal-slope case would have the same counts as the prescribed all-odd slope, with $q=i$ by unique factorization, already excluded by the scaled barrier. A word with smaller gain costs more than $N$ on sufficiently large actual members of its class. CRT computes the uniform ternary probability of each finite test on the fixed binary cylinder. For each fixed finite site family, the physical long-arrival upper density tends to zero by Weaver 177 §8 (3), applied to each affine injective target map on that cylinder. This justifies the first two conditional conclusions. The numerical bound is for the whole ascent since an arrival at any earlier site continues to its crest. The single common Haar event makes its upper bound independent of the number of chosen sites; no countable interchange of physical input densities is needed.

## Uniform growing shells, including prime starts

For unscaled ascents there is a joint finite-size version. Let $X$ be large. In the octave $[X,2X)$ prescribe any odd binary class modulo $2^D$ beginning with $H$ odds, where $1\le H\le D\le0.004\log_2X$. Intersect with $N\equiv1\pmod{3^K}$, $K\le0.004\log_3X$. For a good $K$ as above the unconditional-within-class failure fraction among all integers is

$$O(R^{-0.10}+X^{-0.008}). \tag{3}$$

This is meaningful with $R$ growing and $K$ chosen, for example, in a growing interval inside the allowed depth range. For $K=41,141,357$ one may replace $O(R^{-0.10})$ by the explicit bound in (2) at the indicated $A$.

**Proof.** There are $S=X/(2^D3^K)+O(1)\gg X^{0.992}$ class members. It suffices to test the unique crest. Truncate incoming words at $V=\lfloor(\log_2X)/2\rfloor$. A competitor from below must have $q>H+K$, by the pointwise scaled barrier at scale one. If its gain relative to the physical ascent is smaller than required, its inverse slope is $D'=2^e(2/3)^{q-H}>1$, with $e$ incoming evens. The shifted numerator error is at most $2^e-1$ as in (2) of the ramp paper. A smaller source would force $N+1<2^e3^{q-H}\le3^V<X$. Equality of slopes is impossible here. The remaining strictly contracting legal finite words constitute an event periodic in $u$ modulo $3^V$. Counting it in the binary cylinder costs at most $O(3^V/S)=O(X^{-0.19})$ relatively.

All actual targets lie below $Z=3Xa^H\ll X^{1.0024}$. A visitor missed at the deadline charges the least ancestor's orbit, whose first target arrival stays above that ancestor. The uniform physical long-arrival bound $|E_V(Z)|\ll Z2^{-V/25}+Z^{49/50}$ from PR211 then costs $O(X^{-0.009})$ relative to $S$ using the injectivity of the single target map. This proves (3) with slack. [PR211 uniform physical tail from Weaver's proof](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/4b44cded44af5bb7c3ce67fb16a70c898ad505d5/research/astra-reciprocal-rigidity-20261127/descent/postpeak/allclock/SCALED_TARGETS.md).

Unconditionally, by Paper 209 in its powerful-modulus range, the corresponding failure fraction among **prime starting values $N$** is $O(R^{-0.10}+X^{-0.003})$. Indeed apply Theorem E at outgoing date $D$ on the prescribed single odd parity class, with the reduced ternary restriction modulo $3^K$ (contained in its allowed modulus). Sum the outputs $k\le H$, using the single-parity-class error $X^{1-0.0123+o(1)}$ in integer units and its uniform multiplicative $1+o(1)$. The denominator is comparable to $S/\log X$, giving additive relative error $X^{-0.0043+o(1)}$. Thus the all-clock exclusion transfers even on these growing thin prime classes. Prime exponents of Mersenne integers are a different sparse family: this argument gives no such all-cost density estimate for them. [Paper 209 v1, Theorem E and its single-parity clause](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/209).

## Maximal postcrest words and a prescribed later smaller merger

Let the upper word be $O^H W$ with $J=|W|\le R^{1/3}$, $f=Q(W)$ and $q=J-f$. Suppose $W$ is maximal in the PR211 equal-signed-offset sense and $H\ge 100(K+J+1)$. In **the specific** ternary class $N\equiv1\pmod{3^K}$ and **any** binary refinement of this word, the all-clock failure fraction through the *end* of $O^H W$ is

$$O(R^{-0.10})+5\cdot3^K6^J4^q2^{-\lfloor H/12\rfloor}. \tag{4}$$

The last term decreases exponentially in $K+J$ in this range. If desired, exclude the additional $O(\log R)$ smallest positive depths to make the entire right side $O(R^{-0.10})$. In particular one may impose a subsequent release word previously selected to merge a finite specified set of actual smaller sources. The proof retains the improvement after conditioning on this *particular* binary release.

To see (4), use the all-clock stopped reverse comparison of PR211. Write $h=H+f$. A genuinely competing finite word must exceed the upper gain, the case with equal counts being excluded by maximality. Stop after $h$ inverse odds. The published large-even part, with $\ell>\lfloor H/12\rfloor$ even letters, costs at most $5\,6^J4^q2^{-\lfloor H/12\rfloor}$ in unrestricted ternary Haar measure. Conditioning on the ball of mass $3^{-K}$ costs at most the displayed $3^K$ factor, with no independence assumption. For $\ell\le\lfloor H/12\rfloor$, the signed trap in that proof leaves $\delta=f-u\ge0$, $\ell'=\ell-(J-u)\ge0$, and a signed prefix with $j=H+\delta$ odds and $\ell'$ evens from $-M$ to $-1$. Its actual stopped source is $Z_S=2^{\ell'+\delta}(N+1)-M$, and the further tail must cross gain $2^{\ell'+\delta}$. The **same** signed prefix yields an integral inverse prefix from the scaled ramp site $\widetilde Y=2^\delta a^j(N+1)-1=3^{H+\delta}v-1$ where $N+1=2^H v$. Its endpoint is the same $Z_S$. This follows by lifting the signed prefix at $-1$ with its $j$ ternary digits of displacement. Its gain cancels the gain from $N$ to $\widetilde Y$ up to the factor $2^{\ell'+\delta}$. Thus the continuation supplies a strictly contracting full inverse word for a scaled site with $\delta\le J$. The already common estimate (1)–(2) controls the union over all such short-even origins. Fixed-word finite-to-ordinary transfer now proves (4). [PR211 full stopped calculation, short/long even totals](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/4b44cded44af5bb7c3ce67fb16a70c898ad505d5/research/astra-reciprocal-rigidity-20261127/descent/postpeak/allclock/POSTPEAK_VISITORS.md).

The finite-shell version of (4) adds $O(X^{-0.008})$ under $H+J\le D\le0.004\log_2X$ and $K\le0.004\log_3X$. Use the same cutoff $V$, combined progression size $S$, and long-arrival count as for (3), with $Z\ll X^{1.0024}$. On the finite test, a smaller competitor's strictly larger gain is forced by PR211's unsigned affine estimate (its uniformly growing heights section applies since $H+J\le(\log_2X)/90$ and the stipulated $H,J$ imply its cone bound); each finite legality event still costs $O(3^V/S)$ in CRT rounding. The additional binary digits need only contain the prescribed upper word. The corresponding prime-start version adds $O(X^{-0.003})$, via Theorem E exactly as above with mark $k\le H+J$.

Finally fix any supplied integer $s$ and its related ternary-unit root $y(s)$ used in [the prime-midpoint construction](PRIME_MIDPOINTS.md). For each fixed good $K$, choose $J$ and the later binary release there, then $H$ large enough for the present bounds and for that construction's height inequalities. The release supplies the prescribed positive smaller witnesses after $O^H EOE^{J-2}$; take $W=EOE^{J-2}$. In the marked prime progressions whose two wings visit their fixed terminating targets and whose midpoint eventually visits $y(s)$, all on $1\pmod{3^K}$ and the specified binary class, the relative fraction having **no** earlier smaller arrival on any of their three coordinates is at least one minus three times the right side of (4), in the limiting source octaves. For finite ternary tests, Paper 221's orbit-label replacement lets one use the underlying prime progression count with the same marks' fixed positive factor. Each coordinate in this conditioned progression count has uniform ternary lifts: given common modulus $3^K$ and the fixed dyadic class, all compatible higher ternary progressions have equal local factor, and each of their three marginals is uniform. The union bound costs at most three single-coordinate probabilities. Remove the reverse deadline by the prime all-clock approximation and restriction estimate in the midpoint packet, taking each parameter fixed before the octave height tends to infinity. This preserves the supplied component through the actual midpoint. It does not make that prime smaller than $s$, nor imply that its independently terminating wings share its component. [Prime marking and Fourier inputs](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/221).
