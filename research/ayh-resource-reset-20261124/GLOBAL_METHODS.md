# Nonlinear digit interpretations for the mixed-base Collatz rules

## A three-coordinate obstruction

Work on positive integer triples, ordered componentwise. A strict comparison requires a strict increase in coordinate zero and a weak increase in every coordinate. Interpret each symbol by a map preserving weak and strict comparisons. All eleven AYH rules must be weakly oriented in the decreasing direction. Only the map $A$ interpreting binary zero is required to be polynomial. Its three coordinate polynomials have nonnegative integer coefficients and positive outputs throughout the positive orthant. They may have arbitrary degree, omit variables, and reset a coordinate to a positive constant.

**Theorem.** No individual rule can be strictly oriented under these hypotheses. The same conclusion holds in dimensions one and two by adding unused constant-one coordinates to the interpreted maps. A strictness hypothesis can be tested on the positive orthant of an interpretation originally defined on all naturals whenever every symbol preserves this orthant.

**Proof.** Each variable actually occurring in a nonzero polynomial contributes a positive monomial at least as large as that variable on the positive orthant. The output is monotone in just the variables it uses. Strictness in the distinguished coordinate forces the first output of $A$ to depend on its first input. Thus the first coordinate $X_t$ of $A^t(s)$ increases weakly at every step.

Write $Y_t,Z_t$ for the others. An edge from $Y$ to $Z$ means that the output for $Z$ uses $Y$, hence $Z_{t+1}\ge Y_t$. A self-edge at $Y$, or a two-edge cycle joining $Y$ and $Z$, gives $Y_{t+2}\ge Y_t$ for every $t$. With neither, if the output for $Y$ omits $Z$, it uses only $X$ and is weakly increasing from time one. Otherwise the output for $Z$ omits $Y$. It either dominates its own previous value or depends only on $X$. In either case $Z_1\le Z_3$. Since the output for $Y$ then uses at most $X,Z$, this gives $Y_2\le Y_4$. Interchanging the names of the two free coordinates gives $Z_2\le Z_4$. We have proved $A^2(s)\le A^4(s)$ for every positive triple.

The ordinary shortcut trajectory is $41,62,31,47,71,107,161$. Normalizing to binary after each dynamic step gives a 32-rule derivation from `cabaabd` to `cabaaaabd` that uses all eleven rules. The target inserts two copies of binary zero into the internal run of two zeros. Context monotonicity and the preceding inequality give the reverse comparison between the evaluated source and target. Strict orientation of any one rule would give a strict decrease along the complete derivation. The two comparisons contradict irreflexivity.

The Lean theorem `PositivePolynomialBarrier.no_strict_rule` combines the graph argument and the literal derivation. It uses `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. The polynomial syntax in `PolynomialPump.lean` uses one, variables, addition and multiplication. Every nonzero polynomial of the stated type has such an expression after deleting zero terms and writing positive coefficients as sums of ones. The context comparisons and literal trace reside in `SelfEmbedding.lean`. The compiled theorem concerns the forward presentation. Reversing every word in the derivation supplies the corresponding mathematical result for the reversed presentation.

**Scope.** The 32-step kernel proof assumes every symbol preserves the positive orthant. The final section treats whole-orthant maps with identically zero coordinates using a different trajectory. General multivariate polynomial interpretations in arbitrary dimension remain open here. The result is a proof-method obstruction. It implies neither an ordinary divergent orbit nor termination. The foundational equivalence for this particular eleven-rule presentation is Theorem 3.17 of the AYH paper. [Yolcu, Aaronson and Heule](https://emreyolcu.com/research/rewriting-collatz.pdf)

## Arbitrary dimension for multivariate monomials

For any finite string rewriting system, the following two interpretation classes have precisely the same weak and marked-strict rule orientations in a given dimension.

- A positive monomial interpretation has $(F_s(x))_i=c_{s,i}\prod_j x_j^{E_{s,ij}}$, with $c_{s,i}\ge1$ and nonnegative integer matrices $E_s$, on the positive orthant.
- A natural affine interpretation has $F'_s(y)=E_sy+b_s$, with nonnegative integer vectors $b_s$, on the nonnegative orthant.

The rule order in both cases uses an everywhere-strict distinguished first coordinate and weak comparison in each other coordinate. If contexts must preserve strictness, a letter must have $E_{s,00}\ge1$ in both classes.

Composing monomial maps multiplies their exponent matrices. A rule $l\to r$ is weakly oriented exactly when $E_l\ge E_r$ entrywise and its vectors of multiplicative constants satisfy $c_l\ge c_r$. Necessity follows by setting all variables equal to one and then sending one variable at a time to infinity. Sufficiency follows by multiplication. Strict orientation adds $c_{l,0}>c_{r,0}$.

Let $p$ range over the finitely many primes dividing the generator constants. For $K\ge1$ send $p$ to the natural number $w_p(K)=\lfloor\log_2(p^K)\rfloor$. Extend the map additively over products by prime factorization. Every equality among constants is preserved exactly. The charge of any fixed positive integer formed from these primes, divided by $K$, converges to its binary logarithm. Thus for sufficiently large $K$ every strict comparison among the finitely many rule constants is also preserved. Use the resulting integer charges of $c_{s,i}$ as the entries of $b_s$. The charged vectors compose by the affine rule with the same $E_s$. This proves the forward transfer. The reverse transfer takes $c_{s,i}=2^{b_{s,i}}$.

The compiler `monomial_transfer.py` computes the weights by integer bit lengths and tests the finite candidate using exact arithmetic. Repeatedly doubling $K$ terminates for every valid input by the argument above. Its bundled controls cover nonlinear variable exponents, a coordinate reset, unequal weak and strict constants, the equality $6=2\cdot3$, a round trip, and a reversed false orientation. The transfer theorem is a written proof, independent of any Collatz-specific result. Applying a published no-removal theorem to it inherits the exact hypotheses of that theorem. Nashida's r5 primary source states that a natural affine interpretation in any finite dimension with every top-left entry at least one can weakly orient all eleven rules only if it strictly orients none, for the forward and reversed systems. The explicit transfer yields the corresponding monomial consequence. We inspected the author's frozen r5 source and verification scope. We did not rebuild its Lean development. The author's earlier r3 paper concerns a more restricted exponential-growth barrier. ## A trace calculation for natural matrix carries

Write $P=A+B, Q=E+F+G, M=P+Q$. Each of the six carry inequalities has a nonnegative matrix difference. Their sum $S$ satisfies $PQ=QP+S$, whence $PM=MP+S$. For each $k\ge0$, the cyclic invariance of trace gives

$$
\operatorname{tr}(SM^k)=\operatorname{tr}(PM^{k+1})-\operatorname{tr}(MPM^k)=0.
$$

If $(M^k)_{ji}>0$, nonnegativity of the diagonal summands of $SM^k$ forces $S_{ij}=0$. Each individual carry difference also vanishes there. The three matrix lemmas through returning-path exactness are kernel-checked over the naturals in `CarryTrace.lean`. The underlying positive-commutator phenomenon also appears in operator theory. [Kandić and Šivic](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4988)

Every positive entry of an individual difference lies on a forward path in the digit graph, since it is bounded by the corresponding left product. It crosses strictly between strongly connected components, by returning-path exactness. Consequently any product containing at least $d$ individual carry differences in dimension $d$, interspersed with arbitrary nonnegative digit words, is zero. The same argument applies to affine natural interpretations after the usual extra-coordinate homogenization, with its augmented dimension. These two consequences have written proofs.

The left boundary can return a transient edge. For a full-system weak interpretation take $A=B=P_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, $E=F=G=Q_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)$, $C=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$, and $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$. Every carry difference is the matrix unit $J_{12}$. Direct calculation gives $P_0D=Q_0D=D$ and $CQ_0=CP_0^2\ge CP_0$. All eleven rules are weak, with all top-left entries at least one. Yet $J_{12}CJ_{12}=J_{12}$. Digit-only nilpotence therefore cannot justify adding boundary matrices to the intervening words.

## A semantic obstruction from two regular base readings

Suppose a nonnegative integer sequence $V(n)$ is both 2-regular and 3-regular, and $V(Tn)\le V(n)$ for every $n>1$. No such $V$ decreases strictly on all even inputs or on all odd inputs. Bell's Theorem 1.5 gives a rational generating function with all poles roots of unity. For some $m,e$, its denominator divides $(1-z^m)^e$. Expanding this denominator shows that $V$ is eventually a polynomial on each residue modulo $m$. Every such polynomial that is nonnegative along its unbounded progression is eventually nondecreasing there. [Bell, Theorem 1.5](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~slc/s/s54Abell.pdf)

For a sufficiently large positive multiple $k$ of $m$, replay
$$
8k-5\longmapsto12k-7\longmapsto18k-10\longmapsto9k-5.
$$
This uses both parities. Its first and last arguments are in one residue class and the last is larger. Monotonicity on that progression contradicts a marked strict step. This argument is a written proof and uses the rank sequence itself, independently of any regularity assertion about an orbit's parity language. An example to which it applies is an exact representation-invariant integer matrix reading of the AYH words. In pure ternary use the boundary prefix `c` for leading digit one or `ca` for leading digit two. In pure binary use `c` for leading digit one. Each remaining digit applies a fixed matrix, an ordinary finite linear reading in the corresponding base. Weak administrative orientation alone supplies no representational equality.

## Higher-dimensional checkpoint

For a positive polynomial zero-digit map in dimension $d$, mark the distinguished coordinate as already increasing. From the graph of occurrences on the remaining coordinates choose a maximal collection of vertex-disjoint directed cycles. All vertices outside the cycles and the distinguished coordinate induce an acyclic graph. Let $p$ be a common multiple of the selected cycle lengths and let $h$ be the number of the remaining vertices. Then $A^{h+p}(s)\ge A^h(s)$ for every positive $s$. Indeed, every selected vertex dominates its own input around its cycle after $p$ steps, and hence at every shifted time. An unmarked vertex depending only on established coordinates gains the same comparison one step later. A topological ordering of the remaining graph completes the argument.

The theorem identifies an exact requirement for this particular all-dimension approach. Finite AYH derivations covering all marked rules need compatible length gains at adequate starting depths for each admissible cycle partition. As literal checks beyond the main proof, inserting binary zero alone in 515 to 2051 gives depth seven and gain two, 259 to 2051 gives depth six and gain three, and 1042601 to 8906921 gives depth zero and gain four. Canonical normalization replays these examples in 308, 306 and 150 rewrite steps respectively, each using all eleven rules. With at most four free coordinates the cycle partition contains a four-cycle, a three-cycle with at most one remaining vertex, or only cycles of length one and two. These three examples meet those respective requirements. This is a paper-level extension through dimension five. The machine-checked headline is dimension three. No uniform family for unbounded dimension or global Collatz termination proof has been established.

A fixed finite list of nonzero gains cannot by itself enforce this single-symbol comparison at every dimension. Choose a prime larger than every listed gain. Let $A$ fix coordinate zero and cyclically permute that many auxiliary coordinates. This is a positive polynomial map preserving both orders. On a vector with one auxiliary entry two and all others one, $A^{h+p}$ and $A^h$ are incomparable for each listed gain $p$ at every starting depth $h$. This control concerns the comparison law for the digit map in isolation. Extending it to a weak interpretation of all eleven rules is a separate question.

## A written extension allowing zero coordinates in dimension three

Assume all seven maps act on the whole nonnegative integer orthant, preserve componentwise weak comparisons, and preserve comparisons strict in the first coordinate. Suppose only binary zero has nonnegative-integer polynomial coordinates. Allow its coordinate polynomials to be identically zero. The same full-rule non-removal conclusion follows in dimension three, by a longer finite derivation.

For fixed auxiliary inputs the first output of each letter is a strictly increasing function of the first natural input. Induction shows that this output is at least its first input. Hence the set on which coordinate zero is positive is invariant under every letter, even if an auxiliary output vanishes.

For the zero-letter map $A$ on that set, record which of the two auxiliary coordinates are positive. A nonnegative polynomial is positive if it has a monomial with positive coefficient all of whose variables are positive. Thus the support mask evolves by a monotone self-map of the four-element Boolean square, independently of magnitudes. Every mask enters a cycle within three steps. The points of a cycle of a monotone map on a finite partially ordered set form an antichain: a strict comparison between two points would iterate around their common cycle to give a point strictly below itself. Thus the period is one or two. A two-cycle consists of the two incomparable singleton masks.

On an invariant mask substitute zero for the permanently inactive coordinates. Each active output is a nonzero polynomial on the remaining positive orthant. Its first coordinate remains strictly monotone. The positive-coordinate theorem gives a two-step componentwise comparison after two more iterates. On a two-cycle take $A^2$ on either singleton mask. There is one free positive coordinate in addition to the first. For such a map $G$, $G^2(v)\ge G(v)$: if its free output uses that free variable it dominates the input, otherwise the free output depends only on the already increasing first coordinate. Applying the inequality on either phase gives $A^{n+2}(v)\ge A^n(v)$ at every $n\ge5$ in both the fixed-mask and alternating-mask cases. The inactive coordinates match because the masks are identical at these compared times.

The literal normalized derivation from 515 to 2051 starts at the word $c\,a^7bbd$ and ends at $c\,a^9bbd$. All eleven rules occur among its 308 rewrites. Apply the two-step comparison to the suffix configuration after $bbd$, whose first coordinate is positive if the trace is evaluated at a positive-first seed. Monotonicity of the prefix and strictness of any marked step would contradict each other as in the 41 certificate. This extension has a written proof and an exact replay, separate from the Lean theorem for the strictly positive orthant. The checker verifies the decoded physical value after every rewrite. It also enumerates the 36 monotone self-maps of the Boolean square and finds maximum transient length two. The written argument uses the weaker counting bound three.
