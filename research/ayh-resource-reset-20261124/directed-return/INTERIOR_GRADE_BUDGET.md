# A finite width budget for aligned interior head genealogies

**Status.** PROPOSED written estimates on the ordinary rows of PR 197 at `b0538639df0ab7edd239281bd897e705160f732c`. Seven finite algebraic and counting identities are isolated in `InteriorGradeBudget.lean`. The ordinary regeneration, physical seeds, track bound, and source projection keep their cited source qualifications. Neither a third-arm moment nor a fixed-target density is used here. All colors below are actual binary shortcut-invariant colors on positive integers.

Put \(R=81^j,d=\log_2R\), and use a fixed late interval \(I\subset[3d,3.1d]\) of length comparable to \(d\), optionally with a fixed interior mantissa arc \(J\subset(7,8)\). Grades are the exponents in the **same** remaining high quotient of the original coarse integer \(m\) of `crossing-step/LATE_WITNESS_TRACKS.md`. A four-trit child of grade \(g\) and its parent occupy \([g-4,g]\). Order is by these grades, not by the size of a polynomial correction. The estimates below include a four-trit conclusion at certified clocks in the final section.

1. At prescribed clocks with gaps at most \(C V\), \(p\asymp d/V\) generations, an actual path of four-trit heads may start at any permissible head. A good step means that the old pair has never tied since birth and the next entire four-grade interval is strictly inside the two old grades at the new clock. Even allowing \(c p/(\sqrt V\log d)\) completely arbitrary steps, the original mass supporting a path is \(O_A(d^{-A})\) for \(V=\lfloor d^\alpha\rfloor,0<\alpha<2/3\). The estimate holds for any one bounded prefix-stopping schedule of these lengths and for a polynomial bank of such schedules. It is meaningful even after dropping the tests of genuine colors. It does not presume that marks exist on a deterministic mesh.

2. Actual regeneration clocks involve a bounded future lookahead. There is a fixed multiple \(K\) of four for which a related conclusion holds on **certified regenerated chains** at these clocks. A \(K\)-grade edge spans \(K/4\) consecutive actual four-trit edges of one such chain, with its endpoints on a prescribed aligned sublattice. A genuinely discordant \(K\)-edge contains at least one genuinely discordant constituent. For \(V=d^\alpha,0<\alpha<2/3\), or \(V=\lceil 4(\log d)^6\rceil\), outside \(O_A(Rd^{-A})\) original children every good discordant source admits an array of \(\asymp d/V\) certified clocks of gaps \(\asymp V\) with the following property. Choose any truly discordant aligned \(K\)-edge at every clock that supplies one. For **every** such choice, at least
\[
                       c\,d/(V^{3/2}\log d)
\tag{1}
\]
adjacent pairs of array clocks both supply chosen true edges and violate at least one of the following conditions. The earlier edge keeps its strict birth order up to the next comparison, without a grade tie. The later entire grade interval lies inside the old interval with margin at least \(H+1\) at both ends. Here \(H\) is a fixed finite detection lookahead. For polylogarithmic \(V\), (1) is \(\gg d/(\log d)^{10}\). This estimates a multi-time configuration on the actual original sources. A two-class partition at a distant deadline need not satisfy the two conditions; in particular the earlier edge may touch a tie soon after birth and reopen in the same order.

## One source coordinate and one child per grade

Fix the original suffix and discretize the original parent-height interval into a fixed number of bins of sufficiently small *relative* width if a mantissa test is needed. At the initial clock of \(I\), list both original physical arm seeds of `unit-exit/LOCAL_COLOR_INFORMATION.md`, section “One-bit-lag physical transport”. The joint list has polynomial cardinality. For a fixed seed, the current larger-grade arm is a publicly tracked odd-affine form \(Y=3^G q+c\) after the low \(\Delta_t=t-\lfloor2.9d\rfloor\) bits of \(m\) have been exposed, with a fixed tie rule. For \(0\le u\le G\), the trit truncation is exactly \(3^{G-u}q+\lfloor c/3^u\rfloor\). Thus at a specified child grade there is **one** cut of this arm and its four-trit parent is the next exact cut four grades below. All actual regenerated chains on the good sample use the larger-grade arm, without a tie at a true regeneration mark. The seed is enumerated once for the whole tree. No independent bounded-rounding factor is paid at successive births.

At a fixed clock every genuine candidate in the retained row and arc has the same child grade residue modulo four. Indeed write its height as \(z=3^k2^{-t}n+O_A(1)\), \(7\cdot81^l\le z<8\cdot81^l\), on the original parent interval of endpoint ratio \(8/7+o(1)\). All the integers \(k-4l\) lie in an interval of length at most \(2\log_3(8/7)+o(1)<1\), since the children are exponentially large. Subtract the common integer coarse shift from \(k\) to get the child grade. This accounts for both floor cells and parent truncations. We can prescribe the residue at each clock in advance, with any default when the candidate list is empty. For the upper tree all cuts in that residue and in the polynomial original grade envelope may be retained. At a truly certified chain all consecutive cuts have precisely that residue. For aligned \(K\)-blocks choose one subclass modulo \(K\) of its cut *vertices*, using the same prescription at that clock for all parents.

All finite probability estimates below use a single complete uniform binary grid for \(m\), at depth below \(.31d\), for each suffix, bin and seed. The ordinary good sample is contained in these lists; the original projection (4) of `LATE_WITNESS_TRACKS.md` bounds a full grid event by one absolute comparison factor. Ignoring a bin or inconsistent seed on the enlarged grid causes only upper counting. Failed original envelopes can be chosen to cost \(O_A(Rd^{-A})\).

## Fair stopped width and strict packing

At birth of an ordered interval of width \(k\), run both native rays. Keep the signed higher-minus-lower grade as long as it is positive and pay zero forever at its first tie. This integer \(S\) starts at \(k\). Given a prefix, the parity of each odd-affine ray equals the next bit or its complement. Whether the two parities match is known *before* that bit. Equal parities give a zero grade increment. Opposite parities give \(+1,-1\) equiprobably. Consequently \(S\) is a nonnegative martingale, with bounded increments. At a tie the width is exactly zero. On any bounded stopping prefix after birth its conditional expected value is exactly \(k\); this also follows by adding the two child widths at each node of the finite grid, with the complete lift multiplicity for stopped nodes.

From any positive entering width, the probability that \(S\) is still positive at **every** one of the next \(n\) clocks is at least \(c/\sqrt{n+1}\), uniformly in the prefix. Generate the uniform grid using independent fair coin streams for active and idle steps. At each step the prefix already says which stream to use; at an active step use its next coin as the desired grade-increment sign and invert the known parity convention to obtain the original bit. At an idle step use an unused idle coin as the bit. This constructs fair bits by conditional iteration, while the sequence of available active signs is a full independent simple walk even if only a random initial segment is used. If its first \(n\) partial sums are all nonnegative, the killed process starting at any positive integer cannot tie by clock \(n\). The reflection count for this event is \(2^{-n}\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\gg 1/\sqrt{n+1}\): the nonnegative paths ending at \(b\ge0\) number \(\binom n{(n+b)/2}-\binom n{(n+b)/2+1}\), summed over endpoints of the right parity.

For width four at a prescribed next stopping clock, admissible strictly interior child intervals are disjoint except for possible common endpoints because their lower grades have one residue modulo four. When \(S>0\) their number is at most \((S-1)/4\), including the case of an empty list. When \(S=0\) give no offspring. For a gap at most \(CV\), conditioning at the old birth therefore gives expected offspring at most \(1-c/\sqrt V\). This proves the single-generation bound for the first assertion.

## Releasing a certified choice after bounded future lookahead

A certified regeneration time \(t\) can be detected by \(t+H\), uniformly in \(d\), on the original grid once the bin and seeds have been listed. At \(t\) form the up to four truncation chains of the larger original arm down to the smaller original grade as in `LOCAL_COLOR_INFORMATION.md`. Require the public effective exponent/correction bounds of that note, and require the candidate children to be certainly inside the chosen arc throughout the original height bin. This last test uses their monotone affine descriptions on the coarse-height interval corresponding to the bin. The bins can be made fixed and narrow enough to pass all marked children of a compactly interior subarc, because their heights are exponential and the relative variation from a bin is arbitrarily small. Require the top cut to meet the larger arm symbolically by the fixed regeneration horizon, and the bottom cut to meet the smaller arm by its fixed 13-step certificate. Both tests use only the next \(H\) bits, with \(H\) enlarged to include 13 and the upper certificate. The ordinary arithmetic and success frequencies in `two-class-descent/ROUNDED_HEAD_DESCENT.md`, `HEAD_INCIDENCE.md` and `LATE_WITNESS_TRACKS.md` guarantee at least one such certificate in each \(Q=\lceil(\log d)^6\rceil\)-clock interval of the good discordant sample, using the compactly interior subarc with its margin. At a genuine source every clock passing this check supplies a chain with opposite endpoint colors by the two ordinary meanings of its symbolic meetings. Additional certified clocks can be kept. The arc forces a single eligible initial trit residue. Require a nonempty chain of four-trit edges. All growth envelopes invoked here are public interval or affine-record tests; no oracle for the actual colors occurs in the schedule.

Choose \(K=4q>H\) fixed. Starting at the released clock \(\sigma=t+H\), a newly born \(K\)-edge has signed width in \([K-H,K+H]\) and it could not have tied during the lookahead. At the next detected time \(t'\), take the contiguous run of all aligned \(K\)-blocks of its certified chain contained in the old open chamber with margins at least \(H+1\). If the old chamber has previously tied, set the run empty. Endpoints of adjacent blocks are literally the same trit cut. At release \(\sigma'=t'+H\), the signed width of each is still positive. Their **sum** telescopes to the terminal grade of the top cut minus that of the bottom cut. If the run is nonempty, this is at most
\[
  S_{t'}-2(H+1)+H \le S_{\sigma'}-2 .
\]
Here the old interval cannot tie from \(t'\) to \(\sigma'\) if the run is nonempty: its width at \(t'\) exceeds \(2H\). Both the old width and the difference of the two outer cut grades change by at most one per bit. If the run is empty its released mass is zero. Thus for every grid outcome the total good released child width is at most \(S_{\sigma'}-\mathbf1_{S_{\sigma'}>0}\). This bound also holds if no certificate appears in a bounded search window and a dummy release is prescribed with no good offspring.

The time that a bounded search detects its first certificate is a stopping time at **release**, since every comparison time is tested at its fixed offset \(H\). If successive release lags are at most \(CV\), the preceding fair-width identities give conditional expected good offspring mass at most \(S_\sigma-c/\sqrt V\), hence at most \((1-c'/\sqrt V)S_\sigma\) since \(S_\sigma\le K+H\). Conditioning is at the release, when the child widths are known and can differ. Individual births have **no** claimed fresh law at their earlier, selected comparison times. Positivity of the individual released widths is why the bounded-lookahead result uses aligned blocks of width larger than \(H\).

## Finite tree estimate and arbitrary resets

Count all paths with their multiplicities, allowing different earlier paths to lead to the same cut. For either construction the initial number or total width is polynomial, and good-step expected mass contracts by \(q_V=1-c/\sqrt V\) conditionally on the registered prefix. A reset may jump to any permissible current cut or block, irrespective of grades, past ties or color. Their number is polynomial in \(d\), and each released mass is bounded by a constant. Include one dummy of weight one at each clock, with no good transitions to or from it. Thus a reset multiplies expected mass by at most \(d^{C_1}\). For a specified set of allowed reset indices, induction using conditional expectation bounds the expected leaf mass; there is no independence assumption across siblings. Each live path contributes at least one unit. With at most \(b\) resets the total original probability is bounded by
\[
 d^{C_2}\sum_{i=0}^{b}\binom pi d^{C_1 i}q_V^{p-i}
       \le d^{C_3}\exp(-c_1p/\sqrt V)
\tag{2}
\]
for \(b=\lfloor c_2p/(\sqrt V\log d)\rfloor\), taking \(c_2\) small. This includes the seed and starting-anchor bank. Since \(p/\sqrt V\asymp d/V^{3/2}\gg\log d\) in the stated ranges, it beats every fixed power. In the first assertion each node may also choose its next stopping clock by its own prescribed prefix rule; the conditional induction applies node by node. Formula (2) establishes the assertion on four-trit meshes. A retrospective union over exponentially many schedules at full cost is not implied.

## Many genuine block comparisons on one source

Split \(I\) into \(p+1\asymp d/V\) consecutive windows of length \(4V\), dropping the remainder. Independently of the grid choose a start uniformly among the first \(V\) clocks in each window. Search for the first certificate in the next \(Q\le V\) clocks, and release after \(H\). If it fails, release a dummy at the end of that search plus \(H\). Successive search and release intervals are ordered with gaps \(\asymp V\), and all fall within the coarse grid budget. For each fixed vector of starts, (2) applies. Average over starts rather than taking a union.

At any certificate where the two original colors differ, the aligned full \(K\)-blocks partition the chain except for initial and terminal pieces of fewer than \(K\) grades. If all full blocks have equal endpoint colors, the telescoping change in the whole chain implies a truly discordant four-trit edge in one of the two remainders. Its grade is within \(K+8\) of one original physical endpoint grade, including the initial zero-to-three trit alignment and the final four-trit remainder. This statement also applies to a chain with no full block. The same original endpoint grades form predictable tracks on the polynomial physical seed bank. Apply the proof of `LATE_WITNESS_TRACKS.md` (1), which bounds every genuinely discordant candidate on its prescribed bounded-correction menu, including extra certified clocks. Outside the \(O_A(Rd^{-A})\) exception there are at most \(C_{A,K}\sqrt{d\log d}\) such clocks in \(I\).

For a fixed good discordant source, any particular one of these boundary clocks can be the first certificate for at most \(Q\) possible starts. Consequently the expected number of selected clocks with no true aligned block, **over the independent choice of starts**, is at most \(C_{A,K}(Q/V)\sqrt{d\log d}\). This is \(o(b)\), because \(Q(\log d)^{3/2}\sqrt{V/d}\to0\) for both choices of \(V\). Markov gives probability \(1-o(1)\) of fewer than \(b/8\) missing true blocks on this very source. Conversely (2), Fubini, and Markov show that outside a further superpolynomially small original mass, a random vector of starts has probability at least \(3/4\) of admitting **no** path with at most \(b\) resets. Therefore some same vector has both properties. Select its actual true blocks, using dummies at the missing positions. Every chosen path has more than \(b\) failed transitions, of which at most \(b/4\) touch a dummy. This proves (1), without choosing a fresh law after any color query or paying the number of retrospective clock arrays.

**Remaining issue.** A long two-class configuration can regenerate a different local interface after an early grade tie. It can also meet a chamber boundary at a short lag while staying order-preserving at a distant deadline. A genuine constituent four-trit edge is guaranteed inside a discordant aligned block; a given four-trit choice need not induce a discordant aligned block containing that particular edge. The delayed-tie estimate in the companion note removes a positive clock density of ties in a later age range, not the early obstruction or a global color class. The full ordinary disagreement series is still open.

## The lookahead obstruction on actual four-trit cuts

There is also a version of the certified-clock theorem on the **original four-trit edges themselves**, without grouping or replacing the chosen witnesses. Fix the starts deterministically in the windows above, for example the first clock of every window. At each detected certificate consider its full genuine truncation chain, and at each comparison take the contiguous run of its four-trit intervals with margins at least \(H+1\) inside the old chamber. At release, let \(g'_0,\ldots,g'_s\) be the grades of their shared cut vertices in their *original* increasing cut order. Define the observable integer
\[
           F=\sum_{i<s}(g'_i-g'_{i+1})_+,\qquad x_+=\max(x,0).
\]
This measures total downward grade change across the released cuts. Empty runs have \(F=0\). On all good discordant originals outside \(O_A(Rd^{-A})\), every choice of one truly discordant four-trit edge at each certified clock has at least the number (1) of adjacent transitions satisfying at least one of the following:

* the chosen older pair has tied since its birth by the comparison time, or the younger pair ties during its own \(H\)-clock certification window;
* the younger edge does not fit into the old interval with margins at least \(H+1\);
* the released interior run has \(F\ge2\).

This holds for a polynomial bank of fixed schedules by enlarging the exponent in (2). On the good original sample the indicated certified clocks all exist and all their chains contain a true four-trit edge. An inversion contributing to \(F\) is on an actual ordinary truncation pair in the same original physical chain; its colors need not differ. The estimate thus detects a spatial fold even if an observer looking at just the chosen four rays sees order preserved at a later deadline.

Indeed, discard any candidate child that has already tied by its release. Each remaining signed width is positive and at most \(4+H\). Their sum is at most the sum of the positive parts of **all** signed widths in the interior run, hence is bounded by the telescoping endpoint difference plus \(F\). For a nonempty run the telescoping endpoint difference is at most \(S_{\sigma'}-2\) by precisely the old margin calculation, regardless of inversions. If \(F\le1\) this leaves a loss of at least one. If \(F\ge2\) permit no good children at this transition. An old pair already tied by its own release can be entered in the arbitrary reset menu with unit weight and no good offspring. Otherwise the fair-width survival bound at its registered positive width is uniform as before. Empty runs and old ties contribute zero offspring. This proves the same one-step contraction and the same reset bound (2) with true four-trit choices at every detected clock. The only extra failure compared with the wider blocks is measured explicitly by \(F\) and the newborn tie before release. Subdivision into the wider blocks removes the possible early newborn tie and forces every released block width positive, which explains the separate unconditional block statement.
