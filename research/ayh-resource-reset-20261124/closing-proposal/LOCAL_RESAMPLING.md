# Actual opposite replies after changing a logarithmic block of coarse bits

This is a written consequence of PR197's source-qualified native merger and ordinary regeneration statements P and R. It gives a necessary condition for a selected population of true basin disagreements. It does not exclude such populations.

## Statement

Use the source data, late slab of length \(W=\lceil\sqrt{d\log d}\rceil\), common coarse coordinate \(m\), and two physical seeds of SELECTED_TRIT_CELLS.md at commit 7ad81ec56947146fac854d2586651ca0c934a536. Fix \(P_0,A>0\) and a selected set \(S\) of good truly discordant parents, of row fraction \(w\ge d^{-P_0}\). One may prescribe the original orientation. Choose \(u\ge0\) with \(u+\log d=o(W)\) and give the ternary advice \(m\bmod3^u\). Fix also a power \(J>0\). Put \(g=\lceil K(\log d+u+1)\rceil\), where the constant \(K\) may depend on the fixed source and error parameters and on \(J\).

Except for \(O_A(d^{-A})\) of \(S\), every original has at least \(cW\) different genuine regeneration clocks at which a reached physical trit query has the following property. Both replies occur in \(S\). Here the two branches reach the same adaptive query formula and ordinal, after identical earlier replies at that clock. They evaluate that formula at different ordinary arguments and obtain opposite colors. They share the native residue, seeds, ternary advice, every preceding coarse bit, and every coarse bit strictly above a specified cutoff only \(g\) to \(2g\) bits ahead. Thus the upper and lower bits outside a single short contiguous block are the same for both branches. At each such split the differences between the ordinary arguments have the form \(3^{e+u}v\) with \(|v|<2^{2g+1}/3^u\), using the actual public exponent \(e\ge0\) of the common query. Both branches have at least \(d^J\) distinct ordinary arguments. More precisely, each has at least \(w d^{-C_A}2^r/3^u\) of them on a lattice interval of at most \(2^r/3^u+1\) candidate sites, where \(g\le r\le 2g\).

For \(u=O(\log d)\) these sites occupy only polynomially many consecutive indices in the actual query lattice. Their ordinary spacing is \(3^{e+u}\). In particular this does not imply a polynomial *ordinary* diameter. The alternative sources and the short block may change from one clock to the next. No low-bit randomness conditioned on \(S\), its replies, or its ternary advice is used.

## Proof

Write \(m=\ell+2^{\Delta_t}q\) at clock \(t\), with the notation of SELECTED_TRIT_CELLS.md. Its shared source cap \(A_*\) bounds the number of parents at a specified native residue and a specified \(m\). The actual arguments of each shared query are \(3^e q+c_\ell\). On the original full exterior cell the physical program's admissibility checks are fixed and the two seeds and exposed low bits determine its history. Retain those checks. Giving an additional exterior quotient is allowed without changing the query program.

Tile the slab by full clock blocks of length \(g\), dropping fewer than \(g\) final clocks. For a block \([t_*,t_*+g)\) give its observer the entire quotient \(\lfloor m/2^L\rfloor\), \(L=\Delta_{t_*}+2g\), its two seeds, the native residue, and the value of the ternary advice. The full-slab construction has \(W\) reserve bits, so this cutoff and every fixed test horizon fit for large \(d\). At a clock of the block the unexposed middle length \(r=L-\Delta_t\) lies between \(g\) and \(2g\). At each clock simulate the modal reply path on the actual sources of \(S\) sharing the native residue, seeds, advice, added exterior, and previously exposed low bits. Within this one clock also condition on the earlier modal replies. The policy is a deterministic function of these quantities. An empty prefix uses a dummy.

On the unrestricted binary middle cube, with the advice and seeds fixed as *parameters*, PR197's finite nonmerger test for the selected head passes with conditional probability at most \(p_0=C_0/\sqrt h\) for fixed sufficiently large \(h\). The source applies to the odd-affine parent, with fixed bounded suffix. Split the clocks into their \(h\) classes to expose disjoint \(h\)-bit tests in each class. The conditional exponential-moment bound from SELECTED_TRIT_CELLS.md gives probability \(O(e^{-\lambda g})\) that the predictable policy passes at \(\eta g/2\) or more clocks in this block, where \(\eta\) is its whole-slab regeneration frequency. The statement is uniform in the previously exposed prefix. For each native residue the number of possible exterior quotients is \(O(c_0N/2^L)\), where \(N\) is the row population and \(m=\lfloor c_0n\rfloor\). Consequently counting cube points and multiplying by the cap \(A_*\) cancels the exterior scale \(2^L\), just as in the original proof. The polynomial number of seeds, \(3^u\) advice values, and at most \(d\) blocks cost at most \(C d^C3^u e^{-\lambda g}/w\) of \(S\). Choosing \(K\) sufficiently large makes this \(O_A(d^{-A})\).

Every retained individual now has fewer than \(\eta g/2\) predictable passes in each block, while R on the **whole slab** supplies at least \(\eta W\) actual marked clocks. The omitted block has fewer than \(g=o(W)\) clocks. If the real path agrees with every modal response at a marked clock, the modal procedure returns the same true head and passes its finite nonmerger test. Hence at least \(\eta W/2-O(g)\) marked clocks have a first actual dissent. This needs no per-microblock regeneration assertion.

For completeness, retain the ordinary-site multiplicities. At a fixed clock refine each public cell by its entire actual reply word, including length. There are at most \(d^C\) words. Delete a source in any such cell containing fewer than \(\epsilon A_*2^r/3^u\) parents, with \(\epsilon=w d^{-C_A}\). There are at most
\[
 2^{a_0}\,O(c_0 N/2^L)\,d^C\,3^u\,2^{\Delta_t}\,d^C
\]
cells. Since \(r=L-\Delta_t\) and \(2^{a_0}c_0 A_*=O(1)\), the union cost over the slab is \(O_A(d^{-A})\) of \(S\) on enlarging \(C_A\). Use this removal to test the actual individual's refined cell; the original modal policies still use all of \(S\). At first dissent the dissenting branch in \(S\) has at least this mass and the modal branch is at least as large. Divide by \(A_*\) and use injectivity of \(q\mapsto3^eq+c_\ell\). Fixing the earlier bits and \(m\bmod3^u\) fixes one residue of \(q\bmod3^u\). Its candidates in the exterior cell form at most \(2^r/3^u+1\) consecutive lattice sites. Increasing \(K\) gives the claimed \(d^J\) sites, since \(r\ge g\), \(w\ge d^{-P_0}\) and \(g\gg\log d+u\). This proves all the quantifiers.

The selected-mass lower bound takes the form \(\Omega(wW)\) after averaging over the original row and summing over clocks. In contrast, the rank, count or density of a single query need not control the adaptive choice of the first differing query. PR146's pair tail on a complete binary grid concerns a preset slope and shift. Its relative bound on a length-\(M\) lattice of odd stride \(3^v\) is \(O(\min(1,\sqrt{(1+v)/\log(2M)}))\) for adjacent lattice sites, by the permutation of residues modulo \(2^h\). At a microblock here \(M\) can be polynomial in \(d\) while \(v=e+u\) can be of order \(W\). This estimate then allows every site to change. Neither that tail nor conditional positive density for a fixed inverse target proves an opposing actual-source upper estimate.

## A finite test with no basin oracle

For any proposed finite horizon \(h(d)\), use **all** positive ordinary sites in the candidate lattice interval at a potential query. Join two sites when their shortcut orbits intersect at some two (possibly different) clocks at most \(h(d)\). Take the connected components of this finite graph. A fixed invariant color is constant on every graph edge and hence on every component. The two branches above therefore imply that **each** graph component omits at least \(d^J\) distinct candidate sites. This applies at every horizon, with the same genuine branches. The finite proof, including the ordinary stride formula, is in LocalResampling.lean.

For implementation, fix integer timing schedules, grid cutoffs and advice depths satisfying the displayed bounds and fix a positive rational clock fraction below the proved constant. Rational or integer choices can be hardcoded at these one-sided bounds. Count a clock only when its candidate set is nonempty, round the required number of omitted sites up to an integer, and round the required number of clocks up as well. For \(J=1\) one may equivalently use the integer threshold \(\lfloor d\rfloor\), which weakens the test by less than one site. These conventions specify a finite arithmetic scan for each row and each supplied integer horizon; they do not presume an algorithm to recover arbitrary real constants from asymptotic estimates.

For a fixed suffix let \(B_d(h)\) be the fraction of all row parents having at least \(cW\) clocks of the stated slab at which *some* arithmetically possible physical query has this graph property. To compute a finite row, one can enumerate the actual two arms, their seeds and exterior data, and all formal binary reply paths of the bounded physical query program. Throw away dummies and use every remaining candidate query, without asking whether its replies are true. Computing bounded ordinary prefixes and connected components also requires no basin oracle. Including extra arithmetically permitted queries only increases this statistic.

The theorem gives \(B_d(h)\ge(1-O_A(d^{-A}))w\) for every such row and horizon. Under the separately pinned fixed-target density input M, the earlier divergence reduction supplies a fixed suffix and infinitely many rows with good truly discordant mass \(w\ge d^{-3}\) under a counterexample (absorb fixed constants into this exponent). For instance take \(P_0=3\), \(u=0\), and \(J=1\) in the present theorem. Thus the following missing estimate is sufficient, subject to the stated inputs: exhibit finite horizons and prove \(B_d(h)=o(d^{-3})\) on all sufficiently large rows of each of the 81 suffixes. This is a finite arithmetic statistic on each row. No such asymptotic estimate is established here. A finite-graph failure can also result from late merging of two ordinary convergent orbits.

### A lower limit on the graph horizon

There is an elementary reason a short clock bound gives no useful edges. Suppose the candidate sites lie in \([Z,Z+L]\), with common spacing \(S_0\). If \(Z>(L+2)6^H\) and \(S_0\ge2^H\), every connected component of the horizon-\(H\) graph is a singleton. To see this, suppose \(x\) and \(x+\delta\), \(0<\delta\le L\), meet after \(i,j\le H\) steps. With odd counts \(p,q\) and the standard integer affine numerators \(0\le B_i<3^i\), \(0\le B'_j<3^j\), this requires
\[
 (2^j3^p-2^i3^q)x=2^i3^q\delta+2^iB'_j-2^jB_i.
\]
The right side has absolute value below \((L+2)6^H<x\). The integer coefficient on the left must vanish, so unique factorization gives \(i=j,p=q\). If the odd bits of the first word are at positions \(0\le s_1<\cdots<s_p<i\), then \(B_i/3^p=\sum_{l=1}^p2^{s_l}/3^l<2^i\), and likewise for the other word. Equal terminal values imply \(\delta=|B_i-B'_i|/3^p<2^i\), whereas distinct lattice sites have \(\delta\ge S_0\ge2^H\).

For the PR197 rebase one can take the constant in the lead time \(t_*-t_0\asymp W\) sufficiently large. All the actual permitted trit exponents between the two physical grades then satisfy \(e\ge cW\) on the stated good envelope, including bounded endpoint corrections. Their heights are exponential in \(d\) whereas the physical diameter is \(2^{O(W)}\). The two elementary inequalities above hold throughout such windows at every \(H=o(W)\). This argument compares finite ordinary trajectories only. It gives no evidence that the individual sites have different eventual colors.
