Use the positive shortcut map $T(2n)=n, T(2n+1)=3n+2$. For a clock $h$, write $p_h(x)$ for the number of odd departures, and set $L_h(x)=(T^hx,p_h(x))$. On a finite set of sites put an edge between any pair meeting at clocks $i,j\le h$, allowing the clocks to differ. The deficiency is the total number of sites minus the size of the largest connected component. For a partition by labels use the same definition with its largest fibre. Assign deficiency zero on an empty set.

## Finite theorem on one collection of sites

At any nonnegative bases $b_1,\ldots,b_m$, lift each site to $2^hK+b_v$, using the **same** nonnegative integer $K$. For $0\le i\le h$, induction on the actual steps gives

$$
 T^i(2^hK+b_v)=2^{h-i}3^{p_i(b_v)}K+T^i b_v.                                      \tag{1}
$$

1. Each fibre of $v\mapsto L_h(b_v)$ lies in a single connected component on the lifts, for every $K$. The two lifts actually coincide at clock $h$. This also holds on *any* subset of the sites, since such a pair has its own edge. In particular, if the actual deficiency on a subset is at least $D$, the labelled deficiency on the full collection is at least $D$. Adding a vertex to a partition increases the largest class by at most one.
2. There is an explicit list of at most $m^2(h+1)^2$ exceptional integers $K$. Away from that list, the connected components on **every subset** are exactly the fibres of $L_h$ on that subset. To form the list, take all $v,w,i,j$. If the two coefficients of $K$ in (1) differ, at most one integer can make the two expressions equal. Overlisting the truncated quotient of intercept and slope differences, including impossible, zero and nonintegral crossings, is harmless. For equal slopes, their 2-adic valuations give $i=j$, then their powers of three give $p_i(b_v)=p_i(b_w)$. Equality of the intercepts gives the same base future and therefore the same full label at $h$. Labels agree along a chain of such edges. This establishes both directions. If $K$ runs through multiples of a fixed positive stride, the same count bounds the number of exceptional quotients.
3. These assertions do not depend on a large symbolic prefix being typical. For any two positive translates of the same lattice differing by an integral multiple of $2^h$ in its index, the **partition** by $L_h$ is identical. To see this, apply (1) at clock $h$ to the larger translate, with $K$ the lattice stride times the index displacement divided by $2^h$. The odd grade is unchanged since the first $h$ parities depend on the residue modulo $2^h$. Within the same grade all terminal values shift by the same amount. Thus one may choose arbitrary positive base representatives, including when a query formula has a signed constant.

[LiftedLattice.lean](LiftedLattice.lean) checks (1), the universal inclusion, the finite exception cardinality, exact clean partition and the coarse occupancy count below. Its graph is precisely the one from PR197's LocalResampling module. The asymptotic and source-specific applications here are written proofs.

## What a prescribed pair actually buys

PR146, **f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437**, `research/upper-bit-mergers/GROWING_ADDITIVE_GAPS.md`, gives symbolic coalescence for a fixed additive displacement δ on a complete native grid. Its nonabsorption bound is $O((1+\sqrt{\log(2+|\delta|)})/\sqrt h)$. Absorption identifies both terminal values and odd grades. For a prescribed query formula, low prefix and fixed two offsets separated by $j$ indices the physical gap is $Sj$. Sampling the *starting index* uniformly modulo $2^h$ yields a full binary grid of first-argument residues because $S$ is odd. When sampling an aligned block, take the mean over all of its site pairs **before** summing over full-grid quotients. For each fixed $1\le j<M$, allowing all starting indices only enlarges the number of failed pairs (including those crossing a block edge), so alignment costs at most an absolute factor in the mean over at most $M-1$ shifts. The average fraction of ordered pairs in different label classes is thus
$$
 O\!\left(\min\{1,(1+\sqrt{\log(2+SM)})/\sqrt h\}\right).
$$
If class proportions are $p_i$, then $1-\max_i p_i\le1-\sum_i p_i^2$, hence the same bound holds for mean *fractional* labelled deficiency. For $S=3^{O(W)}, M=d^{O(1)}, h=\lfloor d/3\rfloor$, this is $O((\log d/d)^{1/4})$. Applying a test only within the $O(\log d)$ bits of one selected microblock previously allowed the trivial bound throughout this stride range. The improvement is a preset-menu average at available native precision. The threshold for graph omission can be only $d^J/M$, the mark may be chosen adaptively, and the selected mass can be as small as $d^{-3}$. The displayed marginal gives no required joint bound in (2).

**Every odd stride also has a lower marginal.** At a fixed prescribed full aligned window of size $M=2^r$ with $1\le r\le h$, average over its translations through all $2^{h-r}$ index blocks. The mean fractional labelled deficiency is at least $c\sqrt{r/h}$, with an absolute $c>0$ and every odd stride $S$. The positive-level walk and reflection input used here is PR146's proof of sharpness, not an independence assertion about the later adaptive queries.

To see it, choose two indices independently and uniformly from the same block. At clock $r$ the two grades are independent binomial $(r,1/2)$ random variables over those two index choices, because the $M$ initial values run once through every residue modulo $2^r$ and parity coding is bijective. For the grade difference $Y$ one has $\mathbb E Y^2=r/2$ and $\mathbb E Y^4=(3r^2-r)/4$. Hence a fraction at least $1/12$ of ordered index pairs have $|Y|\ge\sqrt r/2$. Fix such a pair. Its grade gap is the positive level of the oriented synchronous chain at clock $r$; earlier symbolic absorption is impossible. Varying the block index now supplies all $h-r$ remaining bits: its multiplier in the lower-grade endpoint after $r$ steps is $S3^{p_{\min}}$, an odd integer. PR146's fixed-state positive-level argument bounds the chance to stay above zero for the remaining physical steps below by $c_1\min\{1,|Y|/\sqrt{h-r+1}\}$. Indeed at jumps the signs are fair and fresh, and supplying unused signs for holding times shows that survival for all $h-r$ jumps suffices. The standard reflected simple-walk estimate applies also with zero remaining steps. The labels are different while the level is positive. Finally $1-\sum_i p_i^2\le 2(1-\max_i p_i)$ converts the mean count of pairs in different classes into the asserted lower marginal. This lower holds for the complete-grid statistic before imposing the original seed selection.

In particular, if $D/M=o(\sqrt{r/h})$, the probability that one such window has absolute labelled deficiency at least $D$ is $\gg\sqrt{r/h}$, using boundedness of its fractional deficiency by one. For the PR197 choice $h=\lfloor d/3\rfloor$, $r\asymp\log d$ and a large enough microblock constant that $d^J/M=o(\sqrt{\log d/d})$, this excludes an $o(d^{-3})$ bound on even a *prescribed single marginal* for every odd stride. A positive fraction of marked clocks for one parent is a different condition. This lower does not force the joint event at its required positive clock fraction.

Multiple clocks do not automatically improve this rate. ## Worst translated windows

For any $H,M,S\ge1$, take the bases $S,2S,\ldots,MS$ and $K>2^H SM$. All their base intercepts at clocks $i\le H$ are $<K$ by $T^i x\le2^ix$. In (1) division by $K$ now forces equality of coefficients and intercepts at any meeting. Hence exact equality with the labelled partition holds for this translate with no list search. For every $x>0,j>0$, $L_H(x)\ne L_H(2^jx)$. If $H\le j$, equality would force an all-even run from both starts and the same endpoint, contradicting distinct sources. If $H\ge j$, the larger base spends its first $j$ steps halving into $x$; equality would force a $j$-step loop from $T^{H-j}x>0$ with zero odd departures, which is impossible. Thus each component contains at most one index per family $o,2o,4o,\ldots$ for odd $o\le M$: every component has at most $\lceil M/2\rceil$ sites. This applies to arbitrarily high ordinary translates for each horizon and each stride (even $S=1$), not to one fixed translate uniformly for all future clocks. It refutes a uniform giant-component assertion over unrestricted origins.

A narrow source of this obstruction is negligible on the actual grids in (2). If two candidate sites on one affine lattice in an index window of width $M$ have base offsets $x,2^jx$ from *some* common multiple of $2^h$, then for their two index offsets $i,k$,
$$
  2^j(S(q+i)+c)-(S(q+k)+c)\equiv0\pmod{2^h}.
$$
For fixed $i,k,j>0$, the coefficient $(2^j-1)S$ of $q$ is odd, giving at most one residue. All $j\ge h$ give the same test. Taking offsets relative to the actual $q$ with absolute values $<M$ costs at most $(2M+1)^2(h+1)$ residues. This bound is uniform in $S=3^e,c$. Sum over the allowed fixed formulas and low-prefix fibres before multiplying by $A_*$. At $h=\lfloor d/3\rfloor$ the total original proportion is exponentially small, including the incomplete-period error. The explicit examples therefore do not by themselves supply the selected many-clock deficiency.
