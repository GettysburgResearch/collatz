# Returning a high-grade physical pair to smaller genuine head edges

On an actual row $7R\le x=81m+r<8R$, $R=81^j$, let $d=\log_2R$. Use any orbit-invariant color $f$ (in particular the basin of one). An edge is discordant if the colors at its ends differ. The following is a descent for the remaining high-grade branch. All probability exceptions count original children and are independent of the choice of $f$.

**Theorem.** For each $A>1$ there are constants $C_0,C_1,D$ such that, apart from $O_A(Rd^{-A})$ children, the following holds simultaneously. Put $M=d^D$, $t_0=\min(\tau_M(x),\tau_M(m))$, and $b=\lceil C_0\log_2d\rceil$. For every integer $h$ with $h+b\le t_0$ and $s_h\ge4b$, there is a clock $t\in[h,h+b)$ with the following properties. Orient the original arms there as $X,Y=3^s(X-W)$, with $Y$ of larger affine grade.

1. For some $k\in\{0,1,2,3\}$, $Z_0=\lfloor Y/3^k\rfloor$ is a genuine head in $[7\cdot81^l,8\cdot81^l)$ and its orbit meets the orbit of $Y$.
2. For $q=\lfloor(s-k)/4\rfloor$, put $Z_a=\lfloor Z_0/81^a\rfloor$, $0\le a\le q$. Each $Z_a$ is a genuine head. The orbit of $Z_q$ meets that of $X$ at clock 13 on both arms.
3. If the original two colors differ, at least one of the actual four-trit edges $(Z_a,Z_{a+1})$, $a<q$, is discordant. Its existence follows from meetings and transitivity of equality of colors. Its location is between the two physical scales at time $t$: $Z_q\ge X/2$ and $Z_0\le Y$.

A concrete consequence uses an intermediate floor $P=2^{\theta\sqrt d(\log d)^9}$, $1\le\theta\le2$, and $t_* =\min(\tau_P(x),\tau_P(m))$. Whenever $s_{t_*}\ge5b$ and the edge is discordant, the returned discordant head has child height in

$$P/d^{C_1}\ \le Z_a\le P\,2^{C_1\sqrt{d\log d}}.\tag{1}$$

This is a strict descent from the original row for large $d$. The constants and the absolute exception are uniform in $\theta$. The low-grade coarea result of the companion work removes a summable original discordant mass for almost every one common $\theta$ under its prescribed small-gap bound. The present conclusion is a pathwise smaller witness for the large-gap branch, with no bound here on the multiplicity assigned to an individual smaller witness.

## Arithmetic at the chosen clock

If $|W-1/2|<1/100$ then for $i=0,1,2,3$,

$$\lfloor3^i(X-W)\rfloor=3^i X-(3^i+1)/2.$$

These four integers are $X-1,3X-2,9X-5,27X-14$. On $X=8192u+2242$, all four and $X$ have the common clock-13 endpoint $729u+200$. This is the kernel-checked `RoundedHeadDescent.lean`, using the ordinary affine lift; the five parity words are respectively `1010100101100`, `0010100101100`, `1001001000001`, `0001001000001` and `0101011100100` for $X$ itself. For $i=s-k-4q$ the formula gives precisely $Z_q$.

We arrange that the mantissa of $Y$ belongs to a compact subinterval of $(7\cdot3^e,8\cdot3^e)$ for an integer $e\ge0$. Choosing $k=e\bmod4$ puts $Z_0$ in the stated head interval: the bounds have integer endpoints after division by $3^k$. Successive divisions by 81 preserve the leading head until $Z_q$, which is comparable with $X\ge M/2$ and thus still has a nonnegative head exponent. We still need an actual meeting of $Z_0$ with $Y$ and the simultaneous lower-arm progression. The two arms have coupled bits. We obtain their marks by separately controlled **frequencies at the same indexed times**.

## Uniform concentration of lower-arm marks

Use the scalar affine maps $A_e(w)=(3^e w+e)/2$ of [HIGH_GRADE_SHADOW.md](HIGH_GRADE_SHADOW.md). During the length-$b$ window a grade of at least $4b$ initially guarantees that the orientation is fixed and the real perturbation from the pure affine trajectory is at most $2^{-b}$ at all its times. The absolute entering offset is at most a fixed power of $d$ on the known scalar sliding-test complement, by equation (6) there. The exponent can be fixed **before** choosing the larger window $b$: first choose the old growth-test length $b_0=K_A\log_2d$, then increase the floor exponent to accommodate the tests below. For an unconditioned fair scalar window of length $b$, outside $O(e^{-cb})$, every initial subword of length $l\ge b/3$ has $3^{p_l}/2^l\le(3^{.55}/2)^l$. This follows by a geometric sum of Hoeffding bounds. Thus, for $C_0$ sufficiently large, the actual real offset and the scalar proxy started from zero differ by less than $1/1000$ at all times in the last half of the window on the good scalar sample.

The lower mantissa phase on an ordinary window satisfies

$$\log_3 X_{h+l}\equiv \log_3 X_h-l\log_3 2+O(b/M)\pmod1,\tag{2}$$

for every $l\le b$ while both orbits are above $M$ (the single landing endpoint is allowed). The correction is bounded by the product of the individual odd-step factors. The same fractional logarithm holds for $Y$ to within $O(|W|/X)$, which is $o(1)$ at a successful mark. Fix a finite mesh of starting phases so that for every true phase a mesh point lies within a small fixed margin. The target arc is the fractional-log image of $[7,8]$, with successive fixed interior margins to absorb the mesh and both errors. Irrationality of $\log_32$ gives a fixed integer $H_0$ such that every $H_0$ consecutive points of every translated rotation visit this interior arc: finitely many powers form a sufficiently fine net of the circle. For each mesh point we can therefore choose deterministic candidate times in the last half of the window, one in every block of a fixed length $B_0$, at prescribed positions in $[Q+1,Q+H_0]$ inside the block. The constants $Q$ and $B_0=Q+H_0+13$ are fixed below. Consecutive candidates are separated by at least 13 clocks. The same finite schedules are tested on both arms.

Fix one schedule. A lower success at a candidate means that the zero-start scalar proxy is within $1/200$ of $1/2$ and the next 13 parity bits are the known word for $X\equiv2242\pmod{8192}$. Such successes occur at a fixed positive fraction of the candidates except for $O(e^{-cb})$ of all scalar words. Here are details of the frequency estimate.

For the proxy $z$ write $V(z)=\sqrt{|z|}$. The independent branch law has $\mathbb E V(A_e z)\le\rho V(z)+1$ with $\rho=(1+\sqrt3)/(2\sqrt2)<1$. Thus over any skeleton block the expected terminal $V$ is at most $\rho V(z)+B_1$, $B_1=(1-\rho)^{-1}$. Choose $T>2B_1/(1-\rho)+1$. For $V(z)\le T$ prescribe zero bits up to one step before the selected candidate, then one odd bit. For $Q$ large depending on $T$, this places the proxy within $1/200$ of $1/2$. Prescribe the next 13 bits by the exact progression. The chance of success is at least $p=2^{-B_0}>0$, uniformly in the starting state in this compact set and in the candidate position. Tilt the transition by $e^{-1}$ on success and by one otherwise. For $F=C+V$ with $C(1-e^{-1})p>2B_1+1$, this tilted kernel contracts $F$ by a fixed fraction uniformly: outside $V\le T$ the drift subtracts at least $(1-\rho)V/2$, and inside it subtracts at least $C(1-e^{-1})p-B_1$. These deductions bound the full tilted expectation including its terminal $V$. Iteration bounds $\mathbb E\exp(-\#\mathrm{successes})\le C'\rho_1^N$ for $N$ blocks from a zero-start proxy, including the burn-in by its bounded expected $V$. Exponential Markov now gives constants $\delta,c>0$ such that fewer than $\delta N$ lower successes costs $O(e^{-cN})$. All constants are independent of the designated mesh point. A successful lower mark transfers to $|W-1/2|<1/100$ using the preceding uniform-forgetting estimate.

## Upper-arm failures at the same times

At each candidate with upper value $y$, ask for all four pairs $(y,\lfloor y/3^i\rfloor)$, $0\le i\le3$, to have a symbolic meeting by a fixed horizon $H$. Conditional on the incoming residue $y\bmod27$, each projected integer on a complete fresh binary grid has uniform binary residues: division by $3^i$ has odd inverse modulo every power of two after subtracting its fixed trit remainder. The pair is $3^i z+r,z$ with $0\le r<3^i$. The uniform pair survival estimate applies to this finite menu. Choose $H$ large enough that the total conditional failure probability is at most $\delta/8$. The bound is pointwise in the current ternary residue, so it holds at every tested candidate conditional on its scalar binary past without any stationarity assumption about the trits.

There may be overlap between the length-$H$ tests of neighboring candidates. Split their deterministic index set into a fixed number of congruence classes so successive tests in each class are disjoint. In each class, conditional on the binary past the failure probability is at most $\delta/8$; exponential Markov applied to the successive Bernoulli indicators gives $O(e^{-cN})$ probability that its failure fraction exceeds $\delta/2$. The number of classes and $H$ are absolute fixed constants. In particular, except for this exponentially small scalar word event, the upper failures cannot cover all lower successes for the chosen phase. This is a deterministic intersection of frequency bounds at common times; no independence between the arms is required.

The upper test can be evaluated from a finite word of an ordinary starting value, with its ternary residue carried modulo 27. Indeed each shortcut branch updates that residue using the inverse of 2 modulo 27. At a candidate, $y\bmod 2^H$ is recovered from its next $H$ parities; together with $y\bmod27$ it determines every projected residue modulo $2^H$. Thus the fair-word failure bound is uniform in all 27 seed residues. The union over all seeds, and over the fixed phase mesh, is still $O(e^{-cb})$. This gives an ordinary **scalar binary word test**, regardless of which arm will be upper or lower.

## Source transfer and the scale of the new witness

Transfer these tests for **every** length-$(b+H)$ window starting at any time up to either arm's first entrance to $M$, on the original sources. Use the decreasing stage schedule and actual entrance fiber bound (3) of [FIRST_PASSAGE_MARKS.md](../literature-step/FIRST_PASSAGE_MARKS.md). At stage $i$ inspect every start through $k_i$, hence at most $O(d)$ starts. The binary prefixes of length $k_i+b+H$ fit below $\log_2Y_i$ once $D$ is chosen sufficiently large. The per-stage union has fair mass $O(d e^{-cb})$. The initial complete-period comparison or the later $O(d^3)$ source-fiber factor, followed by the $O(\log d)$ stages, gives $O(R d^{-A})$ on taking $C_0$ large first. Both possible choices for the lower scalar arm are covered. The argument uses only windows ending by $t_0$ when (2) or a constant pair orientation is needed. The last upper certificate itself may use $H$ more future clocks; its deterministic modular test is already included in the scalar word and its validity requires no barrier. This proves the first three conclusions.

For (1) take $h=t_*-b$. The first entrance is far enough from the beginning and occurs by $t_0$; for large $d$, $P\ll R/81$ and reaching it takes far more than $b$ clocks. Then $s_h\ge s_{t_*}-b\ge4b$. At $t_*$ one arm is in $[P/2,P)$; for $t\le t_*$ in this window both states are at least $P/2$. The smaller-grade arm equals the smaller-valued arm throughout the block because its grade difference is large and $|W|\le d^C\ll P$. Its value at $t$ is at most $2^bP$, since each step decreases by at most two. Outside a further $O_A(Rd^{-A})$ absolute exception all single-arm partial odd counts up to the relevant $M$-passage differ from half their clock by $O_A(\sqrt{d\log d})$, by the multistage concentration in [LANDING_WEIGHT_TRANSFER.md](../joint-renewal/LANDING_WEIGHT_TRANSFER.md). On this common window $s_t\le C_A\sqrt{d\log d}$. Hence $Y_t\le3^{s_t}(2^b P+d^C)$, while $Z_q\ge X_t/2\ge P/4$. The stated looser bounds (1) follow uniformly in the choice of floor.

The first missing estimate is a count of **how many original discordant sources can charge the same smaller head or band of heads**. Strictly decreasing witnesses outside a summable exceptional set do not alone rule out an exceptional smallest discordant edge or its preimages. A purely finite symbolic resolution of every child in the returned chain would, by transitivity of the certified equal-clock meetings, also certify the original pair. The existing square-root pair-survival obstruction applies to such a finite certificate.
