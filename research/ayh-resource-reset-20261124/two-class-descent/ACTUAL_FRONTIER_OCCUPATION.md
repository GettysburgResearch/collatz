## Polynomial-cost tests at a bounded-grade physical visit

Use the good actual simultaneous sliding-word and scalar descent sample, of exceptional mass O(Rd^(−A)). In the scalar proof take Y₀=(C₀+1)R, Y_{i+1}=Y_i^λ, L_i=log₂Y_i, k_i=floor(L_i/2), for the indicated fixed λ>1/2, and stop when Y_J≤M. Each arm crosses Y_{i+1} within k_i clocks of its first entrance to Y_i. At stage zero its initial time plays the role of that entrance. Put s_i=min(τ_{Y_i}(x),τ_{Y_i}(m)), clipped at t₀, with s₀=0. The corresponding earlier arm certifies s_{i+1}−s_i≤k_i. There are O(log d) half-open stages [s_i,s_{i+1}); the single clock t₀ may be added at the end. Before the end of such a stage both heights are at least Y_{i+1}, so a physical test of length k_i from **any clock within it** uses at most log₂ of either current height. We use the full length regardless of how much remains in the stage.

On either arm, for i≤t≤t₀ one has y_t≤d^c y_i with fixed c. Indeed each complete sliding block of b=O(log d) steps has multiplier ≤1 by the scalar test while its inputs are ≥M. Over the remaining <b positive-integer steps each multiplier is at most 2. Thus y_t≤2^b y_i. The physical correction in y_t=n 3^p 2^(−t) exp(ε_t) satisfies simultaneously
0≤ε_t≤Cd/M and ε_t≤Cd^(c+1)/y_t.
All physical times here are at most O(d).

At a given t<t₀ sort the arms by their affine grade, write their values y,3^s y+c₀ with s=S_t (use either orientation at a tie). The exact affine formula over the original residue class guarantees c₀ is an integer. The ratio (3^(−s)y_hi)/y is within 1+O(1/R) of exp(ε_hi−ε_lo), in either orientation, since x/(81m)=1+O(1/R). It is consequently 1+o(1), uniformly. Multiplying the sharper correction bounds by y and using y≤O(R d^c) and y/y_hi=O(1) gives |c₀|/3^s≤d^c' for a fixed c'. Thus for s≤K the menu of signed offsets and grades is of polynomial size in d. Both arm choices cost a constant.

For each t, p and each specified endpoint y of one specified arm in [Q,2Q), the original sources whose past is good as above lie in an interval about 2^t 3^(−p)y of length O(R d^(c+1)/Q). With the integrality rounding this gives O(1+R d^(c+1)/Q) possible sources. Here Q≤O(Rd^c), so enlargement of the polynomial absorbs 1. Mapping from original arm to children costs at most 81. Sum over t,p≤O(d), dyadic Q≤O(Rd^c) and the polynomial menu above. For any collection of uniform binary-grid tests, each at a specified stage tested from one of these states, with length k_i and fair mass ≤ρ, its actual original-source union costs at most R d^C' ρ on this good set. Indeed 2^k_i≤sqrt(Y_i)≤Y_{i+1}/2 for large d, so any intersecting dyadic Q exceeds 2^k_i up to a harmless endpoint factor, and complete-period covering gives O(Qρ) candidate y residues. Tests can depend on the incoming state, time and stage. For the canonical pair z,3^s z+c₀, the next k_i base bits are exactly equally distributed on a complete period of 2^k_i consecutive z, by the parity bijection. We overcount each adaptive visit by a union over its deterministic time and incoming state. This argument supplies no conditional fresh-bit law for a selected actual endpoint.

## Killed full-grid estimates sufficient at the restart

For the exact canonical chain we use Duran's state table and small-set absorbing words and the proved entry drift in the following primary source. L. Weaver, discussion of David Leen's exact tail and attribution, Lemmas 3–5, equations (4),(16), [source](https://rstation.ai/r/collatz/posts/20). A level-two entry is a transition 1→2. The chain of offsets at successive entries, killed after fusion, has V(c)=(|c|/9)^α with α=1/8, V(dead)=0, and E_c V(next)≤λ₀ V(c)+B with λ₀<1. Every fixed finite set of level-two offsets has probability p>0 of fusion by some common finite physical clock and hence by M₀ subsequent entry transitions. Excursions above level one return almost surely in the fair model.

**Exponential entry count.** Put B'=B/(1−λ₀) and choose L>2B'/(1−λ₀)+1. For V≤L take the preceding small-set p,M₀. Set W(c)=C₁+V(c) when live, W(dead)=0, with C₁>2(B'+1)/p. Iterating the drift gives E V(Y_{M₀})≤λ₀ V(c)+B'. If V>L then W−EW(Y_{M₀})≥(1−λ₀)V−B'≥(1−λ₀)V/2. If V≤L it is at least p C₁−B'. Both bounds exceed a fixed positive fraction of W in their respective ranges. Thus the probability of surviving n further level-two entries is ≤C(1+V(c)) exp(−cn). At an actual level-two restart V(c) is at most polynomial in d.

**Frontier decisions.** At each level-one step with odd offset, the fair, predictable mixed-step bit sends the pair to level two with probability 1/2. The other outcome fuses or moves to zero. The bits at successive such steps are fresh fair bits until that selected sequence ends. At most N entries but ≥4N of these decisions therefore has probability ≤Ce^(−cN) when starting at any frontier state (pad with independent fair bits after the last sampled decision). A run at level one before the next mixed step has a tail ≤C 2^(−l) after l clocks, by the state table: from an even level-one offset the two next offsets have opposite parity. A live grade-zero run with initial integer gap g>0 lasts ≤1+v₂(g), since a shared update divides by two and possibly multiplies by three. On the original good paths this is O(log d) from any cut inside the run, by the actual offset bound. Thus a stage with O(log d) entries and decisions, and no exceptionally long level-one waits, has O((log d)^2) frontier clocks.

**Positive-excursion occupation.** Begin at any interior grade ≥2 and stop on first reaching level one. The mixed-step grade is a simple symmetric walk until that hit. Its expected visits to l≥2 are 2 min(s−1,l−1), and the expected physical wait per visit is ≤e_l+2, with e_l=v₂(3^l−1). Consequently the expected physical occupation of [2,K] before hitting one is at most C K², uniformly in the starting level and signed offset, since ∑_{l≤K}e_l=O(K). Apply Markov's inequality at successive occupation times separated by ceil(2CK²)+1. The strong Markov property for the fair chain gives a tail ≤2^(−m) for occupation above m(ceil(2CK²)+1), uniformly in the entering state. Truncating the test at any deterministic word length preserves the bound.

## Returning all three estimates to the actual row

Take N=C₂ log d. Each of the following is detected by a length k_i parity test starting at a physical visit of grade at most K or two. They can be united over all starts using the polynomial cost above.

1. A stage contains more than N level-two entries before absorption. Start at the first such entry. The finite-prefix event of N−1 further entries while alive has mass at most a polynomial times exp(−cN) by exponential entry count.
2. A stage has at least 4N+4 frontier decisions but at most N entries. Start at its first level-one decision. The necessary finite-prefix event is that the first 4N such fair decisions all occur and send at most N of them to level two. A possible transition at the half-open stage endpoint costs one in the thresholds. Also rule out a level-one wait >N at any point of the stage using the fair geometric bound. Enlarging N by absolute factors absorbs both tests.
3. A maximal stage segment in the interior, before absorption, spends more than N(ceil(2CK²)+1) clocks in [2,K]. Start at its first visit to [2,K]. Fair interior occupation bounds the finite-prefix event until hitting level one or the deadline.

After increasing C₂, all exceptions have original mass O(R d^(−A)); the polynomial loss is fixed first and is independent of C₂. A stage has O(N+1) maximal interior segments and O(N+1) frontier episodes. Its low-band clock count is O(K² N²+N²). Summing over O(log d) stages proves Theorem 1. This proof never bounds the length of the long positive excursion during which a grade is much larger than K.

## Floor coarea and signed grade

For any Y>M in the stated intermediate range, let t*=min(τ_Y(x),τ_Y(m)) and L=|τ_Y(x)−τ_Y(m)|. Take the later arm's grade minus the earlier arm's at t*, denoted G, breaking ties arbitrarily. The early height is in [Y/2,Y). If L>0 the other height is at least Y and their ratio is 3^G exp(O(d/M+1/R)). This forces G≥0. If L=0 both heights are in [Y/2,Y), so G=0. Since during the next L steps the other height can fall by at most 2^L and ends below Y, 3^G≤(2+o(1))2^L; in particular L≤k log d implies S_{t*}≤C_k log d. This deterministic implication suffices for Theorem 2.

For fixed source and physical clock t=t*, the thresholds Y for which this is the earliest first passage of the two arms have log₂-length ≤1: the running minimum of the two heights falls by at most a factor two in one step. The measure of θ for which a discordant pair has close first-entrance clocks is therefore ≤O((log d)^5/B_d) on the good set. The total discordant sources in the row are O(R/√d) by the ordinary head full-grid merger bound at its native deadline. Multiplying these two estimates bounds the θ-integral of normalized discordant close-clock mass by O(d^(−A)+d^(−1)(log d)^(5−C)). This is summable for C>6. Tonelli gives the single-θ assertion. A polylogarithmic W_j is simply restricted at its original source in this application. For a fixed intermediate root-counting floor Y_j itself the summability also applies directly to its own ordered weights, since colors are invariant to first passage.

## A computable floor and polynomial entrance lag

Both coarea conclusions hold with the color test replaced by the following finite event. Require timely entrance of both arms at the indicated floor, failure of counted fusion through both the native deadline h_j=floor(log₂(R/81)) and the earlier floor entrance, and the stated upper bound on the entrance-clock separation. Simulate only to the common deterministic cap H=ceil(Cd) in the scalar theorem, enlarging C to include h_j. At every genuinely discordant timely pair these certificate failures hold. The multiplicative O(d^(−1/2)) in the coarea proof is the actual fraction failing the native certificate and applies to this larger event. The occupation bound applies until the first fusion; requiring survival to the earlier entrance suffices.

For integer ℓ∈[ceil B_d,floor(2B_d)], use Y=2^ℓ. At most one such ℓ has a given first-passage clock: if the new joint running minimum is y and the old minimum z, then z≤2y and first passages require y<2^ℓ≤z. Thus the discrete total count satisfies the same bound as the continuous integral multiplied by the number of candidate floors, up to an absolute constant. Choose the least integer exponent minimizing the indicated finite count across these candidates. This is a deterministic choice computable from finitely many actual head trajectories, independently of their colors. Its normalized close-clock certificate mass on every sufficiently large row is
O_A(d^(−A) + d^(−1)(log d)^(5−C)).
The integral statement for almost every single θ holds for the certificate as well. The elementary one-exponent assertion, finite incidence sum and existence of a floor at most its mean are verified in FrontierCoarea.lean. The stochastic and source-transfer estimates are written proofs.

The same proof permits 2≤K≤Cd with a larger cost only at the restart of test 3. At restarts of tests 1 and 2 the grade is at most two, so their number of state choices remains polynomial, and their per-stage counts are O(log d) as before. At the first observed interior visit to [2,K] the menu is at most d^C₃ times (K+1)3^K, using the uniform normalized-offset bound. Take C₄(K+log d) occupation quanta of order K² in test 3: its fair tail pays this entire menu and the prescribed d^(−A), for C₄ sufficiently large independently of K. Consequently, outside O_A(Rd^(−A)) original sources, total live band occupation ≤
C_A [ K²(K+log d)(log d)² + (log d)³ ].
At K proportional to log d this recovers the stated log-fifth bound.

For 0<β<1/6 and 1/2+3β<b<1, take K=C₅d^β and B_d=d^b. For any fixed lag constant l₀>0, the deterministic grade-to-lag implication includes L≤l₀ d^β by choosing C₅ sufficiently large. The normalized close-clock certificate bound at the chosen floor and the θ-integral are now

The strict gap b>1/2+3β makes this summable. Also for almost every fixed θ the same close-clock certificate mass is summable across all rows. At these floors the earlier true first passage is at d/a+o(d) outside O_A(Rd^(−A)) original children: apply the full-interval scalar concentration through M to both arms and the physical multiplier on the pre-entrance range. In particular it is beyond the native horizon.

For the original polylogarithmic W_j, this proves summability on the actual source-restricted subweights selected by the intermediate small-lag test.
