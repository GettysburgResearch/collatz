# Critical limits on one ordinary Collatz orbit

Let $x_k$ be the positive odd iterates of a fixed odd integer coprime to three. Write $s_k$ for the accumulated shortcut clock and $M_k=3^k/2^{s_k}$. An exact record at $k$ satisfies $M_i<M_k$ for every $i<k$. A limiting script is an infinite backwards gap word such that every finite prefix occurs at arbitrarily late exact records on this one orbit. The record theorems constrain hypothetical escaping orbits. The finite-window theorems also constrain every phase of every sufficiently high bounded-width positive cycle.

## Bounded and unbounded gaps between records

**Bounded gaps.** If every interval of $J+1$ odd indices contains a record, then for every integer $D$ there exists a limiting script that admits no period starting at index $D$. The script may depend on $D$. An eventual bound on record gaps suffices after increasing $J$ to cover the finite beginning. See [HourEscapeLimits.lean](HourEscapeLimits.lean), theorem `record_limit_unbounded_preperiod`.

Each outgoing gap is bounded under this hypothesis. Take the next record within $J$ steps of its endpoint and use its strict clock bound over the entire intervening segment. If one finite length of past gap history predicted the next gap at all late occurrences, two equal windows from the finite alphabet would have identical futures. Their fixed starting heights would agree modulo arbitrarily large powers of two and hence be equal. Nonrepetition therefore supplies arbitrarily late pairs of occurrences of every prescribed history *length* having equal histories and differing next gaps. See [HourEscapeBranching.lean](HourEscapeBranching.lean).

For each of these pairs take a record between $D+1$ and $D+1+J$ steps after its differing gap position, measured from the input of that gap. Diagonal selection keeps both offsets fixed and yields two limiting scripts from the original ray. Viewed from the differing gaps their complete older histories agree. Periodicity from index $D$ for both scripts would recover their differing letters from the common older history by moving back a common multiple of the two periods. This proves the assertion without a bound on the periods or on the number of possible limiting words.

**Unbounded record gaps with bounded drawdown.** Suppose $M_i\le B M_j$ for all $i\le j$ and some finite $B$. If record gaps are unbounded, there is a *single* limiting script $e_0,e_1,\ldots$ for which, setting $S_b=\sum_{j<b}e_j$,

$$
2^{S_b}<3^b\le 2B\,2^{S_b}\qquad (b\ge1).
$$

It has no eventual period. The Lean statements are `drought_limit` and `aperiodic_drought_limit` in [HourEscapeDrawdown.lean](HourEscapeDrawdown.lean).

For each $n$ choose a record-free interval of $n+1$ sites, and take the first record $k$ after it. The maximum at times below $k$ is attained at some strict record $h$ before that interval. The final odd step multiplies the coefficient by at most $3/2<2$, so $M_k\le2M_h$. For $j\le n$, the entire suffix lies after $h$ and the drawdown hypothesis yields $M_h\le B M_{k-j}$. Strictness gives the other inequality. Each single gap satisfies $2^{e_j}\le3B$, so diagonal selection over a finite alphabet preserves all these simultaneous bounds. An eventual period with $p>0$ gaps and total $s$ would repeat the multiplier $3^p/2^s$. It differs from one, and its powers violate one of the two bounds.

Combining the cases, bounded drawdown on an escaping ray forces limiting scripts that escape every uniform preperiod cutoff. A bounded-gap or bounded-drawdown hypothesis cannot currently be deduced for an arbitrary escaping source from these results.

## Rational exclusion at both finite and infinite depths

The Mathlib theorem `irrational_of_local_plateaus` requires only arbitrarily long terminal windows on which the uncorrected coefficient stays within a fixed factor of its record. It supplies a single limiting script whose ternary coordinate is irrational. Its contrapositive gives a restriction using one fixed ordinary source. If every limiting critical script has a rational ternary coordinate, then for each $H\ge1$ there exists a single $n$ such that **every** critical $k\ge n$ has some $1\le j\le n$ with $M_k>H M_{k-j}$. The unbounded-drought theorem above supplies the local windows when historical drawdown is bounded. [HourEscapeLookback.lean](mathlib/HourEscapeLookback.lean) · [HourEscapeSigned.lean](mathlib/HourEscapeSigned.lean).

Here rationality is expressed at every finite ternary precision. For a backwards script $e$ put $C_0=0$ and $C_{j+1}=2^{e_j}C_j+3^j$. A rational candidate $A/d$ in lowest terms in the 3-adic integers has $d>0$ prime to three. Equality with the script's ternary coordinate is equivalent to $3^j\mid 2^{S_j}A-dC_j$ at every $j$. These divisibilities would give a signed integer path $3y_{j+1}+d=2^{e_j}y_j$. The formal exclusion holds for every $d>0$.

For a bounded strip of clock coefficients, the signed drift lemma gives $|y_j|=O(j+1)$ and $y_j\to-\infty$. Removing the two-part of $d$ on a finite tail makes the odd forward map deterministic. A repeat of period $p$ ending at time $j$ forces a return to the first normalized state at time $p\lfloor j/p\rfloor \ge j/2$, so a sufficiently late tail is injective. A window of $5r$ gaps has total at most $8r+L$ for a fixed $L$. The positive words of length $b$ and total at most $s$ satisfy $2^b\,\#W(s,b)\le3^s$. This follows by separating first gaps equal to one from first gaps greater than one. Equal length-$b$ gap words force equal signed terminal numerators modulo $3^b$. The ratio $(27/32)^r$ between the word count bound and $3^{5r}$ tends to zero. Linear height and injectivity then give more distinct signed numerators in an interval of width below $3^{5r}$ than there are words. [HourEscapePacking.lean](HourEscapePacking.lean) · [HourCycleShadow.lean](mathlib/HourCycleShadow.lean) · [HourEscapeSigned.lean](mathlib/HourEscapeSigned.lean).

The finite ordinary consequence allows every positive gap. For fixed $d>0$, $A\in\mathbb Z$, and integer width factor $K>0$, one precision $N$ excludes $d x\equiv A\pmod {3^N}$ at the terminal site of every $N$-edge ordinary window in $[m,Km]$ with $m\ge KN$ and $m>0$. The same precision excludes the residue at **every phase** of every positive cycle in that band, independently of its period. This is an existence result for the precision. [HourEscapeWindows.lean](mathlib/HourEscapeWindows.lean).

The dyadic result starts at the input of an ordinary forward window. A fixed denominator odd at two and a congruence of arbitrary dyadic precision would supply an integer forward path $2^{e_j}y_{j+1}=3y_j+d$. Dividing $y_j$ by the positive weight $w_j=3^j/2^{S_j}$ gives increments $d/(3w_j)$. Bounded positive weights imply linear size and eventual growth beyond every integer. All numerators are odd and the forward dynamics are deterministic, so the sequence is injective. The same finite word estimate now packs *forward endpoints*, using congruence modulo $3^b$ from equal incoming words. Reflection of a finite height window supplies the uniform bounds on the weights. [HourEscapeBinary.lean](mathlib/HourEscapeBinary.lean) · [HourEscapeBinaryWindows.lean](mathlib/HourEscapeBinaryWindows.lean).

In particular, for any prescribed rational coordinates in the dyadic and ternary integers and any fixed integer width factor $K>0$, one precision $N$ excludes both corresponding congruences at every phase of all cycles above the floor $m\ge KN$. Consequently along any sequence of phases of positive cycles with minimum tending to infinity and uniformly bounded maximum-to-minimum ratio, a converging subsequence in either completion must have an irrational limit. The precision depends on the prescribed rationals and the width factor. This permits unbounded-width cycles and bounded-width families whose accumulation points are irrational. See `simultaneous_cycle_phase_exclusion`. [HourEscapeBinaryWindows.lean](mathlib/HourEscapeBinaryWindows.lean).

## Finite critical histories and positive starts

Every finite list of positive gaps whose nonempty terminal suffixes satisfy $2^{s}<3^b$ occurs on an ordinary odd unit orbit as a strict record since the initial index. Its immediately following odd state can be any prescribed positive odd unit, including one. Both the start and the record can exceed a prescribed height. The source varies with the finite word. The construction uses the exact simultaneous translation and one-gap cylinder from [SynchronousShadow.lean](SynchronousShadow.lean). See `critical_word_to_target` in [HourEscapeCylinders.lean](HourEscapeCylinders.lean).

## Relation to neighboring proposals

The written rational classification R1 shows that a rational ternary coordinate of a critical script forces a periodic backwards tail after at most $v_2(d)+1$ normalization steps for the reduced denominator $d$. Consequently the bounded-gap theorem rules out confinement to rational coordinates with uniformly bounded dyadic denominators. The updated renewal packet independently proves a stronger written bounded-gap conclusion. A *single* rational cluster point forces a full tower with unbounded powers of two in its denominators. Its isolated-defect example demonstrates why the uniform-preperiod conclusion does not itself imply the presence of an irrational cluster in the bounded-gap case. In the unbounded-gap case our bounded-drawdown conclusion yields one genuinely aperiodic balanced script. The Subspace Theorem proposal in PR 210 implies transcendence under an additional repetition hypothesis (or bounded linear factor complexity in the balanced case). The bounded discrepancy proved here yields $S_b/b\to\log_2 3$, but gives no such repetition or complexity bound. Neither the row counts nor the thin-band lower estimate of PRs 195 and 201 provide a conflicting deterministic upper estimate on these actual records.
