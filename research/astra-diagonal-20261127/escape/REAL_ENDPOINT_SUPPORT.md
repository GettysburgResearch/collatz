# The upper endpoint of the physical correction law

Let \(C\) be an escaping component, \(P(x)=x/F(x)\) its corrected height, and \(H_C\lambda_C\) its past limiting real law in the [canonical path construction](CORRECTED_PHASE.md). In that construction a path traverses every grade, visits a specified unit \(b\) on its grade with probability \(h(b)/(H_CP(b))\), and carries the full two-sided product \(J=\prod_i 3x_i/(3x_i+1)\) with law \(\lambda_C\). There are absolute constants \(A,B>0\) such that, for every sufficiently large integer \(M\) (the starting threshold may depend on the component),
\[
                  \lambda_C([1-A T(M),1])\ge H_C^{-1} e^{-BM}.             \tag{1}
\]
Here \(T(M)\to0\) uniformly bounds the sum of reciprocals of the distinct sites above \(M\) on a simple odd orbit. The component law is singular continuous under its named real-fibre input. Equation (1) places 1 in its support. [Reciprocal packing](../global/ORDINARY_HARDY.md) · [real fibre and its canonical path](../../astra-six-hour-20261127/escape/REAL_FIBRE_AND_FRONT.md).

Write \(K\) for a uniform bound in \(x\le P(x)\le Kx\) on escaping components, supplied by the same reciprocal packing. On an escaping forward ray the [successive permanent corrected minima](FINITE_RECORD_CURRENT.md) have increasing unbounded heights, and each next height is at most \(3/2\) times the preceding height. Take one such unit \(y\) with \(2KM\le P(y)\le3KM\). All of its future sites exceed \(M\).

The ordinary odd parents of \(y\) are \((2^e y-1)/3\) in one allowed parity of \(e\). Every run of three consecutive allowed exponents contains exactly two unit parents, by the order of 4 modulo 9. The first \(M+1\) unit parents have exponent \(O(M)\), and their genuine precursor trees are pairwise disjoint. Indeed a positive source visiting two different parents would then revisit \(y\), which escapes. At most \(M\) of these trees can meet \([1,M]\). Take an avoiding parent \(b\). Its height satisfies \(P(b)=2^eP(y)/3\le e^{O(M)}\), and \(h(b)\) has the absolute positive unit floor. On the canonical event of visiting \(b\), the entire past lies in its avoiding tree and the future continues through \(y\). Every finite segment has distinct sites above \(M\); therefore \(-\log J\le T(M)/3\). The probability of the event proves (1).

There is a fixed-target consequence with no escape assumption on the target's future. At each nonperiodic positive odd unit \(a\) the canonical real intercept \(R_\infty\) has 0 in its support. Distinct parents of \(a\) again have disjoint precursor trees, as two visits to \(a\) would make it periodic. For any \(M\) choose a unit parent \(b=(2^e a-1)/3\) whose whole precursor tree avoids \([1,M]\); after any prescribed exponent threshold there are still infinitely many unit parents from which to choose. On the positive-probability cylinder using this first inverse edge, write \(R_\infty(a)=2^{-e}+3\,2^{-e}R_\infty(b)\). Along every finite physical path to \(b\), the affine intercept divided by \(b\) is one minus the product of \(3u/(3u+1)\) over its departing sites. Thus \(R_\infty(b)/b\le T(M)/3\) almost surely on the canonical continuation. Consequently \(R_\infty(a)\le 2^{-e}+aT(M)/3\) on this cylinder. Let \(M\) and the exponent threshold grow.
