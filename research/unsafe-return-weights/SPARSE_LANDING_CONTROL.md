# A logarithmically sparse change preserves all high successful first landings outside 33 labels

**PROPOSED logical control, source-qualified.** Use the [FS-018 sparse perturbation of the true ordinary Collatz map](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/7bfa54c6e729cffa1f8feeaaaf001f70741a7ddc/research/fixed-floor-shells/SPARSE_PERTURBATION.md). Its changed odd vertices are the increasing spine `v_(j,i)=(16^j(13+6i)−1)/3`, for `j≥4, 0≤i≤32`, ordered as in that packet. The true U-successor of each v belongs to `Y={13,19,…,205}`; the altered odd successor is the next larger spine vertex. There are `O(1+log X)` exceptions below X. All true odd successors from Y onward lie in a directly checked finite set F with largest element 3077. Every unaltered odd edge and every even step are the ordinary Collatz ones. The altered system has an escaping component of positive lower density.

Fix any threshold R>205 and any positive odd start. Up to the first landing at or below R, either the true path encounters no changed odd vertex above R, or it first encounters such a vertex v. In the former case both first-landing paths agree, including failure. In the latter the true next odd state is some y∈Y below R, so its first landing is y; the altered path remains above R forever. A changed vertex first encountered at or below R does not modify the prior first passage. Consequently for **every** successful odd label `a≤R` outside Y, the true and altered first-landing source sets agree exactly, with all arrival times and all preceding parity histories. This includes every diagonal label `a=R>205` at every positive odd integer threshold, even the modified vertices themselves. At each such spine v the true global minimum is at most 205 and the altered global minimum equals v. Thus exact diagonal incoming histories do not determine the missing future-minimum condition in the first-moment reduction.

In any source shell `(X/4,X]` with X≥4R, new failures have probability at most the true probability of landing in Y. By [rStation 102, Theorem 1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/102/v1), this is at most `F_R(33/R+R^(−g))`, with `F_R=C exp(C√log R)` and `g=1−H₂(log₃2)+1/351<1`. Together with its true failure bound this proves the same `O(F_R R^(−g))` estimate in the altered system. All successful atoms are unchanged or reduced, so its successful set bounds also hold, with the same rates. The bounded range R≤205 can be absorbed in the constants.

The full positive first-landing lower bound, including the two clock windows, also survives. Fix a unit odd `a≤M`. Choose an integer e within a bounded distance of `log₂(M/a)`, so that `z=(2^e a−1)/3` is an odd ternary unit, `z>M`, `z>3077`, and `4∤e`. For example begin at `⌈log₂(6(M+3078)/a)⌉` and inspect twelve successive exponents. Integrality fixes the parity, and two of the three associated residue classes modulo six give units modulo three for z. Each such class has two lifts modulo twelve, differing by six, and at most one lift is divisible by four. This also gives `z≤C M` absolutely. Since every changed vertex has true valuation a multiple of four, z is not a changed vertex and its true odd successor is a. No true incoming path to z can have passed any changed vertex, because its true odd states thereafter are in F. Thus every first-landing path to z at its *own* threshold and its next step z→a are unchanged. Apply [rStation 12, Theorem 14 and (41)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/12/v1) to z when `X≥C′M`. This supplies `≫X/M` odd starts first landing at a at threshold M. The odd clock gains one and the shortcut clock gains e; replacing `log(X/z)` by `log(X/M)` costs only O(1), and `e=log₂(M/a)+O(1)`. Hence the original odd and shortcut clock centers with their square-root errors hold uniformly. This counting argument applies to the modified system by following exact unaltered ordinary paths; no mixing theorem for modified dynamics is assumed.

This control addresses simultaneous finite-scale inequalities and exact high incoming rows. A result that uses the exact positive Collatz formula at every vertex, or dependencies between the labels in Y and their subsequent futures, can distinguish the two systems.
