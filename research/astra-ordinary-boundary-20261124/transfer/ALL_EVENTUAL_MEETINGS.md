# A central deadline for the full eventual meeting probability

**PROPOSED written theorem with an additional relative-landing input.** Use the positive shortcut $T$, the independent ordinary patches, $R=81^j$, $d=\log_2R$, and $C_*=2/\log(4/3)$ as in [MEETING_CLOCKS.md](MEETING_CLOCKS.md). Denote by $x\sim y$ the existence of any two finite, possibly different clocks with a common value. There is an absolute finite $B$ such that, writing

$$ L_R=C_*\log R-B\sqrt{d\log d},\qquad H_R=C_*\log R+B\sqrt{d\log d}, $$

one has on every two resolved independent patches

$$ \mathbb P(X\sim Y)-\mathbb P(\exists\, i,k\in[L_R,H_R]: T^iX=T^kY)\longrightarrow0. \tag{1} $$

The windows use separate physical clocks for the two original integers. All eventually meeting components contribute, including a hypothetical escaping component. The upper-deadline estimate, without the lower cutoff $L_R$, holds for **every coupling** of the two prescribed marginals. It applies in particular to the actual head pair ($X=x,Y=\lfloor x/81\rfloor$), whose two marginals each fill a fixed-ratio interval at scales comparable to $R$. This latter approximation has an $o(1)$ absolute error. Summing it over $j$ or dividing it by the mass of an arbitrarily rare selection is unsupported. The lower cutoff in (1) uses the independent sampling.

## Descent to a fixed floor under the central deadline

Use the source-qualified relative comparison in [*First-landing bounds and canonical inverse laws*, §6, (15)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/12/v1), credited there to Leen's zero-frequency passage theorem. For odd starts uniform on $[L,2L)$ let $\Lambda_{L,y}(g)$ be the expectation of $0\le g\le1$ of the first *odd* iterate at most $y$, with value zero on a miss. Uniformly in $g$ and $L\ge y^{1.001}$ it compares to one common nonnegative functional with multiplicative error $O((\log y)^{-c})$, $c>0$, and additive remainder $O_A((\log y)^{-A})$. The following duration argument adapts [PR193, RETURNS](../../astra-closure-20261124/transfer/RETURNS.md). No marked conditional law on the eventual merging population is used.

For large $y$ put $Q=y^{1.001}$, $m=y^{.99}$ and $J=\lfloor(\log_2 y)/2\rfloor$. From an odd start in $[Q,2Q)$, except on $O(y^{-c'})$ of the starts, the shortcut process hits $[1,m]$ within $J$ clocks. Indeed except for an incomplete-grid error $O(2^J/Q)$ the first $J$ parity bits are a forced odd followed by independent fair bits. A binomial tail gives at most $.6J$ odds with power-small exception. If no $m$ hit has occurred, the exact multiplier at $J$ gives an endpoint at most $2Q3^{.6J}2^{-J}\exp(J/(3m))<m$ for large $y$, a contradiction. Here $1.001+(.6\log_2 3-1)/2<.99$.

Define $g_y(v)$ at the first odd $y$-landing $v$ to indicate failure of its future to enter $[1,m]$ within its next $J$ shortcut steps. If a near-scale start first hits $[1,m]$ by $J$, then either the odd $y$-landing has already occurred, or that $m$ hit is in the decreasing halving segment ending at this first odd $y$-landing. In the second case the odd landing itself is at most $m$. Thus $\Lambda_{Q,y}(g_y)\ll y^{-c'}$. Eliminating the common nonnegative functional between $Q$ and arbitrary $L\ge Q$ gives $\Lambda_{L,y}(g_y)\ll_A(\log y)^{-A}$. A successful first **shortcut** $y$-landing reaches its odd part within $\log_2 y$ additional halvings, so this mark controls failure to pass $m$ from that landing in $J+\lceil\log_2 y\rceil$ steps, whenever that test is needed.

For integer sources in $[c_0R,C_0R]$ split $n=2^s u$ with $u$ odd. If $c_0 R/2^s\ge Q$, cover the odd source interval for $u$ by a bounded number of admissible octaves and sum the counts over $s$. Initial halvings either leave the first $y$ passage unchanged in the odd tail or enter $m$ early; the latter extremely divisible sources are also covered by the next bound. If $c_0R/2^s<Q$, divisibility by a single power of two $\gg R/Q$ bounds the remaining fraction by $O(Q/R+1/R)$. Consequently the unconditional probability of *having a first shortcut $y$-passage but failing the $O(\log y)$ subsequent deadline for $m$* is

$$ O_{A,c_0,C_0}((\log y)^{-A}+y^{1.001}/R). \tag{2} $$

Choose once $A=2$ and a sufficiently large fixed $D$. First enter the polylogarithmic floor $Y=d^D$ using [MEETING_CLOCKS.md, (2)](MEETING_CLOCKS.md). On a fixed-ratio annulus this costs at most $C_*\log R+B_0\sqrt{d\log d}$ clocks except on $O(d^{-2})$ of its sources. Using at-most thresholds consistently is obtained by moving the strict real threshold by less than two. For fixed large integer $K$, descend along $y_i=Y^{.99^i}$ while $y_i>K$. Sum (2) at the original source for each $i$. The logarithms form a geometric progression, giving total duration $O(\log Y)$ and total exception $O((\log K)^{-2}+Y^{1.001}/R+d^{-2})$. In estimating the geometric sum of $y_i^{1.001}$ use the uniform ratio at most $K^{-.01001}$ while $y_i>K$. The final $m\le K$ certifies a $K$ passage. The first $Y$ hit supplies the base case. No estimate on the arbitrary future of a failed $y$ passage is used.

It follows that the miss probability for the fixed floor $K$ at deadline $C_*\log R+(B_0+1)\sqrt{d\log d}$ is $O((\log K)^{-2})+o_R(1)$. To place this on a shrinking patch, use [MEETING_CLOCKS.md, (6)–(7)](MEETING_CLOCKS.md) with the target $[1,K]$. The comparisons are above $K$ for every fixed $K$ and sufficiently large $j$, hence carry its exact first-hit clock just as for a singleton. The complement form of (7) reads

$$ \int_W(1-G_j^{H+q_j})\le |W|(1-p_j^H)+O(e_j). $$

The global logarithmic miss $1-p_j^H$ obeys the same bound as its uniform-ordinary counterpart by the bounded density ratio on the full annulus. After normalization by $|W|$, the last error vanishes on every resolved patch; conversion to uniform integers preserves this *upper bound on misses* up to an absolute factor. The extra $q_j\le j^{1/4}$ is absorbed by another fixed unit of $\sqrt{d\log d}$. The constants in the central deadline can be held fixed as $K$ grows in the later, outer limit.

## Finite ordinary roots and the order of limits

For each fixed $K$ there are finitely many ordered pairs $(v,w)\in[1,K]^2$. For those that eventually meet, take the least possible maximum of their two physical meeting clocks. The largest of these finite numbers, $b(K)$, is finite; the pair $(1,1)$ is available. No decision procedure or growth bound for $b(K)$ is assumed. On two timely $K$ passages, $X\sim Y$ implies their two landing values also have a common future. In a deterministic map any two intersecting orbits share a tail; moving either starting clock forward by a fixed amount preserves intersecting futures. Starting at the two $K$ landings, at most $b(K)$ additional clocks on each arm therefore produce a meeting.

At fixed $K$ this constant fits in one more unit of $\sqrt{d\log d}$ for large $j$. A union bound on the two marginal miss probabilities gives a limsup of $O((\log K)^{-2})$ for the difference between the full eventual and the upper-deadline events. Now let $K\to\infty$. This proves the upper conclusion, including under dependence. For the independent samples, (1) in [MEETING_CLOCKS.md](MEETING_CLOCKS.md) removes *every* intersection whose clock on either arm is earlier than $L_R$, at cost $o(1)$. Enlarge $B$ once to cover the two estimates. This proves (1) here. The same proof by annular summation holds for independent uniform positive integers up to $N$ with $R$ replaced by $N$.

## The limit and what would close the problem

Optionally use the established density input C of [*First-landing bounds*, §1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/12/v1): a set invariant above a fixed floor, every member of which enters that floor, has ordinary natural density. For a shortcut component $C$, truncate its basin to the integers whose orbits meet $C\cap[1,K]$. This set is invariant above $K$ for the shortcut map. To apply the ordinary-map formulation directly, use the union of the ordinary predecessor sets of $C\cap[1,K]$. All of those targets belong to the same ordinary component as their shortcut component; any skipped even intermediate $3n+1\le K$ is immediately followed by a shortcut state $\le K$ in that component. Conversely, a shortcut hit is an ordinary hit. Thus this finite-target union equals $\{n\in C:\tau_K(n)<\infty\}$, is ordinary-map invariant above $K$, and every member meets $[1,K]$. Write its natural density as $\delta_{C,K}$.

The annular miss estimate and dyadic summation show that the upper natural density of ever missing $K$ is $O((\log K)^{-2})$. Consequently the full ordinary density $\delta_C=\lim_K\delta_{C,K}$ exists and the countable component masses sum to one: the finite set of components represented in $[1,K]$ already captures all but $O((\log K)^{-2})$ in the limiting count. This density description is also available by pushing forward the previously published tight law of the global orbit minimum under its component map. See [Leen, *Brownian motion in Collatz orbits*, v2](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23269488). The single-orbit clock and minimum laws in that literature precede this joint meeting argument.

On the whole annulus the log-coordinate share of a fixed component tends to $\delta_C$ by partial summation of its natural density. On a resolved moving patch its **ordinary** fraction likewise tends to $\delta_C$. Indeed the PR197 invariant-color discrepancy gives every log subinterval error $O(e_j)$ from its length times the annular mean, uniformly in its endpoints. To pass to ordinary counting, multiply by the smooth bounded weight $81^u\log81$ and integrate by parts on the patch; uniform discrepancy for subintervals bounds the weighted error by $O(e_j)$ as well, plus $O(1/R)$ for partially cut cells. Divide by the ordinary patch weight $\asymp|W_j|$. Thus the law of the component on each resolved patch converges in total variation to $(\delta_C)_C$ by the finite-tail criterion for probability measures on a countable set. For independent arms,

$$ \mathbb P(X\sim Y)\longrightarrow \sum_C\delta_C^2. \tag{3} $$

If $Z_R$ is the least possible $\max(i,k)/\log R$ among physical common iterates, put $Z_R=+\infty$ when they never meet. Equations (1) and (3) give weak convergence on $[0,\infty]$ to the two-atom law with mass $\sum_C\delta_C^2$ at $C_*$ and its complement at infinity (an atom may have zero weight). In particular, under fixed-target positive lower density, Collatz is equivalent to the probability of a **finite central-window coincidence** tending to one on one resolved pair of independent patches. This is a bounded-clock replacement of PR197's all-future collision criterion. A counterexample supplies a positive-density component distinct from that of one. We have no independent estimate proving $\sum_C\delta_C^2=1$. The source-qualified comparisons give an equivalence and the location of the finite mass, not its value.
