# Removing a rare source residue while retaining the whole ordinary future

**PROPOSED written theorem.** Use the hypotheses and notation $X,q=2^hm,r,p,Q=3^pm,Y,J=\lfloor Y\rfloor$ of [the moving-progression theorem](RESIDUE_EQUILIBRIUM.md). Here no limiting basin density or unknown rate of convergence to that density is needed. Let $n$ be uniform on one nonempty $q$-class in $[X,2X)$ and $U$ uniform on **all** integers in $[J,2J)$. For $1\le t\le K$, $Q\le J$, and $q\le X/2$, put

$$e=C\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(2Q)}{t+1}}+\frac{(2^t+1)Q}{J}+\frac qX\right).$$

Then

$$d_{\rm TV}\big(\mathcal L(T^{h+K}n),\,\mathcal L(T^K U)\big)\le e.\tag{1}$$

The bound is uniform over all $K\ge t$, without an upper bound. It compares ordinary integer endpoints. Mapping each endpoint to its entire positive forward trajectory gives the same bound for the complete post-clock path, including any subsequently selected stopping-time or terminal mark. The test can vary with $X$. This is a single-source comparison with the indicated change in source scale and clock. It is not a product replacement for two arms sampled from one common integer.

For a fixed positive odd $b$, let $\tau_b$ be its first shortcut hitting time, with $\infty$ allowed. When $X/2^h>b$ and $J/2^t>b$, one also has the whole extended-clock law comparison

$$d_{\rm TV}\big(\mathcal L(\tau_b(n)),\,\mathcal L(h+\tau_b(U))\big)\le e,\qquad h+\infty=\infty.\tag{2}$$

**Proof.** The exact binary lift identifies $T^h n$ with the uniform measure on a single odd-$Q$ progression cut by $[Y+s,2Y+s)$. Replacing the interval by $[J,2J)$ changes $O(1)$ selected integers, hence $O(q/X)$ in total variation. If the latter interval has exceptionally small cell count the bound is already trivial by enlarging $C$; when $q/X$ is small its size is comparable to $X/q=Y/Q$.

For any set $B$ of positive integers put $F(y)=\mathbf1_{T^K y\in B}$. On a successful simultaneous fusion of $y,y+d$ by time $t$, $F(y)=F(y+d)$ because $K\ge t$. This pointwise inequality with the binary exceptional grid is precisely the input to the shift averaging in paper 13, Proposition 1. For clarity take $0\le d<Q$ and $w(y)=Q\mathbf1_{y\equiv a\ (Q)}$. The CRT exceptional sum of $w(y+d)$ over $[J,2J)$ is bounded by $(J+2^t Q)C\sqrt{\log(2Q)/(t+1)}$ (or use the grid probability capped at one). Replacing $\sum F(y)w(y+d)$ by $\sum F(y+d)w(y+d)$ pays only this failure; shifting back to $[J,2J)$ costs $O(Q)$ at the two ends. Averaging over $d$ uses $Q^{-1}\sum_d w(y+d)=1$. Dividing by $J$ gives the displayed error, including replacement of the normalized count by the actual AP cardinality. It is independent of $B$, so we may take the supremum over *all* endpoint events. Likewise, take an arbitrary extended-time set $D$ and $F(y)=\mathbf1_{\tau_b(y)\in D}$. Both starts $y,y+d$ stay above $b$ through time $t$ as $y\ge J$ and $J/2^t>b$. On synchronous fusion their first-hit clocks are exactly equal, including infinity. The same averaging applies; the first $h$ steps of the original source stay above $b$, giving the clock shift in (2). [Uniform pair bound and shift averaging, paper 13, equation (1) and Proposition 1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/13)

## The sharp leading independent meeting clock on residue-filtered samples

Let $q=X^{o(1)}$ and select any nonempty union of its classes in $[X,2X)$, however rare. The modulus and choice of residues can change at every $X$. One may instead select any nonempty union of leaves of a terminating adaptive residue-query tree whose maximum pathwise least common multiple is $X^{o(1)}$, allowing different leaf moduli. Make independent selections on two arms and draw each sample uniformly on its selected set. Put $C_*=2/\log(4/3)$. For $0<\epsilon<C_*$ the following hold uniformly in these selection rules within any fixed subpower budget.

* The probability of any common iterate with the **smaller** of the two shortcut clocks at most $(C_*-\epsilon)\log X$ tends to zero, regardless of the other clock. The single-arm bound against a prescribed whole forward trajectory is uniform in that trajectory.
* For any prescribed positive odd ternary unit $b$, a positive fraction on each arm hits $b$ by $(C_*+\epsilon)\log X$. Consequently a positive fraction of independent pairs has met by that deadline on **both** arms. The positive constant may depend on $b,\epsilon$.

**Proof.** Condition on an individual leaf. The power bounds $h=o(\log X)$, $J=X^{1+o(1)}$, $Q=X^{o(1)}$ hold uniformly. Take $t=\lfloor(\log_2 J)/2\rfloor$, except that for the lower statement with $(C_*-\epsilon)\log X$ very small we first prove it for sufficiently small fixed $\epsilon$ and then use monotonicity. Now $e=o(1)$. At the lower deadline $K_0=\lfloor(C_*-\epsilon)\log X\rfloor$, apply (1) with $K=K_0-h\ge t$ and $B=O(y)$, the complete prescribed partner trajectory. A visit to $O(y)$ by $K_0$ is equivalent to membership at $K_0$ because $O(y)$ is forward-invariant. On the comparison source $K\le(C_*-\epsilon/2)\log J$ for large $X$. The fixed-ratio annulus lower-time exclusion in [the meeting-clock theorem](../transfer/MEETING_CLOCKS.md) bounds this probability by $o(1)$ uniformly in $y$. This use is valid on $[J,2J)$ directly, by its one-arm first-passage proof at arbitrary fixed-ratio annulus scale. Mix the leaves, condition on the independent partner, and use the other orientation to treat the smaller clock.

For the upper deadline use (2). On $[J,2J)$, the positive *timed* lower-mass proof of the meeting-clock theorem, equation (7), applies to any fixed-width arc of a full $81$-annulus. Indeed $[J,2J)$ cuts at most two such annuli and contains an arc of logarithmic width at least $(\log_{81}2)/2$ in one of them. The proof of (7) works on the whole circle and uniformly in the arc's location. The scale of that annulus differs from $J$ by at most a fixed factor. Allowing half the desired slack gives a uniform positive fraction of comparison starts hitting $b$ by $(C_*+\epsilon/2)\log J$. After paying $h$ this is at most $(C_*+\epsilon)\log X$. Mix the actual leaf probabilities and multiply on the two independently sampled arms. This proves the positive conclusion. The same upper conclusion, with a possibly smaller constant, extends to $q\le X^{\kappa(b,\epsilon)}$ for some positive exponent: first choose $\kappa$ small enough to absorb $h$ and $\log J-\log X$ in the clock slack, then small enough that the $O(\sqrt\kappa)$ error loses at most half of that comparison mass. [Original ordinary hitting input, Shaik, Theorems I.1–I.2](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00523v1)

This gives exactly the leading threshold for positive-proportion meeting under shrinking-density original-source filters. For a small fixed exponent the lower-time error from this argument is only $O(\sqrt\kappa)+o(1)$; the vanishing full two-sided result uses subpower budgets. At the native head clock a specified surviving profile can consume all available parameter bits. Neither (1) nor (2) then produces the post-conditioning comparison needed for universal convergence.

## The exposed parity excess shifts the entire finite meeting mass

Use the additional relative first-landing and component-density inputs of [the full central-window theorem](../transfer/ALL_EVENTUAL_MEETINGS.md). Take the two independently sampled original arms above and condition on their separate selected cells. Attach the quantities $h_i,p_i,J_i$ to arm $i$. If a union of cells or leaves was selected, these quantities are functions of the individual chosen transcript. Put

$$c_i=h_i+C_*\log J_i=C_*\log X+C_*\log3\,(p_i-h_i/2)+o(1),\qquad w_X=\sqrt{(\log X)\log\log X}.$$

The equality uses $C_*(\log2-(\log3)/2)=1$ and the single rounding $J_i=\lfloor3^{p_i}X/2^{h_i}\rfloor$. There is a fixed $B<\infty$ such that all but $o(1)$ of the probability of eventual meeting is captured by actual meetings with the two clocks in $[c_i-Bw_X,c_i+Bw_X]$ respectively. In particular the probability of such a finite meeting tends to $\sum_C\delta_C^2$, the sum of squared ordinary component densities. Both conclusions hold uniformly over individual cells or selections under a common subpower budget. The upper-deadline approximation uses only separate marginal estimates for visiting a fixed floor and applies also under any coupling of those selected marginals; the lower-time exclusion and the product formula displayed here use independence.

Here are details to justify the changes in scale and the uniformity. On each unfiltered comparison interval $[J_i,2J_i)$ the fixed-floor central-deadline proof in the cited theorem gives, for each large fixed floor $F$, a miss probability $O((\log F)^{-2})+o_{J_i}(1)$ at $C_*\log J_i+B_0 w_X$. The absolute $B_0$ works uniformly as all $J_i=X^{1+o(1)}$. This is its one-arm fixed-ratio estimate, before shrinking-patch transport. Apply (2) to the first time of entry in $[1,F]$: its proof only used that no target is visited by either merging prefix, and the same bound $F$ guarantees that condition. As $F$ is fixed until after the large-$X$ limit, all constraints in (2) hold. Two timely landings from components which eventually meet have a meeting within a further bounded number $b(F)$ of clocks on each arm, by finiteness of the floor. Absorb $b(F)$ in one further $w_X$, union the two marginal misses, and take $F\to\infty$. This proves the upper approximation without coupling the selected arms to independent processes.

On the lower side apply (1) at the integer part of $c_i-Bw_X$ minus $h_i$, with the complete prescribed partner trajectory as the tested set. On the unfiltered arm the lower-time exclusion costs $o(1)$ uniformly in that trajectory. It bounds visits at *any earlier clock* because membership in a forward-invariant trace persists. Under independence we may condition on the partner and take the union across orientations. Finally (1) also gives total-variation convergence of the component law of an individual selected arm to $(\delta_C)_C$: the component map is constant along the actual tail, and its law on $[J_i,2J_i)$ tends in total variation to that ordinary density vector. This last convergence on a countable set uses pointwise convergence of all masses and that their sum is one, as proved in the cited theorem. Thus an independent pair meets eventually with probability tending to $\sum_C\delta_C^2$. Every bound is uniform once the subpower budget fixes a common lower bound tending to infinity for the $J_i$; averaging over the chosen leaves preserves the result.

There are actual small source filters on which the shift exceeds the entire unconditioned window. For $h=\lfloor(\log X)^{3/4}\rfloor$ choose $n\equiv-1\pmod{2^h}$ on both arms. All first $h$ inputs are odd since $T(n)+1=3(n+1)/2$ while $n$ is odd. Thus $p_i=h$, and the positive shift is $(C_*\log3)h/2+o(1)\gg w_X$. For every fixed $B'$, the probability that either original arm meets the other's entire trace by $C_*\log X+B'w_X$ tends to zero. The actual eventual meeting probability on these same independent sources tends to $\sum_C\delta_C^2\ge\delta_{C(1)}^2>0$, and its finite mass is recovered in the shifted window. The full convergence question remains precisely whether more than one actual component has positive mass. This calculation neither assumes nor proves that answer.
