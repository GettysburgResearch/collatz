# Basin frequencies in individual moving progressions

**PROPOSED written synthesis, conditional on the specified analytic inputs.** On positive integers use the shortcut map $T(n)=n/2$ at evens and $(3n+1)/2$ at odds. Let $0\le f\le1$ have ordinary natural mean $\delta$ and satisfy $f(n)=f(Tn)$ for $n>F$, for some fixed $F$. The convergence indicator and each fixed odd-target hitting indicator qualify by the named natural-density result. Complete invariance of the first indicator will also be used below when following long orbits. Define

$$D_f(L)=\sup_{J\in\mathbb N,\ J\ge\max(1,L)}\left|J^{-1}\sum_{J\le y<2J}f(y)-\delta\right|=o_{L\to\infty}(1).$$

Take integer $X\ge2$, positive $q=2^h m$ with $m$ odd, and a single residue $a\bmod q$. Let $r\in[0,2^h)$ be its binary residue, $p$ the number of odd inputs on its first $h$ steps, $c=T^h(r)$ (extend $T(0)=0$),

$$Q=3^p m,\qquad Y=3^pX/2^h,\qquad J=\lfloor Y\rfloor.$$

If $X/2^h>F+1$, $J$ is large enough in terms of $F$, and $Q\le J$, then

$$\left|\frac qX\sum_{\substack{X\le n<2X\\n\equiv a\pmod q}} f(n)-\delta\right|\le D_f(J)+C\left(\sqrt{\frac{1+\log Q}{\log J}}+\frac{Q}{\sqrt J}+\frac qX\right).\tag{1}$$

The absolute constant can be enlarged for the bounded lower cutoff. Using actual cell cardinality in the denominator costs $O(q/X)$ when $q\le X/2$. Applying the same bound to each of finitely many partition indicators controls their joint categorical law, with the sum of their errors.

**Proof.** For $n=2^h u+r$, parity lifting gives $T^h(n)=3^p u+c$. The bound $0\le c<3^p$ follows by reading one binary digit at a time. At a stage with low term $c'<3^v$, adding the high bit lifts it to either $c'$ or $c'+3^v$, both below $2\cdot3^v$. The next even shortcut ends below $3^v$ and the odd shortcut below $3^{v+1}$. All the initial $h$ steps preserve $f$, since $T^i n\ge n/2^i>F$. The condition modulo $m$ fixes $u$ modulo $m$. Thus the selected endpoints form one progression modulo $Q$ cut out by $[Y+s,2Y+s)$, where $s=c-3^p r/2^h$ has $|s|\le3^p$. Replacing that interval by $[J,2J)$ changes at most $O(1+3^p/Q)=O(1)$ selected points, uniformly even when $r,Q$ change. The identities $Q/Y=q/X$ and $|J-Y|<1$ absorb renormalization. For $t=\lfloor(\log_2 J)/2\rfloor$, apply paper 13 Proposition 1 to the odd weight $Q\mathbf1_{y\equiv b\ (Q)}$ of mean one. Its absolute error in the normalized cell mean is at most $C[\sqrt{\log(2Q)/(t+1)}+(2^tQ+Q)/J]$. The assumption $J>2^t(F+1)$ holds eventually. This proves (1). [Odd-modulus comparison, paper 13, Proposition 1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/13)

Paper 18 already transports *individual binary cylinders* to odd-modulus progressions for its occupation theorem. We use the same arithmetic mechanism. Formula (1) retains a simultaneous arbitrary odd divisor of the input modulus, the exposed odd count and the empirical scale of the endpoint; we claim no priority for the binary-cylinder method. The previous repository theorem for fixed automatic tests gave an absolute $o(X)$ discrepancy; (1) divides by the size of each potentially vanishing cell. **Consequences on ordinary sources.** Put $\theta=\log_2 3$. We have $Q\le q^\theta$ and $Y\ge X/q$. Uniformly for all $q\le X^{1/8}$ and all their residues, (1) is at most

$$D_f(\lfloor X^{7/8}\rfloor)+C'\sqrt{(1+\log q)/\log X}+O(X^{-1/5}).\tag{2}$$

Indeed $Q/\sqrt J\ll X^{\theta/8-7/16}=O(X^{-1/5})$. Thus every nonempty union of classes of *one* modulus $q=X^{o(1)}$ has conditional mean $\delta+o(1)$ uniformly, even a single class. The generalization to nonnegative periodic weights follows by a normalized weighted sum of (2). If $f$ is binary and $0<\delta<1$, choose an absolute $c_0>0$ sufficiently small and $\kappa=c_0\min(\delta,1-\delta)^2$. For all sufficiently large $X$, in **every** residue of **every** $q\le X^\kappa$ the conditional proportions of the two values are at least $\delta/2$ and $(1-\delta)/2$, respectively. In particular, assuming an exceptional component exists, no growing initial residue filter of this size can have a homogeneous convergence color. Fixed-unit predecessor positivity ensures both global proportions are positive under that assumption. [Fixed-target positive density and natural-density inputs, paper 12, Theorem 1 and §1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/12)

A macroscopic partial source interval $[A,B)\subset[X,2X)$ of length at least $\epsilon X$, for fixed $\epsilon>0$, admits the same $o(1)$ conclusion. For the odd progression version, the shift-averaging proof of paper 13 Proposition 1 bounds the unnormalized centered sum on *any* such subinterval by $O(Y[\sqrt{\log(2Q)/(t+1)}+(2^tQ+Q)/Y])$: in that proof the CRT exceptional count on an interval of length $L$ is at most $(L+2^tQ)\overline F q_t(d)$, and the two shift endpoint costs are $O(Q\overline F)$. Divide by the actual length, costing $O_\epsilon(1)$. The unweighted mean discrepancy now uses $\sup_{y\ge Y/2}|y^{-1}\sum_{n\le y}f(n)-\delta|$ and subtraction at the two endpoints. Binary transport gives the claim by the preceding endpoint argument. A submacroscopic interval requires its own length factors and is not being treated as dyadic data.

**Profile-sensitive form.** Equation (1) still applies for long binary prefixes when $\log Q=o(\log J)$ and $X/2^h\to\infty$. For example the all-even class reduces *exactly* to sampling the original label on $[X/2^h,2X/2^h)$. A typical native prefix uses order $\log X$ odd/even inputs and leaves only order one parameters if $2^h\asymp X$. The square-root term or the cell count then prevents this argument from supplying a fresh color law. The assertion also concerns original interval sampling. Reweighting by a stopped pair's surviving profile need not be a short congruence test.

## Joint convergence colors on a prescribed early head profile

For $n\in[X,2X)$ compare the actual parent $n$ to $81n+r_0$, for any $0\le r_0<81$. Write $H'=\lfloor\log_2(\#\{X\le n<2X:n\equiv a\pmod q\})\rfloor$ for the fresh binary budget, and suppose $H'\ge1$. Once the $h$ source bits in $q=2^hm$ are fixed, let $s$ be the absolute difference between the two grades after those $h$ clocks (the child starts at grade four). Then for the exact convergence color $f$, conditional on this particular class,

$$\Pr\{f(n)\ne f(81n+r_0)\}\ll\min\left(1,\frac{1+s+\sqrt{1+h}}{\sqrt{H'}}\right).\tag{3}$$

Indeed put $u=(n-r)/2^h$. The two post-prefix endpoints are affine in $u$ with coefficients $3^{v_1},3^{v_2}$, where the $v_i$ are the grades. Their constant terms have magnitude at most $82\cdot3^h$: for the child, $81r+r_0<81\cdot2^h$ and the high quotient costs at most $80\cdot3^h$, while its reduced low term is below $3^h$. The parent is smaller still. Orient by grade, calling the resulting smaller-grade endpoint $z$ and the other $3^s z+D$; $|D|/3^s\le164\cdot3^h$. The low binary digits of $z$ after conditioning range over an arithmetic progression with **odd** common difference $3^{\min(v_1,v_2)}m$. A full block of $2^{H'}$ consecutive remaining parameters therefore gives every future residue of that depth once; an incomplete block costs a factor of at most two by nonnegativity. Apply PR146's *binary-prefix* upper probability bound, before its interval-transfer step, with this exact initial pair and $H'$ fresh clocks. Failure of equality of true colors requires failure of this symbolic merger. [Growing affine-pair estimate, PR146 at `f4310782`](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437/research/upper-bit-mergers/GROWING_ADDITIVE_GAPS.md)

With $q=X^{o(1)}$ and $s=o(\sqrt{\log X})$, (1) and (3) show that the joint two-label law tends in total variation to $(1-\delta)\delta_{(0,0)}+\delta\delta_{(1,1)}$. This holds uniformly for families satisfying those bounds, even when the original class has vanishingly small probability. The same conclusion holds on nonempty unions of admissible classes of one such modulus. In particular every early binary prefix with $h=o(\sqrt{\log X})$ qualifies without testing its grades. Equation (3) continues to apply on later individual prefixes with a fresh high quotient; at native depth that quotient has bounded cardinality. The bound is not summable over head levels merely because its right side tends to zero.

## An unconditional information bound for a fixed ordinary hitting question

A residue-query algorithm receives the scale $X$ and arbitrary advice depending on $X$. It adaptively asks for the full residue of the hidden $n\in[X,2X)$ modulo any positive integer of its choice. At any leaf, let $Q_{\rm leaf}$ be the least common multiple of the moduli actually queried along that branch. There is no common bound on the number of leaves or on the least common multiple across different leaves. Each nonempty leaf is exactly one progression modulo $Q_{\rm leaf}$ cut by the input interval: every input satisfying the same answers follows the same queries and the same branch. We require termination on the finite source interval.

For a binary $f$ as above, if $\max Q_{\rm leaf}=X^{o(1)}$, (2) gives $\Pr(f=1\mid\text{transcript})=\delta+o(1)$ uniformly in nonempty transcripts, including very rare ones. Thus the entire transcript has mutual information $o(1)$ with the binary label, by continuity of binary entropy, and every guess based on it has error probability at least $\min(\delta,1-\delta)-o(1)$. For $0<\delta<1$ and the positive-power budget $\max Q_{\rm leaf}\le X^\kappa$ from (2), the error probability is still at least $\min(\delta,1-\delta)/2$ on every sufficiently large interval. Conditioning on independent internal randomness proves the same for randomized query trees whose paths obey the budget.

Take $f(n)$ to ask whether the actual orbit ever visits 5. It is invariant except at 5, and $\delta$ is positive. The disjoint positive-density basin of 85 gives $1-\delta>0$. Consequently there is an absolute $\kappa>0$ for which this linear lower bound on the number of mistakes is unconditional on the truth of Collatz (with the stated density inputs). The machine and its advice may change at every scale, unlike the earlier fixed-automaton obstruction. This model bounds congruence information on each individual transcript, not the power of an algorithm allowed to inspect the unbounded source directly.
