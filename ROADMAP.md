# Research Roadmap

This roadmap is a map, not a fence.

Contributors may work inside these lanes, combine them, or open a new lane.

## Lane A — Proof of termination

Possible themes:

- descent principles;
- global stopping-time bounds;
- invariant measures with ordinary-integer consequences;
- rewrite-system termination;
- automatic or formal certificates;
- reductions to a genuinely weaker theorem.

## Lane B — Explicit disproof

Possible themes:

- positive nontrivial cycles;
- divergent ordinary seeds;
- invariant sanctuaries;
- exact finite certificates;
- integer-first constructions;
- physical replay of symbolic candidates.

## Lane C — Ordinary extraction and infinite consistency

Possible themes:

- least-root boundedness or escape;
- archimedean tightness;
- transported residue stabilization;
- top-boundary recurrence;
- ordinary sections of `2`-adic constructions;
- proof that a complete architecture has no ordinary point.

## Lane D — Full-denominator cycle arithmetic

Possible themes:

- valuation-word synthesis;
- entire-denominator divisibility;
- mixed-place height forcing;
- prime-power compatibility;
- resultant and Bézout bounds;
- exact replay certificates.

## Lane E — Symbolic dynamics and automata

Possible themes:

- rewrite systems;
- sanctuary languages;
- PDR and inductive invariants;
- finite-state and infinite-state boundaries;
- complexity and recurrence obstructions;
- exact translation to integer trajectories.

## Lane F — Literature and theorem transfer

Possible themes:

- reconstruct classical Collatz results;
- locate exact theorem statements;
- audit source normalizations;
- import useful results from remote fields;
- distinguish prior theorem, analogy, and new reduction.

A known open problem is a valid frontier for new work.

## Lane G — Computation and proof-carrying artifacts

Possible themes:

- candidate searches;
- exact finite exclusions;
- certificate-producing solvers;
- independent implementations;
- reproducible manifests;
- compact verifiers;
- data-driven conjecture discovery.

## Lane H — Formalization

Possible themes:

- canonical definitions;
- map equivalences;
- parity and valuation formulas;
- ordinary versus `2`-adic distinctions;
- load-bearing lemmas;
- certificate checkers;
- resolution-spine formalization.

## Lane I — Alternative and comparative systems

Possible themes:

- partial maps related to Collatz;
- `mx+1` analogues;
- negative cycles as construction templates;
- transfer theorems;
- controlled laboratories that produce reusable insight.

Any transfer back to standard Collatz must be proved explicitly.

## Lane J — Research organization

Possible themes:

- claim schemas;
- dependency graphs;
- dashboards;
- review protocols;
- human–model steering;
- loop detection;
- integration strategy;
- public contributor onboarding;
- evaluation of the project itself.
