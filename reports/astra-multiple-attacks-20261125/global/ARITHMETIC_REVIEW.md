## Normalizations and the uniform one-window result

Use $|p|_p=p^{-1}$, the ordinary real absolute value, all places in $V=S\cup\{\infty\}$, and $d$ independent rational linear forms at each place. For a nonzero rational $y$ with denominator supported in $S$, the product of the absolute values over $V$ is its prime-to-$S$ numerator mass $H_S(y)$. Scaling a vector by an $S$-unit leaves the entire product of forms unchanged. After clearing denominators, passing to a primitive integral representative by its gcd $g$ multiplies the product by $H_S(g)^{-d}\le1$. An exponential height bound $H\le\exp(BN)$ and a product $\le2^{-\eta N}$ allow any fixed $0<\delta<\eta\log(2)/B$ as the Subspace Theorem exponent. Bounded primitive height can also be disposed of directly by taking a repeated vector. [Schlickewei, *On linear forms with algebraic coefficients and Diophantine equations*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(77)90073-7) · [Varjú, *On the Multiplicative Group Generated by Two Primes in $\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z$*, Theorem 5, integral rational-form statement](https://publications.hse.ru/pubs/share/direct/809451625.pdf)

For $U_N\ne0$, independent forms $(a+U_N,U_N)$ at two and the coordinate forms elsewhere give precisely the bound $2^{-N}2^{v_2(a)}H_S(a)H_S(C_N)$. In particular the first replacement keeps the unmodified second coordinate at two. The integer endpoint gives $|a+U_N|_2\le2^{-N}$ even when the endpoint is even. Clearing by $3^{P_N}$ leaves height at most $\max(3^Na,3^N)$, hence $\exp(O_A(N))$ in the proposed range. A sequence violating (2) repeats a rational line, so $U_N/a=t$ with fixed $t\ge0$. Positivity excludes $t=-1$. From $v_2((1+t)a)\ge N$ the factor $2^{v_2(a)}$ alone eventually exceeds the proposed violating bound. With zero carry all departures are even and $2^N$ divides $a$. Any violation of (1) has $a<2^N$, and $2^{v_2(a)}H_S(a)\le a$. This proves uniformity for moving $a$, including ternary powers in (2). At positive even $N$, the cycle from 1 yields $C_N=2^N-3^{N/2}$, below $2^N$. Thus exponent one is sharp in the stated sense.

## Boundedly many parts along an escaping orbit

The pinned simple-path estimate applies uniformly to every finite segment of distinct positive ordinary values. For $u=\lfloor(\log_2 X)/20\rfloor$, heavy length-$10u$ words have at least $6u$ odd steps and contribute at most $2X[(5/4)^{10}/(3/2)^6]^u$ by the binary cylinder bijection. The light words from below $X$ have distinct chronological endpoints at most $X(729/1024)^u+(3/2)^{10u}$ after at most $10u$ terminal exclusions. Both exponential factors have fourth powers below $1/2$. This gives $O(X^{79/80})$ visits below $X$ and a uniformly bounded reciprocal sum by geometric shells. Positivity and passage to finite prefixes give summability on the actual injective orbit. In particular its reciprocal **time tail** tends to zero. The product of the literal one-step carry corrections now gives $\sup_N U_N(x)=O_x(1)$ and $\sup_N U_N(x_q)=o(x_q)$ as $q\to\infty$. Each partial discrepancy has magnitude at most their sum, so the real smallness holds simultaneously over every allowed partition. In a failure sequence $q,N\to\infty$, $D=x_q-x>0$ eventually. All nonzero coordinates have common denominator dividing $3^N$ and the failure bounds $D$ by $2^{(1-\eta)N}$ for some fixed $\eta>0$. Each partial discrepancy after clearing has magnitude at most $2N\,2^N3^N$. Zero parts can be omitted because $H_S(0)=1$ and they contribute nothing to the dyadic form. There are at most $K$ remaining parts. Take a subsequence with fixed dimension at each stage.

The endpoint form is $D+\sum Y_I$ and has dyadic magnitude at most $2^{-N}$. The remaining coordinate products cost at most $D\prod H_S(Y_I)$, with finite-place factors for $D$ at places other than two bounded by one. A fixed proper subspace containing infinitely many of the resulting vectors gives a nonzero homogeneous rational relation. Since $D\sim x_q$ and all $Y_I=o(x_q)$, its coefficient of $D$ is zero. One of its discrepancy coefficients is nonzero, and solving for that coordinate leaves coefficient one at $D$ in the dyadic form. The other coefficients can acquire rational denominators. This is permitted because the forms at each repeated application are fixed rational forms and the unchanged input coordinates still have denominator dividing $3^N$. The eliminated prime-to-$S$ factor is at least one. The product inequality, the height bound, and linear independence at every place therefore persist at each reduction. With no discrepancy coordinates left, $|D|_2\le2^{-N}$ would force $D\ge2^N$. This includes the case of an initially all-zero discrepancy vector. The bounded number of parts is essential to fixing the dimension and performing finitely many reductions.

## Fixed shift and limits of the deduction

For a fixed $q>0$ on an injective orbit, $D$ is fixed and can have either sign. It is nonzero. With one discrepancy $R$ the same small-product estimate uses $|D|$ and produces a repeated line, hence a constant rational $R$. Equation (4) along unbounded $N$ gives $D+R=0$ exactly. A zero $R$ on an infinite subsequence is impossible for the same dyadic reason even before the line argument. The exact endpoint ratio would then be an integral power of three, possibly of negative exponent. The first odd departure on the escape has a ternary-unit endpoint and all later steps preserve this property, so the endpoints on both sides have ternary valuation zero eventually. The power must have exponent zero, forcing equality of the two states at different clocks. This contradicts injectivity. The proof of this fixed-shift assertion uses one discrepancy coordinate. The moving-shift dominance argument has a different hypothesis.

For a balanced changed interval, equal grade on the two sides cancels the external odd prefix after the interval. Its contribution equals an $S$-unit times an integer difference of two nonnegative length-$\ell$ carries each at most $3^\ell-2^\ell$. Its prime-to-$S$ numerator mass is at most $3^\ell$, also in the zero case. This establishes (5) through (3). A proof that every escape admits a partition whose outside-prime masses violate (3) is still missing. The standard positive cycle already produces large one-window outside-prime mass, so the lower bound alone supplies no contradiction to ordinary integrality.
