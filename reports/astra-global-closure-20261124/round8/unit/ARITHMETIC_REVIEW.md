Clear denominators by $D=e^{o(k)}$ and write $Z_0=D3^{p+h}t>0$. Delete signed zero subsums among the other indexed coordinates. The surviving right side still sums to $Z_0$, so it has a pivot even if the original terminal atom list was empty. A proper zero subsum involving $-Z_0$ would have a complementary nonempty zero subsum on the right. Divide the full remaining tuple by its collective gcd $g$. If only one right coordinate survives, its primitive absolute tuple is $(1,1)$.

Write $V=|Z_0|/g$, $H=\max_i|Z_i|/g$, and $P$ for the product of the primitive outside-$S$ parts. Each right coordinate is $b_i2^{u_i}3^{v_i}$ up to sign. Each of its primitive outside parts divides $|b_i|$, even with repeated primes across coordinates. Thus $P\mid VR$, $R=\prod_i|b_i|$. For any surviving $j$, combining $3^{p+h}\mid gV$ and $g\mid |b_j|2^{u_j}3^{v_j}$ and removing only the coprime power of 2 gives $3^{p+h}\mid 3^{v_j}VR$. Since $3\nmid P$, these imply $3^{p+h}P\mid3^{v_j}VR$. Now $V\le H$, $v_j\le h+o(k)$, and $R\le x_kr_k e^{o(k)}$ because each of the two unsplit physical multipliers appears at most once. Consequently $P/H\le e^{-\delta k+o(k)}$. This excludes the two-coordinate case at large $k$.

Setting its coefficient parameter to 1 and its exponent to $1-\epsilon$, then absorbing finitely many exceptions, gives $P\ge c H^{1-\epsilon}$. Using the same corollary once more at $\epsilon=1/2$ and $P\le(t)_{S'}x_kr_ke^{o(k)}$ proves the claimed superexponential outside part. The targets' height hypothesis gives $\log(x_kr_k)=O(k)$. For a nonempty root of length $b$, odd count $a$ and carry $c$, $2^b\ne3^a$, including $a=0$. The $d$-fold formula gives two signed atoms with coefficient $\pm c/(2^b-3^a)$ of logarithmic rational height $O(b)$. Preceding binary lengths and subsequent ternary counts merely shift the two powers, keeping the ternary exponents within the physical total. Empty repetitions and all-even roots contribute zero. For the advertised two-root family, the indicated unit congruence for $2^j$ makes $(2^j+1)/3^d$ a positive odd integer and puts $y=2^d(2^j+1)/3^d-1$ in the required $x_k\bmod3^{p_k}$ class. Large $j$ makes the lift positive and distinct. Its first $k$ states cannot hit 1 as its starting value exceeds $2^k$. The next $d$ odd states rise from $y>1$ to $2^j$ and the following $j$ halves give the first hit. This proves existence in particular for $d=k$. It imposes no upper bound on the outside part of the resulting displacement.
