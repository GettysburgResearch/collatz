## PR197: an actual descent and a remaining information inequality

The exact ordered descent on the residue class
\[
                 x=48t+17\longmapsto y=32t+11
\]
preserves the two required ordinary components. Its predecessor routing
changes height and can exit on an unserved residue; the displayed contraction
is not yet an indefinitely applicable rule on an arbitrary failed unit.
The unit-exit calculation separately transfers the two orientations of a
binary invariant label. Its weighted conclusion uses the native square-root
pair estimate and a finite pressure certificate. In round eight it is used
with precisely that extra logarithmic weight, not silently replaced by
unweighted oriented summability.

The disjoint-crossing and nested-refinement arguments obtain additional
survival costs on the original source grid. Repeated three-class tests reach
more mesoscopic regimes. They do not cover the persistent order-preserving
two-class configuration. The local-information calculation is especially
useful for locating this remainder: on a sufficiently large family of actual
discordant sources, the selected true-color queries must carry positive
information about a future bit block after the stated past and exterior data
are disclosed. No upper bound contradicting that requirement has been
established.

A fixed-degree polynomial-phase estimate from PR200 does not by itself
bound this conditional information. The queried sources lie on large-slope
progressions and are selected using true component labels. Replacing that
conditional law by independent fresh labels would remove exactly the
unproved step.

## PR193: rank, fixed components, and sparse head schedules

The Pell/Lucas argument bounds actual odd transitions whose adjacent
products lie in a low-rank multiplicative coset. Its cycle consequences retain
the external period and verification bounds. The concentration premises for
the resulting convergence classes have not been proved on every ordinary
orbit. Our earlier fixed-window rank observation was not packaged as a new
closure route: high rank is compatible with the unresolved case.

The paired concentration theorem fixes one ordinary endpoint and selects a
second, small-on-a-logarithmic-scale endpoint. The inverse-ray strengthening
fixes a companion on a positive-probability set of scalar inverse rays, with
relative source mass along selected low-profile vertices. It does not make
the upper witnesses into one compatible inverse ray or prescribe that
companion's component.

The head-sampling argument gives recurrence for each supplied unit target
and, on sufficiently sparse schedules, many sequences of target visits.
Those visits belong to different starting integers. The paper also computes
the proposed circle/entropy extension: the resulting Haar measure can retain
an independent constant component bit. Positive entropy of that extension
therefore does not identify the components. The moving-window lower bound
on actual head disagreement has no matching upper bound.

## PR192: the previous positive-lift theorem is credited

The latest parent-certificate note already proves the exact triadic
denominator formula, superexponential lift cost with bounded odd tails, and
the late power-of-two congruence/equality alternative. An initial independent
derivation in this continuation was discarded as a new result once this
overlap was found. Round eight extends the arithmetic question to a bounded
signed presentation of the entire suffix carry, including arbitrarily long
powers of varying short words. The distinction is in the suffix language
and the controlled part of the lift displacement; it is not a new claim
about the previously proved power-of-two case.

The lifetime-gain construction is genuinely monotone on one source, but its
same-clock, same-grade comparisons become ineligible after an explicit
clock on a least hypothetical counterexample. Its repeated-imbalance
examples explain why another local imbalance cannot automatically be charged
to a new gain.

Canonical reselection reduces a one-odd parent's failure to a first bad
Mersenne value with odd exponent. The reselected canonical height need not
retain the original multiplicative bound above the least bad value. Our
unit lane consequently uses the directed unit rank and its established
small-failure constants when claiming strict descent.

The paired-cluster construction provides many actual short intervals at a
fixed first target. Its large discrete-logarithm padding is part of the
ordinary height bill. A dense short interval at a very large imported center
does not yield the required global pointwise paired mass.
