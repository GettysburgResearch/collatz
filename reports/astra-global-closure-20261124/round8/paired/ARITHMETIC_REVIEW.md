For a fixed positive source \(n\), once \(2^k>n\), positivity and \(m_k\ne n\) force \(t_k\ge1\). The two physical affine equations give \(2^\ell r_k-3^h x_k-C=3^{p_k+h}t_k\), with the positive carries on the correct side. From \(3z+1\le4z\) on each positive odd input, \(x_k r_k\,3^{-p_k}\le n r_k(2/3)^k\). Thus fixed \(r_k=1\) qualifies. The general assertion instead requires its stated strictly negative exponential bound on that expression.

Clear all coefficient denominators by their product \(D\), subexponential because the column count is bounded. Discard signed zero subsums of the right-hand columns until none remains. The right sum is still the nonzero left column. Any proper zero subsum after adding the negated left column would either be on the right or leave a nonempty zero complement there. Divide the surviving signed columns together by their full positive gcd \(g\). This gives *collective* primitivity. I inspected the typeset original page 227, equations (9)–(12): collective gcd one and nonvanishing proper sums are exactly the hypotheses. Choosing the left column to lower-bound \(H\) cancels its outside factors in \(P/H\), leaving at most \(g_{(3)}/(Z_0)_{(3)}\) times the other outside parts. Each other right column costs at most its original integer multiplier. This proves \(P/H\le x_k r_k3^{-p_k}\exp(o(k))\le \exp(-\delta k/2)\). For every *fixed* \(\epsilon>0\) the corollary now gives \(\liminf \log H/k\ge \delta/(2\epsilon)\), hence divergence as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).
