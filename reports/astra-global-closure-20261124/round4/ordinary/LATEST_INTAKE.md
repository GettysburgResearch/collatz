# PR202 intake and the independent-prefix count

**Source-qualified intake.** PR202, `3b10b24d12d4b349cb20f4cbc83086c8a09f807e`, `research/synchronized-long-basin-blocks-20261124/README.md`, Git blob `9ce54625c8cc09d2585a3a9c17cb95695196bce6`, §§1–5. The ordinary-prefix source used below has SHA-256 `dc57012b30256c4439e8dcd8c6a73f4f6a9c331f5a7a8ea6b1cacaaf9cad5dc7`. I inspected PR202's theorem, clock transport, unit-population conversion, localization and conditional closing criterion. Its `ClockTransport.lean` (blob `b0081dbb22740f89e7d369c9933d704d3074a539`) is identified there as an uncompiled draft. The reported finite controls in blob `18a63b7daa530f77c3f335b0556159014ae09b11` were not rerun for this intake.

PR202 applies the whole-interval synchronization cylinder S (PR146 `f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437`), pointwise common-length and fixed-total router R (Weaver, Paper 37, Theorem D), and fixed-target predecessor density M. For each fixed positive odd ternary unit \(a\), fixed \(0<d<1/2\), and \(m=\lfloor e^{(\log X)^d}\rfloor\), its theorem gives \(X^{1-o(1)}\) disjoint blocks of \(m\) consecutive integers in any fixed macroscopic \([AX,BX]\), all in the basin of \(a\). Within each block the shortcut paths synchronize at one intermediate unit after a clock \(H_X=o(\log X)\) common across blocks. The intermediate unit and subsequent common clock to \(a\) may depend on the block. The shrinking-interval version, uniform on widths at least \(X/(\log X)^\sigma\) in \([X,2X]\) for \(0<\sigma<10/133\), also uses D, the stated Rhin bound. These upstream inputs retain their original qualifications.

**Joint consequence.** Fix \(0<c<1/4\), and test \(t=\lfloor c\log_2 X\rfloor\) odd states. In each interval covered by the corresponding PR202 theorem, \(X^{1-o(1)}\) of the disjoint synchronized blocks can be kept with a dominating prime at every position of **every odd member's** first \(t\) states. Applying the ordinary theorem to the enclosing macroscopic interval gives \(O(X^{1-\kappa})\) exceptional starts; take a slightly larger constant below \(1/4\) to absorb the fixed interval-length ratio. For a shrinking interval use the enclosing \([X,2X]\), so the same bound applies uniformly. Each exceptional start can spoil at most one of the disjoint blocks. This proves the claim at the ambient \(\log X\) scale. Since \(H_X=o(\log X)\), the tested odd prefixes extend beyond synchronization for all sufficiently large \(X\). Their independence is individual to each original odd source; the members subsequently share an actual trajectory. In the case \(a=1\), none of these prefixes can contain one. Applying the translated theorem separately inside **each** original block instead gives \((1/2+o(1))m\) good odd starts at the shorter horizon \(\lfloor c\log_2 m\rfloor\).

**Weaker count without M.** Krasikov–Lagarias, *Acta Arithmetica* 109 (2003), Theorem 6.1, gives at least \(X^{0.84}\) shortcut predecessors of \(a\). Stripping powers of two loses at most \(1+\lfloor\log_2 X\rfloor\) in multiplicity. For an odd predecessor divisible by three use PR202 §3's unit conversion. Its output is at most \(8X\), and an output has at most five possible odd inputs including the direct unit case. Thus the unit pool has exponent at least \(0.84\). For every fixed \(\eta<0.84\) it satisfies PR202's \(Y^\eta\) hypothesis eventually. Applying that variant and then discarding \(O(X^{4/5})\) blocks at \(t=\lfloor(\log_2X)/16\rfloor\) gives \(X^{0.84-o(1)}\) disjoint synchronized blocks with the same all-odd-member conclusion. Take \(4/5<\eta<0.84\) first and then let \(\eta\) increase. S and R remain assumptions, and D is still used for the shrinking intervals. [Published predecessor theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0205002)

Under the respective hypotheses both the basin of one and a hypothetical nonconvergent basin support these blocks. To contradict the latter, one would still need a fixed verified-floor hit somewhere in every candidate block on a qualifying interval, or a sufficiently small count of blocks avoiding that floor. Within-arm multiplicative independence, including beyond a common synchronization clock, supplies no such hit.
