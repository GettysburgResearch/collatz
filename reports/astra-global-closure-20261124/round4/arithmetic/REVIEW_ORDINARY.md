## Finite source count

For a large divisor $d$ in a set of diameter $Y$, a partial prime-factor product $g\le Y$ immediately followed by its first crossing $gp>Y$ assigns the pair $(g,p)$. This pair determines at most one $d$ in the set since two of its multiples would differ by at least $gp$. The cap $g\le Y$ is imposed on a divisor of $P$ even for intervals entirely above $Y$. Small divisors contribute a separate $D(P,Y)$ term. In the Rankin estimate, the number of large prime factors exceeding $Z=J/\log J$ is at most $J/\log Z$. Their total $p^{-\sigma}$ cost is of the same order as the small integer sum $Z^{1-\sigma}$ at fixed $0<\sigma<1$. All constants here are free of the interval location.

For one realized gap word, adding the prime valuations of the pairwise gcds after truncation proves that its covered state divides the product of actual carries. Each carry is at most $2^K(3^s-1)/2$ under the short total clock, and there are $s$ factors. Thus $\log P_j\le J_0$ as used. Its fixed-source projection has slope $3^j/2^{A_j}>0$ and is injective, including at $j=0$. The image of the same original translated interval has diameter at most $\Lambda L$ once this slope is admitted. No maximum original height enters that estimate.

If the $s$-th cumulative division clock exceeds $K$, at most $s$ odd inputs appear at clocks $0,\ldots,K-1$. Every length-$K$ word can capture at most $\lceil L/2^K\rceil$ sources because two realizers have difference divisible by $2^K$. In the complementary case the $s$ partial sums lie in $[1,K]$ and determine the word injectively. To discard a prefix with multiplier greater than $\Lambda$, its first crossing of the weak threshold before time $K$ suffices. On each such first-crossing cylinder the Bernoulli-$3/4$ mass is the fair mass multiplied by the actual $3^u/2^h$. The cylinders are disjoint, giving the claimed bound on their total fair mass and hence on original sources in any interval. The empty crossing at threshold one is harmless. These three counts give (3). Taking $\Lambda=(3/2)^s$ admits every projected row, so discarding the first-hit term in that use is valid.

## Logarithmic range and target

For $s\le\delta\log_2L$ with $\delta<1/4$, choosing $2\delta<\alpha<1/2$ genuinely puts the binomial parameter below one half. At $\sigma=1/2$ the Rankin correction has logarithm $O(\log L/\sqrt{\log\log L})$. The first term has exponent $1-\alpha(1-H_2(\delta/\alpha))<1$, the crossing term has exponent $1-\eta<1$, and the last exponent is at most $(1+\eta)/2+\alpha<1$ by construction. Rounding $s,K,Y,H$ costs subpowers uniformly in all admitted $t$. For the special length $\lfloor(\log_2L)/16\rfloor$, using $H_2(1/8)<11/20$ and $\log_2(3/2)<3/5$ in the deterministic-slope version gives limiting exponents below $4/5$. The statement asserts no power saving at exactly $\delta=1/4$.

For $s=o(\log L)$ and $K=\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor$, the whole short-word and long-clock combinatorial multipliers are subpower by the entropy bound. The rank correction is subpower when $(1+\beta)(1-\sigma)<1$. Taking the upper limit first and then decreasing $\sigma$ verifies the endpoint exponent $\beta/(1+\beta)$, including $\beta=0,1$.

In Theorem 6.1 of the original paper, $\pi_b(X)\ge X^{0.84}$ holds for every fixed positive $b$ coprime to three once $X$ exceeds the target-dependent threshold. Its map is exactly the shortcut map. An odd $b$ visited from a source is still visited by its odd part, and at most $1+\lfloor\log_2X\rfloor$ sources up to $X$ have a given odd part. Subtracting $O(X^{4/5})$ from $X^{0.84}/(1+\lfloor\log_2 X\rfloor)$ loses a vanishing relative fraction. This establishes the fixed-basin statement for both convergent and hypothetical nonconvergent targets, with no individual forward implication.
