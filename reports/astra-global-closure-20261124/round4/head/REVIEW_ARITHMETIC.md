## Physical denominator and the Paley calculation

On an actual binary block of length \(b\) with odd positions \(i_1<\cdots<i_k\), its carry is \(\sum_{s=1}^k2^{i_s}3^{k-s}\). Appending an odd step replaces \(C\) by \(3C+2^b\), so the maximum over all blocks is \(3^b-2^b\). The parity of \(C\) records the first bit and the stated removal equation recovers the suffix. Thus the templates of equal length and weight really have different carries. A completely rotated word has the same denominator because the two physical multipliers are units at each prime of \(Q^N-P^N\), an integer prime to six. The positive rational fixed point realizes its input parity and the same argument applies at the next rotation.

A cut at a replacement boundary, or any index outside all interiors, writes the concatenation as a product of affine blocks with the *same* pre- and post-change interval slopes. A replacement beginning \(a'\) slots after the cut changes the full numerator by \(Q^{a'}P^{N-a'-h}J\). Division by \(P^{N-1}\), multiplication by \(t^bP^{H-1}\), and \(t^N=1\) give exactly the displayed exponents at the original numbered slots. All operations are legal modulo \(g=\gcd(S,C)\). For every prime \(\ell\), the lower bound on \(v_\ell(d)\) is \(\max(0,v_\ell(S)-v_\ell(C))\), giving the entire divisor \(S/g\) of \(d\).

For prime \(N\equiv3\pmod4\), the Legendre-character sum on a nonzero translate is \(-1\). The zero corrections from the two residue indicators have opposite characters and give the uniform off-origin multiplicity \((N-3)/4\). The sum of the two periods is \(-1\) and their product \((N+1)/4\) in the full residue ring at \(t\). For each prime dividing \(S\), either \(t\equiv1\) and the geometric sum forces that prime to be \(N\), or \(t\) has order \(N\) in its residue field. Thus every such prime is at least \(N\), and the period is a unit at **every** prime power in \(S\). The unaltered identity \(\gcd(S,C)=\gcd(S,\Delta)\) follows.

Pigeonholing the \(K^r+1\) simultaneous-coordinate vectors gives a nonzero \(u<N\), invertible modulo the prime \(N\), with all short signed exponents in \((-N/K,N/K)\). After clearing negative powers the modular root is valid even if \(B\) is not a unit. At a complex common root \(x\), \((2E(x)/B-1)^2=-N\). Over \(\mathbb Z_{(N)}\) the binomial's monic normalization has squarefree reduction because \(N\nmid uPQ\) and every residual root is nonzero. The same holds for the monic minimal factor. In its free power-basis quotient, reduction is therefore reduced. Writing a putative square root \(a/b\) with minimum coefficient valuation zero among numerator and denominator, the equation first forces \(a\in NO\), and after cancellation forces \(b\in NO\). This rules out every conjugate root, including when the original binomial factors over \(\mathbb Q\).

The Laurent-polynomial coefficient bound is \((r^2+r+c)3^{2HL}\) and its shifted degree is at most \(4\max|s_j|\). Combining with \(S\ge Q^{N-1}\) proves (1). ## Cyclotomic degree and reflection

Reduction of the exponents of \(V(X^b)-V(X)\) modulo \(N\) gives a polynomial of degree at most \(N-1\) and constant coefficient zero. Vanishing at \(\zeta\) therefore forces all its coefficients zero by the prime cyclotomic polynomial. This gives the stated stabilizer and the degree \(m\). The monic minimal polynomial \(M_\alpha\) has \(\Phi_N\mid M_\alpha(V)\) over the integers, so reduction mod \(g\) retains all its divisibility. Clearing the \(m\) factors gives a polynomial of degree at most \(2mT\) and coefficient sum at most \(((N+r)3^{HL})^m\). The estimate includes all conjugates without multiplicity.

In an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q_N\), take the finite residue-field extension that splits \(X^u-Q/P\). Its distinct nonzero residual roots lift into the unramified extension by Hensel. The valuation of \(N\) there remains one, so \(\Phi_N(1+X)\) is Eisenstein and \(\zeta\) retains degree \(N-1\). If a conjugate \(V(\zeta^b)\) belonged to this field, subtracting its value from the reduced-exponent representative would force all its nonconstant coefficients equal. This contradicts the explicit off-zero hypothesis. Consequently no root of the binomial zeros the cleared polynomial, including for a reducible global binomial or composite \(g\). The same leading-coefficient and integer Bézout arguments then prove (5). The local unramified/cyclotomic distinction agrees with Sutherland's lecture notes. [MIT lecture 19, pp. 2–3](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2015fa/LectureNotes19.pdf)

For the undamaged reference, \(\alpha\ne0\) by the same off-zero coefficient test. Parseval subtracts exactly the constant Fourier term. The variance of the carries is at most \(\Delta_A^2/4\), and each distinct conjugate occurs \((N-1)/m\) times. The geometric-mean step gives the norm bound (6), while the evaluation at \(t\) gives full divisibility of \(M_\alpha(0)\) by \(g\). Reflection contributes the nonidentity multiplier \(-1\), so \(m\le(N-1)/2\). Moving all odd positions as late as possible bounds the carry by \(2^{L-p}(3^p-2^p)<2^{(2-\log_3 2)L}\), using \(p/L<\log_3 2\). With \(\log N=o(L)\), substitution in (6) yields the claimed per-step lower limit \((\log_3 2)/2\). All claims use a prime slot count and matched interval weights. No assertion that arbitrary primitive ordinary cycles admit these schedules is justified by these arguments.
