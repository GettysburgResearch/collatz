## Actual scalar and finite words

* **PHASE_SEPARATION.md, PS1–PS3.** Let h be the discounted series of actual accelerated odd gaps, c the integer carry and y=h−c−1/2 the centered right-closed ternary representative. The exact physical and carry equations give 2^(b+2c)X' = 3X+2^(−c) for X=q/2^c≥1. On an all-one canonical run starting with c=0 the next carry remains zero; oddness at the last state supplies the extra factor of two in 2^(L+1) dividing q+1. With positive carry, X falls by a factor of at least two at each step until the zero-carry suffix. On an all-three run, X'−1/5 ≤ (3/8)(X−1/5). These give the stated height-dependent run bounds on one actual source. Applying them to the first digit different from the extreme digit at each shift, then using q_n+1 ≤ (3/2)^n(q_0+1), yields the lower and upper grid-distance exponents in (6). The 2^k−1 example certifies sharpness of the **local** lower exponent with varying q. It says nothing about optimality at all depths of one source.
* **A separation bound cannot imply rationality.** For a symbolic example, interleave the constant ternary digit 1 with the digits of any aperiodic sequence over {0,2}. Every shifted tail lies in [1/9,8/9]. Its distances from the two nearest ternary grid points at precision n are at least 3^(−n)/9, although the number is irrational. This is a counterexample to the inference from separation alone, not an ordinary realization. Crucially, #182's exact convergence criterion is h∈Z[1/3]. PR192's exclusion of y∈Z[1/3] is compatible even with q=1: h=1 and y=1/2.

## Cycle polynomial and ordinary evaluation

* **CYCLE_FACTORS.md, §§1–2.** With p odd symbols, g=gcd(A,p), b=p/g, and n=deg(F/gcd(F,Q)), the projected root-order LCM equals g by the cited PR185 result. Rational conjugacy supplies all primitive roots for an occupied order; cyclotomic supports force each nonprincipal fibre to have size at least b/gcd(b,2). Assigning each maximal prime power q of g to an occupied multiple bounds the sum of its totients by n, including the possible single totient one. If m distinct primes occur then m(m+1)/2≤n and each q≤2n. Consequently log p≤log n+sqrt(2n)log(2n), so n/log p→∞. In the primitive positive cone excluding 10/01, the cited PR185 inequality |F₀(3)|>(3/2)^(n−1) gives superpolynomial growth in p. The last implication retains PR185's external linear-forms and finite-certificate inputs. This audit does not replay them. For the prime-power refinement in §3, the possible half-degree split is identified through the square class of 2η in Q(η); no use of that additional classification is needed for the uniform bound just reconstructed.
* **CYCLE_FACTORS.md, §4.** For the two length-28, weight-16 examples I independently calculated gcd(F,Q)=1 over Q[X], the exact closing rational and all literal parity steps, and all six norm saturations. On the specified minimal-discrepancy rotation, substitution W=T⁴/2 simplifies the element whose norm is sought to (T−1) times the sum of 2^(d_i−2i)T^i. Computing resultants against T⁴−2, T⁴+2 and T⁸+4 reproduces the triples (2157911, 9937323, 87054402105125) and (−29281, 411939, 16950045125). Each is componentwise divisible by (47,209,22945). The fixed rationals reduce to 577703/1078415 and 174449/82955; the second orbit's minimum is 88181/82955>1. Thus the norm lower bound can equal one while the complete numerical denominator is much larger. These examples refute a universal strict saving by that test; they neither provide an integer cycle nor prove that full numerical cancellation is impossible elsewhere.
