**Dyadic valuation, (8)–(10).** Each matched shortcut parity reduces the dyadic valuation of the physical difference by one, because both possible linear numerators have odd coefficient. Two physical inputs with difference of valuation \(k\) have exactly \(k\) matching bits. The scalar coding, by contrast, adds one extra dyadic valuation for each matched odd bit. The next scalar bits at the first disagreement have distinct parities. After the first \(L\) matching bits in a prefix and its periodic extension, the first genuine tail starts at \(x_L\). The periodic extension starts again with the original \(n\)-prefix. Its first \(k_L=v_2(x_L-n)\) bits agree, and the following bit exists inside that first period since \(k_L<L\) for all sufficiently large \(L\), by the fixed-source real bound. Thus the additional weighted length is \(k_L+p_{k_L}\), proving the precise formula. A fixed odd denominator in the scalar candidate cannot alter this dyadic equality.

For \(j=i+h\) and \(\min(k_i,k_j)\ge h\), propagating the \(x_i,n\) congruence through \(h\) actual steps gives \(T^h n\equiv x_j\bmod2^{k_i-h}\). The \(x_j,n\) congruence makes \(2^{\min(k_i,k_j)-h}\) divide \(T^h n-n\), which is nonzero by injectivity. Its real upper bound gives (9). If both \(k_i\ge\eta i,k_j\ge\eta j\), this inequality gives \(j-i\ge \eta i/(2\log_2 3)\) for all sufficiently large \(i\). Such clocks have upper Banach density zero. Adding the cancellation exceptional sets therefore proves (10) for the original scalar, without asserting rational realization of the approximants.

**Periodic starts and positive preperiods.** On a finite physical orbit all scalar values are rational by eventual periodicity of the bits. Clear their denominators once. Different physical states have different scalar values by the exact first-disagreement argument. At a nonreturn, the two finite nonzero differences bound the respective entire cancellations by divisibility. At a return with least period \(r\), positive physical carry yields \(2^r>3^{p_r}\); on repeated laps the physical raw logarithmic rate is \(\log2\). The companion rate is at least \(\log3\), its reduced denominator being fixed. Off-return bounds and these limits give the claimed eventual classification for every fixed \(0<\epsilon<\log2\). It distinguishes a periodic **start** from one with a nonempty preperiod.

**Outside-prime part.** I inspected Zheng Xiao's arXiv v3, Definition 1.2, Theorems 1.3–1.4, and the local gcd definition. At \((u_L,y_L+a)\), reduction away from two and three gives the same finite local minimum for \((u_L-1,y_L-s)\) and for the clock difference and carry. This includes primes placed in \(S\) other than two and three; Xiao's gcd still counts them. The outside-\(S\) height of the point is exactly the logarithm of the outside-\(S\) part of the numerator of \(y_L+a\): the first coordinate is an \(S\)-unit, and the second has no outside-\(S\) denominator. The two linear polynomials are coprime and \(U-1\) is nonzero at the origin. After finite zero exclusions this is a torus point. in either row. Only the onset depends on the starting integer, places and translate. Indeed Xiao's Theorem 1.4 specifies \(C=6(\deg f+\deg g)n^2=48\) for two linear polynomials and two torus coordinates. Its right side is \(48\sqrt\delta\,(h(u_L)+h(y_L+a))\). Since log gcd dominates \(\log G_L\), contraposition gives at least \(\delta L\log2\). Equality at the outside-height threshold is covered.
