## Consequence for the forced peak on ordinary arms

Under F and the PR146 scalar pair estimate there are absolute \(c,c'>0\) such that, for every large \(Y\) and each \(4\le m\le c\sqrt{\log Y}\), at least \(c'Y\) odd \(a\in[Y,2Y]\) yield convergent arms \(A=4v,B=8v+1\), \(v=(2^ma-1)/3\equiv2\pmod9\), with every undirected path between the arms visiting \(z=3^{m+1}a-1>(3/2)^m B\). The population is counted in the parameter \(a\) for each specified \(m\). The separate full-orbit bound \(\max O(A)=A\) in the frozen peak theorem was proved for its interpolated family.

Indeed take \(H=\lceil K(m+2)^2\rceil\) with \(K\) large. On the uniform full binary grid, PR146 with initial grade \(m+1\), offset \(-1\), bounds the failure of symbolic fusion of \((a,z)\) by \(C(m+2)/\sqrt H\le1/8\). The scalar martingale bound in the peak proof gives probability at most \(1/4\) that any lower slope through \(H\) exceeds four. Removing both bad sets from the odd half leaves at least \(2^H/8\) residue classes. Choose \(c\) small so \(H+3\le\eta\log Y\) and \((3/2)^H\le Y\). Summing PR203 (4) for target 1 across these binary classes and the single residue \(2^ma\equiv7\pmod{27}\) gives the asserted count. Every lower prefix intercept is bounded by \((3/2)^H\) by the shortcut recursion, so \(T^i a\le5a<z\) through \(H\). If \(T^t a=z\) at a later clock, fusion at \(H\) gives \(T^t z=T^t a=z\), impossible for \(z>2\) in the basin of 1. The initial path of \(A\) to \(a\) stays below \(z\), while \(B\) visits \(z\). The forward hitting-set cut in the peak proof now forces every connecting path through \(z\).
