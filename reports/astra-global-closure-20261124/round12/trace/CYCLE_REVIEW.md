# Independent review of the factor obstruction for the bounded cycle branch

I reconstructed the determinant and checked the written factor estimates in the round 12 cycle README. The conclusion applies to the bounded least-tripling-disagreement branch with its coprime positive period pair, near-critical height, existing rank polynomial, and actual denominator formula.

For the finite divisor calculation, evaluate each row of the integral Sylvester matrix against \((1,y,\ldots)\), ordered to match that row's coefficients. When a composite \(g\) divides both evaluations, the product vanishes modulo \(g\), including \(g\) divisible by bad primes. Its adjugate then kills the determinant on the whole power vector. The constant entry shows \(g\mid\mathrm{Res}\). A nonzero constant auxiliary polynomial works by its usual resultant. The bound for products over shared primes uses the elementary valuation inequality for \(\min\), so pairwise coprimality is unnecessary. The rank identity follows termwise from \(u^h=3,\ u^p=2\bmod D\): writing \(jh=p\lfloor jh/p\rfloor+r\), the exponent is \(h(p-1)-r\), differing from \(p-1-r\) by a fixed unit power. This gives the precise gcd for the reduced denominator.

The three-variable collision box has more lattice points than available integer values because \(R^2 B\ge6pR\) and \(tB\le R^2B/4\). When \(j\ne0\) the two-term polynomial reduces to one nonzero monomial modulo three. Both extreme coefficients of \(H\) survive modulo three, so Gauss's lemma precludes a common factor, even when \(k=0\). When \(j=0\), reduce \(t/p=a/b\) to coprime positive integers; then \(y^b=2^a\bmod D\). Every monic rational factor of \(Y^b-2^a\) is monic integral and a degree-\(f\) factor has constant-term absolute value \(2^{af/b}\); unique factorization forces \(b\mid f\). The resulting irreducible polynomial has a positive real root, which is forbidden for \(H\), since \(H\) divides \(F\) after substitution \(X^t\) and the coefficients of \(F\) are positive. The resultant bound includes the leading coefficient via \(M(H)\), and \(\sum\deg(H)t\le p\) holds with multiplicity. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz to \(\sum n\sqrt t\) and \(\sum\sqrt{pn\ell}\) gives the stated global bound.

For the cyclotomic factors the \(2,3\)-unit subgroup count is valid at every composite \(g\), with no appeal to discrete logarithms outside \(\langle u\rangle\). The root-multiplicity estimate follows by integrating the negative contribution over an arc of length \(m/(ep)\) and using the nonzero integer constant term in Jensen's lower bound. The large-order contribution is \(O(pT^{-1/4})\), and the small-order contribution is \(O(T^{3/2}\sqrt{p\log p})\). With \(T=p^{2/7}\) both are \(o(p)\). Compressed noncyclotomic factors stay noncyclotomic and irreducible. Dobrowolski applies to a monic such \(H\) and a nonmonic primitive \(H\) already has Mahler measure at least two. Consequently the number of occurrences is \(O((\log p)^4/(\log\log p)^3)\). On an ordinary candidate \(d=1\), the exponential determinant and global bound require sum of compressed degrees \(\gg p/\log p\), hence the displayed lower bound for the largest one. This leaves open whether a surviving high-degree factor can absorb the required divisor.

The logarithm estimate, root multiplicity bound, and Dobrowolski application remain written mathematical inputs. The finite divisor accounting itself does not prove complete nondivisibility. [Reviewed factor obstruction](../../../../research/astra-global-closure-20261124/round12/cycle/README.md)
