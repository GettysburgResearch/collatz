# Independent review of the augmented merger and two-arm template theorems

The arithmetic README's Theorems 1 and 2 follow on their stated scopes. I read the written arguments and the Lean file, and reconstructed the following points independently.

For a non-eventually-periodic reference \(y=3^sw\), a common-clock augmented merger cancels the powers of three from the product identity, so both distortions are equal. An unequal linked exponent needs a positive clock. The first odd departure from \(y\) therefore contributes at least \(1+1/(3y)\). The uniform finite-simple-path reciprocal tail forces every linked source to visit some odd \(z<M(y)\) strictly before its witness. This uses one threshold chosen with the reference fixed. If two such sources visit the same \(z\) at \(i,j\), their mutual common-clock coalescence persists at \(t+i+j\), giving \(T^{t+j}z=T^{t+i}z\). Injectivity of the future of \(z\) gives \(i=j\). At a fixed time only finitely many positive integers reach \(z\), by \(x\le2^iz\). No gate selection is required to agree with the reference. Constant \(w\) need not be prime to three for this conclusion.

In the eventually periodic case choose a prescribed phase \(z\) of the common actual cycle. The prefix before its first hit has distinct departed states; later visits to \(z\) add whole laps. Hence \(L(r+P_h(x_r))-qh\) is constant on the class. The coefficient \(L-q\log_2 3>0\) follows from the strictly positive lap charge. The uniform upper bound for the prefix distortion then bounds \(r\). The direct \(1,2\) balance remains invariant under extra laps. I checked the signs and factors of two in the explicit cap \(4^r2^k\le4^{s+P_k(x_s)}3^rw\). For the elementary reciprocal estimate I checked the heavy-word Chernoff factor, the distinct common-clock images of the remaining states, the end correction, both rational fourth-power inequalities and integration from a fixed floor.

For Theorem 2, the comparison \(w\mid b\) uses both the oddness of \(w\) and \(3\nmid w\). Normalize by the arm with larger total odd count. Its distinguished term is the starting integer; every correction has ternary valuation bounded above by its fixed coefficient valuation. A minimal vanishing subsystem containing that term has no proper zero subsum, so ESS gives a finite set of projective solutions for the fixed pair of word lists, orientation, subsum and \(b\). A zero pair without a correction forces the exact equalities of clock and augmented grade and \(b=w\). When a correction occurs and the primary source is the contender, its bounded ternary valuation bounds \(s\). At bounded seed, each powered block either fixes that seed or has bounded repetitions by the nonzero integer \(Dn-A\) and the factor \(2^{Ee}\). This holds for zero centers and all-even words. With the contender then ranging over finitely many endpoints, the same nondegenerate subsystem argument bounds \(r\) on the other arm.

Removing initial halvings can leave a nonempty suffix of one bank word plus the remaining whole powers, increasing the block budget by at most one. If the target clock stops inside the initial halvings, the meeting value is even. An actual positive-time departure from the odd source then permanently makes the path a ternary unit, so \(s=0\). The factor \(2^j\) in the terminal term adds one group generator with zero ternary valuation; the same bound on \(r\) applies. Zero clocks and zero-length lists are covered. Infinitely many class minima follows from finitely many members per class, with the bank and budget fixed first.
