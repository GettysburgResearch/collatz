For an odd reference, equality of its positive-time correction with the source correction gives a strictly positive lower bound on the latter log. The uniform small tail on a finite simple path forces a visit to a bounded odd state. Two linked sources sharing that state at times i and j meet synchronously themselves by transitivity. Advancement to time t+i+j gives equal future states from the gate with elapsed times t+i and t+j, and hence i=j by injectivity. For a fixed gate at that fixed time there are at most twice-per-step growth bounds on its preimages, namely source at most the gate times 2 to that time. An even base contributes a common initial halving prefix. The finite simple-path estimate handles distinctness of equal-time images by retaining only indices with their whole segment present; this costs at most the word length at the end. Its heavy-word generating function and its light-endpoint bound each give the asserted saving.

For a periodic reference, the first visit to the designated phase has no repeated prior state. A repeat would place that phase either outside the future or on a cycle already traversed. Distinct roots linked to that reference share the cycle and the phase. After their common meeting, advance to that phase, beyond both first visits. Between either first visit and this last visit each full lap adds L departures and q odd departures. Thus the invariant is L times the augmented grade minus q times elapsed time. Expressing elapsed time from the exact correction gives the positive coefficient L minus q times log base two of three, because the actual positive cycle has lap correction strictly greater than 1. The uniform simple-prefix correction bound applies even if the first visit follows an entire partial lap. The invariant then bounds every exponent of the linked class.

For clocks h,k joining a tower start and arbitrary fixed nonperiodic reference, let d=h-k and g be the tower exponent plus the source odd count minus the reference odd count. Substitution in the endpoint formula gives three to the g equal to two to the d times the reference/source-base ratio times the reference/source correction ratio. Both log corrections lie between 0 and 160. For a fixed bounded lag this leaves finitely many integer g, uniformly over both clock lengths. Two witnesses at equal (d,g) can extend along the reference until their reference clocks coincide. Their source times and grades then coincide as well, so they lie in one finite class. A periodic reference has no such simple-path implication without further hypotheses.

For the sharper high-path estimate, fixing the first parity bit at 1 leaves j-1 free bits. More than m odd departures require at least m ones in those remaining bits. Among lighter starts the additive part is bounded by iterating the odd branch m times from zero, since an intervening halving can only decrease it. Their distinct retained endpoints lie in the advertised interval. Each dyadic interval of the reciprocal integral contributes at most the upper-end count times 2 to the minus j. The simple-path bound above the last interval gives the stated remainder. This application counts all small odds in the upper bound and so also bounds any restricted large-odd tail.

I recomputed the 1,936 heavy tails using Pascal recursion across successive *lengths*. If the threshold m stays constant, the next tail is twice the current tail plus the binomial coefficient at (j-1,m-1). If it rises by one, the next tail is twice the current tail minus the coefficient at (j-1,m). Starting with the direct length-65 binomial sum, Horner accumulation and the final tail give the upper bound rounded upward to six decimals **2.836949**. This confirms the exact cutoff inequality. Its consequence is strict separation of the same-clock correction ratio from 3 and 1/3, forcing zero augmented-grade difference. The application to every nonperiodic orbit additionally uses the published finite ordinary verification. The bounded-lag theorem itself needs no such external range.

I checked the periodic Lean statement's quantifiers. Its invariant is stated for any supplied period L and any two hits of the designated phase; the written estimate takes a first hit and a positive least period for its stronger analytic use. These are compatible. The conclusions concern fixed towers and a fixed reference/lag bound. Existence of a lower-source merger with arbitrary lag is still open.
