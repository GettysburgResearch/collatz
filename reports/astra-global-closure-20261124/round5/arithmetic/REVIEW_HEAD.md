*Uniformity in the initial data.* The scalar pair estimate in PR146, f431078244e868dc699fad011044f0d5e7f76437, GROWING_ADDITIVE_GAPS.md (1) and its proof, is uniform in the signed integer offset and the initial level. For a triple, before any fusion the event is contained in each pair's survival. Setting $H_0$ of order $1+A^2+\log(2+Z_*)$ both makes the scalar tail $O(\sqrt{H_0/t})$ and absorbs the initial square-root-offset moment into the exponential remainder of its convolution, since $\rho<1$. On the triple-live prefix each pair upper-bounds the triple's subsequent low-level occupation. PR197, 9ab91d964b38fc870d1034bac628d62f59488467, KILLED_GRADE_OCCUPATION.md (3),(5) has absolute constants before summation over the three labeled pairs. The $A^2+H_0$ cost of the initial product follows from the second moments of the signed grade martingales, with the stated bounded increments. A threshold of size $K=H^{3/8}$ charges the small-gap probability by $H^{-1}K^2P(H/2)$ and the large-gap one by the product moment divided by $K^2$, yielding the exponent $-3/4$. The second bootstrap gives uniformly $O(H_0)$ for the product moment and $O(H_0/t)$ for survival. The larger potential costs $O_\gamma(H_0^{1+\gamma/2})$ initially. With the reciprocal survival estimate inserted, its two boundary costs are $O_\gamma(H_0 H^{(\gamma-1)/2}\log(2H)^{(1+\gamma)/2})$ per dyadic shell. These are summable for $\gamma<1$. Absorbing the small-spread shell against $[H/4,H/2]$ uses only survival monotonicity and bounded overlap. Thus the stated $Q^p$, not an uncontrolled constant depending on the offsets, follows.

*The interior timing assumption.* The proof of Theorem 6 of Weaver's paper 13 v1, lines 174–181 at the current canonical page, explicitly starts at any $2\le k\le K$ with arbitrary integer offset. Equation (21) bounds the unconditional probability of completing $\ell$ mixed jumps with inaccurate elapsed time; it includes the quota-completion event. With $K\le3H$, $\ell\asymp H^{1/4}$ and starting gaps above $K_1H^{1/4}$, a pair cannot reach one on or before the upper quota. Each waiting time is finite almost surely. Here $R=O(\log H)$, $\ell\ge4R^2$ eventually and the error tends uniformly to zero for fixed deviation. All three pair marginal futures arise from the same still-fair binary suffix. Independent increments across *different pairs* are unnecessary: the third pair's mixed count supplies the cross-covariance via polarization. The covariance, Hessian, Taylor error and dyadic shell charges agree with PR197's TRIPLE_SUBCRITICAL_MOMENTS.md (3)–(11). The external conditional-on-excursion theorem alone would not justify this step. [Weaver, paper 13 v1, (21)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/13)

*The selected original sources.* Paper 124's (7)–(12) constructs a strictly positive sibling on every lift of a good stopped word, with equal counts and equal endpoints, and bounds the failure at remaining length $k$ by $k^{-8}+O((\log k)^2/k)$. Summing over the rightmost representatives costs $O((1+\log D)^3)$. Sorting representatives uses only the shared exposed prefix. After it, each of the three profiles is fixed in the same binary quotient, whose law is Haar; odd parameter slopes preserve that law. An actual third label requires all three full profiles to survive to the common deadline, since all labels transform by the same permutation at equal clocks. The argument only bounds this necessary finite event and never conditions on a basin. At a later deterministic clock it averages representative counts conditionally before summing over the still-alive head prefixes, preserving the scalar survival factor. Two length-$2^h$ grids cover each original parent interval; restricting to $J$ costs nothing in the upper bound. The reported series conditions give $\beta<1/12$, $\alpha<1/4$ after the union over clocks, and $\alpha<1/2$ at a single clock. The outstanding finite mass of the remaining two-label population is untouched. [Weaver, paper 124 v1, (7)–(12)](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/124)
