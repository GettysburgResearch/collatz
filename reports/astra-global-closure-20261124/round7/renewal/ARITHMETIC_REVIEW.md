* **Actual coordinates.** Splitting the $D(n)$ series gives $C_i/Q_i=\alpha+U_i s_i$. When two physical values agree for $t$ initial parity bits, the companion tail at the first disagreement differs by an odd dyadic integer. Each earlier shared step contributes one plus that step's bit to the difference valuation. This gives $t+p_t\le2t$, with $t=v_2(x-y)$ by the physical shortcut. Multiplication of the two physical one-step estimates gives $2^p\ge2^{3h/5}(x_i/x_j)^{3/5}$, hence the claimed $\log(256/243)/5$ and the record-trial bound by summing the geometric decay. Injectivity permits the stated floor.
* **Content.** Put $b=A-Q$. Each row entry is divisible by $\gcd(b,C)$. Any prime dividing $Q$ is excluded from the row gcd by $b^d$, since $Q$ and $b$ are coprime. For every other prime, the two entries $Q^{d-1}b,Q^{d-1}C$ give the exact minimum valuation. This covers arbitrary powers, signs, degree one, and $C=0$.
* **Local product.** Each column subtraction is bounded at its place because the omitted pivot has at most that monomial's envelope there. The generator substitutions are invertible integral triangular changes at the relevant completions. Eliminating a pivot whose value at $R$ is a unit identifies the kernel lattice with its complementary coordinates. The unrounded product is bounded by a constant times $A_-^d(Q_-/Q_+)^d[(Q_+/Q_-)(A_+/A_-)^2 e^w]^E$. The clock ratios at most $3^K$ and $4^K$ yield the factor $48^K$. Since the row contents are prime to six, removing all 2- and 3-parts of a nonzero integer determinant preserves divisibility by their full product.
* **Leading valuation.** Strictly increasing $a_i$ force the minimal row assignment to put largest degree on earliest rows. Swapping a degree inversion pays at least $g$. Increasing the degree of a lower-column summand to $d$ pays at least $a_i\ge N$. In the minimal allocation each row contributes a fixed $U_i^k$. All permutations internal to its equal-degree groups then sum to a block Vandermonde determinant. Dividing the top columns $s^j-(-\alpha)^j$ by $s+\alpha$ leaves monic polynomials of successive degrees. The sum of the valuations of all the differences, including the top factors from the initial fixed source, is bounded by $2r[d+\sum_k\binom{m_k}{2}]$. The strict gap dominates every competing summand; equality at the gap would not suffice. The short multiplicative interval covering and dyadic summation give both exceptional-clock counts.
* **Algebraic coefficient case.** At one clock the three envelopes use the same primitive vector $z/G$. Scaling by $G$ divides the real product by $G^M$ and changes neither finite-place norm. Each local basis is algebraic at its own place when the stated actual dyadic embedding of $\alpha$ is algebraic. I checked the mathematical typesetting of Evertse's Theorem 1.1, which explicitly allows $r=n\ge2$, general position at each place, primitive rational-integer vectors and an exponent $r-n-\eta$. It does not require all conjugates to have the small local sizes of the chosen dyadic embedding. The bound on the height is exponential in $i$ for fixed degree. Taking the limsup for fixed $d$ before sending $d$ to infinity gives the stated zero fractional loss. [Evertse, Theorem 1.1](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~evertsejh/dio2011-padicsubspace.pdf)
* **Exceptional hyperplanes.** A rational relation of the primitive rows gives a nonzero homogeneous form; substituting $(1,U,UW)$ remains injective on that homogeneous degree. An infinite rational subsequence chooses a geometrically irreducible component fixed by Galois. Discarding finitely many singular points, normalization is single-valued at the remaining rational points; integral generators justify one fixed enlargement of $S$. Siegel forces genus zero and at most two geometric punctures. The nonconstant regular unit $U$ needs at least a zero and a pole, so there are exactly two, each rational. A rational coordinate $t$ makes $U=ct^r$, $r>0$, and $UW$ Laurent. Since $v_2(t)\to+\infty$, boundedness of $UW$ removes negative terms and gives a rational limit. The actual limit is $D(n)/3$, irrational on an injective ordinary orbit by the pinned signed-rational companion argument. Therefore each exceptional subspace contributes finitely many clocks.
