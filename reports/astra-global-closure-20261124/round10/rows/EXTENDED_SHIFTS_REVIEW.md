For \(t>p/(\ln p)^2\) the bound \(s\gg p^{1000/479}\) implies \(X/p^3=st/p^3\to\infty\) uniformly. The integer-valued **multiplicity** of any given lifted gap is at most \(1+4p^3/X<2\), so all \(p\) are distinct. Two equal \((b,J)\) lists give the same middle gap by the two coprime moduli, even when those lists include input cuts. Therefore the \(p\) starting ranks have distinct decoded lists.

A cyclic input-cut interval \(\{p-t,\ldots,p-1\}\) has at most two boundary jumps after any fixed rank translation. Across positions of an \(L\)-window this gives at most \(2L\) jump locations. The vector of \(L\) cumulative output-wrap counts adds at most \(L\) further jumps on a linear ordering of starting ranks. Taking that common refinement proves the joint bound \(3L+1\). I also tested this counting inequality at 8,048 independent parameter tuples with \(10\le p\le31,\ p<h<2p,\gcd(h,p)=1,\ 1\le t\le p/3,\ L\in\{1,2,5,9\}\).

In the probability code, a mismatch always transmits the ordered pair of base colors, which reconstructs \(J\) on either side of the input cut. On a match, \(J=0\) away from the cut and \(J=c_r\) at the cut, explaining the three-way extra label only there. Code injection follows from decoding the joint list; multiple encodings of a hypothetical unused word are irrelevant. Uniform membership in the set of starting ranks gives actual mean \(LE_t/p\) mismatches and \(Lt/p\) cut visits by translation at each phase. For a window with \(m\) mismatches and \(c\) unmarked cuts the cost is

The Bernoulli code probabilities make no independence assumption about the actual starting ranks or their colors. Finally, additive order at least ten makes \(0,t,\ldots,9t\) distinct. Their ten positive circular gaps sum to \(p\), and a shortest gap \(v\le p/10\) equals \(\pm jt\bmod p\) with \(1\le j\le9\); this includes the wrap-around gap. Reversing or translating a shift preserves its Hamming distance. Applying the triangle inequality \(j\) times gives \(p/83<E_v\le9E_t\). For small additive order, the temporal rank shift \(h\bmod p\) gives a second estimate. On each physical edge \(a_r=b_r+k_r-k_{r+h}\), where \(a_r,b_r\in\{1,2\}\). A marked change thus forces \(a_r=3-b_r\), and an unmarked edge gives \(a_r=b_r\). Equal normalized words and masks determine equal sequences of actual exponents including their order. At \(N=\lceil\log_3(1+8s)\rceil\) the affine numerator for this physical word determines the terminal odd residue modulo \(3^N\), since the power-of-two multiplier there is invertible. Both positive terminal sites are below \(8s<3^N\), so the residue is injective on them. Translation by \(Nh\) permutes the primitive cycle's distinct sites. Encoding the at-most-\(N+1\) normalized words and the mask therefore gives \(p\) distinct physical codes. The mean mask size is \(NE_h/p\), with no independence needed for the actual masks. At \(\theta=1/20\) the code cost bound under \(E_h\le p/20\) contradicts \(\ln p\), since \(N\le(5.12/\ln3)\ln p+O(1)\). I checked its exact arithmetic \(20^{640}<19^{608}3^{125}\) independently.

For a shift \(t\) of additive order \(2\le d\le9\), every nonconstant \(t\)-coset contributes at least two cyclic mismatches among its \(d\) vertices. Their union \(V\) thus has \(|V|\le dE_t/2\). Each coset occupies one class modulo \(p/d\) in rank order, so the contiguous \(b=2\) interval of length \(h-p>p/2\ge p/d\) meets every coset. If the source and destination cosets of a temporal edge are both constant, then any \(b=2\) edge from the source coset has the same two endpoint colors. Its physical exponent is at most two, forcing destination color at least source color. Hence every decreasing temporal edge touches \(V\), and at most \(2|V|\) such edges exist. The signed temporal changes sum to zero and each is \(0,+1,-1\), so \(E_h\le4|V|\le2dE_t\). This proves \(E_t>p/(40d)\) for these orders.
