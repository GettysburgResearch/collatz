## The two evaluations and the exact radii

The positive odd recursion gives $L_{n+1}-L_n=u_n/3$ and $L_{n+1}/L_n=1+1/(3q_n)$ for $L_n=q_nu_n$, $u_n=2^{A_n}/3^n$. The separated-visit estimate on one injective orbit gives $\sum 1/q_n<\infty$. Its hypotheses apply since determinism and injectivity give distinct odd iterates. The real product therefore has a finite limit $\Lambda>q_0$. The positive real series $\sum u_i=3(\Lambda-q_0)$ converges. On the dyadic side $v_2(L_n)=A_n\ge n$ because $q_n$ is odd, so the same finite telescope has limit zero and $F(1/3)=-3q_0$ there. The two rational numbers that would arise from evaluating a rational function must agree. Indeed, if $QF=P$ for coprime rational polynomials, plugging the point into the absolutely convergent real series gives $Q(1/3)F(1/3)=P(1/3)$. A zero denominator would give a common rational root. With the denominator nonzero, evaluation of the same finite polynomial identity in the dyadic field gives the identical rational value, contradicting the opposite signs. This covers both interior and boundary evaluation at the real place.

Distinct positive integers eventually leave every bounded interval. Thus $u_n\to0$, and $\log q_n=n\log3-A_n\log2+O_q(1)$. Writing $s=\liminf A_n/n$, $t=\limsup A_n/n$, yields $1\le s\le t\le\log_2 3$. Applying Cauchy–Hadamard to each local absolute value gives $2^{-t}$, $2^s$ and 1 at infinity, at 2 and at each odd prime respectively. In particular the sign switch from the limsup to the liminf at 2 is necessary. The point $1/3$ is always interior to the dyadic disk. If $t=\log_2 3$, absolute real convergence is uniform on the closed complex disk by the majorant $\sum u_n$. Nonnegative coefficients force a singularity at its positive boundary point: a local holomorphic extension and a Taylor center just inside the circle would converge past that point, and expanding the nonnegative Taylor derivatives at the new center would extend the original convergence radius. The finite radial limit excludes a pole. ## The algebraization theorem applies to the proposed domains

I independently consulted Bost–Chambert-Loir's original arXiv v3, Definition 5.16, Theorem 7.9 and the sufficient condition $2'$ immediately following it, together with Example 7.10. The conclusion is rationality over the base field, provided the capacitary degree is strictly positive. The base is the smooth geometrically connected projective curve $\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Q}$ and the point is its rational origin. [Bost and Chambert-Loir, *Analytic curves in algebraic varieties over number fields*, §§5 and 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0702593)

* At infinity use the given bounded simply connected conjugation-invariant domain $D$ containing 0. Its complement has interior and is nonpolar. At each odd prime use the open unit disk. At 2 use the open disk of radius $r\in2^{\mathbb Q}$, $r<2^s$. The complement of any of these finite-place disks in the projective line is a nonempty affinoid. The rational-power restriction on $r$ permits its definition by an inequality involving a power of the coordinate. Outside the single exceptional finite place the open disks are the reduction tube of the zero section of $\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Z}$. This verifies every tube condition.
* At infinity the assumed extension is single-valued and meromorphic on $D$. At each finite place the original power series converges on the prescribed open, hence represents a rigid analytic function there. The integral coefficient series is a regular formal function on the completed zero section of $\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Z}$. Its graph is formally smooth there with the source coordinate as parameter. Thus the paper's sufficient condition $2'$ holds with $N=1$; local convergence at infinity is already established.
* For a conformal map $\phi$ of the unit disk onto $D$ taking 0 to 0, its Green function at 0 has constant term $\log|\phi'(0)|$ after subtracting $-\log|z|$. The finite disks give the logarithms of their radii, in the normalization of Example 7.10. The degree on the tangent line is therefore $\log\rho(D,0)+\log r$. If $\rho(D,0)>2^{-s}$, density of $2^{\mathbb Q}$ gives a strictly smaller-than-convergence $r$ with positive sum. Theorem 7.9 and the opposite-sign evaluation then contradict each other.

When $s=t$, the initial disk has radius $R=2^{-s}$. Any meromorphic continuation across a point of its circle can be restricted to a small round neighborhood intersecting that disk in one connected lens. A nonreal center also provides the conjugate local germ by real coefficients, with the two small neighborhoods disjoint. Their union with the convergence disk is a bounded simply connected conjugation-invariant domain, with regular piecewise-circular boundary. Agreement in each lens ensures a single-valued meromorphic continuation on the union. Its conformal radius is strictly larger than $R$, by the strict case of Schwarz's lemma applied to the disk inclusion followed by the inverse Riemann map. Hence every point of the circle is singular for meromorphic continuation. For $s<t$ a local continuation can have conformal radius below $2^{-s}$. The proof correctly retains only the conformal-radius ceiling in that case. It does not apply a zero-degree rationality criterion without prior algebraicity.
