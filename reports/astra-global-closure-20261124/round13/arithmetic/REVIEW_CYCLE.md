**Physical population.** Summing the log updates on an arc gives a correction sum between 0 and the complete positive lap sum $\delta$. After removing its linear clock share the error is at most $\delta$. The assumed strict upper bound $\delta<(\ln2)/(2p)$ gives strict order between adjacent ranks and puts the last normalized site below $2s$. With $x_j<8s$, the elementary lower estimate on each log term yields $s\gg p^2$. Hence the minimum adjacent normalized gap tends to infinity. The wrapping edge from $R=2p-h$ has $b=2$, ends at band zero, and forces $k_R=0$ and $z_R=(4s-1)/3$. The gap required for a later band-two site to exceed the ceiling is exactly $11/12$. The earlier band-two sites and both lower bands satisfy it. This confirms the mask used in the uniform model at sufficiently large admissible pairs.

**Reflection and multiplier.** For $r<p-d$, the reflected edge starts at $p-1-r-d<p-d$. For $r\ge p-d$ its start is $2p-1-r-d\ge p-d$. Thus it has the original $b$ and reversed direction in both cases. Forward and reflected increments consequently have opposite weak signs. In a convolution window with constant $b$, nonnegative weights force every positive-weight left increment to vanish. The positive constant weight tests the current changing edge. Each of the two cuts lies strictly inside at most $m$ length-$(m+1)$ cyclic windows. This proves $E\le2m$, including windows whose reduced exponents coincide when $m\ge p$ by the trivial bound. The pinned temporal theorem gives $E>p/20$ for the stated ordinary branch.

For a conjugate pair at angle $\theta<\pi/2$, taking $n=\lceil\pi/\theta\rceil$ makes the internal coefficients of $V_\theta(1-2z\cos\theta+z^2)$ zero by the sine recurrence. Since $(n-1)\theta<\pi\le n\theta<\pi+\theta$, both terminal coefficients are nonnegative, and the highest is positive, including when $n\theta=\pi$. Rescaling by the root modulus preserves signs. For the product multiplier $V$, the nonnegative polynomials are $(QV)^*V$ and $(QV)V^*$, of degree $\deg Q+2\deg V$. The reciprocal gcd has positive reversal sign since it does not vanish at one. For $r>1$ the geometric derivative argument is applied to $1/r$, yielding $r-1\le4r\theta$; $r\le5$ yields the stated $25\theta$ bound. Evaluating the primitive integer factor at one, the leading coefficient is at most four and every other distance to one is at most six. Together with Smyth for monic nonreciprocal factors, the elementary leading-coefficient bound for nonmonic ones, and $M(C)\le4\sqrt p$, this proves both maximum-degree conclusions with multiplicities. It identifies no particular factor of the determinant. [Smyth, 1971](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/3.2.169)

**Fourier block and erased interval.** Every six-edge segment supplies at least two rises and two falls. Greedily lowering the band on each admissible edge stays below a feasible comparison filling and hence respects every prohibition of band two; the backward analogue uses the opposite edge type. Six further edges connect either prescribed binary boundary pair. This leaves the central two edges of a 26-edge block available with neighboring bands $2-b_{j-1}$ and $b_j-1$. Both central bands work with the same exterior and all other sites fixed. They change the residue by $2w_j$. At a nonzero frequency the centered representatives of $2tw_i$ remain nonzero even when $t$ is a nonunit. An interval of $L+1$ small representatives would give exact integer updates because the absolute size of the modular difference is strictly below $g$. Iteration makes the nonzero first representative divisible by $3^L\ge g$. Thus every frequency gets one large-phase switch per full block. Independence after conditioning holds for disjoint interiors with their endpoints fixed. The two ordered switch pairs in the second-moment identity give the claimed $2^{-51}$ gain, and inversion divides the sum of character errors by $g$.

Removing fixed unit factors leaves the positive carry $S$ in (6). For $16m3^m<g$ a residue determines at most one positive $S$ in its allowed range. After dividing by the known initial band coefficient and subtracting the leading power of three, the remaining positive shorter carry is odd, recovering the first exponent exactly by its 2-adic valuation. Iteration recovers $m-1$ exponents. The prescribed two endpoint bands and the $b$-sum fix the final exponent. The six-edge binary boundary choices give the displayed uniform lower bound on fillings even for this fixed exterior. Since $\ln2/(6\ln3)>1/20$, the claimed large-divisor power bound follows with an absolute constant.

**Ledger and scope.** Expanding $\ln\gcd$ as a sum of indicators over all prime powers includes every valuation layer once. The sum of its log weights is $\ln D$, so splitting at $e^{\sqrt p}$ costs at most $O(p e^{-c'\sqrt p})$ in total discrepancy. For the uniform main term, writing $\ln(p!)$ by prime-power valuations proves the claimed $O(\ln p)$ bound after the complementary $q>p$ contribution $\le(\ln D)/p$. The divisor-count union bound then gives the high-cancellation tail for the specified uniform population. A surviving ordinary candidate may be exceptional in that population.
