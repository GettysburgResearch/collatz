# Independent review of the high-endpoint exact-row upper bound

**VERIFIED, written theorem.** The subject is [the round-nine ballot bound](../../../../research/astra-global-closure-20261124/round9/rows/UPPER.md) for the quantity \(N_b(q,\epsilon)\) defined by [PR201, head 2978d84f8a1fda9083593b26c6bf724d5976d859](https://github.com/GettysburgResearch/collatz/blob/2978d84f8a1fda9083593b26c6bf724d5976d859/research/killed-green-head-closure-20261124/escape-analysis/README.md). The additional internal condition in PR201 only removes words. This review does not cover the analytic floor F or the separate conditional estimates in the rows packet.

In a length-\(2b\) word beginning at an odd input, assign height increment \(-1\) to odd and \(+1\) to even. Just before the \(r\)-th odd input counted from the end the height is \(r-D_r\), where \(D_r\) is the number of remaining even steps. This follows by subtracting the remaining height change from the final zero. That odd input contributes exactly \(3^{r-1}/2^{r+D_r}\) to the final real offset, including the immediate \(1/2\). If \(H\) is the largest height, replacing \(D_r\) by \(\max(0,r-H)\) and summing gives \(\beta\le2(3/2)^H-1\). The second geometric sum can be extended to infinity because all terms are positive. Hence the threshold \(\beta\ge\epsilon q\) requires the stated ceiling \(h=\lceil\log_{3/2}((1+\epsilon q)/2)\rceil\), including equality cases.

Reflecting after the *first* visit to a positive \(h\) is an involution between the relevant balanced walks and those ending at \(2h\). The initial down-step remains in place, so the latter have \(b-h-1\) down-steps to allocate in the last \(2b-1\) positions. The number is zero when \(h\ge b\); \(h=b\) is already impossible. For \(h\le0\), all first-down balanced words qualify for this height condition. One word yields at most one input ending at the prescribed ordinary \(q\), because its affine slope is \((3/4)^b\ne0\).

For \(q\ge(3/2)^{351b/352}\), the lower bound on \(h\) gives \(b-h-1\le b/352+O_\epsilon(1)\) independently of \(q\). For each fixed \(\epsilon>0\), optimizing the elementary binomial bound on this interval yields exponent at most \(\log_2(704e)/352\). It is strictly less than \(1/32\), since \(704e<1936<2048\). Counts zero and one satisfy the bound directly. This verifies the strict inequality with \(2^{\lceil b/32\rceil}\) at large \(b\).

The known lower-bound family lies below this cutoff eventually. In PR201 its fixed bad gate \(q_0\) has terminal \(q_i=U^i(q_0)\), with \(i=b-m-2\le31b/32-2\). The exact odd step gives \(U(x)+1\le(3/2)(x+1)\); hence \(q_i+1\le(q_0+1)(3/2)^{31b/32-2}\). The two coefficients are \(31/32=341/352<351/352\). This observation applies on escape and cycle branches and does not assume any unproved frequency law. It is an obstruction to combining the new upper with that particular lower witness.

[A separate bounded implementation](review_rows.py) indexed positive compositions of \(2b\) by their cumulative gap sums, evaluated the integer carry recursively by \(C_{i+1}=3C_i+2^{s_i}\), and solved \(4^b q\equiv C_b\pmod{3^b}\) before replaying every retained ordinary source by exact forward valuations. For \(1\le b\le10\) it checked all 125,476 such compositions, 75 reflection thresholds, and 1,740 endpoint bounds at odd units \(1<q\le90\) and \(\epsilon\in\{1/128,1/32,1/8,1/4,1/2,3/4\}\). There were 189 ordinary source-to-endpoint rows and 209 passing row/threshold triples. These finite checks support the indexing and the ceiling cases. The asymptotic conclusion follows from the preceding argument.
