## Reconstruction

1. **Formal input.** Reducibility and reachability force the triangular substitution. The case of one zero in its initial image generates a single initial zero followed entirely by ones. Otherwise the zero-index series H has polynomial coefficients in Y. If the increases in the preceding ones were eventually periodic, concatenation between successive zeros would be an eventually periodic word. Bell–Smertnig Theorem 1.3(d), applied to the cyclic group generated by any positive rational t unequal to one, partitions the full support of H(X,t) into finitely many progressions with geometric coefficients. Taking a common period and squeezing the increasing exponents between two consecutive period endpoints identifies all progression slopes. Consequently H(X,t) is not D-finite. Removing a common factor of Y−2 before specializing makes a hypothetical bivariate algebraic relation for H impossible. This specialization is legitimate coefficient by coefficient in X.
2. **Passing to the physical completion.** Every zero in an image word comes from a zero input. This yields the exact H=P·(H∘M) identity. The positivity of P on positive rationals prevents division by a zero value. In the physical series the coefficient of H, after eliminating the one-index series, is u₀−u₁(1−x)/(1−y). It is nonzero exactly when the affine fixed points differ. Here x and y are different from one by their strictly positive valuations. The finite products go to zero in the designated 2-adic norm, even when their ordinary real sizes grow. The common-fixed-point counterexample is valid. No inference from real convergence to a 2-adic value enters.
3. **Heights.** If H(x,y) is algebraic, iteration keeps every auxiliary value in its number field K. Each division costs O(V_j+W_j) in absolute logarithmic height. The inequalities W_j=O(V_j) and V_(j+1)≥2V_j make the cumulative cost O(V_n) with a constant independent of auxiliary degrees. At the chosen place, K embeds in Q₂, so the signed valuation upper bound v₂(α)log 2 ≤ [K:Q]h(α) for nonzero α is valid. The constants multiplying polynomial degrees in the height estimate depend on the fixed substitution and point, and the coefficient-height and monomial-count terms are fixed after choosing R.
4. **Slow coordinate, including the Jordan block.** When a≥d≥2, a linear system of D=(m+1)^2−1 equations in (m+1)^2 unknowns over Q(Y) leaves a nonzero auxiliary polynomial. Clearing all its denominators increases only its Y degree and coefficient heights. If a=d then W_n/V_n tends to zero like 1/n, sufficient even if that degree is enormous because m is fixed before n tends to infinity. Integer coefficients of the subsequent X-terms give valuation at least (t+1)V_n. The leading coefficient's least Y-degree term has exactly a fixed constant plus its degree times W_n in valuation for all large n, less than V_n. Thus the auxiliary value is nonzero, enabling the product formula. Finally m can grow. Choosing m and n in the other order would forfeit this argument.
5. **Static d=1.** Specialize to H(X,y) at the outset. Formal transcendence follows for this specific y and not merely generically. All coefficients are 2-adically integral because v₂(y)>0, so after constructing an integer-coefficient auxiliary polynomial the nonzero first term dominates at x_n. This avoids any assumption that a nonzero bivariate polynomial survives an arbitrary fixed specialization.
6. **Comparable scale d>a.** Taking p/q=b/(d−a) makes r_n=y_n^p/x_n^q the a^n-th power of r₀. Positivity excludes a nontrivial rational root of unity. If r₀ differs from one, the first nonempty weighted layer of the auxiliary series consists of finitely many monomials. Factoring at the smallest occupied j leaves a nonzero *ordinary* polynomial in r_n. It has finitely many roots, and its valuation above is O_R(a^n+1) by the rational height bound, even if r_n does not shrink in the 2-adic norm. The next layer costs one positive unit of weight times d^n. For unbounded higher degrees the lower estimate is uniform, by bounding each coordinate below by its weight times (C−ε)d^n; integral coefficients cannot spoil it. The first layer is therefore strictly dominant for large n. If r₀=1, coprimality gives positive rational t with x=t^p and y=t^q. The positions p i+qB_i in H(T^p,T^q) are strictly increasing. An eventually periodic support would have periodic gaps, contrary to the original word. Bell–Smertnig applied to the trivial multiplicative group rules out algebraicity. The specialized series is integral and t_n=t^(d^n), closing the one-variable height argument.
