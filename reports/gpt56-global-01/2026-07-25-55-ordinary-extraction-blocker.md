- a nonnegative ordinary integer exactly when the residue blocks are eventually zero;

This is the exact signed ordinary-extraction criterion.

For the nested positive seed sets `S_n`, an ordinary infinite survivor exists exactly when the least roots

\[

This supplies the valid inference repeatedly missing from finite-prefix constructions.

This proves that finite physical compatibility, computability, an exact infinite path, and conditional supercritical growth are jointly insufficient.

## Failed approaches

### Refund or growth as extraction

The attempt fails logically.  Refund and growth concern the future of a seed already assumed to be legal forever.  They do not prove the intersection of the finite seed sets is nonempty.

### Inverse-limit compactness

This produces a `2`-adic point, not an ordinary positive integer.  `T-7602` gives an exact Collatz-native counterexample to the stronger inference.

### Generated stack capacity

A later runtime state may contain increasing future transported capacity while the initial pulled-back residue still requires eventual stabilization.  Capacity is not content and neither is extraction.

### Finite SCCs or periodic controllers

These produce path abstractions or completion ghosts unless a canonical most-significant boundary closes.  Existing branch results already expose this failure.

### A new finite search

Rejected as out of scope.  Computing more prefix minima without a uniform recurrence would add another bounded exclusion rather than decide the global blocker.

## Potential errors

3. The architecture audit preserves source statuses and does not independently reconstruct every branch theorem.
4. “Narrower than Collatz” refers to the two-sided decision for one fixed subsystem.  The positive existence statement itself is a stronger sufficient condition than the bare falsity of Collatz.

\[
\forall n\exists x_n
