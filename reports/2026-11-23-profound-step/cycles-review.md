## 1. Fixed coefficient and multiplicative rank

The actual accelerated edge identity

\[
2^{a_i}n_{i+1}-3n_i=1
\]

puts the pair \(Y_i=(-3n_i,2^{a_i}n_{i+1})\) on \(Y_{i,1}+Y_{i,2}=1\).
If the positive rational group generated by all states under consideration has
rank \(t\), the pair group generated by

\[
(g_j,1),(1,g_j)\ (1\le j\le t),\quad (-3,1),(1,2)
\]

has rank at most \(2t+2\). Each \(Y_i\) belongs to this group. The fixed
coefficient \(3\) has been paid for by the generator \((-3,1)\); the sign is
torsion and does not require an additional rank. No further arbitrary-coefficient
reduction is being silently used.

Beukers--Schlickewei, Theorem 1.1, bounds the number of solutions of \(x+y=1\)
in the divisible closure of a rank-\(r\) finitely generated subgroup of
\((\mathbb C^\times)^2\) by \(2^{8r+8}\).
The displayed group is contained in its own divisible closure, so the bound
applies to it. Distinct cycle states give distinct first coordinates and therefore
distinct solution pairs. Substitution yields

\[
p\le 2^{8(2t+2)+8}=2^{16t+24}.
\]

The finite-path extension is valid when the endpoint state is included in the
scalar group. For an infinite non-eventually-periodic ordinary orbit, repetition
of a state would force eventual periodicity, so there are infinitely many
distinct edge pairs. Their containment in a single fixed finite-rank rational
group would contradict the same finite bound. This proves the stated conditional
unbounded-rank consequence. It supplies neither an upper bound on the actual
rank nor the exclusion of a single cycle of unrestricted rank.

## 2. Exact ESS target, including the divisible group

I read the primary Evertse--Schlickewei--Schmidt author PDF at

https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0409604

including the affine-vector height definitions (2.3)--(2.4), the finite-rank
algebraic group hypothesis (2.9), the near-group condition (2.10), and Theorem 2.1
on printed pages 814--815. The theorem allows a finite-rank subgroup of
\((\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\times})^d\), not only a finitely generated group. Its
required small-height inequality is

\[
h(\eta)\le d^{-1}\exp(-(4d)^{3d})(1+h(\gamma))
\quad\text{for }Y=\gamma\eta.
\]

The note uses that exact condition. In dimension two, a proper linear subspace
through the origin meets the affine line \(Y_1+Y_2=1\) in at most one point.
Thus the stated finite-solution consequence would follow if the small-height
premise held. The note does not assert that it holds.

The group
\(\langle2,3\rangle^{\mathrm{div}}\times
\langle2,3\rangle^{\mathrm{div}}\)
has rank four modulo torsion, as asserted. Passing to the full algebraic
divisible closure does not introduce a nonzero valuation at a finite place over
a rational prime outside \(\{2,3\}\).

## 3. Independent reconstruction of the height gap

Let \(n\ge2\) be prime to six, let \(Y=(-3n,3n+1)\), and write
\(Y=\gamma\eta\) with \(\gamma\) in that fixed divisible group. Work in any
number field containing \(\gamma\) and \(\eta\), with normalized absolute
values.

For each rational prime \(p\mid n\), the first coordinate of \(\gamma\) is a
unit at every place above \(p\). Consequently the contribution of those
places to \(h(\eta_1^{-1})\) is exactly \(v_p(n)\log p\). Summing and using
the product formula for the scalar height gives

\[
h(\eta)\ge h(\eta_1)=h(\eta_1^{-1})\ge\log n.
\]

This argument remains valid for algebraic roots in the divisible closure:
raising a coordinate to a suitable positive integer power gives a two/three
unit, hence its valuation outside two and three is zero. The normalization
of the local degrees makes the sum above a rational-prime contribution, not
an extra factor depending on the number field.

For the *affine-vector* height, coordinatewise inversion need not preserve
height. The note correctly retains the factor two:

\[
h(\eta^{-1})
 \le h(\eta_1)+h(\eta_2)
 \le 2h(\eta).
\]

Subadditivity under coordinatewise multiplication then gives

\[
h(\gamma)
 \le h(Y)+h(\eta^{-1})
 \le\log(3n+1)+2h(\eta).
\]

With \(u=h(\eta)\) and \(a=1+\log(3n+1)>0\), the function
\(u/(a+2u)\) is increasing on \(u\ge0\). Therefore

\[
\frac{h(\eta)}{1+h(\gamma)}
 \ge\frac{u}{a+2u}
 \ge\frac{\log n}{1+\log(3n+1)+2\log n}
 >\frac18.
\]

The last strict inequality is equivalent to \(n^6>e(3n+1)\), which holds
already at \(n\ge2\), for example from \(n^6\ge32n\),
\(e<3\), and \(3n+1\le(7/2)n\). The ESS threshold at \(d=2\),
\(\tfrac12\exp(-8^6)\), is much smaller than \(1/8\). Thus every decomposition
allowed here fails the proposed near-group hypothesis.

The distinction between scalar and vector inversion is load-bearing; omitting
the factor two would leave the claimed denominator estimate unjustified.
The note handles it correctly. The example \(n=5\) also correctly prevents
misreading the height gap as a property exclusive to hypothetical cycles.
For a larger fixed prime set, the same argument controls the prime factors
of \(n\) outside that set; it does not exclude unbounded new prime support.

## 5. Remaining inference

The two-variable encoding trades the growing number of carry terms for the
uncontrolled multiplicative rank of actual states. The fixed two/three-group
alternative has an explicit height obstruction, including all finite places.
Neither argument yields a uniform rank cap, a new all-state height estimate,
or the failure of the full integrality condition \(2^A-3^p\mid C\).

The note's final OPEN status and its limitation to these proposed applications
are accurate. No blocking mathematical correction was identified.
