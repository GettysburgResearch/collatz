### The one-band charge

For integer X, admissible states in [X,3X) are exactly odd states below 2X and even states at or above 2X. The images of the two groups are disjoint and reverse their order as groups while retaining the within-group order. This is a cyclic-order-preserving injection. At the seam the final low odd state maps below 3X and the first high even state maps to X, so no reversal is concealed by endpoint notation. Interpolating the finitely many domain/image points extends it to an orientation-preserving circle homeomorphism.

The preimages of two partition boundaries through h-1 iterates cut that circle into at most 2h half-open arcs. Each itinerary is constant on each arc, including boundary points with the consistent right-hand assignment. If two integer starts follow the same h bits, their endpoint difference is 3^p times their initial difference divided by 2^h. Integrality and coprimality imply divisibility of the initial difference by 2^h. With 2^h >= 2X, this identifies the start uniquely in the band. Sliding length-h windows along an injective segment gives L-h+1 <= 2h and hence L <= 3h-1. This proves a bounded toll per visit.

The bound does not control returns after exiting. The supplied ordinary orbit example refutes the attempted projection of several bands onto one common circle: the normalized inputs 5,46/9,10 have images 8,23/3,5, in reversed cyclic order. This is an actual example, not a symbolic branch schedule. An escaping orbit may also visit ever higher bands without returning. Neither mechanism has been charged to the original finite source.
