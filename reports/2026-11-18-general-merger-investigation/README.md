# Can one extra step distinguish universal convergence?

**The full Collatz problem remains open.** This round sought a general argument for all positive integers. It produced a proposed analytic characterization of universal convergence and a lower bound showing why a long finite observation cannot supply the required estimate. The cycle arguments identify the ordinary equality that survives several aggregate tests.

## The all-time question

Write $T(n)=n/2$ at even inputs and $T(n)=(3n+1)/2$ at odd inputs. Call a pair $(2n,2n+1)$ separated at lag $d$ if $T^i(2n)\ne T^j(2n+1)$ for every $i,j\ge0$ with $|i-j|\le d$. Denote its set of indices by $B_d$. There is no upper bound on either time.

| Allowed lag | Conclusion for the ordinary integers |
|---|---|
| Zero | $\sum_{n\in B_0}1/n$ diverges. An elementary proof gives this even without a theorem on predecessor density. The set nevertheless has natural density zero by adjacent synchronization. |
| At most one | $\sum_{n\in B_1}1/n$ is finite exactly when every positive integer reaches 1. In that case $B_1$ is empty. If another component exists, the reciprocal sum already diverges over pairs for which exactly one start reaches 1. The converse uses positive lower density of fixed-target predecessor sets. |
| Up to $c_C\sqrt{\log n}$ | If an escaping component $C$ exists, the cumulative reciprocal sum of the still-separated pairs up to $X$ has order $\sqrt{\log X}$ for some $c_C>0$. An upper bound of smaller order for a fixed lag would therefore exclude escape. Additional periodic components require their own test. |

The proof reads the integer prefixes of a real binary expansion. An even child maps back to its parent. At an odd child, synchronization with its even sibling transfers the label under study. If the exceptional reciprocal sum were finite, almost every ray would eventually carry a stable label after the appropriate clock correction. At infinitely many digit positions the same label transfers to three times the real argument. Invariance under two multiplicatively independent dilations then forces the limiting label to be constant. For the zero-lag result, any positive component supports a nonconstant clock phase, since the shortcut map has no positive fixed point. For the one-lag result, the basin of 1 and any second component would both carry positive ordinary density under the cited predecessor theorem. The paper also gives a self-contained sufficient condition. With $\gamma=\log 2/\log(64/3)$, an upper bound $\#(B_1\cap[1,X])=o(X^\gamma)$ would settle the full conjecture using only an elementary inverse tree and the prefix argument. ## The finite clock cannot decide the all-time sum

At the full binary precision of a starting octave, allowing completely independent step counts on the two arms gives the same leading failure proportion as the published balanced, equal-time synchronization law. On a much longer clock, near $\mu\log n$ with $\mu=2/\log(4/3)$, there is a sharper cumulative statement. Stop both arms before that clock by the Gaussian lower-tail margin specified in the proof. Even with *any* two step counts up to that deadline, the sum of $1/n$ over pairs still separated through $X$ has order $\sqrt{\log X}$. This holds whether or not Collatz is true. The proof combines Leen's stated moderate deviations with a lower bound for nonzero linear forms in $\log 2,\log 3$ and the drift of the first time an orbit attains its minimum. The upper tail after the last descent to a small state is a separate question. The first unsupported inference toward a full proof is a sufficiently small upper bound on the **infinite-time** one-lag exceptional set. All-time divergence for same-clock pairs and finite-time divergence for unrestricted pairs are compatible with every positive integer eventually reaching 1. The elementary zero-lag divergence is independent of those inputs. Earlier equal-clock, holonomic-recurrence, and joint survivor results are credited in the linked proofs and corrections.
