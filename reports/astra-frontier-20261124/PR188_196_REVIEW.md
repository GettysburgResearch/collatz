## Canonical growth and collision

Both papers use \(2^{a_i}q_{i+1}=3q_i+1\) on positive odd integers and the exact relation \(a_i+c_{i+1}=b_i+3c_i\) for actual carries. Put \(K_N=\sum_{i<N}c_i\), \(P_N=3^N/2^{\sum_{i<N}b_i}\) and \(R_N=\max_{i\le N}P_i\). On an injective orbit the normalized state \(X_N=q_N/2^{c_N}\) is at least one and satisfies
\[
X_N=X_0 P_N4^{-K_N}\prod_{i<N}(1+1/(3q_i)).
\]
The product is bounded using the same-orbit occupation theorem. Therefore a polynomial \(R_N\) along a subsequence implies \(K_N=O(\log N)\) there. At the least window length \(m\) with \(3^m>\max_{i\le N}q_i\), the physical height comparison makes \(m=O(\log N)\). Only \(O((\log N)^2)\) windows meet a positive carry. The remaining actual gap words equal their canonical words and determine mutually distinct endpoints modulo \(3^m\). This proves that at least \(N-o(N)\) distinct canonical words occur at the chosen length. It is an exact use of the original source.
