## PR193 and the odd and even resource bounds

Write $T$ for the ordinary shortcut map. Use the local notation $T^a(x)=T^b(n)$, $0<x<n$, $2^b\mid n+1$, $e=v_3(n)$ and $p$ odd inputs from $x$. PR193 writes the two clocks in the opposite order. S2 proves $a>b$ and $p>b+e$ for unrestricted competitor words, and gives $n<6^H$ with no merger using two clocks at most $H$. This exactly matches the headline odd-count theorem and its first CRT consequence.

**S3, reconstructed.** Put $t=a-b$, $L=2^t3^{b-p}$ and $v=x-Ln$. For the competitor's formal word $F$, subtracting its linear term gives $F(v)=(3/2)^b-1$, the maximum attained by any $b$-letter suffix from zero. The nonnegative full-word carry gives $v<L$. Suppose $p=b+e+1$. Then $v$ has exact denominator three because $n/3^e$ is a ternary unit. The first $t$ letters replay on this rational under parity modulo two, since $x-v=2^t(n/3^e)/3$. There is an odd in that prefix, and its first odd makes the comparison state integral. The state after $t$ cannot be negative, since every formal suffix from a negative state lies strictly below the maximum from zero.

If $v<0$, nonnegativity is possible only by stepping from $-1/3$ to zero at its first odd input. The remaining physical prefix is even and the suffix is all odd to attain its maximum, giving $p=b+1$ and $e=0$. In fact $v=-2^j/3$, $0\le j<t$, so $3x=2^tn-2^j$. For $t\ge2$ and $n\ge2$ this is at least $2^t(n-1/2)\ge3n$, leaving only $t=1$ and $x=(2n-1)/3$.

If $v>0$, then $0<v<L<1$ by $x<n$. The only candidates are $1/3$ and $2/3$. Each reaches 1 on its first odd input and then alternates between 1 and 2, allowing at most $1+\lfloor t/2\rfloor$ odds in the prefix. Its state after $t$ is positive. For $b=0$ this already contradicts the required endpoint zero. For $b>0$ at least one suffix letter must be even, hence the prefix has at least $e+2$ odds. Thus $t\ge2(e+1)$ and $L=2^t/3^{e+1}>1$, a contradiction. This reconstructs the first-tier classification, including the actual one-step witness when $n\equiv2\pmod3$.

Consequently **S3 gives $p\ge b+e+2$ for $e\ge1$**. Our even-input proof retains the mechanism in S4 of cutting at the extra odd inputs to obtain a positive integral comparison state. It then bounds the next $N$ steps by $3^{k+e}\le 2^N m<2^N3^k$, using the strict terminal carry bound. This yields $a-p>e\log_2(3/2)$. Adding the two inequalities proves

$$\boxed{a>b+2+e\log_2 3\quad(e\ge1).}$$

This combined inequality is a written corollary of the two pinned arguments. PR193 S4 also controls higher excess by examining finite ordinary seeds. S5 exactly parametrizes competitors *at $T(n)$* by a prefix and an ordinary tail to 1. S6 obtains unbounded excess even at fixed $v_3(n)=1$, $v_2(n+1)=2$, by centering at 3 for the endpoints $b=0,1,2$.

**Positive overlap.** In S1 write $n=3^H u$ and $3^{H+4}z=1+2\cdot4^H u$. From $x=2^{H+5}z-1$, $H+4$ odd steps reach $1+4^{H+1}u$, and $H+1$ odd/even pairs reach $3n+1$. A halving meets $T(n)$, with exactly $2H+5$ odd inputs and $H+2$ even inputs. The affine slope on $n$ is $(64/81)(8/9)^H<1$ and the intercept is negative. Positivity follows from integer $z\ge1$. This proves $0<x<n$ on one ternary unit class of $u$. CRT permits the same $n$ to meet our binary condition of $H$ initial odd inputs followed by $s=\lceil H\log_2(3/2)\rceil$ even inputs. Every source on that intersection therefore has both the S1 smaller partner at its first endpoint and its own first forward descent exactly at clock $H+s$. Both claims concern each member of a single progression. A prescribed arbitrary $n$ need not be a member.

**Coverage estimates.** In `SHADOW_COVERAGE_LIMIT.md`, for fixed $e\ge1$ and $n=3^eu$, more than $17/500$ in relative lower natural density of odd units $u$ have no smaller merger at $T(n)$ with excess $d=p-e-1$ inside $(9/2)^d\le n/(2\cdot3^{2e})$. S5 represents each eligible diagram by a prefix ending in its $d$-th odd input and a positive seed returning to 1 with exactly $e+1$ odds. The budget forces inverse slope below one, which bounds the number of seeds per prefix. Each prefix/tail fixes one class modulo $3^d$. Summing ordinary rounding errors through the logarithmic cutoff costs $O_e(X^{\log 3/\log(9/2)})=o(X)$. Thus the density refers to ordinary sources at the first endpoint and allows deeper or costlier mergers.

In `escape.md` §§1a–1b, the first $\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor$ shortcut steps partition a translated interval of length $L$ by parity weight. At fixed weight the translation cancels from the image width. High weights have a binomial tail and the remaining widths permit induction with residual common odd clock. At $R=\lceil K\log_2^2(2L)\rceil$ odd steps, there are at most $CL^{H_2(\log_3 2)}/\sqrt{\log(2L)}$ terminal fibers. All except the least source in each fiber obtain smaller ordinary partners. A least nonconverger, if present, must be one of those minima at every clock. This count gives no individual bound on its merger time. The common clock counts odd steps; the two shortcut clocks can differ. Equation (6) bounds an inverse-distance-weighted collision on the original law and retains the unweighted separation obligation.

## PR195 and shortest ordinary walks

**CS1, reconstructed and core recompiled.** For $k\equiv148\pmod{256}$, set $n=2^{33k}u-17$, $x=2^{21k}u-1$, $u>0$. The signed lifting identity for $S(c)=-T(-c)$ is $T^r(z)+S^r(c)=3^{w}v$ when $z+c=2^rv$ and $w$ counts odd inputs on the $S$-arm. The rails of $17,52,1$ give the respective endpoints $X=3^{21k}u-17$, $Y=T^{33k}(3n-1)=3^{22k-1}u-7$, and $X+16$. From $3^{k-1}(X+17)=Y+7$ and $1024\mid Y$ follows $X\equiv52\pmod{1024}$. At 52 and 68 the ten-step paths have three odd inputs and the same endpoint 2. Translation therefore preserves the equality and yields $T^{33k+10}(n)=T^{21k+10}(x)$ with $0<x<n$. The additional guard $Y=2^j$, $j\ge10$, certifies convergence of the different source $3n-1$.

**CS2, written reconstruction.** At the pair consisting of dyadic shadow $-17$ and ternary shadow $z=0$ or $1$, reduce a walk to forward steps followed by inverse steps. Up to equality of the two odd counts, signed magnitudes lie on the 17-cycle or its ordinary inverse tree. Comparing the forward odd targets with the sorted inverse odd arguments gives $17\alpha>B\ge17$ for a nonempty forward prefix, where $\alpha$ is the affine multiplier and $B$ the current magnitude. The first four product ratios exceed one, and later inverse arguments are at least 137 against forward arguments at most 136. At equality of odd counts the multiplier is $2^m$, $m\ge1$. A future fall below one forces a positive comparison seed below $17+z$. By clock 23 every seed in this finite range reaches 1 or 2. The preceding clocks leave only $B=17,26,19,28$, all ineligible for the off-cycle 17 branch. A forward-empty path allows just doubling at $z=0$ and an ordinary inverse history of 1 at $z=1$. Hence $\alpha\ge1$ and the image of $z$ is at least $z$. Forward steps cost at most one binary digit and inverse odd steps at most one ternary digit. Every ordinary walk of total length at most $L$ from an input in the indicated precision-$L$ classes thus ends at or above that input.

On carefully chosen lifts with $L=33k$, a single unit congruence modulo $3^{55k-1}$ supplies $Y=2^j$ and $n\equiv1\pmod{3^L}$. Taking $j$ in one specified multiplicative-order interval gives $33k<D(n)\le54k+20$ and $D(n)=\Theta(\log\log n)$. Here $D$ is the minimum *total length* of any undirected ordinary walk to a smaller positive source. The size asymptotic uses the specified exponent interval. The wider $1024\mid Y$ guard alone does not supply these mixed precision or size conclusions. CS3 excludes a particular four-offset bounded-clock test. CS4 bounds the size of matched off-cycle offsets using distinct targets of odd steps, each congruent to one modulo three, and a telescoping sixth-power estimate. These claims leave other inverse histories available.

**Comparing the metrics and domains.** For any finite witness, reduction of a shortest walk shows $D(n)=\min(a+b)$, whereas $\tau(n)=\min\max(a,b)$, so $\tau\le D\le2\tau$. For the least positive representative $n_H$ of our explicit descending subprogression, the directed descent itself gives $H<D(n_H)\le H+s$ and $3^H\le n_H<2\cdot9^H$. Thus $D(n_H)=\Theta(\log n_H)$ for those sources. PR195 instead gives an additional certificate on the open reverse-closure section $n=27j+1$, $4\nmid j$. Its protected inputs have $v_2(n+1)=4$ and $v_3(n)=0$. The $e\ge1$ lower bound does not cover them. Conversely PR193 S1 never meets $n\equiv1\pmod{27}$, since its height-zero guard gives $n\equiv40\pmod{81}$ and every positive height gives $3\mid n$. The two successful guards leave prescribed inputs uncovered.
