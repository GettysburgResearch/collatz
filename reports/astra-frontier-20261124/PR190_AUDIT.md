## Reconstruction

**Physical source.** For each positive gap $g$, the inverse $I_g(t)=(2^g t-1)/3$ takes an odd $t\in\mathbb Z_2$ to an odd preimage with $v_2(3I_g(t)+1)=g$. Taking the inverse limit of nonempty odd cylinders yields a unique source. For each fixed prefix the terminal odd values converge to an odd 2-adic integer, so every prescribed valuation is retained at the limit. Any actual rational source in these cylinders must equal this completion under the injective embedding into $\mathbb Q_2$. For equally long distinct gap words, the same periodic source would have two different finite strings of exact valuations. For different lengths powers of one common word can share a fixed point, so the distinct-fixed-point guard is substantive. With $\alpha_i$ the inverse slopes and $\beta_i$ the translations, the coefficient of $G_1$ in the physical affine formula is $(1-\alpha_1)\bigl(\beta_1/(1-\alpha_1)-\beta_0/(1-\alpha_0)\bigr)$.

**Regularity.** In the difference of the evaluations of two binary words, their first differing digit contributes the common prefix product with coefficient $\pm1$. Every later term has strictly higher 2-adic valuation. Thus the coefficient numerator $D$ vanishes at positive-valuation points precisely when the two periodic repetitions of the substitution images coincide. If they coincide, each image length is a period of the common repetition, and concatenation of the images repeatedly starts at the same phase. A fixed word is then periodic. This covers proper prefixes of unequal image lengths.

**D-finite obstruction.** A polynomial coefficient recurrence can be shifted so that it is $\sum_{h=0}^r P_h(t)u_{t+h}=0$ for all sufficiently large $t$, with a nonzero coefficient polynomial. In the coefficient extraction from a nonzero differential operator, terms with a common index shift have falling-factorial coefficients of distinct degrees, so the recurrence cannot vanish identically. Divide by $W(j(t))$, with $j(t)$ the last symbol boundary at or before clock index $t$. Each normalized tuple belongs to a finite set. Beyond all integer roots of the associated nonzero polynomials, every tuple that occurs annihilates every coefficient vector of the $P_h$. One nonzero such vector gives a constant recurrence of some order $r'$. A boundary stores the preceding $r'$ clock coefficients divided by its own $W(j)$. Each nonzero stored value is an inverse product of at most $r'$ symbol weights. From this state the recurrence gives the present symbol. Appending its coefficient, padding its clock segment with zeros and dividing by its symbol weight gives the next state. A finite deterministic state sequence is eventually periodic. This proof also covers zero coefficients, negative weights, unequal clocks and a starting transient. If $r'=0$ a zero coefficient tail forces a zero symbol tail.

**Heights and nonvanishing.** Clearing the Mahler equation gives $B_{n+1}=r_n B_n+s_n$ for rational $r_n,s_n$ of height $O(\lambda^n)$, with the coefficient numerator nonzero. Thus $h(B_n)=O(\lambda^n)$ in $K=\mathbb Q(B_0)$ and $h(R(x_n,y_n,B_n))\le Cm\lambda^n+O_R(1)$ for individual degrees bounded by $m$, with $C$ independent of $m,R$. The local bound $v_2(\eta)\log 2\le [K:\mathbb Q]h(\eta)$ is valid at the fixed embedding. For the auxiliary construction the integer-coefficient series has total order at least $\lfloor m^{3/2}\rfloor$. Its convergence and the lower valuation bound use integrality of every coefficient, independently of the size of its archimedean norm.

With irrational Perron weight ratio every integer exponent pair has a different weight and only finitely many pairs lie under any fixed threshold. The least nonzero term therefore wins against the finite nearby set for all sufficiently large $n$. The rest of the infinite series is dominated by choosing one fixed total-degree cutoff and the componentwise positive Perron lower bound. With rational weights $(p,q)$ and varying transverse ratio $r_n=y_n^p/x_n^q$, choose the least occupied weighted degree $t$. Each exponent pair on its line differs from the previous one by an integer multiple of $(-q,p)$. Factoring the monomial at the end with smallest second exponent gives a nonzero polynomial in $r_n$. Distinct positive rational values make it eventually nonzero, with upper valuation $O_R(|\mu|^n)$ by height. The whole piece has valuation at most $Ct\lambda^n+O_R(|\mu|^n)$, strictly below every higher weighted degree eventually. For a constant-ratio subsequence, $A=x_n/x_j,B=y_n/y_j$ satisfy $B^p=A^q$. Choose $up+vq=1$ and use the rational parameter $A^uB^v$. The univariate series is formally transcendental by MPS-6 and converges near zero, including for rational parameters of negative 2-adic valuation. Its first nonzero coefficient dominates eventually. In each case the order-to-degree ratio tends to infinity, contradicting the height bound.
