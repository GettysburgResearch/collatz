## What this checkpoint establishes in writing

The [marked trace argument](../../research/astra-global-20261128/escape/SUMMABLE_ORIGINAL_TRACE.md) controls the original ordinary inverse rows, retaining every visit, every physical target, and every CDF threshold. Its conclusion is a finite sum of the target-weighted CDF variations between successive depths. The unmarked consequence is already present in PR195 and is explicitly attributed there. The new part is the mark-sensitive estimate: freeze a prefix, use the fine-scale residue mixing in Tao's Proposition 1.14, bound the real intercept tail by a fractional moment, and pay the boundary of a moving complete-residue cutoff. The proof uses actual inverse-word compatibility; it does not replace the ordinary trajectories by an unrestricted symbolic law.

The [spatial trace](../../research/astra-global-20261128/escape/SPATIAL_TRACE_TAIL.md) and [all-depth profile](../../research/astra-global-20261128/escape/ALL_DEPTH_SPATIAL_PROFILE.md) then describe the simultaneous limit in both target height and depth. A finite set of low roots reconstructs the trace with a power error from the pinned PR213 ancestor estimate. On a possible escaping component, the surviving profile moves with the actual native grade. The written classification identifies every remaining component amplitude and the way that its finite-depth measures approach the stationary mark law. The amplitudes have not been shown to be zero. The logarithmic saving in the elementary forward-height estimate is credited to the earlier PR193 stopped-rate argument; the new use here is uniform control of all finite rows in the stated height region.

The [cycle-period analysis](../../research/astra-global-20261128/cycles/PERIOD_SPECTRUM.md) treats arbitrary valuation words, both signs, and every translated interval of shortcut periods. It combines the pinned endpoint and full-excursion censuses with a fractional cycle-gap budget and a lattice alternative. For denominator bound $D$, the bound depends on $D$ and the interval length, rather than requiring a prescribed number of mismatches. The exponent from the classical Bondareva–Luchin–Salikhov input is below $0.668$; the separate recent Chudnovsky input would lower it below $1/2$. The principal statement retains its Diophantine hypothesis explicitly. Sparsity of the set of possible periods does not exclude one extra integer cycle.

The [cycle trace law](../../research/astra-global-20261128/cycles/CYCLE_TRACE_LAW.md) gives a uniform positive raw harmonic floor at every positive cycle site. A concrete residue-class subsolution proves the floor. The prior geometric lap decomposition, combined with the whole-basin tail bound, gives a uniform logarithmic coupling to a uniform random variable as the least cycle member tends to infinity. The prior decomposition and the new strengthening are distinguished in the manuscript.

The [delayed contribution theorem](../../research/astra-global-20261128/cycles/DELAYED_CYCLE_TRACE.md) is a direct finite-row consequence of the original atoms. Adjacent depths have disjoint positive atomic supports because their marks have different 3-adic valuations. A cycle of odd period $p$ and logarithmic gap $g$ therefore forces a tail contribution at depths of order $p/g$. Summing over every offset around the cycle retains the full square of its odd reciprocal sum. A uniform inverse-depth tail bound would force finitely many positive cycles; a specified numerical version would exclude all extra cycles. Neither upper bound is proved here. The numerical specialization uses Hercher's Corollary 29, the published $2^{71}$ verification, and Paper 217's separate gap certificate. The underlying finite-row inequality does not need those inputs.

The [pin construction](../../research/astra-global-20261128/broad/PIN_APPROXIMATION.md) turns the pointwise problem into the dyadic valuation of a positive rational sum. At each prescribed number of even events there is a unique greedy tuple attaining the largest approximation depth to the supplied ordinary integer. That depth is exactly the next-even-input clock. Boundedness of its discounted rank is equivalent to this same integer reaching 1. The needed bound uniformly in the number of terms is open.

The [fixed-arity Subspace Theorem argument](../../research/astra-global-20261128/broad/SPARSE_APPROXIMATION.md), [growing post-burst estimate](../../research/astra-global-20261128/broad/MERSENNE_POST_BURST.md), [ranking obstructions](../../research/astra-global-20261128/broad/RANKING_OBSTRUCTIONS.md), and [arbitrary-dimension interpolation test](../../research/astra-global-20261128/broad/INTERPOLATION_DETERMINANTS.md) test four distinct routes toward that bound. They prove fixed-arity control, compulsory later even events after a long odd burst, limitations of broad local valuation rankings, and a precise lack of amplification for positive sparse alternants. The last obstruction is restricted to the specified one-variable determinant family. It leaves source-specific extra congruences and mixed-coordinate cancellation open.
