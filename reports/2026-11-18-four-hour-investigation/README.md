## What the terminal residue reveals

For an ordinary positive source, take the least value m on its entire orbit. At each odd input u before its first visit to m, add log₂(1+1/(3u)). On the basin of 1 the resulting sum is the binary logarithm of Roosendaal's multiplicative residue. The proposed proof shows that its limiting natural distribution is **singular continuous and has full lower Hausdorff dimension one**. The closed support itself has zero Lebesgue length and Hausdorff dimension one. Every Borel set of dimension less than one has mass zero. These statements also hold conditional on any minimum of positive limiting probability, including the basin of 1.

The dimension proof counts possible integer first landings. The separate singularity proof uses almost-sure inverse-path statistics to transfer a published joint real–ternary singularity result to *every fixed ordinary inverse root*. Natural predecessor basins then give the assertion about ordinary sources. The support proof builds disjoint sets of sources near each residue on a finite orbit. The resulting lower bound on local mass, together with singularity, gives zero length for the entire closed support. The conclusions address questions explicitly posed in Leen's stopping-time histogram paper. They concern the distribution on a population of sources and do not tell which minimum a specified integer reaches. [Dimension and small-ball bound](../../research/terminal-residue/REAL_DIMENSION.md) · [Singularity and natural-basin transfer](../../research/terminal-residue/SINGULAR_FIBRES.md) · [Closed support and integer fibres](../../research/terminal-residue/SUPPORT.md) · [Leen's open questions](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23252397) · [Prior joint affine law](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/19/v2)

For a source drawn uniformly up to X, record the number of even and odd shortcut inputs before its first minimum. There is an explicit modulus scale D(X) for which these two residues jointly approach independent uniforms, also independent of m, exactly when q=o(D(X)). If q/D(X) tends to infinity the total variation distance tends to one. For consecutive continued-fraction denominators s≤(log X)^(1/4)<t of log₂3, the scale is max(s,√log X/t). A rotation estimate gives the upper bound, and a clock character detected by the singular terminal residue gives the converse. Under logarithmic sampling over a fixed range of powers of X, the sharp scale is √log X. [Clock-resolution theorem](../../research/profinite-orbit-averages/CLOCK_RESOLUTION.md)

## Which positive cycles are still possible?

A positive primitive shortcut cycle cannot have cyclic parity word U^a V^b for arbitrary nonempty binary words U,V and a,b≥2. Two disjoint powers in a nontrivial cycle must leave at least 1/55 of its period uncovered. Only finitely many primitive positive cycles could consist of three arbitrary powers. The three-factor proof uses the Subspace Theorem and supplies no list of the possible finite exceptions. More generally, three disjoint powers leave at least a fixed positive fraction of every sufficiently long primitive cycle uncovered, with ineffective constants. Arbitrary cycle words can avoid all these short factorizations. ## What would exclude escape?

There is a direct forward test using only finitely many steps per source. For each odd start in $[X/2,X]$, follow the orbit for $q=\lfloor\log X/\log(4/3)\rfloor$ odd steps and multiply the factors $3u/(3u+1)$. The limiting mean is at least one half, with equality precisely when **every positive orbit is eventually periodic**. This conclusion still permits a cycle other than the known one. The proof gives the entire Gaussian transition around that cutoff. For $X=2^{22}$ the exact mean across 1,048,576 odd sources was between 0.499505 and 0.499506 at 53 steps. A bound on the limit remains open. [Proof and empirical bounds](../../research/unsafe-return-weights/DISTORTION_CUTOFF.md) · [Finite replay](../../research/unsafe-return-weights/check_distortion_cutoff.py)

- A divergent positive orbit would force the empirical mean of the forward minimum to grow at least as a constant times log X on every sufficiently large source shell. It would also force at least order r log(1/r) of the unmarked terminal-residue mass into fixed-ratio annuli near zero. A subsequence with smaller order would exclude divergence. The available small-ball upper bound has a power strictly below one. [Forward-minimum and residue tests](../../research/fixed-floor-shells/FIRST_MOMENT.md)
- The normalized finite inverse densities converge at every fixed ordinary target. The **uniformity** needed for a hypothetical moving target is a separate issue. A computable sequence of finite first-hit maxima has a limit, positive exactly if there is some escaping component. Another proposed criterion measures the total escaping mass using a signed harmonic sum. Its first 15 computed signs include both signs; they do not determine its limit. Either criterion would still permit a nontrivial finite cycle. - Starting from any fixed positive odd ternary unit, the canonical random inverse path almost surely has mean valuation two, log-height growth log(4/3) per odd step, and the iid geometric frequencies for every fixed valuation word. Along an escaping spine, the reverse walk from a *moving* root can follow that same spine back to its earlier values with high probability. This distinction is explicit in the endpoint law. A second test collects inverse paths for which the accumulated affine correction changes the terminal height by a fixed fraction. Its limiting measure is zero exactly when every positive orbit is eventually periodic. Any positive part is singular continuous, is carried by a compact set of zero length, and charges no set of dimension below one. Averaging the target over a real shift gives an explicit integral. At almost every fixed real shift the time average has an exact finite limit. A single escaping orbit would instead force linear growth at every fixed ternary rational shift. The missing estimate concerns these exceptional arithmetic targets. ## A finite test for both ways the conjecture could fail

At depth $Q$, enumerate the words of $Q$ inverse exponents between 1 and $2Q$. Each gives one positive odd target in the first ternary period. Omit the known odd fixed point 1 and sum slope divided by target over the words whose affine offset exceeds a fraction $\theta$ of that target (including equality). This finite sum is $D_Q(\theta)$.

- At fixed thresholds approaching zero, the growth of $D_Q(\theta)/Q$ detects escaping components.
- At the explicit threshold $1-2^{-32}$, every counted path has already visited a nontrivial cycle. A positive entry at even one depth supplies an ordinary cycle witness. Each such cycle contributes positive mass in the depth limit. The logarithmic threshold average of its endpoint contribution has an exact coefficient determined by the basin density and the mean log correction per odd step.

The two tests can be combined into **one finite rational sequence with a proved limit**. Its limit is zero exactly under universal convergence. It lies strictly between zero and 0.036 for escape alone, equals one for nontrivial cycles alone, and exceeds one if both occur. The weighted part measures whole-orbit distortion; the cycle indicator uses the explicit threshold $1-2^{-32}$. Determining which value the actual map takes remains open. A separate forward test takes the logarithm of the distortion after $\lfloor 2\log X/\log(4/3)\rfloor$ odd steps and divides its negative by $\log X$. Its limiting mean is one exactly when every orbit reaches 1. A nontrivial periodic basin and an escaping basin both reduce that mean.
