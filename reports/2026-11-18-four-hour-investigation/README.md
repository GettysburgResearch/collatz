This research branch starts at `ab7a62cbbb83860ea9436c5d3cf6cf7ce501066a`. It has not resolved the Collatz conjecture. The following are proposed mathematical results, qualified by the external inputs identified in their proofs. The exact finite calculation is reported separately. Numerical controls check arithmetic and examples; they do not certify infinite theorems.

| Ordinary question | What the branch establishes | What would still be needed |
|---|---|---|
| How much of one orbit can occupy a short interval? | Uniformly in its location, an interval of length X contains at most O(X^h/(log X)^{3/2}) distinct values of one injective orbit segment, with h=H₂(log₃2). This gives summability even at exponent h. [Proof](../../research/critical-orbit-mellin/CRITICAL_MOMENT.md) | The estimate permits an infinite sparse orbit. |
| Which parity words can support a positive cycle? | No primitive positive shortcut cycle consists of two powers U^a V^b with a,b≥2, even when the two words and the exponents vary. A pair of disjoint powers in a nontrivial cycle would leave at least 1/252 of its period uncovered. The proof uses a published lower bound on nontrivial period and a linear form estimate. [Exclusion](../../research/synchronized-cycle-blocks/TWO_POWER_EXCLUSION.md) · [Coverage](../../research/synchronized-cycle-blocks/TWO_POWER_COVERAGE.md) | General cyclic words can have many short powers or large parts outside both powers. |
| How compressible can one ordinary itinerary be? | Every fixed number of nonresonant powers from a fixed finite dictionary of parity words starting at x uses at most (1+ε) log₂ x steps for sufficiently large x. The shortest parsing of an N-step prefix of a nonperiodic orbit uses ω(log N) such powers. [Uniform theorem](../../research/sparse-periodic-deviations/FINITE_DICTIONARIES.md) | An arbitrary infinite itinerary need not have a short parsing. |
| How small is the least predecessor m(t) of a typical odd target t? | The natural density of m(t)<θt exists and equals an explicit ternary first-crossing probability. As θ decreases to zero, this density is asymptotic to c_* θ² for a fixed positive constant. [Density transfer](../../research/fixed-floor-shells/PREDECESSOR_MINIMA.md) · [Renewal constant](../../research/critical-orbit-mellin/MINIMUM_CONSTANT.md) | The limit takes the target range to infinity at fixed θ first. It does not rule out a particular unbounded sequence of targets reached from one small source. |
| Can inverse density restrict an individual hypothetical escape? | Along such an odd orbit the density at depth q has a sharp change with q relative to the elapsed age. After normalization its full moving clock profile converges uniformly. The associated scalar component trace has prior work by Valbuena. [Profile, sources and scope](../../research/critical-orbit-mellin/SPINE_CLOCK_PROFILE.md) | A positive incoming mass can follow one chain while all other branches together have finite mass. |

## Restricted survivor models

The 64→81 stack is one particular survivor construction. Every positive periodic increment directive of period at most 587 in that construction has irrational native two-adic value. For arbitrary fixed period r, at most floor(11r/6467) multiplicative argument orbits can have rational evaluations. A separate modular-transcendence argument covers all but at most one member of an explicit infinite family with unbounded minimal periods. These results do not classify every stack directive or every positive Collatz trajectory. [Period 587](../../research/stack-shift-residual/PROOF.md) · [Arbitrary-period sparsity](../../research/stack-combined-moments/RATIONAL_ARGUMENTS.md) · [Modular families](../../research/stack-modular-differences/PROOF.md)

The six-branch model has an exact modular meet-in-the-middle search for the least positive source realizing each finite depth. The receipt gives the full integer, itinerary and endpoint at each level. The new search agrees with the independent previous enumeration through depth 16. Larger finite minima still leave existence of one infinite ordinary source open.
