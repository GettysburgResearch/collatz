## 1. What the two new Lean roots prove

[TwoGradePairOrder.lean](../../../research/astra-critical-rigidity-20261128/cycles/TwoGradePairOrder.lean) exposes an `IntegerOctave p W`. Its data are integer labels, exact doubling after `p` ranks, positive base labels, tags in `{1,2}`, a common affine transition for every supplied offset, and a transitive shift on the finite base ranks. It assumes neither sign agreement nor injectivity of pair sums.

For the difference of two pair sums, the module derives the integer recurrence

\[
2^{b_r}H_{\sigma r}=3H_r+d_r,\qquad -2\le d_r\le2.
\]

A positive integer cannot become nonpositive in this recurrence; the negative statement is symmetric. Transitivity therefore gives a complete trichotomy: all values are positive, all are negative, or all vanish. The module proves the weighted octave identity directly by shifting the finite sum and using exact doubling. Its multiplier is the pair polynomial evaluated at the positive root of `X^p-2`. Eisenstein irreducibility is proved over the integers and transferred to the rationals; the minimal-polynomial degree then excludes a nonzero pair polynomial of degree below `p`. Sorted distinct pairs give a nonzero polynomial even when either pair repeats an entry.

These steps prove `comparison_sign`, including nonvanishing at every rank. The second root, `pair_sum_injective`, constructs the positive radical internally and deduces equality of the two sorted index pairs from equality of the physical label sums. The cycle lane independently read the complete source and confirmed this hypothesis boundary.

The fresh build used Lean 4.34.1, Mathlib source `d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612`, one compiler thread, disabled implicit variables, and warnings as errors. Both imported roots depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. The receipt records the reused dependency-cache scope. No cold dependency rebuild or independent LeanChecker run is claimed. Constructing the integer octave from an ordinary Collatz cycle, and the subsequent code-rate arguments, remain written mathematics. ## 2. Complete denominator factors at fixed prime grade

[PRIME_GRADE_DENOMINATORS.md](../../../research/astra-critical-rigidity-20261128/ordinary/PRIME_GRADE_DENOMINATORS.md) first rules out an important source of false simplification. On a primitive positive rational cycle of fixed odd grade `p`, its complete denominator cannot divide a fixed multiple of `2^K-3^H` along an unbounded family with `0<K<L` and `H<=p`. The coordinator checked the argument at the actual rational phases. Apart from a bounded number of dates near the fixed number of odd departures, phases are arbitrarily small. Choosing a date and its translated date both small turns the alleged congruence into an integer of absolute value below one. The numerators are ternary units, so the two grades must agree; the two phases then coincide, contradicting the least period. The fixed multiplier is retained throughout this argument.

The minimal affine-space reduction writes the clock polynomial as `F=2^a X^k-3^p` and the carry as a Laurent polynomial `B` in several dyadic parameters. Every proper affine hyperplane contains only finitely many retained parameters. Factoring out exactly the common rational polynomial factors gives a residual `V`. The fixed-target gcd estimate, Levin–Wang Theorem 1.13(a), makes the remaining common factor subexponential. The hypotheses are checked using the affine-space minimality: a translated subgroup relation would become one of the forbidden affine hyperplanes. The argument uses part (a), which does not impose the origin condition from part (b).

The full denominator exponent is therefore `deg(V)/k`. Local valuations at two and three have been accounted for. A positive real root cannot cancel because the carry has positive coefficients. The preceding shorter-congruence obstruction forces the retained cyclotomic orders to have the full required least common multiple. At prime grade this leaves the full exponent one, including clocks divisible by that prime. For primes at least five, the component irreducibility follows from the two unramified valuations. The proof separately checks grades two and three; the latter uses the relevant quadratic extension at three instead of silently treating that prime as unramified. At grade four the retained orders give exactly the two exponents `3/4` and `1`.

The sharp calendar families have an exact denominator formula. With `X=2^n`, grade `p` a power of the selected prime `ell`, and `b=p/ell`, it is

\[
q=\frac{(X-3)(X^p-3^p)}{(X^b-3^b)\gcd(X-3,\ell+1)}.
\]

The proof explicitly retains the bounded extra gcd; the finite control includes an instance where it equals five. The unique changed gaps prove primitivity. The full-exponent grade-four family is checked separately. The broad fixed-grade quantization and the earlier coprime-clock case in PR212 are credited, rather than relabeled as new results.

## 3. Why every occurring exponent has an effective affine witness

[DECIDABLE_FIXED_GRADE_SPECTRUM.md](../../../research/astra-critical-rigidity-20261128/ordinary/DECIDABLE_FIXED_GRADE_SPECTRUM.md) adds two logically distinct steps. First, every exponent arising from arbitrary calendars also arises from one affine calendar family. Second, existence of each possible affine-family pattern is decidable.

For the first step, thin the original minimal affine sequence until each cyclic gap is constant or tends to infinity. A difference of two parameter points increases all the growing gaps simultaneously and fixes all the constant ones. This gives an integral ray on which every gap is eventually positive. Rotating at the outset makes the first odd date zero. Primitivity is maintained by avoiding the finite list of proper affine conditions associated with nontrivial cyclic gap shifts. The coordinator checked the order of this choice: constraints depending only on the initial clock coordinate are avoided first, and the remaining conditions are proper hypersurfaces in the other initial dyadic coordinates.

For each residual clock root, the residual carry is a nonzero Laurent polynomial in those other coordinates. Otherwise its irreducible factor would already have been removed from `F`. Zariski density of the dyadic torus grid therefore permits simultaneous avoidance of all new root collisions as well as the primitivity conditions. After specialization, the residual univariate polynomials are coprime. Their evaluated gcd divides a fixed integral Bézout constant, after clearing fixed scalar denominators and Laurent powers. Consequently the full denominator has logarithm `sigma L+O(1)`, not merely `sigma L+o(L)`.

Only finitely many local gcd factors remain. At odd primes they are determined by bounded congruence precision, so they stabilize on a progression in the ray parameter. The two-adic valuation of the residual monic clock factor stabilizes; the three-adic unit computation is unchanged. Its real leading term is fixed. This establishes the stated finite nonzero limiting ratio on a progression. No effective modulus for the original arbitrary sequence is inferred from this construction.

The effective step removes the potentially unbounded real radical over the maximal abelian extension. If the primitive radical has degree `k'`, its degree over that extension is exactly `k'/gcd(k',2)`. The coordinator checked both inequalities. The original rational degree follows from the two constant-term valuations. A real radical subfield inside an abelian extension is normal; its irreducible binomial cannot have degree greater than two because it would have nonreal conjugates. Conversely the square root of a rational number is already abelian. Thus at most `2p` root-of-unity characters remain, even when the calendar slopes are unbounded.

Writing each slope as a residue plus a fixed bounded multiple of this remaining degree reduces each root cancellation to a sum `sum alpha_i 2^B_i=0` with computable fixed algebraic coefficients. Such a predicate is effectively definable by integer addition and comparisons. After passing to an integer coordinate basis, a sufficiently large binary gap forces the entire lower block to vanish: its size is below the next binary place, while vanishing of the total makes it divisible by that place. All gaps inside a block are bounded, so their offsets can be enumerated. Negative offsets and repeated exponents are allowed.

The other requirements—eventual order, positive cyclic gaps, exact equality classes of slope residues, primitivity, and the gcd with the fixed grade—are linear integer conditions once the finite character data are fixed. No product of two unbounded variables is left in this encoding. Presburger decidability therefore yields a terminating existence decision and an exhaustive search yields a witness after a positive decision. The coordinator checked the original publisher abstract of Oppen's decision-procedure theorem for this standard input; a new proof of Presburger decidability is not claimed.

The [executable](../../../research/astra-critical-rigidity-20261128/ordinary/solve_denominator_spectrum.py) implements this reduction using exact SymPy cyclotomic arithmetic and Z3's linear integer solver. Its coefficient representation, common denominator clearing, bounded binary-gap representatives, and conjugacy masks were reviewed separately. The positive root is always retained, and the proper-shorter-congruence obstruction justifies the least-common-multiple pruning. An option disables this character pruning. The code stops on any unresolved solver answer. Every satisfying model is independently checked by an exact univariate polynomial gcd, chronological order and cyclic gap primitivity. This does not turn Z3's negative answers into Lean certificates.

The central normal and optimized runs both compute `{1}` at grades one, two, three and five, and `{3/4,1}` at grade four. The grade-four witness for `3/4` has length `108n+104` and odd dates `0, 28n+51, 55n+77, 81n+78`. A separate author's grade-six query timed out; no grade-six answer from that run is claimed. Later explicit classifications or larger solver runs require their own reviewed checkpoint.

## 4. Six-sum rigidity without a coprime cycle clock

The chosen neighboring denominators satisfy a product bound in the real minimum, while the published irrationality estimate supplies `N` at least a power of that minimum, up to logarithms. The grid is built from the actual extended odd sequence and its exact native exponents. It works for either stride sign and for nontrivial gcd of the full period and division sum.

At width below three, the short spatial columns have tags one or two and an exact common recurrence. If a six-sum difference vanishes once, reducing around the complete rational circuit shows that its denominator divides it at every phase. Since the complete denominator is odd, the integer divisibility on forced rows is recovered as well. The signed three-edge analysis forces a zero on a fixed angular arc. Under the polynomial-height contradiction hypothesis, the previous sparse recurrence theorem forbids such an arc. This proves six-sum uniqueness in the regime in which it is needed, rather than assuming the unscaled rational labels are integers.

## 5. Positive descending intervals and actual integer lifts

[POSITIVE_DESCENDING_INTERVALS.md](../../../research/astra-critical-rigidity-20261128/ordinary/POSITIVE_DESCENDING_INTERVALS.md) strengthens the real occupation argument by adding the negative signed separation to its potential. Active steps increase the potential by at most two, while the relevant common-even steps halve a nonzero integer quantity. This bounds negative or balanced dates linearly in the combined odd grade. Together with the previous small-separation bound, any superlogarithmic family spends almost all of its dates at positive tiny separation. For actual integer arms the consequence is one-sided: the upper integer has a long initial string of twos among the relevant low ternary digits. The note does not assert that interpretation for arbitrary rational arms.

For a positive reduced fraction `m/3^n`, every odd departure removes precisely one factor of three from its denominator. Every nonzero numerator permits only finitely many consecutive even divisions, so the first integer landing exists after exactly `n` odd departures. A universal entry route and a congruence lift make an actual positive ordinary pair follow the whole landing word with one arm even throughout. The positive final separation prevents an earlier physical merger. This proves `J(n)<=K(C(n+1))`, and hence the previously established `o(n^2)` upper bound.

Conversely a single positive upper-even interval starts with a controlled height. Its initial part above one is charged directly, and its remaining duration is bounded by the corresponding `J`. This is a statement about one interval. Reusing that height bound separately on an unbounded number of intervals would require an additional argument and is not done here. The powered-motif calculation keeps both the full numerator and the changing power of three; it excludes a superlinear interval assembled from a bounded number of short powered motifs without asserting universal such compressibility.

It exhausts all 2,186 reduced positive starting numerators through denominator `3^7`, including landing at one and at larger integers, and tests 122 actual positive integer lifts.
