## PR192: polynomial residual growth and top-factor splitting

Put $g=\gcd(A,p)$, $(a,b)=(A/g,p/g)$, $t=b/\gcd(b,2)$, $F=X^p-2^A$, $G=\gcd(F,Q)$ and $n=\deg(F/G)$. Every root of a monic rational factor has modulus $2^{a/b}$. Its constant coefficient forces its degree to be divisible by $b$. Reversal and complex conjugation compare complementary coefficients and give $b\mid2i$ at each occupied exponent $i$, so each such factor belongs to $\mathbb Q[X^t]$. The principal projected order contributes the full $b$ roots since $Q$ is positive at the positive real root. Each further surviving projected order $h$ contributes at least $t\varphi(h)$ roots. The surviving root-of-unity orders have lcm $g$: a proper lcm in the original Fourier support would make the normalized coefficients $2^{d_i}2^{-Ai/p}$ periodic, forcing a proper cyclic translation of the actual parity word, and projection to $b$-th powers divides the support lcm $p$ by $b$. Assigning each maximal prime power in $g$ to one surviving order gives the totient-sum bound with at most one unit exception. Counting the distinct prime bases then gives $n/\log p\to\infty$. Combined with the specifically cited PR185 linear-forms and finite-gap input, the polynomial residual $|(F/G)(3)|$ grows faster than every fixed power of $p$. I reconstructed the degree argument and treated the imported numerical gap as source-qualified. The same root-count argument gives full irreducibility of the homogeneous cyclotomic component $H_h$ for $b$ odd. If $b$ is even there can be at most two factors, and a split occurs exactly when $2^a\eta$ is a square in $\mathbb Q(\eta)$ for primitive order-$h$ $\eta$. In a half-size fibre $\alpha^{b/2}$ is invariant under the absolute Galois stabilizer of $\eta$. Conversely a square gives the two binomial factors over that field and the minimum fibre size forces equality of degrees. Removing the odd-order part of $\eta$ reduces the square test to $v_2(h)$: at 0 or 1, the odd-conductor field is disjoint from $\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)$; at 2, $\pm2i$ is a square; at larger valuation $\sqrt2$ is already in the field whereas taking a square root of a primitive $h$-th root increases its conductor to $2h$. Thus splitting occurs exactly for $b$ even and $v_2(h)=2$. Since the lcm argument forces a top survivor at prime-power $g$, the whole top survives outside $g=4$ with even $b$, exactly overlapping the corresponding ABR5 conclusion. For mixed $g$ lcm survival need not include the top, and ABR5's examples cancel it in full.

## PR193: a bound on the actual reduced denominator

C5 concerns the common denominator $d$ after all cancellations. Let $m$ be the minimum of a primitive positive rational shortcut cycle of length $A$, and $e=dm$. If $h=P(k)<A$, then for each $s+t=h$ the $h+1$ starts from $-s$ to $t$ around that minimum are distinct, yet two share a $k$-bit factor. Their scaled numerators differ by a nonzero multiple of $2^k$. Backward in time the scaled state is at most $2^s e$; forward its sum with $d$ is at most $(3/2)^t(e+d)$. Comparing with $e+2^k$ gives C5.1 exactly. Balancing the two directions and using $m\le p/(3\Lambda)$ from the cyclic product gives the stated word-dependent lower bound. Its strength requires a qualifying scale with small factor count; the bound can be ineffective once $P(k)=A$ or $P(k)/k$ is large. C3 separately produces positive rational cycles whose denominators are prime to any prescribed modulus and grow exponentially, using a single large final gap. No inference from these examples to all cycle words is warranted.
