## Cases and physical data

A strongly connected two-vertex incidence graph is primitive if it has a loop. Without a loop each letter's image consists only of the opposite letter, contradicting the necessary agreement of first symbols for an infinite fixed point. A disconnected diagonal case has pure images. If the word contains an expanding letter, the prefix of fixed letters preceding its first occurrence can be deleted and substitution iteration supplies an infinite constant tail. When both letters are fixed, every word is a fixed point of the identity; the theorem appropriately excludes this case. A reducible graph with a single cross direction can be renamed so the pure image is $1^d$, the mixed image has $a$ zeros and $b\ge1$ ones, and $d\ge1$. The case $a=0$ omits zero from the entire substituted word. Thus $a,d\ge1$ exhausts the nonperiodic possibilities.

All monomial iterates have strictly positive binary valuations. At any such pair, the earliest differing symbol in two infinite binary words has a prefix multiplier of strictly smaller valuation than every later contribution to the difference of the prefix-one sums. This also distinguishes unequal-length image repetitions unless those repetitions literally agree, which would make their every concatenation periodic. Thus the coefficient used to solve the affine Mahler equation is nonzero at all physical iterates, including in the fixed-letter case. Assuming the first evaluation algebraic keeps every subsequent evaluation in the same number field embedded in the binary-adic numbers. The rational multipliers and fixed-point inequality make the completion a rational affine function of that first evaluation with nonzero slope. Removing or reattaching a finite prefix preserves algebraicity.

## Two triangular growth regimes

For $d>a$, the mixed-coordinate valuation is a positive multiple of $d^n$ plus $O(a^n)$, as is the pure-coordinate valuation with its own leading multiplier. The weight ratio is positive rational with coprime coordinates $p,q$. The exact transverse monomial is $(y^p/x^q)^{a^n}$. If it moves, its rational height is $O(a^n)$ and the leading occupied weight line eventually gives a nonzero term with excess valuation $o(d^n)$. If it is fixed, Bézout parametrizes the common weight curve by one rational parameter, and the weighted one-variable series is formally transcendental by MPS-6. This covers $a=1$ and also $y^p/x^q=1$.

For $a\ge d\ge2$, let $V_n=v_2(x_n)$. The ratio $d^n/V_n$ tends to zero, including the repeated-eigenvalue case $a=d$ where $V_n$ has order $n a^n$. The rational heights of the two coordinates are $O(V_n)$ and $O(d^n)$, respectively. The affine equation incurs an additive height cost $O(V_j)$ at step $j$, and $\sum_{j\le n}V_j=O(V_n)$. At each zero-count exponent, only the finite run of ones before the next zero contributes to the corresponding coefficient of $G_1$. Thus the auxiliary equations really are linear over $\mathbb Q(Y)$ with polynomial entries. Clearing denominators gives a nonzero polynomial of individual $X,W$ degrees at most $m$; formal transcendence of $G_1$ over $\mathbb Q(X,Y)$ prevents its substitution from vanishing identically.

The first nonzero coefficient $P_t(Y)$ evaluates, for large $n$, at valuation $O_R(d^n)$ because $y_n\to0$ and its leading nonzero monomial dominates. Higher $X$ coefficients specialize to binary-adically integral values. Their tails have valuation at least $(t+1)V_n$, strictly greater than the first nonzero contribution once $m,R$ are fixed and $n$ is sufficiently large. The evaluation is nonzero and has valuation at least $t V_n$, with $t\ge(m+1)^2-1$. Its height is bounded by $C m V_n+O_R(d^n+1)$. The constant $C$ does not depend on the auxiliary polynomial, and an arbitrary $Y$ degree is confined to the lower-order term. Taking $m$ first and then $n$ establishes the contradiction with the fixed-field height inequality. No uniform-in-$m$ asymptotic for the discarded term is required.

## A fixed pure letter

When $d=1$ the initial ones are unchanged by the substitution and can be removed. The mixed image must then start with zero. If $a=1$, iteration at this initial zero gives the fixed word $01^\infty$. Otherwise $a\ge2$. Numbering zeros from zero, the ones count $c(k)$ before zero $k$ obeys $c(ak+r)=c(k)+bk+e_r$: the first $k$ zero images contribute $bk$ ones, the preceding original one letters remain fixed, and the first $r$ zeros of the current image account for its internal prefix. The normalization by $\tau=y^{b/(a-1)}$ on the positive real embedding gives $f_{ak+r}=d_r f_k$ and $d_0=1$. As a stress case, $0\mapsto001,\ 1\mapsto1$ gives $f_k=y^{-\operatorname{popcount}(k)}$, agreeing with the recurrence at every $k$.

If the resulting germ $H$ is algebraic, substitution into its monic minimal polynomial gives another monic annihilator of the same degree $l$, hence the same polynomial. The constant coefficient $C(z)$ is nonzero because $H(0)=1$. The quotient $H^l/C$ is a fixed Laurent series under $z\mapsto z^a$, so comparison of order and then the first nonconstant coefficient makes it a nonzero constant. For $R=H^l$, the functional equation implies all nonzero finite divisor orders at $\xi$ and $\xi^a$ agree modulo $l$. An exceptional order would propagate to the $a^j$ distinct preimages at each fixed depth, contradicting finiteness of the divisor. The order at zero is zero and the total divisor has degree zero, giving the infinity condition too. Over algebraically closed constants the rational function is an $l$-th power. Its formal $l$-th root differs by a constant root of unity from that rational root, proving rationality of $H$.

An eventual rational coefficient sequence is an exponential polynomial with distinct nonzero bases. The bases on the left of $f_k=f_{ak}$ all occur among the powered bases on the right by uniqueness of the pole decomposition. Since that right support has cardinality at most the original cardinality, taking powers permutes the support and no collisions remain. The bases are roots of unity. At a common iterate of the permutation, comparison at each pole gives $Q_j(a^h k)=Q_j(k)$, forcing every coefficient polynomial to be constant. A polynomial series with no poles would instead have eventually zero coefficients, impossible here. Thus the coefficients have finite eventual range. Repeating a nonzero radix-$a$ digit $r$ produces the positive real powers $d_r^j$, which have finite eventual range only for $d_r=1$. Hence $c(k)=bk/(a-1)$ at every zero index, forcing periodicity. This contradiction establishes transcendence of the weighted formal zero series, and the telescoping identity transfers it to the one series. The physical fixed value $y$ is nonzero, rational and distinct from one. Its binary-adic valuation is positive, so the specialized rational coefficients are binary-adically integral. The same quadratic-order one-variable auxiliary construction now applies in the original physical embedding. The positive real embedding was used only for the formal coefficient-range argument.

No counterexample or unsupported inference was found in these cases. A finite coding out of a larger alphabet need not have a binary fixed-point presentation. Transcendence here concerns a specified itinerary. No general substitution representation of an arbitrary ordinary trajectory follows.
