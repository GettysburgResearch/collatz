1. **Attachment to an ordinary source.** Every inverse gap step (2^g t−1)/3 sends odd 2-adic t to an odd 2-adic value at which the valuation is exactly g. The inverse compositions contract strictly. Their nested cylinders have one limit, so a rational source realizing the entire itinerary in the prescribed order must equal that limit. A finite attachment is invertible rational affine. Eliminating G0 yields q=a/(1−x)+(e−a(1−y)/(1−x))G1(x,y); the second coefficient vanishes exactly when the two rational affine maps share a fixed point. Distinct finite gap words of the same odd length cannot share one because valuations at each state on the fixed orbit are unique. All these limits are 2-adic. The map to an ordinary source introduces neither fresh starts at finite horizons nor a real-limit identification.
2. **Mahler recurrence and invertibility.** Splitting s=σ(s) into consecutive images gives G1=P0 G0(X',Y')+P1 G1(X',Y'). Eliminating G0 gives (3) with the displayed determinant D. Both 1−X' and 1−Y' are units. The two periodic image repetitions give P_i/(1−X_i'); at the first unequal bit only one common-prefix monomial occurs at the least valuation, forcing different evaluations. Equal periodic repetitions would make every concatenation of these two image words periodic, contradicting the fixed word's assumed aperiodicity. This explicitly handles proper-prefix images and shows D never vanishes at a physical iterate.
3. **Formal-function transcendence.** D-finiteness of an algebraic formal series follows by differentiation inside its finite separable algebraic extension. Coefficient extraction gives a genuinely nonzero polynomial recurrence: group differential monomials by index shift, then use distinct degrees in the falling-factorial basis. After normalization at the last symbol boundary, only finitely many possible short tuples occur because fixedly many clock positions cross boundedly many symbols. A polynomial in the clock index associated to an actually occurring tuple either vanishes identically or has finitely many roots. After a finite threshold only the first alternative survives, producing a constant-coefficient recurrence. At boundaries the normalized last r clock coefficients form a finite state, and the leading recurrence coefficient determines the next symbol, even when weighted clock zeros occur inside tiles. This forces eventual periodicity. The diagonal restriction of a multivariate algebraic relation is legitimate after removing its common X−Y power. These arguments cover nonzero rational rescalings even when weighted coefficients are unbounded.
4. **From the function to the value.** There is no general inference that one rational or algebraic specialization makes a formal function rational. The proof instead assumes B0 algebraic, propagates all B_n into a single number field using the nonvanishing determinant, and obtains h(B_n)=O(Λ^n). An auxiliary polynomial of individual degree m has evaluation height at most C m Λ^n+O_R(1), with C independent of m and R. Its order of vanishing grows superlinearly in m. The value at moving iterates is nonzero for sufficiently large n as checked next. Only then can the place-by-place product formula give the contradiction. Choosing m first and n sufficiently large absorbs O_R(1); no bound on auxiliary coefficients uniform in m is needed.
5. **Moving points, including degeneracies.** With irrational Perron weight ratio a least occupied weighted exponent is unique; finite truncation and positivity of the coordinate growth control the infinite tail. At rational weight (p,q), the transverse monomial y_n^p/x_n^q is exactly r_0^(μ^n). For |μ|≥2 and r_0≠1, these are distinct positive rationals and the height of the least-piece polynomial evaluation is O_R(|μ|^n), strictly smaller scale than Λ^n. Higher weighted pieces have a valuation gap at the Perron scale. When the ratio is constant on an infinite subsequence, the rational parametrization (a z_n^p,b z_n^q) follows from Bézout and r_n being constant. The weighted univariate transcendence of part 3 supplies a nonzero auxiliary series, and z_n→0 gives leading-term dominance despite coefficients with valuation bounded below only linearly. In the uniform case the same analysis uses Λ=b and the secondary integer c1−c0; the forbidden values ±b have no aperiodic binary substitution fixed point.

Coons and Borwein's original paper, arXiv:0806.1563, proves the bounded-integer-coefficient Fatou result. It alone would not cover the unbounded rational weights above. Bugeaud and Yao's *Hankel determinants, Padé approximations, and irrationality exponents for p-adic numbers* concerns special p-adic Mahler numbers. PR190 supplies its own weighted recurrence and moving-point argument rather than importing either paper as a universal value-lifting theorem. Standard number-field heights, the product formula and Perron–Frobenius are the external mathematical inputs.

The proven scope at the level of these written arguments is a non-eventually-periodic word **fixed by the binary substitution itself**, with either uniform images of common length at least two, or arbitrary nonempty images with primitive 2-by-2 incidence, and two physical affine maps with different fixed points. The proof supplies no reason an arbitrary exceptional Collatz itinerary has such a representation. A letter coding of a larger substitution is not covered automatically. This review makes no Lean transcendence claim.
