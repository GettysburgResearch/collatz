# Independent review of the two-face collision obstruction

Reviewed manuscript SHA256 is 7f52a8e5696f408f9e8c7de0084f342baaab66c1369a117d81ec325273825740.

1. The roots of every successive real linear polynomial \(P_i\) strictly decrease with \(i\), because \(P_i=-1/3\) at the root of \(P_{i+1}\) and both leading coefficients are positive. Two disjoint nonempty products of their factors cannot agree identically. A fixed exponent vector thus has finitely many terminal integer roots, and the uniform elementary root bound gives \(\log(1+Y)=O(A)\) on the stated fixed-length and fixed-order family.
2. The diagonal subsequence makes all integer characters constant or divergent with a fixed sign. The constant-character lattice is saturated. Coordinate differences in its kernel have integral coordinates in a basis of that kernel. Any further constant character there would extend rationally to the original ambient space, contradicting the extracted definition after clearing its denominator. Distinct torus monomials are therefore eventually separated by divergent ratios and form no fixed relation on any infinite subsequence.
3. At either singleton face, every denominator and discriminant factor has a singleton extremal monomial. Their strict selecting inequalities define a cone containing the limiting direction in its interior. An integral simplicial subcone may straddle that direction internally. Passage to a fixed lattice coset gives positive new coordinates of size comparable to \(A\). The signs \(2^{-n_j}\) at infinity and \(2^{n_j}\) at two make all nonleading Laurent monomials have positive exponents after the selected extremal term is divided out. Scaling the root clears any coefficient poles. The rescaled monic discriminant is a monomial times a unit.
4. The roots give an unramified complex cover of a product of punctured disks. Pulling back coordinatewise by the \(d!\)-th powers kills monodromy, including every loop in the product torus. Boundedness of monic roots on a smaller polydisk permits holomorphic extension. Coefficientwise automorphisms over the algebraic closure of the rationals permute at most \(d\) formal roots in a domain. Hence every individual Taylor coefficient is algebraic. At any fixed order \(B\), the difference between the true polynomial and the product of the truncated linear factors has zero Taylor coefficients through total degree \(B\). Clearing the rational polynomial denominator with nonzero constant coefficient yields a finite polynomial identity of that vanishing order. It survives any chosen algebraic local embedding. At the dyadic place this argument operates entirely with the finite truncation.
5. Fix the prescribed saving beyond the \(Y\)-height exponent before choosing the truncation bound. After fixing residues modulo \(d!\) every surviving fast monomial is a rational integral power of two times an algebraic constant absorbed into its coefficient. The form systems are triangular with nonzero determinant. Each monomial has two-place product one, and the only remaining \(Y\) factor costs at most the fixed original height exponent. Clearing all dyadic coordinate denominators by one common power leaves this product unchanged. [Schlickewei, printed pp. 245–246, (1.3)](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1992__82_3_245_0.pdf)
6. A persistent exceptional rational relation must contain \(Y\), by the separated monomials. Its exact rational expansion has a unique least dyadic exponent eventually, including fixed coefficient valuations. Odd integral \(Y\) forces that exponent bounded. All surviving macroscopic monomial weights are consequently nonnegative. The rational system of zero weights and strict positive weights admits an integer direction with the same signs. Along its monomial arc through an actual positive sample every \(T_i\) and the reconstructed \(Y\) are real polynomials, and at least one \(T_i\) vanishes at zero. Some \(P_i\) has a zero strictly below one since \(P_i(0)=-1/3\) at such an index. At a greatest zero of all these polynomials only \(P_0\) can vanish: any \(P_i\) with \(i>0\) vanishing there forces \(P_{i-1}=-1/3\) there, so positivity at one forces a greater zero. This contradicts the disjoint product identity because the first surviving index has nonzero multiplicity.

The proof establishes the stated necessary two-face condition. It does not remove tied extremal faces or require a moving-coefficient form theorem.
