# Independent review of the lifetime gate-return experiment

1. On the first-hit basin of one, its future deficit \(g(1)\) equals zero. Therefore on the physical incoming path the two normalized output marks \(g(y)\) and \(b_{\tau_y}(y)\) converge jointly to \(-\min B\) and \(B(1)\). This is a continuous mapping of a single path. When adjacent sources coalesce before their first hit at clocks at most \(h=o(\sqrt{\log Y})\), subtracting the two premerger surpluses bounds the difference of their terminal marks by \(2h\), since the common continuation is identical. Early first hits are negligible. This gives the fixed-residue extension. Continuous marginals imply uniform convergence of the joint distribution functions on all threshold rectangles. The coarse ternary density is replaced in Haar \(L^1\) against those bounded rectangle tests exactly as for the previous marginal.
2. At a fixed middle clock, the endpoint height at the start of the retained future is \(z-w\). Both its positivity condition \(g(y)<z-w\) and its final surplus bound \(b_{\tau_y}(y)\le t\sqrt{2\log X/D}-(z-w)\) are tests on that same physical output. The complete-column mixing estimates retain the factor \(p\), the surviving initial-block probability, since this independent initial block was averaged only after applying its mark. Under the comparisons already established by sharp gate sampling, an independent terminal Brownian segment supplies both the minimum and final value jointly. The resulting three-block event equals, up to absolute \(o(p)\) error, the limiting comparison for a fair geometric walk surviving through \(N=\lfloor\log X/D\rfloor\) odd gaps with the prescribed endpoint bound. The exact ballot factor \((2N-s)_+/N\) gives the size-biased Gaussian, hence the Rayleigh distribution after conditioning on survival. The internal comparison boundaries have zero limiting mass.
3. A starting genuine gate in the terminating basin can complete exactly \(G-1\) further returns, where \(G=b_\tau\) includes the starting gate departure. Off that basin, any unbounded orbit has positive lower gate flux, and every different cycle has positive integer surplus per lap because its odd values are at least three. The sharp gate sampler gives the terminating basin asymptotic mass \(d_1\). This yields the finite-\(t\) curve and by tightness the unbounded-\(t\) limit \(1-d_1\).
4. For \(a\) finished gate words, the genuine shortcut count is \(s=2q-a\). Every odd departure is at least three and the product bound gives \(1\le n2^a(5/6)^q\). When stopping at one first, replace \(a\) in the exponent by the number of gate departures, at most \(a\). The induced transitions guarantee a finite stop under both alternatives. Heights and all additional suffix-membership horizons fit into one prefix of \(O(\log(2X)+a)\) shortcuts. In the independent Catalan law the reversed words concatenate as a fair walk up to its first hit of \(a\). The maximal subgaussian bound controls the event that this happens before the true deterministic clock cap. Including the cemetery alternative yields the one-sided total-variation lower bound.

The comparison establishes a survival statistic equal asymptotically to the ordinary counterexample density at supercritical return counts. Its finite-budget argument gives a lower bound on total variation. The upper bound on true survival required for universal convergence remains open.
