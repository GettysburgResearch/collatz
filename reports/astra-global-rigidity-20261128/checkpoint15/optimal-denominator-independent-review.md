For fixed native mismatch budget \(s\ge1\), write \(P=\#\{i:a_i=1\}\). On any positive-deficit sequence the normalized \(\log_2q/p\) stays bounded. The two-log bound at the rational minimum gives \(\log_2(y_0+q)\le\log_2q+O(\log p)\). The numerator difference at matching-run starts is nonzero for a proper lag, and absorbs every binary-clock factor on that run. The at most \(s\) leftover exponents are bounded by the source height. The full clock is consequently \(O_s(p)\), even for exponential \(q\).

With \(d=Kp+r\), paired sources stay distinct and all coordinates have logarithmic height \(O_{K,s}(p)\). At fixed \(K,s\), at most \((K+1)s+1=k_0\) runs cover a proportion tending to one of the virtual comparison interval. In the chronological fixed-form product the source numerator costs \(\log_2q+O(\log p)\), and the \(2k'-1\) sums of positive affine increments cost \((2k'-1)\log_2q\). The remaining multiplier charge is bounded by \(\log_2(3/2)\) times the exponent-one count on the entire two-copy interval, by using \(\max(y_i+q,2q)\). All field dimensions, boxes and small coverage losses are fixed along this first limit. Taking the limsup of the normalized deficit and then \(K\to\infty\) establishes the coefficient \(2s\). Its uniform little-oh follows by the same violating-sequence formulation. In particular \(P\le p\) gives \(\log_2q/p\ge(2-\log_2 3)/(2s)-o_s(1)\).

For fixed large \(s\), choose the indicated two primes \(\ell\asymp s\) and \(L>\ell^2\). In an upper-convergent mechanical template, the frequency of the native pair \((1,2)\) is bounded below as the length grows. Two separated interior swaps move \(110\) to \(101\), changing exactly those two consecutive native exponents at each changed marker. Advancing \(\ell\) cells preserves the \(\ell\) marker and interchanges membership in the \(L\) marker at exactly \(2\ell\) cells. The mismatch count is \(4\ell\le s\). A repetition multiplicity divides the common number of cells since the template length and odd count are coprime, and its associated whole-cell shift must preserve both markers. This forces the full native period. The exact value from PR212, including its gcd with \(A-B\), bounds the **complete reduced denominator** by \(N2^{n(\ell+L-1)}\). Its ratio per native odd period has limsup \(O(1/s)\) with an absolute constant.

The result concerns fixed \(s\), increasing period, and only then the dependence on \(s\). In positive rational cycles exponent-one departures and literal numerical rises need not coincide. For example \(1/17\mapsto5/17\mapsto1/17\) has native exponents two and five. The stronger bound uses the exponent-one count; replacing it by the number of actual numerical rises gives a weaker valid inequality.
