1. For nonzero rational $y$, a polynomial relation over $\mathbb Q(X)$ reduces coefficientwise at every prime outside a finite set after clearing its coefficients. Exclude the numerator and denominator primes of $y$ and the primes killing the whole polynomial. The support of the reduced coefficients $u_t y^{s_t}$ is exactly the support of $u$. Christol's characterization gives recognizability in two distinct prime bases and Cobham gives eventual periodicity. In the bivariate case, removing the common power of $Y-1$ before specialization leaves a nonzero relation for $y=1$. ## Effective enumeration

The compactness order bound in this proof also has a terminating search for a given substitution. Compute the legal adjacent pairs by iterating the finite pair closure. If $k^t\ge J$, all legal words of length $J$ can be obtained by listing the factors of $\sigma^t(ab)$ for legal pairs $ab$. Every sufficiently deep occurrence crosses at most one boundary of its $t$-blocks, and primitivity gives arbitrarily deep occurrences. Fix the rational auxiliary polynomials by finite linear algebra. Enumerate $J$ until every legal $J$-letter prefix has a nonzero composition coefficient of order below $J$ for its assigned polynomial. The preceding compactness argument proves termination. The resulting finite coefficient list supplies elementary height and rational-root bounds. Since $|\delta|<k$, the index at which terms of order $O_m(1+|\delta|^j)$ are below a prescribed positive fraction of $k^j$ is computable. Ordinary Weil-height inequalities give explicit constants for the finite list of rational transformations.
