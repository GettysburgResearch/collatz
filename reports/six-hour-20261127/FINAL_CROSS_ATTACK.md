# Final cross-attack: three precise coupling barriers

**Collatz remains open.** After the main packets were published, the three attacks were compared at the level of one fixed ordinary component. The elementary statements below were reconstructed by the root. They add no Lean declaration or new finite-control claim. They distinguish a refuted strengthening from the remaining open paired-source problem.

## A literal paired-predecessor proposal is impossible

For the ordinary shortcut `T`, the image of an odd integer is two modulo three. Halving a number prime to three keeps it prime to three. Hence once an ordinary trajectory has had an odd departure, it can never subsequently be divisible by three.

It follows that, for positive integers `x,q` and `h>=0`,

`T^h(3*x)=3*q  =>  x=2^h*q`.

All the preceding departures must have been even; cancel three after reversing those halvings. Consequently simultaneous sources satisfying `x -> q` and `3*x -> 3*q` are exactly the dyadic copies `x=2^h*q`. There is at most one such source in any half-open octave `[X,2X)`. A proposed positive-density or `X/(log X)^gamma` lower bound for this literal paired target is therefore **REFUTED**, even when both targets are known to converge.

For a meaningful paired-predecessor question, replace the second target by a true unit successor, for example `T(3*q)=(9*q+1)/2` when `q` is odd. A large *joint* population reaching `q` and that successor from `x` and `3*x` remains open. Separate predecessor or first-clearance estimates do not imply its existence.

## A fixed ordinary atom cannot be shifted at arbitrarily fine real scale

Let a positive rational start `a/3^K`, with `3` not dividing `a` and `K>=1`, follow a fixed shortcut parity word until its `K`th odd departure. If that first-clearance clock is `t` and the ordinary landing is `y`, then

`2^t*y = a + C`,

where `C` is the positive affine intercept of the word. If the first odd departure occurs at index `i0`, its contribution to `C` is `2^i0 * 3^(K-1)`. Thus

`2^t / 3^K > 2^i0/(3*y) >= 1/(3*y)`.

Changing the target by one while preserving this word and denominator would change the real input by `2^t/3^K`, bounded below as displayed. Changing the target by three preserves reducedness and changes the real input by more than `1/y`. The numerator changes by a multiple of `2^t`, so the parity prefix really is preserved in the latter case.

Accordingly, arbitrarily long parity agreement and high ternary denominator do not make such a change of a **fixed** ordinary atom arbitrarily small in the real source interval. A real-continuity or compactness argument cannot identify the two ordinary labels by this particular same-word construction.

## The bounded critical census cannot put q and 3q on one word

Fix a length `m` and the integer `K` characterized by

`2^m <= 3^K < 3*2^m`.

Suppose two positive rational starts have denominators dividing `3^K`, both lie in `[1/6,13/2]`, and realize the same length-`m` parity word. Assume its odd count is `g>=K`, so the endpoints are ordinary integers. Write the difference of the starting numerators over `3^K` as `d`. The ordinary parity-cylinder identity gives `2^m | d`; write `d=2^m*b`. The endpoint difference is exactly

`3^(g-K)*b`, with `|b| < 19`.

The bound follows directly by multiplying the real source difference, at most `19/3`, by `3^K/2^m < 3`. If the endpoints were `q` and `3*q` with `q = 17 mod 48`, their difference would be `2*q`, which is prime to three and at least 34. Positive excess `g>K` is impossible by divisibility by three. At zero excess `g=K`, the size bound is impossible. Both cases are needed; the congruence argument alone does not rule out zero excess.

This applies to the old/new critical censuses whenever their starts are compared on one common word after the larger denominator has cleared. It does not rule out a paired construction using **different** words. Such a construction must control their two clocks and two full affine intercepts jointly. ## A noncancelling target for a future transport argument

Let `b` be the indicator of a nonempty ordinary component disjoint from the basin of one, and let `m>=2` be its least element. For any positive strictly decreasing summable weight `w`, absolute convergence and reindexing give

`D = sum_{x>=1} w(x)*(b(x+1)-b(x))`

`  = sum_{y>=2} (w(y-1)-w(y))*b(y)`

` >= w(m-1)-w(m) > 0`.

For example `w(x)=1/x^2` has all the required properties. Unlike an arbitrary signed sum of canonical disagreements, this expression has a definite positive contribution from the fixed least element.

Use the newly constructed directed canonical paths through one to connect each `x` to `x+1`, and separately each `x+1` to `x`. Their lengths are `O(log(x+1))`. Weighting by `1/x^2` defines two summable nonnegative canonical-arrow incidence functions `L+` and `L-`. Every ordinary edge leaves `b` unchanged. Telescoping each actual path, with `Delta(q)=b(3*q)-b(q)`, yields

`sum_q L+(q)*Delta(q)=D`,

`sum_q L-(q)*Delta(q)=-D`,

and therefore

`||L+ - L-||_1 >= 2*D >= 2*(w(m-1)-w(m))`.

Thus a transport construction that made these two incidence functions sufficiently close while preserving this fixed-source signal would exclude that component. The current height and length bounds do not do so. Strong connectivity of the augmented graph supplies paths, but it does not make their extra arrows preserve ordinary basin membership. The available unit exchanges synchronize the two trials after fixed prefixes, while the nonunit exchange offers alternatives; neither supplies independent repeated chances of ordinary fusion.
