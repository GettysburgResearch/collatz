The binary ternary-tree condition is exactly the all-depth modular midpoint restriction on one fundamental population. At the first differing ternary digit, a nontrivial midpoint triple needs all three children. Conversely, three occupied children of one prefix supply such a triple modulo the next power of three. A ternary unit or a common integer translation permutes the children over each prefix and preserves the condition.

For $d_0=\log_3 2$ and $p_0=1/d_0$, a finite ternary tree with $n$ leaves has a binary subtree with at least $n^{d_0}$ leaves. On sorted child sizes $n_1\ge n_2\ge n_3$, the needed inequality is $n_1^{d_0}+n_2^{d_0}\ge(n_1+n_2+n_3)^{d_0}$. Setting $a=n_1^{d_0}$ and $b=n_2^{d_0}$ reduces it to $(a+b)^{p_0}\ge a^{p_0}+2b^{p_0}$ for $a\ge b$. The increment is increasing in $a$, and at $a=b$ the identity $2^{p_0}=3$ gives equality. Removing such subtrees drops the concave potential $n^{1-d_0}$ by at least $1-d_0$ each time. Therefore the stated number of classes is sufficient, with no probabilistic assumption.
