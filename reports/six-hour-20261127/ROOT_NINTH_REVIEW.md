## Signed dyadic flux and its exact obstruction

For an eventually invariant binary labeling of ordinary positive integers with both colors of positive lower density, the signed opportunity on a real dyadic ray is `E_j=b(2*n_j+1)-b(n_j)`. Adjacent ordinary counted fusion implies `E(E_j^2)=O(j^(-1/2))`. Every local weak-* cluster of the physical shell profiles is invariant under dilation by two and three, hence constant by irrational log rotation. Both colors' ordinary predecessor populations keep that constant strictly between zero and one. Testing the cluster on a measurable set of eventual constant color proves that every predetermined infinite subsequence of levels sees both colors almost surely. The exceptional null set may depend on the subsequence.

At an opportunity, `E=1-2*b`. Its next fair child bit flips the sign exactly when it changes the current label. Opportunity times are predictable, and almost surely infinitely many occur, so the signs after the first are independent fair signs. The resulting unweighted predictive sum has ordinary random-walk fluctuations on the opportunity clock, although the realized label increments telescope to a bounded value. In particular bounded labels do not imply a finite total predictive quadratic variation.

Split it into the centered fair-bit martingale and twice the realized increment sum. The first has summable expected quadratic variation; the second converges by summation by parts. Powers `w_j=j^(-s)` work for `s>1/4`, while the available direct absolute estimate needs `s>1/2`.
