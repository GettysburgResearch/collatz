## Four resonant defects and a continuum of reduced denominator slopes

2. Advancing `v=aB` matches corresponding odd dates in blocks `j` and `j+B`. Their difference is `bB` except when the phase shift changes the slot-zero pulse. 3. A pair contributes the affine numerator `3x+1+s` over four. The resulting within-block Jacobian gives weight `3^(a-L)·3^(L-1-i)·4^i` to slot `i`; iterating `Qx'=Px+c_j` gives `C=P^(n-1)F(Q/P)`. Write `M_l=P^φ(l) Φ_l(Q/P)`. In `Z[1/P]`, `Φ_l(Q/P)` and `M_l` are associates. For `l>1` the background vanishes, and the pulse supported on `t_i`-multiples contributes `n/t_i` precisely if `l|t_i`. Positivity of its weights prevents cancellation. All inactive distinct irreducibles divide the polynomial jointly in `Z[X]`. Their *product* therefore divides `C` after specialization, since its value is a unit at 2 and 3. For an active order the integer gcd divides `K_l`. In particular primes common to several `M_l` present no loss in the upper denominator bound. For the lower bound, `v(gcd(C,∏M_l))≤Σv(gcd(C,M_l))` for every prime, and `D/gcd(C,D)` is at least the corresponding quotient for the active subproduct. This recovers both sides of (C1).
4. Order one can cost at most one degree in the logarithm of numerator or determinant. The fixed-`n` nontrivial factors each have degree-size `φ(l)log P+O_n(1)`. Since `n` varies only after this fixed-parameter estimate, the order-one loss is `O(1/n)` in the slope, and all fixed-`n` error can be made `≤1/n` separately. CRT gives active-degree proportion `1/2+(1-∏(1-1/r_i))/6`. Euler divergence with tail terms tending to zero gives all interior products and both endpoints. Taking `a>n²` makes full positive drift tend to zero. Each odd site's individual distortion is bounded by the full product; the minimum therefore diverges. Native sites between consecutive odd departures are bounded below by the smaller endpoint.
5. The cyclic comparison multiplies the class of the phase carry by `(Q/P)^B-1` up to a unit modulo the *entire* `D`. The survivor orders are exactly the divisors of `t_0` that do not divide `B`. For them the integral resultant of `Φ_l` and `X^B-1` bounds the gcd after evaluation, since `P` is a unit at all the possible primes. Inactive products divide jointly as above, and the bounded resultants multiply only after fixing `n`. The remaining degree fraction is exactly `1/3`, so the residual gcd fraction tends to `2/3` independently of the original denominator slope.
