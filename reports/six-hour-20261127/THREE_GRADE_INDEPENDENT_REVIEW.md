## What the modular block actually bounds

At a maximizing cut the full positive cycle has depths $f_i+z_i\in[0,121/50]$, with $z_i\in\{0,1,2\}$ and $z_i=2$ permitted at the equality $f_i=21/50$. The exact binary condition is $z_{i+1}\in\{z_i,z_i-1\}$ for mechanical increment one, or $z_{i+1}\in\{z_i,z_i+1\}$ for increment two. For a segment with fixed starting grade $z_0$, division dates are $M_i+z_0-z_i$. Multiplying its numerator by $2^{2-z_0}$ therefore turns its digits into the integral $2^{M_i}2^{2-z_i}$. Ordinary fourfold equality implies that its partial signed sum $D_j$ is divisible by $2^{M_j}$, since the remaining terms are divisible by that power and the suffix multiplier on $D_j$ is odd. The recursion $2^{m_i}C_{i+1}=3C_i+\delta_i$ follows with *signed* integral $C_i$.

For a 64-step block let $\nu$ be the single-path counting measure on residues modulo $2^{M_{64}}$, allowing both endpoints all their permitted grades. If $H=\nu*\nu$, the number of ordered fourfold solutions at residue $r$ is exactly $\sum_x H(x)H(x-r)$. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds this by $\sum_xH(x)^2$, with no condition on $r$. A particular inherited integer carry $C$ calls for $r=-3^{64}C$, and retaining any given previous endpoint grades produces a subset of these solutions. Thus the same zero-residue bound applies after every possible fixed history. Multiplying block bounds can overcount common endpoints, which is harmless for the upper estimate. With $C=0$ the zero-residue count requires every intermediate carry integral, while the terminal carry is free. Allowing a terminal carry does not impose an ordinary zero-sum equality.

The independent implementation used the two *unordered* grade pairs, retaining their ordered multiplicities, and iterated signed carries. I independently searched all possible changes of slope arrangement. Two distinct phase cuts can collide only at $d\alpha\in\mathbb Z+\{0,21/50,-21/50\}$ with $1\le d\le64$, where $\alpha=\theta-1$. The exact rational search finds no such slope in the closed prescribed interval. The fixed cuts at intercept zero and $21/50$ are included through $j=0$. Thus the complete order of 130 distinct cuts is constant throughout the slab, producing 130 point and 130 open faces. Direct computation of every resulting itinerary gives the same 130 distinct schedules and the same range of modular fourth powers as the author. The maximum is $448804760794336774447153725940272863$ and its fifth power is strictly less than $3^{384}$. The two ends of the slope interval also give exactly the midpoint schedule set. The separate direct ordinary path/residue enumeration checked all 88 short schedules of lengths one through eight, 121,542 residue atoms and 440 nonzero inherited carries. These controls support the exact long-block computation.

It follows that arbitrary fixed-endpoint ordinary fourfold equality on a segment of length $a$ has at most $O(3^{6a/5})$ solutions: use the preceding bound on each complete block and an absolute finite factor for the remainder. ## Height and physical ratio

The raw affine reciprocity from the original four-segment argument uses the same grid carry in its binary and ternary lifts. In the scaled numerator recurrence the difference equals $d\sum_i\sigma_i B_i$. The placements have $h\asymp K$, $2^m,3^n\ge2^h$, $|b_0|\le4\,2^{m-h}$, $|a_0|\ll K3^n$, and $|b_0|\ll K2^m$. The strip width contributes only an absolute endpoint error in $T$ and in the decomposition of local dates. Here $B_i=O(K2^T)$ and $T\le(1-\eta)h+O(1)$. Because $Y\ge P$ and $\ln d=o(\ln P)$, the factor $d$ still gives $|d\sum\sigma_i B_i|\le 2^h e^{-c_\eta K}$ for large $K$. The positive margin in $m-r_i$ also gives $d<2^{m-r_i}$ wherever termwise reciprocity uses that inequality. These are the actual hypotheses of Weaver's separation lemma. It forces ordinary zero and justifies applying the modular *upper bound* to each quantized fourth moment. The four conditioned interiors have a product counting measure, since their cut grades and exterior choices are fixed. Fourier Hölder and the bounded circular quantization remainders then give the displayed count $P\ll_\eta (b+1)3^{b-4a+O(1)}3^{6a/5}$. Since $b\sim(4+\eta)h/\theta$, $a\sim(1-\eta)h/\theta$, $\log_2Y\sim(3+\eta)h$ and $\theta\to\log_2 3$, taking $\eta\downarrow0$ yields $\limsup\ln P/\ln Y\le(6/5)/3=2/5$. For the physical-band variant, each of the at most $2^b$ exponent words gives only one terminal numerator modulo $3^b$. Taking $3^b>Y$ distinguishes all actual positive odd numerators at the end of their cycle windows. Thus $Y\gg P^{\log_2 3}$ and $P/\min x_i\to0$ when $d=P^{o(1)}$ and $X/\min x_i$ is bounded. Summing $\log_2(3x_i+1)-\log_2(3x_i)$ over at most one lap changes the discrepancy–height relation by $O(P/\min x_i)=o(1)$. ## Complementary orientations and the entropy margin

For ordinary phases canonical height at most two implies coprime clocks. One may also see the specific case directly: $F(x)=3x+\operatorname{lowbit}(x)$ is nondecreasing on integers with 2-adic valuation at most two. At difference one its possible worst loss in the lowbit is three, so equality can occur but reversal cannot. At fixed mechanical rank a whole return permutes a finite set of distinct physical phases, hence its nondecreasing map is strictly order-preserving on that set and fixes it. This rules out a shorter rank period. The scaled integer $K_r=2^{2-z_r}x_r$ and its lift $K_{r+P}=2K_r$ therefore traverse the rank circle. Its normalized value $K_r2^{-r/P}$ changes by $O(1)$ per chronological step using $(2^\theta-3)X=O(1)$, so $K_r=C2^{r/P}+O(P)$ with $C\asymp X$. It follows directly that real-gap magnitude is $O(Xt/P+P)$ and any one integer signed gap has rank multiplicity $O(1+P^3/(tX))$ at every offset $1\le t<P$. [Weaver 114/v1](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/114/v1)

At rank phase $f$, offset $u=t/P$, wrap $c$, and grade difference $v$, use $w=v-c$. The signed gap drift is $(2^{-w}-1)2^{2-z}$; it vanishes at $w=0$. At the reverse rank the new profile is $-1-w$ and the offset $1-u$. Thus the two displays use the same width $D_w=(121/50-|u+w|)_+$ for their possible $f+z$. For a closed interval of this length the entropy cost conditional on the actual phase and $w$ is at most the log of its number of lattice points, bounded below by zero when it is empty. Averaging over a full circle gives the stated piecewise linear $B(D)$, with at most a singleton exceptional arc if the interval has integer length. Recording the option *sets* for all six $w$ values costs $O(\ln b)$ for an entire window: at each date there are only boundedly many moving intercept cuts.

In 53 dates the discrepancy of any arc is at most $7/265$, since $53^2|\alpha-31/53|<1/5$ and a full translated 53-grid costs at most one extra point. The nonconstant part of each lattice-point cost has height at most $\ln2$. Freezing the first $w$ of a block, then comparing with the actual $j$th value, costs at most $j\epsilon\ln3$, where $\epsilon=D_h/P$; only the union bound on intervening changes is used. This proves the $26\epsilon\ln3$ error even though the actual grade sequence can depend on all the phases.

With $u\le1/2$, central states $w=0,-1$ charge opposite orientations. The two uncharged-on-one-side coefficients are $A=B(L-u)$ and $B_0=B(L-1+u)$, $L=121/50$. If their combined mass is $s$, the smaller central expense is at most $s AB_0/(A+B_0)\le s(A+B_0)/4\le s B(L-1/2)/2= s(23/50)\ln2$. Here the arguments stay in the concave range $[1,3]$. Every other state charges at most $B(L-1)=(21/50)\ln2$ in either orientation. The smaller full charge thus has the printed upper bound. The conditional change word has at most six initial choices and at most five new choices for every change. Subadditivity and the binary change entropy give $H(\epsilon)+\epsilon\ln5$ per date without an independent-change assumption.

The rank lower bound divides $\ln P-\ln[O(1+P^3/(tX))]$ by the chosen logarithmic window length $b$. Along a subsequence $\xi=\lim\ln X/\ln P$ lies in $[5/2,3]$ and $a=\lim\ln t/\ln P\in[0,1]$. The resulting coefficient is $\ln3\min(1,\xi+a-2)/(\xi+a-1)\ge(\ln3)/3$. This remains true when the preferred orientation switches to a small offset. The upper endpoint 3 uses the separately quoted rational-logarithm-ratio theorem applied to $a=3,b=2$, with all irrationality-exponent slacks sent to zero.
