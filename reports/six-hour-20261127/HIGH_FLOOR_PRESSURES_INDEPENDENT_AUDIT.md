For the lower bounds in powers $p>1$, choose an ordinary odd initial octave with binary log $tn+O(1)$, $2-\log_2 3<t<2$. The full odd-Syracuse density envelope of Inselmann Theorem 3.8, or its previously reviewed elementary shorter-clock version, is applicable because $t>2-\log_2 3$. After taking sufficiently small fixed error, every intermediate height on almost all those true sources is exponential in $n$. Telescoping controls $G/v=o(1)$ and $A_n=2n+O(\epsilon tn)+O(1)$; since $t<2$ this certifies that each source is its word's principal representative. The distinct-source reciprocal sum is uniformly positive on the octave. Its contribution through $Z^{p-1}/(v+zG)$ gives the lower rate after taking the limits in the stated order. With a moving floor of log slope $\alpha<\log_2 3$, the same step needs precisely $t>2-\log_2 3+\alpha$ and gives the claimed $\alpha$ subtraction.

At positive $z$ the affine identity bounds $Z$ by a fixed multiple of $3^n$. At $z=0$ an actual finite path decomposes into a simple stem, complete laps and a simple remainder. The uniform finite simple-path reciprocal bound controls the two nonperiodic parts, and a lap's true correction multiplier is $(r_C/3)^{P_C}$. Every complete lap lies strictly above the floor on the asserted event. Conversely every phase of a cycle entirely above the floor eventually is its own principal representative and contributes the same exponential rate. Taking a supremum across cycles requires no maximizing cycle. The centered exponent at floor one is therefore a genuine cycle-detection identity with its actual value unresolved. For a rising floor the correction accumulated in $n$ steps is at most $\exp(n/(3H_n))$, which is subexponential, including when $\alpha=0$.

For $p\le1$, $Z\ge1/[2\max(1,z)]$ and the unfiltered first moment is at most $n+1$. A visit to height $H$ by the principal physical window gives $Z(0)\ge 2^n/H$ independently of visit date. A moving floor below $3^{(1-o(1))n}$ with fixed strict log slope is exceeded with exponentially high probability by combining Markov on $Z(0)$ and the negative-drift exponential estimate on all terminal sums $\sum_{i=n-j}^{n-1}(\log_2 3-e_i)$. These are original unrestricted words. Conditional Jensen for negative $p$ gives the lower bound with the stated positive power of the event probability. The finite decision at the boundary $\alpha=\log_2 3$ depends on the actual floor, and no uniform boundary statement was imported. The separated expression uses precisely the same rotation lower bound and tilted upper bound for the gate rate; it involves a product of marginals, although each underlying estimate is on genuine ordinary paths.

For the sparse-even remainder, $r$ consecutive odd shortcut departures require $2^r\mid x+1$ and $x_t+1\le(3/2)^t(m+1)$. Enumerating the $D+1$ odd runs gives $A\le(k+2)(\lambda^{D+1}-1)/(\lambda-1)$ with $k=\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor$, $D=A-n$ and $\lambda=\log_2 3$. For $D<(1-\epsilon)\log_\lambda n$ this forces polynomially large $k$, where the two-sided shell bound has a decaying geometric tail. For larger $D$ one sums its stated geometric cost, with the logarithmic factor allowed at $p=d$. For $p>d$ the sparse-seed count suffices to sum the weighted powers of $k+2$. The two exponents agree at $p=d$. No part of the polynomial improvement proves first-power decay.
