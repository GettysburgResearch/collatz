## Reconstruction of M1 on a single finite ordinary window

Write $Z=x+1$, $Y=x+(2H+2)^4$. On one nonzero shadow, clearing its two endpoint denominators gives $2^E\mid q_t(q_sx_s+p_s)$. This works even when $q_s$ and $q_t$ have different binary valuations. Its length is at most $2\log_2Y_s$. An arbitrary singleton always has that length bound. Since $Y$ grows by at most $3/2$ per departure, induction gives the two bounds M2, including suffixes or truncations of a piece. In a high packet $(x+t)/Z$ lies between $1/2$ and $3/2$, so each shadow's multiplicative distortion is at most three. The physical singleton has distortion at most two. This proves M3.

I expanded from the right end using the actual affine maps. A shadow from clock $i$ to $j$ contributes $1-t_i$ at $(E_i,\ell-i)$ and $t_j-1$ at $(E_j,\ell-j)$. A singleton from $i$ to $i+1$ has incoming coefficient $2$ at $(E_i,\ell-i-1)$ and outgoing coefficient $-1$ at $(E_{i+1},\ell-i-1)$. At a shared boundary the binary entries coincide. The preceding outgoing ternary entry is at least the following incoming one, strictly larger when the next piece is a singleton. Hence deletion, without any amalgamation, preserves the two opposite weak orders. The one-edge orbit $17\mapsto13$ has root $56$ and total $54$. The original incoming exponent would give $58$.

Using $3Z_u\le2^{e_u}Z_{u+1}$ on the suffix bounds every term-to-root ratio by $(H+1)/Z_i$ or $(H+1)/Z_j$. On the singleton incoming side there is additionally the numerical factor $1/3$. The strict height cutoff makes the absolute sum of all these terms less than half the root. This rules out a deletion involving the root. Exhaustively deleting any remaining zero subsums keeps the prescribed sum and ends with every nonempty subsum nonzero. The singleton identity also shows that the root strictly exceeds the total as soon as some $e>1$. A high non-all-one packet consequently retains dimension at least two. For all ones, $Z_t=(3/2)^\ell Z_s$ and $2^\ell\mid Z_s$ already prevent a fast record.

Let $g=2^u3^v\gamma$ be the common divisor after deletion, with $\gcd(\gamma,6)=1$. Any retained nonroot implies $\gamma\mid c_i$, the total implies $2^u\le 2^{v_2(C)}Z_s$, and the root implies $3^v\le Z_t$, since $3\nmid C$. Dividing the root by these upper bounds proves M4, since $C/2^{v_2(C)}\ge1$. After clamping the binary and ternary powers independently, the residual is a nonzero integer formed by dividing the old coefficient by positive divisors. Its absolute value is at most $M$. For M5, the coordinate products at infinity, two and three are the prime-to-six parts, except that the root's three-adic absolute value is replaced by that of the total. The ratio is $3^{-\ell-v_3(Z_s)+v_3(Z_t)}$. Primitive scaling divides this whole product by $\gamma^d$. This recovers the stated upper bound even for an even packet-specific $C$.

For a good non-all-one packet I recovered M6 by moving the $\eta\ell\log2$ term to the left and writing $E=\ell+F$. The positive term in $F$ in the resulting bound for $\ell\log(3/2)$ is strictly smaller than $F\log2$. A sufficiently large old record therefore rules out a good fast record. At a fast record $Z_s<\sqrt{Z_t}$, and $\log2/\log(3/2)>5/3$, giving $H'\ge Z_t^{1/3}/M$. The upper bound in M7 follows from $H'\le C3^{m+\ell}Z_s$ and M2. The shift $Y-Z=G-1$ is harmless once $Z_t\ge G,M^{12}$. The startup dominates each of these thresholds. If a prefix under consideration ever returns below the high cutoff, its remaining suffix has at most $m$ pieces, so M2 bounds its terminal record by startup.

## The height-interval input

I checked Evertse and Ferretti's authors' paper, Corollary 1.3 on pp. 5–6 and Lemma 15.3 on p. 67 of the Leiden report. The field is $\mathbb Q$ and the places are $\infty,2,3$. Their height is the projective sup height. For a primitive integer vector its finite-place sup norms are one and its real height is the root. Each local catalog consists of $d$ independent forms from the coordinate forms and their sum. The exact projective exponents therefore sum to $\log Q'/\log H'-d$.

For each clamped nonroot let $p,b$ be its two weighted exponents divided by $\log H'$, and let $t_2,t_3,t_R$ be the analogous logs of the binary, ternary and remaining absolute parts of its coefficient. These three nonnegative quantities sum to at most $1/K_d$ above startup. The three projective exponents are $-p-t_2$, $-b-t_3$ and $p+b+t_2+t_3+t_R-1$. Rounding $p,b$ downward and using the real bound in the manuscript increases their sum by less than $8/K_d$ while bounding all three individually from above. Primitivity and the real size of the total put the two root-related valuations in $[0,1]$ and $[0,2]$ as asserted. With $K_d=20000d$ the aggregate rounding error is below $1/200$. The two fixed-dimensional ordered grids, before common rescaling, have at most $\binom{K_d+d-1}{d-1}^2$ choices. The two root grids add the displayed polynomial factor. Rescaling toward zero makes the exponents nonpositive with sum exactly $-d-1/200$.

These are precisely the individual inequalities and exponent sum in Corollary 1.3, with $\epsilon=1/200$. Its initial height interval ends at $d^{d/\epsilon}$. The remaining intervals have number at most $10^6 2^{2d} d^{12}\epsilon^{-2}\log(6d/\epsilon)$ up to integer rounding and have log-log width $\log(2d\epsilon^{-1}\log(6d))$. The exceptional space is proper and Lemma 15.3 gives at least one nonempty vanishing coordinate subsum for every vector in it. Every retained vector avoids it by the deletion already justified. The forms, grid and bounds are independent of the individual packet denominators. The exceptional interval locations may be ineffective. Their number and width are effective, as needed here. [Authors' report](https://math.leidenuniv.nl/reports/files/2010-17.pdf) · [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2013.177.2.4)

Take the actual prefix ending at the first maximum of each record-raising packet. A fast increment from old record $Y_o$ to $Y_t$ associated with a height interval $[u,v]$ lies wholly in $[u-\log D_m,v+\log8]$ on the log-log axis by M7 and $Y_s\le Y_o$. These record increments have disjoint interiors, even when numerous packets, dimensions or grids share an interval. Startup and sub-threshold primitive heights occupy at most $O(m+1)$ total progress above the initial log-log height, as $\log(n+G)\ge4\log(2H+2)$. There are at most $\lceil k/m\rceil$ slow increments. Thus (after adding the log of the time-per-piece bound) the cost has shape $O(\log(k+1)+k/m+\exp(Cm))$, and the stated logarithmic choice of $m$ yields M1. Weaver 15/v2, Theorem 9, (A7)–(A13), already has this two-place and disjoint-record method for charged periodic dictionaries. The new ingredients here are the moving rational endpoints, packet-specific possibly even clearing, and their high-state distortion and divisibility estimates. [Weaver, version 2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/15)

For M8, on a discrepancy the valuation identity puts the minimum of the two relevant depths below $C\log(2H)\log(\max(3,e))$. Yu's Theorem 1 applies over $\mathbb Q$ to $2$ and the unit ratio of the two boundary coefficients, at $p=3$, with exponents $(e,1)$; a nonunit right numerator gives valuation zero directly. The physical exponent is bounded within the local window. Each shallow clock can touch at most two mismatches, and $D$ mismatches require at most $2D+1$ pieces. This gives M8 with the stated quantifiers and the near-exponential alternative. The signed case requires the stated coincidence provisions. ## Companion claims

For the terminal clock budget the transported ternary numerator has order at least $k+r$. Multiplying its real bound by the physical growth bound cancels $3^k$ and gives $2^{k+s}3^r\le H(x_0+1)$. The full positive height ball has a unit-denominator integer anchor attaining at least $\lfloor\log_3H\rfloor$ initially, so a matching run has $2^k<3(x_0+1)$. This gives a clock depending on $H$ inside an interval independent of $H$. I also checked the equality example at $2^{k+1}-1$ and the varying dyadic denominators at the fixed ordinary state 1.

For L1, two adjacent good lag starts give $e_{i+h}=e_i$ exactly. A maximal interval of good starts encodes the edges strictly between those starts by the period beginning at its first start, even when the interval is shorter than $h$. The remaining edges are incident to bad starts. For L2 the fallback dictionary word has length one, and every stored word has length $h<P$, so none represents the primitive $P$-cycle. The complement lag preserves the cyclic defect count whenever the comparator lies in the attainable total range. The same conclusions follow directly by M1 and the rational centers $B_i/(3^h-2^A)$, using the separate cycle-minimum input to remove $\log n$ in L2. A nonrepeating ordinary edge segment has unequal endpoints at proper positive lag. The affine offset and unit denominator bounds give the claimed phase heights and nonzero displacement.

For D1, every depth-$d$ residue fixes all of its $d$ past letters because the generating alphabet has exactly one representative of each parity. Each fixed block phase has at least $\lfloor d/3\rfloor$ independent binary choices in such a word. The union bound and dyadic Borel–Cantelli pass to all times and all polynomial height cutoffs. Either terminal lift has exact ordinary exponents: the full affine residue successively cancels the final letter, oddness supplies exact valuations, and the inverse is positive. At the last block boundary every reversed suffix has mean at most $3/2<\log_23$, which places all earlier states below the least odd terminal in the principal lift. The distinct lift ending one step before 1 uses the full unit group modulo $3^{L+1}$. It need not use the least representative. The bound for a small initial integer counts at most one allowed forward word per such integer. The final bound on the grammar's contribution is pointwise at one chosen terminal; it excludes neither other scripts at that terminal nor a fixed escaping integer.

## Finite evidence and boundaries

I ran the handoff's packet/decoder program in standard and optimized Python. Both outputs were byte-identical to `11fc312708d93a57c1def0ce9b08f49c96217698ae23ce711eec812fe646a484`. My separate program reads actual words forward from 101 small odd seeds, preserves each word in a high ordinary lift, and checks every four-edge cut with independently varied singleton and rational phase assignments. It checks all irreducible outcomes of zero-subsum deletions on each packet, the two position orders, real dominance, primitive product bounds and an exact rational mesh sample. The receipt records 44,036 packets, 18,180 shadow-to-singleton boundary cases, 40,387 even clearings, 1,398 packets with more than one irreducible outcome and 845 mesh samples. These controls do not test the external theorem or establish the all-length estimate by enumeration. No full Lean formalization of M1, M8, L1–L2 or D1 is claimed. This reviewer did not count a fresh Lean build as evidence for the analytic arguments.

The global implication remains open. The moving-shadow bound excludes sustained compression of one ordinary ray by very few bounded-height rational tracks. Its shallow clocks do not alone identify a smaller ordinary merge source or preclude near-exponential growth.
