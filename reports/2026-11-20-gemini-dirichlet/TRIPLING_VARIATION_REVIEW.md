# Independent reconstruction of the homogeneous variation bound

**Reviewed source:** PR146, commit
`7d04c4dd756cfe495eb3eee78eba35a99085cd2a`,
`research/upper-bit-mergers/TRIPLING_VARIATION.md`, SHA256
`1a3aef543f1ec76db6b585041108eaa105f1d4ceb0c04fe421f1b15ed47d21bb`.

This review reconstructs the mathematical dependency used in the new
Dirichlet argument. It is a review of an existing theorem, not a novelty
claim or a Collatz proof. It covers arbitrary binary labels invariant
under the ordinary shortcut map. The proof in fact uses only equality
and transitivity, so arbitrary label sets work as well.

Let \(f(Tn)=f(n)\), and define

\[
D_f(X)=\#\{n\le X:n\text{ odd},\ f(3n)\ne f(n)\},\qquad
V_f(R,bR)=\#\{R\le n\le bR:f(n)\ne f(n+1)\}.
\]

For \(b>1\), set \(L_b=1+\lceil\log_2 b\rceil\). One conservative
fully explicit consequence of the source argument is

\[
V_f(R,bR)\le527L_b\,D_f(3000\,3^{30}bR)\qquad(R\ge1). \tag{RV1}
\]

The constants are intentionally loose. Their independence from \(R\)
and from \(f\) is the relevant fact. In particular, there is no additive
constant and no extra factor \(\log R\).

## 1. Minus comparison

Write \(g(y)=f(y-1)\), \(y\ge2\). The ordinary odd branch gives
\(g(2u)=g(3u)\). Consequently \(g(4u)=g(9u)\). If the odd source
\(8u-1\) passes its tripling test, then

\[
g(8u)=f(8u-1)=f(24u-3)=f(36u-4)=g(9u)=g(4u).
\]

Here \(36u-4=4(9u-1)\), and invariance under doubling is unconditional.

For \(h=2^v w\) with \(w\) odd, this reduces \(g(4h)\) to
\(g(4w)=g(9w)\), using tests
\(8\,2^a w-1\), \(0\le a<v\). Next

\[
g(9u)=g(4u)=g(8u)=g(27u)
\]

increments the power of three in \(g(3^k w)\) at the cost of the one
test \(8\,3^{k-2}w-1\). Since \(\langle3\rangle\) modulo 16 has
order four and index two, a \(j\in\{2,3,4,5\}\) makes
\(Y=3^j w\) congruent to 3 or 5 modulo 16. In either case the source's
finite affine table gives \(g(Y)=f(Y)\). Finally remove the \(j\)
factors of three by tests \(3^k w\), \(0\le k<j\), obtaining \(f(w)=f(h)\).

There are at most three fixed affine test families
\(8\,3^a w-1\), \(0\le a\le2\), and five families \(3^a w\),
\(0\le a\le4\), besides the dyadic stripping family. All their tested
odd arguments are at most \(81bR\) when \(h\in[R,bR]\).

For a failed stripping test \(m=8\,2^a w-1\), its value determines
\(a=v_2(m+1)-3\) and \(w=\operatorname{oddpart}(m+1)\). Once \(w\)
is fixed, at most \(L_b\) values \(h=2^v w\) lie in \([R,bR]\).
Every fixed affine family is injective in \(w\) and has the same
lift bound. Thus

\[
\#\{h\in[R,bR]:f(4h-1)\ne f(h)\}
\le9L_bD_f(81bR). \tag{RV2}
\]

## 2. Plus comparison

Put \(J(y)=f(y+1)\). The two ordinary steps from \(4u+1\) yield
\(J(4u)=J(3u)\). The paths

\[
48u+3\longmapsto72u+5\longmapsto108u+8
\longmapsto54u+4\longmapsto27u+2,
\qquad18u+1\longmapsto27u+2
\]

show that a successful tripling test at \(16u+1\) gives
\(J(16u)=J(18u)\), hence \(J(16u)=J(32u)\) using the two-step
exchange. This strips the twos from \(J(16h)\), with tests
\(16\,2^a w+1\), \(0\le a<v\).

After \(J(16w)=J(9w)\), the chain

\[
J(9u)=J(16u)=J(32u)=J(64u)=J(27u)
\]

uses the two tests \(16u+1\), \(32u+1\) and increments the exponent
of three. The subgroup generated by 3 modulo 128 has order 32 and is
exactly the units congruent to 1 or 3 modulo 8. Its two cosets contain
49 and 5, respectively. Therefore some \(j\in\{2,\ldots,33\}\)
has \(Y=3^j w\equiv49\pmod{128}\) or
\(Y\equiv5\pmod{128}\), the latter implying \(Y\equiv5\pmod{32}\).
The corresponding finite affine mergers give \(J(Y)=f(Y)\). Removing
the factors of three finishes \(J(16h)=f(h)\).

This uses one stripping family, at most 62 fixed affine families
\(16\,3^a w+1\), \(32\,3^a w+1\), \(0\le a\le30\), and 33
families \(3^a w\), \(0\le a\le32\). Their arguments are at most
\(33\,3^{30}bR\). Again a stripping failure uniquely determines its
odd \(w\), now from \(m-1\). The same multiplicity argument proves

\[
\#\{h\in[R,bR]:f(16h+1)\ne f(h)\}
\le96L_bD_f(33\,3^{30}bR). \tag{RV3}
\]

0,18,24,32,33,36,48,64,63,72,96,99,108,144,192,198,216,288.

difference \(s\). The resulting pair is given below; its order may

can fail. The other rows use (RV2) or (RV3), together with unconditional
doubling invariance.

## 4. Removing smooth multipliers costs bounded multiplicity

For a fixed \(s=2^a3^e\), comparing \(f(sm(n))\) with \(f(m(n))\)
costs the \(e\) tripling tests

\[
\operatorname{oddpart}(3^i m(n)),\qquad0\le i<e.
\]

In this application \(m(n)\) is an injective positive affine function
with nonnegative intercept. On \([R,bR]\), each \(3^i m(n)\) lies
in an interval with endpoint ratio at most \(b\). For any fixed failed
odd argument, its dyadic lifts in this interval number at most \(L_b\).
Injectivity then bounds the number of corresponding original \(n\)'s
by \(L_b\). The potentially large individual valuation \(v_2(m(n))\)
does not cause extra multiplicity.

There are 19 factors of three in total across the 17 differences \(s\),
or 38 tests counting both endpoints. The two terminal comparisons
\(f(288n)=f(n)\), \(f(288(n+1))=f(n+1)\) add four, for 42
fixed test families. The four plus rows contribute at most \(384L_b\)
times the common failure count; the eleven minus rows contribute
\(99L_b\); the two odd rows contribute two more. Therefore the total
coefficient is at most \(525L_b+2\le527L_b\).

The affine parameters in the table are at most \(88bR\), and all
remaining smooth-multiplier tests are at most \(576bR\). Equations
(RV2)--(RV3) consequently fit inside the conservative cutoff
\(3000\,3^{30}bR\). A union bound yields (RV1).

## Review conclusion

The claimed homogeneous variation bound is valid at the pinned source.
It requires neither prior convergence of any tested source nor an
assumption about binary run count. All tests concern ordinary positive
integers. Its explicit proof justifies use for an arbitrary binary
\(T\)-invariant label. The checked affine identities and multiplicity
argument are sufficient; an empirical test of many ordinary labels
would not substitute for this universal argument.
