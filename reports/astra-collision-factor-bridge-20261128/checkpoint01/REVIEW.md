## Load-bearing checks

1. The short-interval theorem supplies a lower count for all but a sublinear set of **integer** sources. Its semiprimes have their smaller factor in a polylogarithmic band and eventually belong to the fixed family in (S). No short-interval asymptotic is assumed.
2. Balanced fusion gives actual common shortcut values on every lift of a successful binary residue. A merely typical or finite representative merger would not suffice.
3. At modulus \(Q=2^t\asymp X^{1/4}\), the larger prime is long enough for Brun–Titchmarsh. The permitted smaller factors have bounded reciprocal mass. Thus every odd residue contains at most \(CX/(Q\log X)\) target semiprimes.
4. Summing this bound over failed residues gives an error of order \((X/\log X)\sqrt{\log\log X/\log X}\) per shift. This is relative to the semiprime scale. The invalid unweighted error comparison is explicitly avoided.
5. Window double counting charges each semiprime at most \(H\) times. All errors and constants are uniform in the selected component union.
6. For fixed-target and common-deadline marks, sources remain above the target before fusion. No past-visit information is lost. The deadline is at least the fusion date.
7. Positive lower natural density does not imply positive density in each octave. A fixed finite dyadic decomposition supplies the required initial-interval conclusion.
8. The ordinary/shortcut normalization is explicit: ordinary predecessors of \(2v\) give shortcut predecessors of \(v\). Targets divisible by three are not assigned a positive-density predecessor theorem.
9. A counterexample produces positive lower relative density of counterexamples in the specified semiprime family. This proves both universal sufficiency and the upper-relative-density-one equivalence. No claim is made for arbitrary density-one subsets of all semiprimes.
