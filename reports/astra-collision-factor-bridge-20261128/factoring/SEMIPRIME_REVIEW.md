## The precise missing strengthening

To turn the sampler into a dynamical proof, one would need a selected nearby semiprime pq together with an ordinary merger or terminating descent from its factor q (or another certified smaller source). The short-interval theorem supplies unmarked products, and the reversible factoring sampler recovers a curve label from product digits. Neither supplies this merger. Even an accurate marked product/factor frequency theorem would state a correlation law rather than force the forbidden mismatch to vanish.

For a prime multiplier p>3, the generic two-adic event that q and pq ever meet is Haar-null: each pair of finite words determines at most one rational q. Thus the needed ordinary product/factor theorem cannot be obtained by merely extending the almost-sure Haar pair-coalescence mechanism to arbitrary multipliers. The written finite-cell theorem and three checked Lean roots in the factoring lane make this boundary explicit.
