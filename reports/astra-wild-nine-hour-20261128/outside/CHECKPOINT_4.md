# Native affine rectangles and the rational-source alternative

For independently varying odd and total shortcut counts, the [source rectangle theorem](../../../research/astra-wild-nine-hour-20261128/outside/NATIVE_AFFINE_RECTANGLES.md) gives an ineffective \(e^{o(t)}\) absolute discrepancy for the permutation \(n\mapsto3^s n\bmod2^t\) on noncollapsing slopes. All slopes are covered after adding the explicit first-rotation term \(O(|J|2^t/3^s)\) when \(3^s/2^t<1/2\). The argument extends the classical rational-power approximation estimate recalled by Corvaja and Zannier, with the same two-place arithmetic input as PR215's cofactor bound.

The native affine intercept of a **true** word at its own source has fractional part \(-n3^{s(n)}/2^t\bmod1\). This identity reduces its small-arc upper bound on the positive source octave to a sum over at most \(t+1\) grades, at any fixed multiple beyond the binary horizon. The shrinking-slope errors sum geometrically. On simple paths, near any prescribed per-grade phase to tolerance \(X^{-\delta}\), at most \(X^{1-\delta+o(1)}+X^{o(1)}\) inputs qualify. At one supplied endpoint only a bounded number of grades is required.

The [rational-source sieve](../../../research/astra-wild-nine-hour-20261128/outside/RATIONAL_SOURCE_SIEVE.md) works on arbitrarily sparse dates. If a script with a noncollapsing slope has a rational binary source, every subexponential-height rational probe with exponentially accurate subexponential-denominator output eventually equals that same source. A wrong fixed probe with infinitely many accurate dates excludes *all* rational sources. A three-variable Subspace Theorem argument permits fixed algebraic fractional phases. Every genuine positive escaping source therefore has a subexponential lower bound on the affine-intercept distance to each fixed algebraic phase modulo one.

A single script can also give every positive ordinary probe its own positive-lower-density set of exponentially accurate dates, with simultaneous positive real escape. Distinct fixed probes' good schedules intersect only finitely often. The binary source is irrational. The data still fail the regular-time test for any one supplied probe.

The [finite audit](../../../research/astra-wild-nine-hour-20261128/outside/check_affine_rectangles.py) passed normally and under Python optimization. It checked 952 full rectangles through 7,129 convergent pieces, 561 nonempty first remainders, and 90,000 true ordinary prefixes with 4,450 selected moving-arc hits. Its grid checks use rational coefficients and explicit finite constants. The asymptotic Diophantine bounds are written applications of the Subspace Theorem; no numerical estimate of its ineffective onset or new Lean root is asserted.

Global closure still requires a reason that a native candidate concentrates in the forbidden small arcs or repeated probe comparisons. A fixed-root canonical inverse measure is not an ensemble of independently chosen word translates, and a compact-null correction set multiplied by a moving height need not remain restricted to a small family of arcs.
