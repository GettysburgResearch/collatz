OVERALL JUDGMENT

The latest work improves the precision of the proof targets and establishes useful local obstructions. It has not shown that the remaining global step is small. The strongest progress is the completed reduction from a very narrow one-sided basin law, and the exact separation of an analytic trace into its difficult arithmetic head and an exponentially decaying tail.

I would prioritize:

1. Adaptive transformation of complete finite certificates.
2. Arithmetic estimates for actual weighted representatives, with cycle exclusion pursued separately; retain the positive occupation estimate as an alternative that covers both failure types.
3. Mixed functional-equation rigidity as a high-variance independent approach.
4. Prime-support and binary-complexity methods only when they produce an actual bound on accumulated resource creation along one fixed input.

The fourth route has interesting theorems, but combining necessary complexity conditions is insufficient. PR157 constructs arbitrarily long actual growing blocks satisfying several of them simultaneously. Finite prime support and bounded binary-run count are effectively alternate ways to ask for eventual periodicity once the new source theorems are applied.

WHAT THE LATEST WORK CHANGES

Directed closure, PR146 and PR157

Let C be the positive integers whose shortcut orbit reaches 1. PR146 reduces full convergence to

    128k+1 in C => 64k+1 in C  (k>0, 4 does not divide k).

It also retains the earlier forward implication

    16k+1 in C => 32k+1 in C  (k>=0).

PR157 gives the alternative

    x in C, x odd, 3 does not divide x => 5x in C.

Its conditional induction has a complete cover of 464 infinite affine cells, with a smaller endpoint at most 19/20 of the input. This is a completed induction conditional on the multiplier law. The multiplier law is exactly the missing theorem.

This is an attractive interface because it starts with an actual finite certificate for a known source. But the output certificate can be much longer. In PR146 even the first nonempty layer of the reverse criterion reduces to all powers 3^(27j-1). The forward criterion at low depth contains Mersenne families. Bounded depth of the input certificate is therefore not evidence that the transformation is easy.

Expanding returns, PR158

Two exact expanding return families survive the specified physical guards. Their two individual valuation resources can return to the same pair of values under switching. This defeats numerical descent at every surviving return and any rank depending only on that pair.

The restricted two-macro rounding-map question is interesting, but its solution would not close the principal argument: the designated known arm already converges, so permanent avoidance of useful sections remains a broader gap.

Prime support and circulation, PR159

For an actual odd-trajectory prefix whose entire prime support has size at most s,

    max n_j <= max{4^s*n_0, (s+1)^(64s)}.

For a cycle with at most s primes, max n_j < (s+1)^(64s).

This is a meaningful bound on the prime-support subclass. It does not give an upper bound on how many new primes an unrestricted orbit can acquire.

The circulation theorem constructs one positive weighting under which inward and outward tripling defects have equal total flux whenever either relevant moment is finite. Equality of two fluxes does not make them zero. A two-vertex directed cycle already illustrates that distinction.

Analytic head, PR158

The all-source moment D_q splits as

    D_q = P_q + R_q,

with

    ((zeta(2)-1)/4)*(3/5)^q <= R_q
       <= (zeta(2)/4)*(3/5)^q.

The remaining object P_q is exactly the congruence-word sum in Problem 02. Thus the tail is controlled; the unresolved part is arithmetic concentration at small endpoints.

The manuscript's target O(q^2) is stronger than necessary for excluding nonperiodic orbits. This review derives the relaxation O(q^(21/10)), or even o(q^(40/19)), using PR153's path-occupancy theorem. The proof is below.

The existing uniform spectral-gap strategy is excluded for the specific operators considered in PR156: approximate unit-circle spectrum already occurs on known convergent inputs. This does not exclude particular-vector growth estimates such as Problem 02.

Final refresh, PR160

The ordered prime-height proof improves the quantitative restriction for arbitrary histories. If x_i=log_2(n_i), xbar is their mean, and Y_p is the accumulated logarithmic contribution of prime p, it proves

    Y_p <= N[2+5 ln(1+xbar)] + E(x_0),
    E(x)=2(x-1)_+ + x ln(max{1,x}).

For x=0 the logarithmic term is defined as zero. Therefore on any hypothetical escaping fixed-source orbit, every prime pool of size o(xbar/ln(1+xbar)) carries a vanishing fraction of total accumulated height. The pool may be chosen after the whole prefix, but is one common pool for that prefix. The missing opposing upper bound on prime creation is unchanged. PR159 retains sharper explicit peak bounds.

Its critical-mass synthesis is explicitly conditional on a separately pinned positive-density input, along with the cited ordinary synchronization and endpoint results. A hypothetical proper invariant component would have Dirichlet mass of order 1/epsilon at exponent 1+epsilon, while its boundary is O(epsilon^(-1/2)) and still diverges at exponent one. Normalizing by the component mass makes both the boundary and every fixed ordinary atom vanish. This explains a failure of the proposed limiting argument; it supplies neither a proper component nor its exclusion. These additions do not change the six problem statements or their implications.

WHY PROBLEM 01 IS EXACT

The representation set E is exactly the set of odd positive integers that reach 1.

For an odd trajectory n_0,...,n_r=1, write a_i>=1 for its successive division exponents and A_j for their partial sums. Telescoping yields

    3^r n_0 + sum_{j=0}^{r-1}3^{r-1-j}2^{A_j}=2^{A_r}.

Conversely, an integer satisfying this identity produces rational suffix values through

    3 n_{i-1}+1 = 2^(A_i-A_{i-1}) n_i.

Each suffix's denominator divides a power of 3. If n_{i-1} is integral, coprimality with the power of 2 forces n_i integral. Positivity propagates from n_{i-1}; the explicit suffix formula makes it odd, and the final suffix is 1. The exponents are therefore exact.

The residue condition x mod 512 in {129,257,385} is precisely

    x=128k+1, k>0, 4 does not divide k.

The requested output (x+1)/2 is 64k+1. This is PR146's reverse criterion verbatim in exponential-certificate form.

Do not assign its multiplier-five variant to another agent expecting a mathematically independent subproblem. The closures are alternative full-equivalent targets. Different proof techniques could still be worth pursuing.

WHY THE 2.1 MOMENT TARGET SUFFICES

This is a deduction made for this review, not a claim of a new proof of the open estimate.

PR153, drift.md, FID-DRIFT-005, proves that for some finite constant C and every finite simple shortcut trajectory, the number of its states in any integer interval of width X is at most C X^(19/20). The constant is uniform in the trajectory and interval location.

Suppose n_j is a hypothetical nonperiodic odd trajectory. Its expansion into shortcut steps is simple: a repeated shortcut state would force eventual periodicity and repeated odd states.

Dyadic summation of the occupancy bound gives a uniform reciprocal budget:

    sum_j 1/n_j
        <= C * sum_{k>=0} 2^(-k/20)
        < infinity.

Let J_q=3^q/2^(a_1+...+a_q). Exact multiplication of the odd recurrence yields

    n_q = n_0 J_q product_{j<q}(1+1/(3n_j)).

The reciprocal budget bounds the product by a finite constant M independent of q. The particular source n_0 contributes a term to D_q, so

    D_q >= J_q^4/n_q^2 >= n_q^2/(M^4 n_0^4).

If Problem 02 has the stated O(q^(21/10)) bound, the exact tail relation gives

    n_q <= C_(n_0) (q+1)^(21/20).

The first N odd values are distinct and all lie below C'_(n_0) N^(21/20). They occur in one finite simple shortcut prefix. Occupancy then forces

    N <= C''_(n_0) N^((21/20)*(19/20))
      = C''_(n_0) N^(399/400),

which is impossible for arbitrarily large N.

The source n_0 remains fixed throughout. The shortcut prefix can be longer than N; counting its N selected odd values is sufficient.

More generally, P_q=o(q^(40/19)) also suffices. The maximum of the first N height bounds is o(N^(20/19)); occupancy would give N=o(N). At the boundary, P_q=O(q^(40/19)/(log q)^epsilon), epsilon>0, is a useful stronger concrete target.

The reciprocal-sum bound fails on a nontrivial cycle; its distortion product grows while J_q decays geometrically. Thus this argument does not exclude cycles. That is why Problem 03 is separate.

WHY PROBLEM 03 IS EXACT

Set n_0=C/D when D>0 divides C. Since C and D are odd, n_0 is a positive odd integer. Iterating the prescribed affine steps gives a cycle with return equation D n_0=C. The congruence for the full word enforces each intermediate division; alternatively rotate the word, giving each cycle state as an odd numerator over the same odd denominator. Each state is positive and odd, so the prescribed exponents are exact.

Every positive odd cycle supplies such a word. The trivial odd cycle at 1 has every exponent equal to 2, and every all-two word has C=D. Therefore Problem 03 is exactly arbitrary positive-cycle exclusion.

The important arithmetic is complete divisibility. Showing that the unreduced denominator has many or large prime factors does not bound the reduced denominator D/gcd(C,D).

WHY PROBLEM 04 IS EXACT

Compare coefficients in the projected functional equation. It gives

    a_(2m)=a_m  (m>=1),
    a_(3k+2)=a_(2k+1)  (k>=0).

These are exactly invariance under the shortcut map. If every positive integer reaches 1, every coefficient equals a_1=0. If any source does not reach 1, the indicator of the complement of that basin is a nonzero Boolean solution.

Rationality of a finite-alphabet invariant implies eventual periodicity of coefficients, and the source gives a complete argument forcing their global constancy. The remaining rationality or boundary-continuation assertion is fully open.

Bell-Lagarias already connects basin rationality with the original conjecture. This is not a new analytic resolution. Adamczewski-Bell rationality for two independent Mahler bases requires two actual equations; the single projected mixed equation does not supply them automatically.
