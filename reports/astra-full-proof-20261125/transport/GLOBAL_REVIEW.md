## Scalar Mahler test — VERIFIED in written, source-qualified scope

Adamczewski, Bell and Smertnig use a Mahler system with rational-function invertible matrix over a number field, a chosen place, and regularity along the complete forward power orbit. Their Theorem 4.3 lifts each homogeneous numerical relation at a regular point to a formal relation specializing to the prescribed polynomial. Their statement applies at the dyadic place. Section 4 also records local analyticity at zero and meromorphic continuation on the open unit disk. [Primary paper, Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.3](https://adamczewski.perso.math.cnrs.fr/HeightMahler.pdf)

I reconstructed the singular specialization as follows. The scalar companion matrix is invertible because both end coefficients are nonzero. Adjoining the constant makes a rational affine relation homogeneous. For a common later point β in both convergence disks, choose the number of power iterates past all nonzero poles of the matrix and its inverse. This is possible even if zero is singular. The order-j derivative of the outer substitution contributes the diagonal multiplier $(kz^{k-1})^j$. All other derivative terms have lower jet order. Thus the jet matrix is triangular in jet order with invertible diagonal blocks. Differentiation can raise pole orders at existing locations. Away from zero the inverse jet matrix is regular wherever both original matrices are regular.

After clearing a pole of order h in the row of the iterated identity, the numerator at the original point is $(z-\alpha)^h(F(z)-a)$. Its dyadic vanishing has order at least h+1. The coefficient of order h is essential to identify the removable value. Each of these finitely many coefficients is a rational linear form evaluated on the jets at β. Apply the lifting theorem separately to the nonzero linear forms. Embedding the finitely many lifted algebraic coefficients in the complex numbers preserves the formal relations and specializes each one to its original rational form at the rational β. Evaluating where the complex series and their derivatives actually converge transfers the Taylor-coefficient zeros. The quotient then has removable value a. This argument uses a formal identity and finite jets to transfer information between the two places.

For the Collatz application, multiplying the one-step factors gives $3x\prod(1+e_j/(3x_j))$, while ordinary telescoping gives $3\cdot 2^n x_n/3^{Q_n}$. The latter vanishes dyadically in the limit. On an injective positive orbit, the reciprocals through n steps sum to at most the n-th harmonic number. Hence the nonnegative partial sums at 2/3 are bounded by a constant times $(n+1)^{1/3}$. In particular each summand obeys that bound, proving real convergence at every smaller positive argument. The removable real value would have to be the limit from the left of nonnegative values and would have to equal $-3x<0$. Boundary convergence of the original real series is unnecessary.

## Rational-state test — VERIFIED in written scope

On the actual orbit subspace each state lies in the regular domain of the update, the observation and the composite observation. The domains may be restricted to their intersection before taking the subspace. The two branch outputs never coincide at a positive embedded integer in characteristic zero. Recovering the parity output algebraically gives a continuous map into two distinct rational points, with clopen fibers. Each iterated parity coordinate is constant on every irreducible component. The subspace of a Noetherian variety is Noetherian and has a finite irreducible-component cover, including when the orbit is not closed.

To check that the infinite parity code separates ordinary heights, take two initial inputs with h common bits. Along their common word, $2^h(T^hx-T^hy)=3^p(x-y)$. Since three is odd, $2^h\mid x-y$. Agreement for all h implies equality in the ordinary integers. The finite cover therefore bounds the number of observed heights. Component intersections are harmless. The stated interpolation converse, the finite-order rational recurrence corollary and the deterministic right-extension argument for recurrent words follow with the indicated eventual-tail qualifications.

Both tests concern boundedness for one fixed orbit. They still need an independently justified representation or functional equation for every desired source and leave nontrivial finite cycles possible.

**Degree growth at a fixed lag without a clock — VERIFIED.** After discarding the last nonrecurrent edge there are at most $v=2^d$ recurrent vertices. Thus at least $\lfloor(N-O_{x,d}(1))/v\rfloor$ observations occur at branching vertices, and one branching word accounts for at least $N/v^2-O_{x,d}(1)$ observations.
