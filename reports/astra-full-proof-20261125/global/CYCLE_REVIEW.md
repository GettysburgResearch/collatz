**Terminal injection.** With fixed reduced denominator \(d\), the terminal numerator on a prescribed word has \(2^{S_k}y_k=3^ky_0+dC\). Subtraction for two occurrences shows \(3^k\mid y_k-y'_k\). The low terminal interval has span \((K-1)ds<Kds\le3^k\), including when the last inequality is an equality. Distinct phases of a primitive cycle have distinct values. The starting bin, mask, marked entering gaps and unmarked cumulative clocks determine every gap inductively from zero. This is an injection of retained phases into codes. The model is allowed to put probability on nonphysical codes.

**Averaging with excursion costs.** Among all \(p\) cyclic starts there are at most \(2Qp\) exclusions by either endpoint. Every high phase enters exactly \(k\) times when all windows are counted with multiplicity. On retained windows the average number of high endpoints is therefore at most \(kQ/(1-2Q)\). For a run of marked endpoints starting immediately after a low height, multiplication from that preceding low height through the *last high* gives a telescoping ratio below one. Its sum of entering valuations costs at most \(r\ln(3+1/s)\). The potentially large valuation returning to low is decoded at an unmarked cumulative clock and is not double-charged. The probability \(2^{-a}\) sums to one on positive valuations. The cross-entropy of the mask increases with the observed mark count for \(t<1/2\). The sign of the inequality thus gives the stated upper bound. The entropy lower bound follows from the injectivity and Jensen's inequality on the probabilities assigned to the uniformly selected physical codes.

**Rotation and paired choices.** The allowed cumulative interval has length \(\log_2 K+2\eta\) between one and two. Its lower endpoint advances by \(\log_2 3\), so double-choice sites form a circular arc up to immaterial endpoints. Comparing each block of length \(q\) with the translated grid at a reduced convergent gives uniform discrepancy \(O(1/q)+O(q/k)\). For \(K=3\), translating an inscribed arc to \([0,\delta]\) preserves the advance \(\delta=\log_2(3/2)\). A first site in \((1-\delta,\delta)\) moves into \((0,2\delta-1)\). The two open intervals are disjoint by \(3\delta<2\). Each selected edge therefore uses two positions and no two edges share a position. A marked endpoint spoils at most one such pair. The minimal available clock increments by one or two. Exactly one of the four choices gives respectively zero or three, leaving three valid pairs for the valuation constraint. Their product probabilities normalize conditionally on the mask. This yields the savings \((2\delta-1-t+o(1))k\ln(4/3)\) in the correct direction.

**Logarithm assumptions and denominator exponents.** Distinct low integer numerators give \(ds\gg p\). Under \(d\le p^\gamma\), \(\gamma<1\), this gives \(k/s\to0\), as required in the clock binning. For large \(p\), \(s\ge1\) yields \(p\le h\le 2p\), so the maximum absolute coefficient of the homogeneous logarithm form is comparable to \(p\). The full-lap estimate yields \(s\ll_\varepsilon p^{\kappa+1+\varepsilon}\). The upper budget for \(\ln(ds)\) then has exponent \(\kappa+1+\gamma+\varepsilon\), with the numerator denominator size included once. The strict entropy margins leave room for a positive \(\varepsilon\). I checked the primary published Wu–Wang statement with exponent 4.1163051 and the independent-rationals hypothesis of Chudnovsky Corollary 1.2 at \(2,3\). These inputs give no explicit onset.
