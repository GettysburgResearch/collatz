# Independent review of the finite LP and residue obstruction

**Mathematical verdict.** Verified at the finite rational LP and ordinary residue-counterexample levels. The reduction does not supply an LP whose feasible solution covers the future of all ordinary sources. The reviewed Lean source PotentialBridge.lean has SHA-256 `33477421b8c720939179927c3c6016a7ca51feaf6af3164d013b6885c2acada2`. Its vendored Farkas input is from openai/math commit `3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6`.

1. For row e, Aₑ is +1 on the target and −1 on the source, adding the contributions when they coincide. Thus Ap≤−c is exactly p(target)−p(source)≤−c. If y≥0 and Aᵀy=0, left multiplication gives 0≤−c·y, contradictory for c·y>0. Applying the sparse alternative to right side −c gives (−c)·y=−1, equivalent to c·y=1. Its support bound is rank(A)+1. The alternative is rational; integer controls carry unnormalized multipliers with strictly positive gain, which may be divided by their gain for that alternative.
2. For every positive m, take n=4m−1, z=6m−1. Positivity, oddness of both values, 3n+1=2z, and their shared residue −1 mod m give a true ordinary gap-one edge. One row therefore excludes every residue-only correction with a strictly negative bound on all gap-one edges. It also excludes the 8−5g cost ansatz, whose cost is 3 there. The m=1 proof includes n=3 and z=5; the requirement of ternary-unit sources requires an additional choice, supplied in the harness by 3|m. At arbitrary fixed H the nᵢ in the note give real positive, increasing, common-residue gap-one paths. Different H can and do use different starting integers.
3. Telescoping the exact path inequalities yields 5Sₖ≥8k+p(last)−p(first). A fixed finite vertex set bounds the potential difference. Since 2⁸>3⁵, the exponential margin eventually exceeds the polynomial factor in the PR 206 clock test. This implication presumes coverage of consecutive genuine transitions on the specified orbit by the *same* potential. Disjoint sample paths, incompatible residue lifts, and separate per-length potentials would not establish it. An infeasibility certificate for a true subsystem refutes a potential asserted to work universally on that subsystem even when its edges arise from different positive integers; it does not declare those integers periodic.
4. The solver uses difference constraints and integer Bellman–Ford. I compared it independently against explicit enumeration of all simple directed cycles on three vertices for all 19,683 graphs obtained by assigning each ordered pair an absent edge, cost −1, or cost +1. Of these, 3,392 had no positive-cost cycle and obtained a potential. The remaining 16,291 had a positive-cost cycle and obtained a balanced positive circulation. All the exported certificates passed their stated exact row tests. The built-in bank produced identical output with ordinary and optimized Python. The helper is a specialization distinct from the upstream general RAM algorithm. The full strongly polynomial program was not rebuilt by this review.

Independent builds compiled the vendored Farkas.Sparse, Farkas.Basic and PotentialBridge modules. Axiom queries for the sparse alternative, circulation exclusion and strict residue no-go returned only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. For the stronger unit-restricted version at any supplied m, choosing the scale 3mL in the displayed witness preserves residue −1 modulo m, ensures both arms are ternary units, and moves them above any fixed floor by varying L.

**Verifier boundary fixes and final replay.** The initial harness missed vacuous graphs, allowed true source annotations on an incorrect identity-projected edge, and accepted some float or Boolean certificate fields. The rebuilt final harness at SHA-256 `f4e3b85848a1e09f9ef488cae21e2bc7def13f0033194ef7df54cb32c063f1c0` returns an empty potential on the empty graph, rejects a fake 99→77 edge annotated by the genuine ordinary 3→5, and rejects floating edge costs, Boolean potentials and Boolean certificate objectives. Independently replayed those boundary cases in both Python modes, the 19,683-graph exhaustive classification after the fixes, and the full bank in both modes with byte-identical output. The corrected PotentialBridge.lean, SHA-256 `a7b9779290eb81954164f3a7105725471357bc86d2383c51f6f5e0c20ecdf2bd`, was independently recompiled.
