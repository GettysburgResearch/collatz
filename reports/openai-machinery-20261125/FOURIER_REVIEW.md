* **Endpoint rank.** A row retains its input index; its column label is an attained natural-number endpoint. Selecting a representative row for each attained column exhibits the identity minor over every field; dimensions bound the rank in the other direction. Moving from separately tagged to aligned output columns decreases rank by precisely the cardinality of the intersection of the two attained endpoint sets. Each common endpoint contributes one regardless of how many input pairs meet there. With the same integer step function on both arms the witness has literal equal iterates at its own two clocks. Positivity and the chosen Collatz step are supplied at specialization; the generic theorem accepts every natural-valued function, including zero-valued inputs. It does not force a meeting or bound pair multiplicity.
* **Exposed paths.** For a fixed common path length and a contiguous cut, a time-j state specifies the entire suffix. Fibers contributing to each prefix vector have disjoint support because the source is retained, and suffix labels begin at distinct time-j states. Dropping source coordinates, aligning residues in place of integer endpoints, or substituting an independent clock-dependent representative would require a different argument. With variable clocks the proved two-matrix meeting criterion remains valid because each source-clock pair is retained.
* **Positive sources.** In the h=3 sibling family one has 3(64n+21)+1=64(3n+1). With n odd the first shortcut step equals 64T(n), and six literal halvings give T(n). In the first tower row, 243(128t+23)=64·3(162t+29)+21, and both bases are positive odd with 128t+23<162t+29, for all t≥0. At t=2s+1, 3w+1=2(486s+287) with odd quotient, proving the low-clock alternative for r=1. The implication of the rank-two exclusion then has false antecedent. No certificate uses the valuation at the wrong affine input: the frontier tests 3ʳw+1, the sibling relation uses 3(3ʳw)+1.
* **Higher rows.** For n=3ʳw, h=3ʳ and B=(4ʰ−1)/3^(r+1), elementary 3-adic divisibility makes B an integer unit. There is one unit class solving 4ʰw+B≡0 mod 3ᵐ. Taking 3ᵐ>4ʰ+B gives 0<(4ʰw+B)/3ᵐ<w for each positive w. This smaller base is odd when w is odd. Simultaneously enforce 3ʳw≡1 mod 8 by CRT to retain the two prior exclusions. The destination is explicitly 3^(r+m)b and so belongs to the comparison set permitted by PR 192 even when b has additional powers of 3. This is an infinite progression per tower row; a supplied counterexample base would have to enter one of these progressions, an independent unmet premise. The written all-r argument has only its r=1 specialization in TowerMerger.lean.
* **Fixed-budget inverse counts.** For an odd 3-unit y, the conditions 2ᵉy≡4 or 7 mod 9 give two positive exponent choices per consecutive block of six. Every chosen inverse is positive, odd, and a 3-unit. At fixed depth two exponent words sharing a source follow the same deterministic forward path, hence coincide. This validates the capped count (2m)^k even at a periodic endpoint. Across depths that injectivity can fail. For an exact-total word instead, the terminal residue is a 3-unit since only the final power-of-two term of its affine offset survives modulo 3. The average histogram therefore divides by 2·3^(k−1), while peak fibers may greatly exceed it or fall well below it. In particular, the inverse-row multiplicity theorem in [the Fourier note](../../research/openai-machinery-20261125/fourier/README.md) concerns sources merging at one endpoint. It does not imply that two separately prescribed batches have a common endpoint.

The two constructions orient the merger differently. TowerRows takes b=6t+3, h=3ʳ and w=4ʰb+B, where 4ʰ=1+3^(r+1)B and B is an odd ternary unit. For this orientation the first accelerated successor of 3ʳw is y=(3·3ʳw+1)/4>w while the second base b remains strictly smaller. Confusing these orientations changes which trajectory supplies the two extra powers of two.

For PositiveTower's lift, once K w+B=3ᵐb, replace w by w+8·3ᵐj, with j≡b mod 3. Since K=4ʰ≡1 mod 3, the new quotient b+8Kj is divisible by 3. Each lift preserves w mod 8 and does not decrease w. Thus n=hw has n+1 twice an odd integer, 3n+1 exactly four times an odd integer, and n cannot be 85 mod 512. The sibling identity instantiates the two actual shortcut clocks 1 and 2h+1. For any supplied lower bound the theorem constructs a suitable w; it does not supply a certificate for an externally prescribed w.
