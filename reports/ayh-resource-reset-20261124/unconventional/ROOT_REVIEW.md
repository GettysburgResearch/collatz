## Ordinary affine merger and periodic walls

**VERIFIED at the formal statements.** `WallAffine` constructs a signed
integer merger class for every power-of-three slope ratio and every
integer offset. Equal slopes are reduced by the absolute offset; higher
levels reduce to lower levels after stripping binary factors. The
prescribed-class version first advances both arms through the supplied

affine continuations. It selects a subclass, not every input in the class.

`WallOrbit` proves the exact open-class coverage of an odd coefficient
times powers of a multiplier congruent to one modulo four. The proof
checks the binary valuation gain under squaring and the odd-denominator
cancellation; no probabilistic equidistribution premise is used.

`WallGeneral` uses two actual signed cycle witnesses. It balances their
odd counts by multiplying each lap schedule by the other cycle's grade.
The resulting endpoints are ordinary nonnegative integers, even though
the reference numerators are signed. The physical meeting clocks and the
two numbers of word blocks may differ. `WallUniform` takes maxima over
finitely many binary classes and finitely many valuations after a
mismatch. `WallWords` separately proves the all-alignment eventual cap.
The source-dependent onset and the word-dependent cap are distinct
quantifiers.

The final `WallLinear` proof pulls a high-valuation address back to a
bounded remainder phase, applies the paired exponential-row bound to a
shifted odd coefficient, and returns to the original source by the address
composition identity. It bounds block depth linearly in the valuation.
`WallDelay` separately lifts all physical clocks through the available
binary precision. The two signed period-18 trajectories have disjoint
residue sets modulo 65536, including their preperiods. Their exact finite
cycles establish separation for all clocks. Thus arbitrarily long
physical delay can coexist with a word-only block cap. The coordinator
read both proofs and checked this distinction in their actual interfaces.

The equal-density improvement in `WallDensity` absorbs the unbounded
valuation into the common odd coefficient, leaving finitely many phases.
It bounds the number of blocks from the first position. The physical
clocks still depend on that valuation. `WallShifted` permits different
within-word phases on the two arms and recovers their colors from a
finite repeated-block endpoint. `WallTernary` and `WallDichotomy` exclude
the purely ternary zero-reference cases except fractions one third and
two thirds. `WallEscape` concludes escape only for an explicit generalized
`3x+d` numerator orbit; it does not assert escape or convergence for the
original ordinary `3x+1` source.

The period-1011 certificate was independently parsed rather than trusted

product with the squared inverse slope. Removing every prepeak phase
costs at most `2^s` gate labels at gate size `2^s`. The five lost ternary
digits cost 243; summing the gates gives `486/F^2`.

The physical-to-slope comparison uses the uniform reciprocal bound on a
simple segment and stops at the first physical crossing. Deleting loops
does not add odd surplus. Ordinary interval rounding is explicitly
bounded; Haar measure alone is insufficient. At the first normalized

odd surplus. Summing gate doublings geometrically and applying the central
binomial estimate gives `O(3^(theta*D)/sqrt(D+1))`, with
`theta=H₂(log₃2)`, and justifies the sharper displayed horizon.

The lower bound uses a fixed legal five-gap preparation, the exact
numerator uniqueness, and a fractional moment of the inverse offset.
It is a lower bound, not an estimate of the true density. All-clock
coverage in the counting theorem remains restricted by its stated surplus
horizon.
