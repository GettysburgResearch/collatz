# Independent review of the sifted original-source bound

Reviewed `SIFTED_DISCORDANT_SOURCES.md` at SHA-256 `973436f60430a547d6d4f477f5a909ac0da63b95e7cc04e632ddd90e2449df96`. The finite CRT/large-sieve argument, ordinary cylinder weights and summability conclusions pass this written audit.

For each fixed four-trit suffix, genuine color disagreement forces failure of the first $h$ counted steps on its actual common binary cylinder. There are $O(2^h/\sqrt d)$ such residues. Every surviving residue still has a full consecutive quotient interval of length $Q\asymp_c R/2^h$ (the fixed 81 factor is harmless). The two actual time-$t$ values have slopes $2^{h-t}3^{a_i+p_i(t)}$, and the initial zero-quotient intercepts bound any nonzero determinant by $\exp(Cd)$. If its determinant vanishes on a live cylinder, the only possible proportionality between the two distinct forms is a nontrivial integral power of three. The larger positive value is then divisible by three, excluding simultaneous roughness for large rows.

For nonzero determinant, each retained prime $p>d^2$ simultaneously excludes the requisite distinct $k=1$ or $2$ quotient residues. The reciprocal sum of excluded large determinant primes is $O(1/d)$, so Mertens gives product size $\gg(\log y/\log(2d))^k$. The normalized squarefree-product weight includes each prime with probability $k/p$. Taking $\eta$ small makes its expected log modulus at most half of $\log P$ for $P=\lfloor Q^{1/3}\rfloor$, so retaining $l\le P$ preserves at least half that weight. For each such $l$, the CRT product of local mean-zero tests has uniform squared norm $g(l)$ and primitive Fourier support. Its constant value $g(l)$ on the sifted set and Cauchy--Schwarz give the stated lower energy bound. Reduced fractions through denominator $P$ are $P^{-2}$-separated on the circle, including zero, so the cited additive large sieve gives the claimed uniform sifted fraction on each ordinary quotient interval.

Summing over the original survivor cylinders gives the additional $d^{-1/2}$ factor. A one-arm predicate may choose its clock and arm after seeing the full original residue $u$, because the quotient interval remains available at that point. The two-arm assertion takes an ordinary union over the $O(d)$ same clocks. Thus its exponent is $1/2-2\beta$, summable for $\beta>3/4$; for a single prescribed observation the exponent is $-1/2-\beta$, summable for $\beta>1/2$. The minimum physical values through $h$ exceed $y$ if $\eta<(1-c)\log 2$ and the row is large, included in the manuscript's sufficiently small choice. Hence simultaneous primes give an actual special case. This bounds a genuine arithmetic subpopulation of discordance without presuming that every discordant orbit has those prime or rough visits.
