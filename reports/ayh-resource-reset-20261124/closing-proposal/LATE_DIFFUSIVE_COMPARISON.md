# Comparison with PR207 at b56c4dc

**Finding.** PR207 gives a different conditional equivalence for ordinary positive Collatz and a quantitative restriction on where its counterexample row mass can lie. Its stated upper and lower bounds have compatible clock ranges. They supply neither a smaller reverse defect at a prescribed positive input nor a vanishing upper bound on the surviving aggregate. The existing fixed-gate clock inequality and the open reverse implication on \(17\bmod48\) therefore persist. This comparison reconstructs the two cited manuscripts at one commit. It does not independently prove their imported results.

## What the row computation counts

Write \(U\) for the odd-to-odd map, \(b\) for the odd-step count and \(G_b=(4/3)^b\). Each row has the exact physical shortcut total \(2b\). Its source \(x\) and terminal \(q\) are positive odd units. The normalized source is \(x/(qG_b)=1/D\), where \(D=\prod(1+1/(3x_j))\) along the recorded path. The escape statistic \(E_b(\epsilon)\) sums counts of simple rows with \(1-1/D\ge\epsilon\), divided by \(qG_b\), across \(\lceil\sqrt b\rceil\le q\le2^{\lceil\sqrt b\rceil}\). The cycle statistic \(C_b\) takes the maximum of \(\sqrt b\) times the corresponding normalized count at \(1<q\le\lceil\sqrt b\rceil\), with threshold \(1-2^{-100}\) and with repetitions allowed. Both statistics are finite arithmetic tests. The claimed escape criterion needs \(\liminf E_b(\epsilon)=0\) for every rational \(0<\epsilon<1\). The cycle criterion needs \(\liminf C_b=0\).

The lower bounds use the source-qualified renewal floor F. At a supplied unit \(a\), it provides at least \(c\,2^t/(3^{n+1}\sqrt n)\) legal inverse prefixes of \(n\) gaps in a prescribed ternary unit class when \(|t-2n|\le\sqrt n\), uniformly in the specified parameters. From a hypothetical escaping \(a\), PR207 appends the **actual** suffix ending at \(q_i=U^i(a)\) for \(i\) from roughly \(\sqrt b/4\) to \(\sqrt b/2\). Taking \(n=b-i\), the remaining physical budget \(t=2b-A_i\) lies in F's central window. The simple-path reciprocal estimate S bounds distortion and rules out too many small terminals. This gives order \(\sqrt b\) distinct terminals at a **single** \(b\), each with normalized count at least \(c_a/\sqrt b\). Appending a fixed number of laps at a hypothetical nontrivial cycle gives a positive lower bound for \(C_b\) at every large \(b\). These lower bounds retain the original suffix and exact physical clock. They do not randomize a gate's continuation.

On the upper side, S forces a simple high-carry row through one of finitely many small odd gates. The stated finite-grid first-hit grade estimate P bounds its per-terminal normalized count by \(O(1/\sqrt b)\). This controls the total weight for selected suffixes of length \(j\le\eta\sqrt b\) by \(O_\epsilon(\eta+b^{-1/2})\). An original-octave first passage bound to a **growing polylogarithmic threshold** controls long suffixes. Outside its exceptions, \(j+\mu\log q\ll_{A,\epsilon}\sqrt{b\log b}\), where \(\mu=2/\log(4/3)\). There are only \(O_\epsilon(b)\) possible terminals from the finite gates, so the long-suffix normalized weight is \(O_{A,\epsilon}(b^{-A})\) after summation. The escape construction appends a suffix of length \(i\) of order \(\sqrt b\). Its fixed gate need not be the first small gate used to define \(j\). The aggregate upper bounds leave room for its mass at intermediate \(j\). The required central-band aggregate upper remains open. These actual balanced single-arm rows also do not measure joint basin orientation or missing sites of the local color graphs in PR197.

## Fixed terminal and shrinking source shell

The companion test counts exact rows to a fixed \(q\) with \(2^{-k-102}\le x/(qG_b)\le2^{-k}\). At \(k=\lceil b^{2/3}\rceil\) and each fixed \(q>1\), it claims an upper bound \(O_{q,A}(qG_b2^{-k}b^{-A})\) for every \(A\). For a nonperiodic terminal a simple row already has \(D<2^{100}\). For a periodic one, write \(p,A_q\) for the odd and physical period, respectively. A source first hits \(q\) and then makes \(r\) full laps. The bounded distortion before first hit gives \(r\asymp_q k\). A nontrivial cycle has \(3^p<2^{A_q}<4^p\), hence \(A_q<2p\), and the first-hit clock is too early by an amount proportional to \(k\) for the original-source passage estimate. Cutoffs may depend on \(q\).

The lower estimate additionally needs **bounded real offset** on the prefixes counted by F. After one fixed predecessor gap \(e\) and \(r\) laps, the required prefix-clock displacement is \(2-e+r(2p-A_q)\). At \(q=1\) the lap term vanishes, so the lower scale \(G_b2^{-k}/\sqrt b\) applies even at \(k=\lceil b^{2/3}\rceil\). At another periodic \(q\), the claimed lower bound applies only through \(k\le c_q\sqrt b\). It is compatible with the upper bound at \(b^{2/3}\). This mechanism quantifies the clock mismatch from retaining a real cycle suffix. It does not provide a physical-clock lower bound in the shell where that upper bound would exclude a nontrivial cycle.

## Evidence and pins

Both cited files describe proposed written results. They identify the renewal assumption, the simple-path estimate, and the further finite-grid input for the stated upper estimate. The authors report bounded BigInt controls and identify their companion Lean source as an **uncompiled candidate** for finite row identities and recorded-path consumers.
