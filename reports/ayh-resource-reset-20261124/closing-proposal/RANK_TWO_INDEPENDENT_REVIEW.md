## The least-defect argument

Write \(z=16t+1\). The first two triple iterates are \(72t+5\) and \(108t+8\), with two odd inputs and no early hit of 1. If those are the only two odd inputs before 1, the second iterate is exactly \(2^e\), giving \(27z+5=4\,2^e\). Modulo \(27\cdot48=1296\) this forces \(4\,2^e=464\bmod1296\). I independently obtained precisely \(e=39,93,147\) below 201. Integer iteration gave first hitting times of the corresponding lower sources 168, 398 and 677. Those hits use 83, 194 and 336 odd inputs, respectively.

For \(e\ge201\), set \(c=2^{e-201}\). The identity \(27z+5=32\cdot4^{99}c\) implies \(z=1\bmod32\). Starting from \(z=32r+1\), five steps have three odd inputs and end at \(27r+2=4^{99}c+1\). Each successive odd-even pair trades a factor of four for a factor of three; 99 pairs end at \(3^{99}c+1\). Multiplying the checked strict inequality \(27\cdot2^{40}(3^{99}+1)+5<32\cdot4^{99}\) by \(c\ge1\) gives \(2^{40}(3^{99}c+1)<z\). The endpoint is outside the basin whenever \(z\) is. Its bounded reverse witness therefore contradicts minimality. The distinct statement about the infinite family uses \(K=27m+18\); the lower prefix has exactly \(3+K\) odd inputs and ends above 2, so it cannot already have visited the cycle of 1. Its upper finite certificate has exactly two odd inputs. This is consistent with the least-defect exclusion.
