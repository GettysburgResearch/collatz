## Approaches attempted

### 1. Re-expressing the XOR coupling in physical parity

`T-0014` uses quotient parity and phase parity. Replacing them by the physical Collatz parity removes the XOR completely. The phase follows the rounded pair

\[
S_0(v)=\lceil v/2\rceil,
\qquad
S_1(v)=\lfloor3v/2\rfloor.
\]

This is exact, not asymptotic.

### 2. Looking for a finite multi-mismatch phase closure

Direct exploration of the maps generated by synchronized and complementary branches showed that unrestricted mismatch phases proliferate rapidly. A small finite phase set is not closed.

Instead of promoting another finite census, the session asked what every finite phase code must conserve.

### 3. Harmonic and prefix-code analysis

The identity

\[
S_0(v)+S_1(v)=2v
\]

shows that the phase magnitude is harmonic under fair physical parity. This yielded exact phase–Kraft identities and a finite complete-graph obstruction.

### 4. Conditioning on phase survival

The harmonic function

\[
h(v)=v-1
\]

vanishes at the absorbing phase and gives an exact Doob transform. The transformed path probability telescopes to the endpoint-size bias

\[
2^{-L}\frac{S_w(v)-1}{v-1}.
\]

The resulting physical odd-step probability is at least \(3/4\), linking phase escape directly to positive Collatz pressure.

### 5. Exact verification

A dependency-free script checked the one-step identities, finite phase–Kraft formulas, Doob path density, drift bounds, and finite-depth absorption distributions.

## New proposed results

### L-0013 — Rounded physical phase identity

For \(n=q-v\) with physical parity \(e=n\bmod2\),

\[
v'=
\begin{cases}
\lceil v/2\rceil,&e=0,\\
\lfloor3v/2\rfloor,&e=1,
\end{cases}
\]

and

\[
T(q-v)=q'-v'
\]

with an explicit integral formula for \(q'\).

The phase endpoint of a physical parity word depends only on the starting phase and that word.

### T-0017 — Phase–Kraft martingale and escape transform

The theorem proves:

1. fair physical parity makes the phase a nonnegative martingale;
2. the phase is absorbed at \(1\) almost surely;
3. every finite complete prefix code satisfies
   \[
   \sum_w2^{-|w|}(S_w(v)-1)=v-1;
   \]
4. every recurrent class of a finite complete exact phase graph is phase \(1\);
5. the Doob transform is
   \[
   \mathbb Q_v([w])
   =
   2^{-|w|}
   \frac{S_w(v)-1}{v-1};
   \]
6. under this transform, physical odd parity has probability at least \(3/4\);
7. both phase magnitude and the formal Collatz multiplier have uniformly positive logarithmic drift.

### Three-measure bridge

One parity code now carries three exact probability weights:

- fair cylinder mass;
- Collatz growth tilt from `T-0016`;
- phase escape tilt from `T-0017`.

At odd phases the growth and escape one-step tilts coincide exactly. At even phase \(v\), their odd-step probabilities differ by only \(1/(4(v-1))\).

This identifies the phase-survival transform as the natural exceptional search distribution.

## Candidate counterexamples

None.

The escape transform produces symbolic paths with positive pressure but does not prove that one such path is the trajectory of an ordinary positive integer.

## Failed or blocked approaches

1. **A small closed multi-mismatch phase set.** The phase semigroup expands rapidly; finite closure is not the right unrestricted object.
2. **A finite complete multi-phase graph.** `T-0017` now rules this out away from phase \(1\). Completion forces fair absorption.
3. **Treating positive pressure as ordinary existence.** The escape measure lives naturally on symbolic/2-adic paths. The ordinary-boundary problem remains separate.
4. **Raw fair search.** Fair parity almost surely collapses the phase to \(1\); it is a poor proposal distribution for the exceptional language.

## Potential errors audited

- The rounded phase formula was checked independently from the four parity cases.
- The martingale absorption argument uses nonnegative martingale convergence and the fact that no phase \(v>1\) is fixed by either rounded branch.
- Phase–Kraft equality is restricted to finite complete prefix codes; incomplete families give only an inequality.
- The graph-rigidity conclusion uses complete finite parsing and almost-sure phase absorption.
- Positive drift under the Doob transform is explicitly not promoted to an ordinary-integer orbit.
- Floating-point calculations are used only to check explicit positive constants; all structural identities use exact integers or fractions.
