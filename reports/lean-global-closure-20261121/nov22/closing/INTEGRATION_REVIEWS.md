**VERIFIED as written mathematics relative to the classical Subspace

For a mechanical word, a factor determines all its partial floor sums.
The corresponding allowed phase is an intersection of intervals, hence
one cylinder interval. Bad approximability plus Dirichlet approximation
supplies a reduced denominator comparable to m and a grid within O(1/m)
of the first m rotation points. Consequently every cylinder interval has
length O(1/m), and equal factors give (11).

Apply the Subspace Theorem to the three fixed independent forms
X, alpha X−Y, beta X−Z at nearest-integer triples. The primary statement
of Theorem E was read in Adamczewski–Bugeaud's author PDF, page 555:
<https://adamczewski.perso.math.cnrs.fr/ComplexityI.pdf>.
Infinitely many violations of (12) would lie in a finite union of proper
rational subspaces. Dividing one infinite subsequence by its first
coordinate gives a rational relation between 1, alpha and beta,
contradicting independence of 1, sqrt(2), sqrt(3). Finite exceptional
denominators are absorbed by a positive constant. This gives the stated
uniform displacement exponent for repeated factors, not merely a typical
return-time assertion.

The binary code's only occurrence of 00 is at a code boundary. A block of
length at least nine contains such a marker, so equal long blocks align
modulo eight; discarding partial end blocks retains the displacement
bound. Polynomial factor complexity gives zero entropy, while the one
frequency is computed by telescoping the two rotations. A long periodic
shadow produces equal blocks separated by at most its preperiod-plus-
period length, contradicting the displacement bound if its excess length
is a fixed positive fraction of that length. The same reasoning works
outside a fixed number of short mismatch intervals.
