## Independent reconstruction

The section relation is exact: adding one to the even autoconjugacy
equation gives g(2n)=(2/3)g(n), including n=0. Its generating series has
constant coefficient zero and coefficient three at degree one. Thus it
is nonzero, and an assumed algebraic realization would be an instance
of AS-B.

I reconstructed the singularity argument using all continued branches,
not only singularities of one Taylor branch. A nonpolynomial algebraic
germ analytic at zero must have a nonzero finite genuine singularity:
otherwise continuation on the punctured plane has monodromy generated
by a small loop fixing the given germ, giving an entire algebraic
function and hence a polynomial. The section identity then gives
S contained in its image under squaring. Finiteness makes that map a
permutation. Every singular point is therefore an odd-order root of
unity and its negative is absent from the complete singularity set.

This last fact controls the relevant cancellation exactly. On
continuing H(z)+H(-z)=2cH(z^2) to a singular branch at alpha, the second
term is holomorphic on every branch there. A first nonholomorphic
Puiseux term of exponent rho must therefore propagate to alpha squared
with the same exponent. The coordinate substitution is 2u-u^2, so its
coefficient magnitude changes by the positive factor 2c*2^rho. Finite
branching gives only finitely many nonzero magnitudes. Iterating forces
that factor to equal one. A root-of-unity phase has modulus one and
cannot defeat this comparison. At c=2/3 the resulting equality between
a rational power of two and three is impossible.

I opened the primary Flajolet--Sedgewick INRIA text and its Theorem
8.11, rather than importing a coefficient asymptotic assertion that
might admit cancellation:
<https://algo.inria.fr/flajolet/Publications/FlSe01.pdf>.

The descent from Q_2 was also checked separately. Taking a separable
polynomial P and a truncation through N>=ord P_Y gives a polynomial
implicit equation after division by z^(N+1+s). The constant term has
the required order by the exact equation for the original series;
the linear term has that order and all higher terms have strictly
higher order. Its nonzero linear coefficient gives a formal recursion
over the field generated by the finite coefficients of P and the
truncation. That finitely generated characteristic-zero field embeds
in C. The implicit equation then gives a convergent complex algebraic
germ satisfying the same rational section equation. No embedding of
all of Q_2 into C, no preservation of a 2-adic norm, and no prior
algebraicity of the individual coefficients over Q are assumed.

Finally the ordinary binomial transform and its inverse are rational
substitutions with nonzero denominators at zero. Algebraicity of the
Newton-coefficient generating series would imply algebraicity of the
original series. Its exclusion follows.

## Scope of the literature consequences

The elementary rational special case agrees with the finite spectrum
argument: squaring permutes the finitely many nonzero characteristic
roots, after which an eventual polynomial coefficient cannot scale by
(2/3)^L under n to 2^L n. The cited simultaneous-Mahler rationality
theorem is used only after hypothesizing both of its finite systems;
the mixed section relation supplies neither system by itself.

The strongest conclusion is a representation obstruction for the
whole sequence. A transcendental generating series may have every
coefficient in Z[1/3]. Thus this theorem neither solves nor refutes
pointwise triadic autoconjugacy, and it must not be reported as a
Collatz proof or as a refutation of every source-dependent method.
