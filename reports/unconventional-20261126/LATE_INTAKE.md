## PR192: a useful elementary dependency

Root read the new adelic A1–A5 arguments at the pinned head. A1 proves
that the product of an even number of distinct nonnegative translates
of a sufficiently large integer is not a square. Its explicit
square-root expansion and polynomial root bound need no unproved Collatz
input. A2 adds three binary coordinates to turn a squareclass dependence
into precisely that contradiction. A3 bounds raw-state rank along one
actual trajectory. This is the source used by our shifted **reset-boundary**
rank argument; the added ingredient is the short-group selection from
fixed-block duration. Our proof credits this dependency rather than
presenting A1 as a discovery of PR200.

The pinned branch also distinguishes high-rank terminating windows from
a contradiction on a single fixed escaping trajectory. Its raw rank-seven
convergence deduction uses a published finite verification; that separate
computation was not rerun here. The reviewed source and the elementary
shifted-square obstruction are recorded in
[PR192_LATE_HEAD_REVIEW.md](reviews/PR192_LATE_HEAD_REVIEW.md).

## PR201: extend the finite exit gate carefully

Root read the finite first-exit construction and its summable-source
proof. For \(s>1\), sorting its gate vector gives an \(\ell^1\) limit of
component atoms \(Z_C(s)=\sum_{x\in C}L(x)^{-s}\). Our extension normalizes
the positive harmonic source weights at \(s=1\). The Abelian residue
agrees with the canonical weighted charge, but the diagonal physical
gate limit requires the extra first-passage peak bound. It does not
follow by taking \(s=1\) in a dominated-convergence argument.

The branch's stronger height law would improve a sufficient horizon in
our finite annular maximum test. That optional input was not substituted
into our already stated weaker horizon. Its signed-target observation
and our reflected-pressure construction are compatible. The full
operator and trace crosswalk is in
[PR201_TRACE_CROSSCHECK.md](reviews/PR201_TRACE_CROSSCHECK.md).

## PR195 and PR197: no hidden universal merger theorem

Root read PR195's final proposal and PR197's current scientific front
door. The independently inspected PR195 equations require an ordinary
smaller-base or earlier-exponent merger for every surviving tuple; their
formal consumer explicitly retains that hypothesis. The surviving
three-odd upper-hit family has \(j=11+162m\), \(x=2^j-1\), and
\(z=(128x-143)/81\). Larger upper odd counts and the first-crossing
alternative are also open. Its conditional inverse-fiber rates
\(o(2^\Delta)\) and \(2^{\Delta-o(\Delta)}\) are compatible, and concern
different normalization from our first-hit gate mass.

PR197 proves ordinary affine merger existence inside every specified
binary class. This overlaps our fusion construction's merger mechanism.
Our particular affine pair has one equal physical clock uniformly on a
subcylinder, whereas their displayed general merger permits different
clocks. Their all-source lower bounds on meeting a prescribed ascent
complement the protected two-block transposition exclusion. Aperiodic
walls and the global `RemainingExclusion` proposition remain open. Their
reported Lean totals are their own verification results; this sprint did
not rebuild those newly fetched packets.

PR195 separately records a review of three **first-checkpoint** PR200
theorems and an exact coupled-control replay. That is useful independent
evidence for that checkpoint, not an audit of PR200's later two waves.

## Literature correction and novelty boundary

The final comparison led root to Weaver's
[Paper 176 v1, Theorems 1–2](https://rstation.ai/problems/collatz/papers/176/v1).
It already gives the harmonic-head limit and stopping-loss identity
reconstructed in our Dirichlet note. Those formulas are prior work, not
new claims of this sprint. The exact failure of global nonincrease and
the reflected-pressure control have their separate derivations here.

Paper 147 already contains the fixed-fragment duration estimate used in
our reset-depth refinement. Paper 201 already proves distinct valley
blocks do not commute. Our protected transposition result addresses the
integrality of a different entrance with an exact common terminal. The
source notes now make these boundaries explicit. The final result is a
set of reviewed arguments and unresolved targets, not a claim that the
repository as a whole has settled Collatz.
