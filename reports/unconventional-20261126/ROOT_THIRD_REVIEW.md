## 1. Complete modular carries and cancellation

For a fixed modulus prime to six, the necessity of the clock-gcd condition
is a two-generator finite abelian group calculation. If primitive reduced
clock exponents identify the powers of 2 and 3, Bezout makes the relevant
power subgroup cyclic. Conversely a cyclic power subgroup admits primitive
logarithm residues. Unimodular continued-fraction neighbors preserve those
residues while placing positive clock pairs just above the critical slope.
The construction keeps the excess comparable to the reciprocal odd clock,
rather than using an unspecified density argument.

In the carry construction, changing a disjoint `12` pair to `21` changes
the carry by a unit modulo the chosen modulus. For a unit \(b\), the union \(S\cup(S+b)\)
strictly enlarges every proper nonempty subset \(S\) of a finite cyclic group; this justifies
the subset-sum coverage used for all carry classes. A forced unique `3`
in a word otherwise made from `1,2` certifies primitivity in that part.
Positive determinant and positive carry make every resulting rational
cycle physical in its stated rational domain.

The full-denominator extension divides the determinant into the primitive
clock factor and the geometric repeated-lap factor. Modulo the latter,
the unmodified carry vanishes; a positive switch sum bounds the common
factor by its size. Modulo the former, the switch pattern becomes
\(F(z)=g+(z-1)\sum_{j\in J}z^{r_j}\). The chosen pattern has no positive
real zero. A short positive-root binomial from the primitive clocks is
irreducible: applying prime valuations to the norm of a possible root
factor forces its degree to be the full degree. Hence its resultant with
\(F\) is nonzero and provides the stated common-divisor bound. Selecting
finitely many low-cost switch sites before increasing the clock gives
sublinear logarithmic cancellation. Unique support in one of the repeated
laps then excludes all smaller periods. This uses a previously developed
resultant method, which the manuscript credits.

The root's separate control does not import the author's carry routines.
It composes rational affine maps directly, checks 7,904 finite words and
2,432 positive primitive cycles, and reconstructs 88 complete modular
images. These checks test the implementation and small examples. They do
not prove the continued-fraction, resultant or asymptotic assertions.

The interpretation matters: fixed-modulus cancellation is compatible with
positive rational cycles of very large **reduced** denominator. Nothing
here sets that denominator equal to one or constructs an ordinary cycle.

## 2. Relative endpoints and common factors on ordinary paths

The relative endpoint argument uses the exact shifted recursion, not an
approximate homogeneous orbit. At a genuine reset the relative correction
is positive and at most the reciprocal shifted landing. Multiplying the
identities gives a strictly positive defect bounded by the sum over resets.
When there are no resets the defect is zero, so that case is excluded from
the logarithmic-form application. The endpoint ratio is first reduced
away from 2 and 3; any further allowed primes are explicitly charged in
the height and constant. Matveev's theorem does not require multiplicative
independence. Its exact published dependence was checked by the author;
the root verified the application and the official bibliographic source,
but did not independently obtain a readable original Matveev PDF.

For a single block, the elementary identity
\(g=\gcd(w,z+1)=\gcd(w,2^b-1)\) keeps the source and landing coupled.
The two-dimensional subspace argument uses fixed forms at infinity, 2
and 3. A projective exceptional line has finite physical fibers: the
positive terminal expression determines its integer landing and then its
reset depth, leaving only finitely many ascent parameters. Merely invoking
the subspace theorem without disposing of those lines would be insufficient.
The sharper exponent under a terminal floor follows by optimizing the
resulting two independent inequalities. The supplied CRT families have
exact odd valuations and are ordinary sources, including multiples of
three; they verify sharpness across changing sources.

For several blocks, unrolling and dividing by the common odd divisor gives
\(r+1\) **positive integral** coordinates. Evertse's Theorem 2 (1984),
printed page 228, applies without any primitive-vector assumption. Root
downloaded and visually inspected the original page: the product of the
three-place coordinate norms, multiplied by the dyadic norm of the sum,
is bounded below by the maximal dyadic coordinate norm times a negative
power of the ordinary height. Here that maximum is one. The product of
coordinate norms is bounded above by \(w_0 2^B/g^{r+1}\), while the sum has
exact dyadic valuation \(T+B-t_0+t_r\). Cancellation of these terms gives
the claimed factor \(g^{r+1}2^{T+t_r}\). Every coordinate height is
\(Y^{O_r(1)}\), so a fixed small exponent in Evertse yields the desired
arbitrary \(\epsilon\). Positivity verifies every subsum condition.

Writing the full gcd as \(2^{\min t_j}g\) and using
\((r+1)\min t_j\le T+t_r\) proves the full-gcd corollary. For an arbitrary
growing number of blocks, restrict to any fixed number first and then let
that fixed number grow. This removes a potential misuse of dimension-
dependent constants. The separate dyadic CRT construction gives the sharp
full-gcd exponent; it need not share the odd-divisor construction's profile.

The depth refinement initially appears to need an Evertse theorem in
unbounded dimension. It avoids that: for each bounded total order ratio,
split the geometric corrections into a fixed positive sum. For large
ratios use the existing coefficient-one duration bound and
\(g<2^e\). A fixed cutoff separates the two arguments. The added duration
reconstruction verifies signed subsums explicitly. Corrections have
strictly interlaced dyadic orders; subsets without the root cannot vanish.
For sufficiently large boundary inputs, all negative corrections together
are smaller than the root, excluding the remaining vanishing subsums.
Cutting at the first bounded boundary handles the general case. This
special case is already in Paper 147 and is credited accordingly.

The root independently generated 10,000 actual complete blocks, including
2,862 protected cases, and 11 CRT examples of the retained-gcd profiles.
Those finite checks are separate from the Diophantine proof. A sibling
reconstructed both the persistent-divisor theorem and the depth refinement.
The universal missing ingredient is a compulsory large persistent factor
or low-complexity endpoint quotient on an exceptional orbit.

## 3. Two-block transposition and shifted squareclasses

The transposition calculation compares two affine compositions with the
same positive slope. Root checked the signed source difference. If it is
integral, reducing its numerator at two ternary scales gives a lower
bound on the total reset depth and a nonzero negative congruence marker.
The latter cannot vanish in a missed exceptional case. The actual original
path's terminal floor bounds the total homogeneous gain; physicality of
the transposed path is not assumed. Together these inequalities imply
minimum ascent at most two, maximum ascent at most ten, and total reset
depth at most twelve. The 2,880-tuples Lean exhaustion is therefore enough.
The exact affine identity is also formalized. The reductions involving
logarithms and the ternary order formula remain written mathematics.
The ordinary merger example below the floor confirms the scope boundary.

For shifted squareclasses, root read the new PR192 A1 proof rather than
assuming its conclusion. Its rational square-root expansion has integral
denominators controlled by a power of four. The tail is smaller than the
integer grid, while a nonzero polynomial and a Cauchy root bound exclude
an exact square above the threshold. Distinct nonnegative translates are
required. At complete shifted reset boundaries the normalized offsets
are strictly increasing; inside an ascent they would repeat. This is why
the statement selects resets.

For a putative rank bound \(d\), choose \(m=d+3\) and
\(K=12(m+3)\) before varying the source. Among \(K\) groups of \(m\)
complete blocks, the duration bound finds a group of length
\(H\le(2\log_2 3/K)\log_2 Y+O(1)\) under the boundary floor. Clearing
denominators gives distinct integer offsets at most \(6^H\). Appending
the two clock parities and the parity of the subset size forces an even
square relation if those boundary values have rank at most \(d\). The
coefficient in the required threshold is \((\log_2 3)/2<1\). Thus large
future minima force unbounded rank. Cutting a cycle at its minimum yields
finiteness at each fixed rank. This is neither the earlier centered
multiplicative-rank theorem nor PR192's unshifted statement.

The accompanying elementary lemma that no complete reset joins two square
shifted entrances also checks. In its difference-of-squares equation,
parity modulo three forces an even reset depth. The consecutive even
neighbors of the odd multiple of three have gcd two, so one is divisible
by the required power of two. Comparing their sizes forces equality and
then the contradiction modulo three. This is a small universal obstruction,
not a bound on all possible squareclasses.

## 4. Annular currents and the harmonic endpoint

The finite annular identity is algebraic: for shortcut divergence
\(f(v)^p-\sum_{T(x)=v}f(x)^p\), every internal edge cancels. The two
remaining ranges are the odd outgoing band and even incoming band. The
passage to the infinite first-hit trace is more delicate. Root reconstructed
the tree exhaustions, weighted cycle correction and component tightness
used to obtain convergence of the total variation of divergence. Uniform
reciprocal-tail bounds apply to distinct orbit states; repeated cycle laps
are handled separately. The resulting boundary current is the sum of
positive escape-charge powers for \(p>1\). The unweighted \(p=1\) identity
has a different total and cannot be reached by a casual continuity argument.

Uniform approximation by mixtures started at a finite core does not make
that current zero: each mixture retains the core states' **entire future**.
The independently checked binary model has diffuse finite first passages,
uniform empirical approximation and a nonzero escaping-ray index. It
identifies the invalid inference from atomlessness to vanishing escape.

In the Dirichlet formula, summing the exact loss of the survival product
gives the charge identity. Cycle repetitions use their finite geometric
factor, and tightness removes the remaining component tail. The centered
periodic harmonic sum has a boundary term
\(c_*=\tfrac12\log(4/3)\); the exponent classes must be summed with their
correct absolute and odd-relative densities. The periodic remainder after
one full period is bounded uniformly, yielding the finite harmonic-head
formula. The final literature intake identified these identities in
Weaver’s prior Paper 176, Theorems 1–2 and equation (6); this
reconstruction does not establish novelty. A domination by the stationary density justifies the Cesaro
limit. Root checked the integer overflow bounds and both truncation errors
in the exact certificate \(B_{14}>B_{13}\). This excludes global nonincrease; eventual monotonicity remains open.

The critical exit-gate proof is an actual endpoint extension of PR201,
not substitution of \(s=1\) into a summable-source proof. Root inspected
the pinned original TeX at `ct-local-log-path`, `ct-stage-TV` and
`ct-time-law`. The local estimate holds simultaneously on both required
source windows. Subtracting two path estimates bounds the intermediate
peak. The lower-rung success and peak event is measurable from the landing
state, so marginal total variation transfers it. Errors are summable at
fixed bottom threshold uniformly in the top rung. Default-valued passage
laws are used for comparison only; success induction makes the actual
concatenation legitimate. Tiling the final partial window changes its
harmonic mass by a bounded factor, and the near-top source strip has
vanishing relative harmonic mass.

After fixing the bottom set, each of its finitely many periodic futures
has bounded height and each escaping future merges before a large enough
gate. Future survival tends to one on those gates. Component tightness
then permits the bottom threshold to grow. A sibling independently checked
this order of limits. Root added the elementary full-to-odd harmonic
identity clarifying the separated-cutoff variant. The primary analytic
chain establishing those density and passage inputs was not rebuilt.

The exact finite gates agree under two independent implementations at
seven cutoffs. These computations prove neither the convergence theorem
nor its missing zero limit. Reflection conjugates the entire inverse
pressure family but reverses the affine sign; a positive fixed orbit then
causes exponential harmonic-head growth. Hence pressure alone omits the
particular arithmetic information needed by the proposed vanishing bound.
