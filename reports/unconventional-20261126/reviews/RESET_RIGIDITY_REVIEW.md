**A and B.** Multiplication of the shifted consecutive identities yields \(W=3^kY_0/(2^A Y_k)\). Each reset contributes exactly \((1-2^{1-a_i})/Y_{i+1}\) to the corresponding \(1-d_i\) factor. It lies strictly between zero and \(1/F\). Thus \(0<1-W<R/F\) whenever there is a reset, including \(R/F>1\) and an identical pair of endpoints. This proves precisely the nonvanishing needed by Matveev. The decomposition of the *reduced ratio* into powers at 2, 3, the selected primes, and the residual base uses an exponent of magnitude one for the residual. All other exponents are \(O(2+k+\log Y_0)\) by physical growth and \(A\le 2k+\log_2 x_0\). Deleting trivial factors, the rational product bound is exactly the one in the cited theorem, after enlarging constants depending on the number of bases. No multiplicative independence is needed. [Rational formulation, Theorem 2.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12188-025-00293-9)

For a block input \(Y=2^tw\), the first \(t-1\) exponent-one steps give the final reset input plus one \(2\,3^{t-1}w\), so the reset output is \(z=(3^tw-1)/2^b\) exactly. Therefore \(z+1\le3^tw\le Y^\alpha\). This iterates across the complete blocks and bounds a terminal partial ascent. It yields precisely the geometric duration bound (6). The positive odd divisor \(g\) divides both \(w\) and \(2^b-1\); multiplication by \(2^b\) and \(\gcd(w,2)=1\) give the reverse gcd equality. Thus \(gz<3^tw\) and \(2^t\le Y/g\), proving B. The least-disagreement source assumptions are exactly `good_below` and `upper_bad` at the cited pin. **C, upper bound.** Set \(u=w/g\), \(v=(2^b-1)/g\), and \((X,V)=(3^tu,v)\). Because \(u,v\) are coprime, the coordinate gcd is a power of three. At the three places \(S=\{\infty,2,3\}\), dividing both coordinates by that content multiplies the complete product of the six forms by one. The pairs \((X,V),(X+V,V),(X,V)\) all have determinant one, and each actual input has positive, nonzero coordinate and sum forms. The primitive maximum height \(H\) is bounded by \(3^tw\le Y^\alpha\), also for \(V\), because \(2^b\le3^tw-1\). This is exactly the \(r=n=2\) case of the primitive-vector theorem. On any exceptional rational line, the positive ratio \(r=X/V=z+(z+1)/(2^b-1)\) fixes a finite set of possible integers \(z<r\). For each such \(z\), \(b\) is unique and the possible \(t,w\) factor a fixed integer. Thus no infinite family is hidden on a line. [Schlickewei (1977)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(77)90073-7) · [Evertse's primitive-vector statement, Theorem 1.1](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~evertsejh/dio2011-padicsubspace.pdf)

**C, lower bound.** For odd \(b\), \(G=2^b-1\) is a unit modulo three. The first CRT condition fixes an odd residue \(u\) modulo \(2^{b+1}\), giving the exact valuation \(v_2(3^tGu-1)=b\). The second makes \(x=2^tGu-1\) divisible by three, and the representative interval has length the full combined modulus. The input is ordinary, \(g=G\), and the consecutive gaps before the reset are physical. The bounded errors in \(\log_2 Y=t+2b+O(1)\) and \(\log_2 z=\alpha t+b+O(1)\) are uniform in \(t\). At \(\sigma=1/2\) a fixed positive additive shift in \(t\) accommodates every prescribed \(\lambda\). At \(\sigma=1\) the same shift makes the entire first block floor-protected, without saying anything about its later trajectory. Matching upper and lower bounds yield the quoted \(0.2695772896908149\ldots\) for one protected block.
