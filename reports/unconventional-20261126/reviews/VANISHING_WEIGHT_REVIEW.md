# Independent review of the vanishing-weight answer to Béhani

Reviewed `BERGMAN_VANISHING_WEIGHTS.md` at SHA-256 `ae176d5704c3283f6803cdfdac767176ab2bff0305685fdffc54ac7c7e7fe333`. I find O10 and its existential adjoint equivalence valid. Béhani's coefficient norm and deleted degrees appear on pages 3–4 of the arXiv paper, and Question 4.3 is on page 15. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.03203)

**Weights and quotient.** The explicit weight obeys \(0<\omega(n)\le n\) at positive degrees, \(\omega(2n)=2\omega(n)\), and \(\omega(u)=1/u\) at positive odd multiples of three. For every \(r<1\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\sum |c_n|r^n\le\|f\|_\omega(\sum\omega(n)r^{2n})^{1/2}<\infty\), uniformly on the closed radius-\(r\) disk. Thus it gives the actual analytic coefficient Hilbert space. The even-preimage weight ratio is two. Any odd predecessor forces the endpoint prime to three, so the extra ratio is below one. The deletion of degrees 0,1,2 is consistent because their forward images remain deleted. Doubling every degree \(k\ge3\) gives the right inverse with norm \(2^{-1/2}\).

**Generalized kernel.** An orbit must encounter an odd input after finitely many halvings. Its next output is prime to three, and all subsequent outputs stay prime to three. For any monomial not killed sooner, the correcting monomial \(e_{2^n T^n k}\) therefore has squared norm at most \(2^{-n}\), and maps under the \(n\)-th transfer power to precisely the same monomial as \(e_k\). Killed monomials already belong to the generalized kernel. This gives density for arbitrary ordinary component structure. The topological mixing approximation uses one dense generalized-kernel vector in each target open set and the decaying right inverse. The periodic approximants work by telescoping its powers, with no inverse identity on the other side needed.

**Gaussian factor.** Each bi-infinite ladder has a finite nonzero negative part and a square-summable positive part. Scalars \(b_i>0\) can be taken, for instance, as \(2^{-i}(1+\sum_t\|x_{i,t}\|^2)^{-1/2}\) with indexing from one. The independent centered complex Gaussian series converges in the mean-square space of Hilbert-valued variables and gives a measurable representative. For full support, prescribe the finitely many dense-span coefficients of an approximation and require the other coordinates of a sufficiently large finite truncation to be near zero. This event has positive probability. The independent remaining tail meets a small norm ball with positive probability by the mean-square bound. The shift calculation \(\mathcal T\Phi=\sum_{i,t}b_i\gamma_{i,t+1}x_{i,t}\) holds in mean square and hence almost surely, rendering the law a factor of a countable product of bilateral independent Gaussian shifts. Its mixing follows, and Birkhoff simultaneously on a countable base gives frequent hypercyclicity. I found no variance or conjugation mismatch.

**Adjoint.** A bounded functional has squared coefficient norm \(\sum_{k\ge3}\omega(k)|f(k)|^2\); coefficient truncations are dense, so evaluation on monomials suffices. The quotient forces zero values at killed degrees. Doubling preimages excludes eigenvalue zero for all such weights. For a nonzero eigenvalue, all states entering a deleted degree must have zero functional value, so existence requires an ordinary component disjoint from the 1–2 cycle. Conversely, \(\omega(k)=k^{-2}\) is another admissible analytic Hilbert weight with bounded transfer and vanishing liminf, and the indicator of any such component is a bounded eigenfunctional at eigenvalue one. This is an existential equivalence over weights. The explicit Gaussian-mixing weight itself has no adjoint eigenvalues by dense generalized kernel and surjectivity. No assertion about a particular unknown ordinary component is used in the construction of that weight.
