**New implication from A1.** [Shifted squareclass closure](../../../research/unconventional-20261126/defects/SHIFTED_SQUARECLASS_CLOSURE.md) applies A1 to \(2^{A_i}(n_i+1)/3^i\) at complete reset boundaries. Its normalized offsets repeat during ascents and increase at each reset. Choose a short physical window with many reset boundaries using the fixed-number-of-blocks duration estimate. This proves that finite squareclass rank of \(x_j+1\) at those boundaries is equivalent to eventual periodicity of a fixed ordinary orbit. It also gives finiteness of ordinary cycles at fixed shifted rank, uniformly in the generators. In fact if every successive \(d+4\)-entrance window has rank at most \(d\), even in changing subspaces, the orbit is eventually periodic by the same tail-minimum argument.

The earlier [centered-defect theorem](../../../research/unconventional-20261126/defects/CENTERED_DEFECT_RETURNS.md) already implies eventual periodicity if every shifted boundary lies in one finite-rank **multiplicative subgroup** of positive rationals, using one mismatch between consecutive reset boundaries. Finite rank *modulo squares* is a weaker hypothesis: all prime squares have squareclass zero and generate a multiplicative group of infinite rank. The new theorem permits all such square parts without restriction. A1–A3 in the new PR and the finite multiplicative-rank result are explicit antecedents. Earlier results on wave primes and smoothness concern different arithmetic data; no exhaustive historical-priority claim is intended. [Verger-Gaugry, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14569)

**An effective elementary two-boundary obstruction.** No complete positive ordinary block has both shifted boundary values square. Suppose the input \(Y=2^t w\) is an even square. Then \(t\) is positive and even, \(w\) is an odd square and \(M=3^{t/2}\sqrt w\) is an odd multiple of three. A square shifted landing \(z+1=n^2\) would give
\[
                   2^b(n^2-1)=M^2-1 .
\]
Modulo 3 an odd \(b\) would give \(n^2=2\bmod3\), so \(b\) is even. Then \(N=2^{b/2}n\) is integral and \(N^2-M^2=2^b-1>0\), yielding \(2^b-1\ge2M+1\). On the other hand \(2^b\mid(M-1)(M+1)\); exactly one neighbor of this odd \(M\) has dyadic valuation one. Hence \(2^{b-1}\le M+1\). The two estimates give equality \(M=2^{b-1}-1\); this is 1 modulo 3 since \(b\) is even, contrary to \(3\mid M\). In particular square shifted boundaries never occur at two neighboring reset entrances. This argument does not classify other squareclasses or all possible cycles.

**Cycle and escape boundary.** Finite squareclass rank characterizes eventual periodicity but has not been established for arbitrary positive trajectories. Every finite cycle has some such finite rank, with no known common bound. The fixed-rank conclusions therefore leave both the possible existence of an escaping orbit and the existence of a nontrivial positive cycle open. The PR's raw-squareclass bounds do not invalidate the rational near-critical examples with prescribed cancellation: multiplying those phases by their common denominator changes the map's addend from 1 to that denominator.
