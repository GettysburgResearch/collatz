# Independent review of the finite local weight obstruction

I reviewed `LOCAL_WEIGHT_OBSTRUCTION.md` at SHA-256 `29e5eda8ec8f450788aa30c9643051f62c0a3af04b09e0b130b1a43c60e119b9`. I find the proof of O7 valid with its stated positive summable weights. It gives a family of one-edge vectors in the domain of the residual, including their closed span in the weighted space. No global boundedness of composition is used.

* Congruence (3) is a ternary unit target and forces odd \(L\). After dividing by \(3^r\) and multiplying by \(2^r\), it gives \(z\equiv-2^{-d}\pmod{3^{2k}}\). Thus \(y\) is odd, \(3^k\mid y\), and \(n\equiv-1\pmod3\). An odd \(e\) makes \(x\) ordinary odd, with \(3x+1=2^e n\). The specified path really has \(k\) odd shortcut inputs before \(2^dz\), and \(r\) before its first power of two from \(z\). All preterminal inputs in the latter segment are odd and greater than one for large \(L\). Distinct values of \(L\) have different first powers and disjoint target cones.
* The limit of \(x\) at 2 and at 3 is the same fixed \(b_e\), because its difference is \(2^{e+k}y/3\) with \(3^k\mid y\). The two limits for \(z\) are computed exactly in the note. The initial parameters \(d,r,e\) can be fixed outside the finite exceptional sets. This yields neighborhoods with local bounds independent of \(k,L\) for large \(k\) and suitably large \(L\).
* For every auxiliary prime \(\ell>3\), both slopes as functions of \(2^L\) are units; their translations can depend on \(k\). The choice \(J=2B3^{2k+r-1}\) kills all orders modulo \(\ell\), and \(v_\ell(2^J-1)=v_\ell(2^{2B}-1)\) since the remaining exponent is a power of 3. This remains true if \(\ell\mid B\) because that factor already occurs in the fixed valuation on the right. The order modulo \(\ell^{H_\ell}\) is precisely \(\ell^{H_\ell-c_\ell}\). Thus each excluded local class costs at most one parameter residue per endpoint for that prime. The moduli imposed on the *same* parameter \(t\) are now pure powers of distinct primes, so the choices really are compatible by CRT; no independence of the original orders is assumed. The remaining complements are compact and separated from the forbidden finite sets.
* The cone contains the small input \(x\), and its unique nonzero edge residual is at its root \(z\). The supports are disjoint at both ends. The resulting pointwise expansion on the closed span puts its residual in the weighted space by the proved inequality, hence also its composition. Finite anchors remove at most one cone each. Neither an extension to nonsummable weights nor boundedness of composition on all vectors follows here.
