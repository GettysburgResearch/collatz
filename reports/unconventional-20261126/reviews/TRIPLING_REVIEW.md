# Independent review of the joint tripling clock

I reviewed `TRIPLING_CLOCK.md` at SHA-256 `15cee3f00762ba31578f1e281f739f192aa75829c28497f1b453886c34ee6770`. I find T1 and T2 valid as stated.

For an odd rational \(x\), the truncated expansion has denominator \(3^{i+1}\) exactly after including term \(i\), since no earlier term contributes modulo 3 after clearing that denominator. On the \(3x\) leg the extra power of 3 in the denominator gives the corresponding exact denominator \(3^{j+2}\). Equality of the two rational prefixes thus fixes their denominator exponents, and the finite physical affine identities then give equality of the products of an actual odd rational and its dyadic clock multiplier on the two arms. Oddness forces equality of the clocks and then equality of the two odd states. The proof needs no ordinary integrality for the individual states at this stage.

For unequal prefixes, their difference times the larger ternary denominator is a nonzero ordinary integer divisible by the next union-date power of two. This divisibility can also be seen directly by subtracting the two finite identities; all remaining rational factors have odd denominators. The two positive prefix sums are strictly smaller than \(2^t/2\) and \(2^t/6\) respectively, even when one of their own last dates is earlier than \(t\). The displayed strict local gap inequality follows. Below the first arm to reach its \(K\)-th date there are at most \(2K-1\) distinct union dates because both arms include zero. This gives the claimed cumulative bound, without choosing a single smaller arm at each separate gap.

Under T2 the first repeated state on the single arm with the smaller total clock occurs by transition \(K\). Its entire recurring block lies in those first \(K\) gaps, and its phase denominator divides \(2^A-3^s\). For a reduced denominator \(D\) prime to six, both tripling and each odd Syracuse transition preserve that denominator exactly: the numerator \(3n+D\) is coprime to \(D\), and the power of two removed is a unit modulo \(D\). Thus the phase gives the asserted denominator bound. At fixed \(K\) the positive gap sum bound gives a finite list of whole prefixes on at least one arm. Each prescribed repeated block has a unique rational fixed point, and each length and affine inverse prefix prescribes at most one source. This last step is needed for cardinality; a denominator bound on its own would not suffice. The theorem allows irrational or aperiodic behavior outside its explicitly stated hypotheses and supplies no ordinary descent witness.
