# Independent check of the measurable inverse entropy argument

I reviewed the proposed Bergman pressure proof in `EXACT_BERGMAN_PRESSURE.md` at SHA-256 `5e93fc264fdbf9fe287c90531bab5875d1d7f9745f2ecd4419e9be9edcf5194c`. The proof claims the exact positive-transfer radius $r(B_p)=\max(1,3p)$ at every geometric parameter. In concert with the induced-operator identity, this gives $\sqrt{20}/3$ for the classical Bergman quotient in Béhani's Question 4.1. I reconstructed the countable, measurable extension in Sections 2–7. I found no invalid conditional-independence assertion or change in the order of limits. This is a mathematical review of a proposed written theorem. Neither a kernel proof of the entropy argument nor a universal Collatz termination result was obtained here.

## Obtaining an ergodic measurable policy

For each maximizing row, the finite Gibbs law has finite expected total letter. Its density with respect to iid geometric words is bounded by $3^n/\|B^n\|$. Recording an independent uniform time $J$ gives conditional entropy $H(E_J\mid X_J,J)$, and discarding $J$ increases this entropy. Since $X_j$ is determined by the starting point and previous labels, the Gibbs identity gives (2) in the claimed direction. In particular the divergences from the moving state law times the geometric label law are bounded. Exponential integrability of a small positive multiple of the geometric letter gives tightness of all empirical edge laws and a finite first moment of the limit. Weak continuity of the endpoint map on the legal edge set follows since a convergent label sequence in a discrete alphabet is eventually constant. Weak lower semicontinuity of joint relative entropy gives the required upper bound at the limiting policy. The extracted mean need not equal the limit of the empirical means.

In the two-sided stationary extension the invariant sigma-field is contained, up to completion, in the past including the present state. For a bounded vanishing transition residual $F=1_{E=e}-q_e(X_0)$ in conditional expectation given that past, multiply the past test by an arbitrary invariant-event indicator. Disintegrating over the invariant field and using a countable generator proves that the same conditional law holds on almost every ergodic component. This works for the edge process, including parallel labels. Conditioning on the invariant field need not preserve an arbitrary process's conditional law given the present state alone. The full-past identity and this countable test supply the needed intermediate step. The component rewards average correctly and have finite mean letters almost surely.

## Conditional information rate, signs, and cells

For fixed $j<n$, $(B_n,E_{<j})$ determines $X_j[n-j]$. The chain rule thus bounds $H(W_n\mid B_n)$ above by $\sum_{d=1}^n H(E_0\mid X_0[d])$. The corresponding full-state entropy is exactly $n\beta$, by determinism of the state updates and the Markov property. The countable entropy $H(E_0)$ is finite by the first moment. Thus the differences of the displayed conditional entropies from $\beta$ have Cesàro mean zero. Also $B_n$ is determined by $W_n$ because each contraction fixes one further ternary digit. This proves both (5) and the full limit of $H_n/n$.

For (7), fix a letter $e$ that occurs and its current ball $b$. Conditional on the entire past label field, the product of the probability of $b$ and the posterior letter probability on $b$ equals the product of the prior letter probability and the conditional mass of the image ball after that letter. The image ball has exactly one fewer digit. Along a sampled path the factors being logged are positive. Taking negative logarithms gives $J_k=J_{k-1}\circ S-\log a_E+\log b_{k,E}$. The last term is the log of the **cell average** of the transition probability. Using its point value at fixed $k$ would be unjustified for a general measurable policy.

For the limit of these logs, each fixed letter posterior is a bounded martingale in the ternary depth. Intersecting countably many full-measure sets covers the sampled letter. Expectations of the negative logs are the conditional countable entropies. Their convergence follows by first collapsing all letters beyond a cutoff into one symbol; the exact entropy cost is at most the corresponding unconditional finite-entropy tail, uniformly in the conditioning. Therefore the log errors converge in $L^1$ by nonnegative almost-sure convergence and convergence of means. In the iterated recursion their total $L^1$ error is at most the sum of the single-depth errors, which is $o(k)$. The remaining integrable increments are translates of $-\log a_E+\log q_E(X_0)$, with mean $h_E-\beta$. The $L^1$ ergodic theorem proves (8).

At fixed $m$, conditioning on any $k$-letter word gives a bijection of the next $m$ initial digits and $m$ terminal digits. After shifting the process, $t_{k,m}$ consequently has the law of the entropy of the terminal $m$-digit law given the preceding $k$ labels. Those conditional vectors converge as $k$ increases with $m$ fixed. On each $B_k$ cell, concavity gives the nonnegative excess $d_{k,m}$ in (9). Its averaged value telescopes only after averaging the *unconditional* $H_{k+m}-H_k$ over scales. The result is
$$
\limsup_n\frac1{Kn}\sum_{k=n}^{n+Kn-1}\mathbb E|s_{k,m}/m-F|
 \le \mathbb E|R_m/m-F|+F-\mathbb E R_m/m .
$$
Here $R_m=\mathbb E(J_m\mid\mathcal G)$, so both error terms tend to zero by (8). This establishes (10) with the double limit as printed. It would not prove analogous uniformity for every scale or every initial point.

## Carry decoding and short-window dependence

The total $A$ is fixed inside each class. For any nonempty prefix the integer $c_j$ is odd. Given $c_{j+1}$, $j\ge1$, the exact valuation of $c_{j+1}-3^j$ recovers $E_j$; knowing $A$ at the end recovers the remaining first label. The range $0<c_n<2^A3^n$ and $A\le Kn$ ensure the decoded word is determined by $(B,A,V[Kn])$. Losing the remaining high-total words costs at most $(\log2)\mathbb E[A1_{A>Kn}]$ in entropy, which is $o(n)$ by $L^1$ convergence of ergodic averages when $K>\mathbb EE$. Revealing the integer $A$ itself costs only $O(\log n)$. These bounds prove (12) without assuming bounded exponents.

Let $C=(B,A)$. The inequality in (13) uses $I(X_0;W\mid B,A)=I(X_0;W\mid B)-I(X_0;A\mid B)$ because $A$ is determined by $W$. Additional conditioning on an arbitrary random variable would lack this property. The functions producing $U$ and $V$ depend respectively only on $(X_0,C)$ and $(W,C)$, so data processing applies.

At scale $i$, condition on $C$ and both input prefixes. Write $u,v$ for their next $m$ digits and $a,b$ for the lower prefixes. The expected KL divergence against the product of the two **own-prefix** marginals is exactly
$$
 H(u\mid C,a)+H(v\mid C,b)-H(u,v\mid C,a,b).
$$
By the conditional entropy chain rule this is $I(U[i+m];V[i+m]\mid C)-I(U[i];V[i]\mid C)$. The expression includes the information one input's new digits carry about the other input's *past*. The mutual information between the two blocks conditioned on both lower prefixes would miss those terms. Summing the displayed differences counts each nonnegative one-digit information increment at most $m$ times. By (13), the resulting total is $o(n)$ for fixed $m$.

The map sending two blocks to the output restricted block is addition modulo $3^m$ followed by a shift determined by $a,b$. For a uniformly chosen window the expected total-variation error between this image law and that for the product law tends to zero by Pinsker and Cauchy–Schwarz. Uniform continuity and the bound $m\log3$ for finite alphabet entropy then give an $o(n)$ error in the sum of expected output entropies. The same proof compares the base entropy with and without recording $A$, this time with total information cost at most $mH(A)$. The multiplication by $2^A$ is a cell permutation at every depth, with a permutation of the next digits on that cell. Only base entropy is transferred by this assertion.

Fix the deficit $c=(\log3-F)/2>0$. Choose a fixed $m$ using (10) and then take $n$ large for that $m$. In expectation, few of these base windows have entropy above $m(\log3-c)$. On the other hand (12) splits into the sum of $Kn$ one-digit noise entropies, each at most $\log3$. A positive multiple of $n$ windows in expectation has the indicated first noise digit entropy. The linear intersection count requires only the union bound. For the fixed $m$, positive one-digit noise entropy forces support at two distinct residues mod $3$. A difference of such points generates $\mathbb Z/3^m$. Strict concavity in the equality case of convolution entropy, plus compactness, gives a positive gain uniform over all selected input laws.

Finally $S[i]=(U+V)[i]$ is determined by $U[i],V[i]$. The entropy of the next output block given these two prefixes is therefore a lower bound for its entropy given the lower sum alone. This gives (15) after summation, including the error control just checked. Individual $g_j$ can be negative or nonmonotone. Neither fact enters the upper bound: the exact sliding-window sum contains the $m$ last boundary values, each bounded above by $H_j-H_{n+j}+H_n+H(A)=o(n)$ for $j=Kn+O_m(1)$, less the first $m$ bounded values. In particular the constants in the finite-group gap may decay rapidly in $m$: $m$ was fixed before the final $n\to\infty$ contradiction.

## Finite controls and source comparison

The accompanying standard-library script `review_pressure_entropy.py` verifies the cell Bayes identity with rational probabilities for 1,080 cases involving nonconstant posteriors. An independently constructed stationary infinite coding law with four finite letters gives 16 more depth-pair tests, detects 3,300 changes of inverse letter posterior as ternary precision increases, and checks 182 strict per-cell mixture gains. Three other finite laws test strong dependence of the base and noise, including carries and interleaved past information. The 27 short-window tests reproduce (14), ordered entropy growth bounds from (15), and the total cost of overlapping windows; 18 distinguish the incorrect alternate KL product. The script also enumerates 55,986 words through six letters and checks fixed-total carries and finite-window injectivity. Entropy comparisons use floating-point calculations with an explicit tolerance; Bayes and carry checks are exact.

Weaver's paper 127/v1 establishes its entropy gain for capped, finite-memory inverse laws. The argument reviewed here derives each replacement required for measurable, finite-mean policies. Béhani's Proposition 4.2 identifies the correct increasing-endpoint slope sums for the shortcut map. The lower orbit $x=-1$ in the ternary problem is legal for $e=1$ and realizes $3p$; preservation of Haar integral supplies the other lower bound.
