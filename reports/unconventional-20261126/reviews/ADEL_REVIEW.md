## M1 and M2 reconstruction

On the other side, $2^{-P_{-j}}B_j$ is integral since all its summands have nonnegative dyadic exponent. Reduction modulo $3^j$ therefore yields an integer ancestor when $j\le b$. The signed recurrence gives $x_i=g_i(X+V_i)$ and $V_{i+1}-V_i=1/(3g_i)$ for positive and negative indices. It makes the initially constructed ancestors positive throughout the truncated window. In particular the proof does not splice two unrelated local approximants.

If two physical states coincide before the truncation, summing the log of $(3x_i+1)/(3x_i)$ around that segment gives an upper bound of order $h/X$ on the corresponding distance from $h\alpha$ to an integer. Any fixed finite-type lower bound excludes this for $1\le h\le(\log X)^2$ and large $X$. For two equal factors of $m$ gaps, $3^m$ times their initial difference is $2^E$ times their final *even* difference. Relative primality then gives the stronger initial divisibility $2^{E+1}$. After choosing $a$ in its prescribed class, the additional congruence modulo the product of primes of $a$ outside $L$ is compatible and removes every common prime factor of $a,b$. Consequently $(A,p)$ is primitive as an integral vector. The determinant identity is $AQ-pP=b$, with positive sign. It yields $\delta=A-p\alpha>b/Q$, which is needed for the upper bound on the real minimum. The upper error uses both convergent errors, each less than $1/Q$. For $L\le Q^\tau$, the resulting $\delta\ll p^{-(1-2\tau)/(1+2\tau)}$ is strong enough for the claimed $p^{-1+\epsilon}$.

The primes in the prescribed finite set and the primes up to $c\log Q$ are controlled simultaneously by the exponents $A=0,p=1\bmod L$.
