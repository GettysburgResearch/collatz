# Independent review of prescribed-prime coupled rational cycles

**Reviewed source.** COUPLED_CYCLES.md SHA-256 62ff4895e9487e729367c9a1287657aa869b610687858d71d0bbc08fc8bac486. I find the stated J1 conclusion follows from the written proof.

1. The carry identity follows by checking the three pairs. The affine relation has the required sign, and it survives composition in chronological order. The input block maps have positive slopes respectively $3/4,3/8,81/4096$ and send $[0,1]$ into itself. The full input fixed point is positive and at most one, with strictly positive output fixed point. The raw denominator $2^N-3^p$ is positive, odd and prime to three.
2. For an arbitrary cyclic rotation of either word, its positive carry divided by the common raw denominator is in the odd-denominator ring, and is carried to the next rotated fixed point by the prescribed edge. Only one shortcut branch takes a given element of that ring back into the ring. Thus every promised letter is its actual parity. The penultimate phase $q$ is positive and odd, and $T(3q)=3X-1=Y$. Each odd step yields a ternary unit and even steps preserve ternary-unit status. Both cycles contain odd inputs, so all their phases are ternary units. The ternary multiple $3q$ hence enters its cycle for the first time after one step.
3. Starts and ends of the blocks ensure the input circular word contains one run of seven zeros, starts C with two ones and never sees three consecutive ones, including the boundary from C into A. The output circular word sees three successive ones exactly once, all in C. This fixes C's location on each circle, forces least period $N$, and prevents a cross-family identification. In both columns A and B have different initial bits. Reading after C distinguishes shuffles at fixed $a,b$. All shuffles with the same reduced denominator therefore still yield distinct pairs, with the input and output families disjoint.
4. Euler’s theorem gives $D\equiv-2\bmod M$ as claimed. The determinant $-1$ even permits the explicit classes $a\equiv3$, $b\equiv-6\bmod\varphi(M)$. For each fixed $M$, balanced shuffles yield $2^{2n+O_M(\log n)}$ distinct pairs and $D=2^{5n+O_M(1)}$. The divisor estimate follows from bounding the contribution of each large prime by $p^{\delta e}$ and multiplying fixed finite constants for the small primes. Some divisor has $D^{2/5-o(1)}$ pairs. Its size grows because the distinct marked phases $X$ all lie in $(0,1]$, where each fixed reduced denominator supports at most $d$ different phases. The choice of $n$ can be repeated *after* each finite enlargement of $M$. This proves the pairwise coprimality induction.
5. The conclusion concerns positive rational cycles at growing nonordinary denominators. It does not supply a pair in the ordinary lattice, invariance away from the two marked cycles, or least-disagreement minimality. The authors explicitly separate these questions.

The independent replay used the position-sum carry formula and rational parity via the reduced numerator, then iterated every edge. It passed for 2,026 shuffles and 140,244 rational phases, with distinct numerical minima for all 2,026 input cycles and all 2,026 output cycles and disjoint minima between the two families. The largest observed multiplicity at one reduced denominator within one fixed-clock group was 222. It also checked the prime-avoidance clocks at moduli 5, 35 and 385. These finite controls support the algebraic review.
