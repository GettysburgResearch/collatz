I checked Yu's original Theorem 1, including (1.2), (1.5), (1.8) and (1.9), on printed pages 337–339. Its nonzero product-minus-one conclusion applies to arbitrary dyadic units, without an independence hypothesis. Taking the fixed field \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\) handles \(p=2\). For equal \(v_2(U),v_2(V)\), the base \(-U/V\) is indeed a unit and the product minus one is exactly \(-(UH+V)/V\). An exponent of one multiplies its variable height, and the height factors of all primes in the fixed set are constants. Zero \(V\), unequal valuations and zero exponents in the smooth number cause no exception. This proves the linear-in-coefficient-height, logarithmic-in-\(\log H\) estimate stated. [Yu, Theorem 1](https://bibliotekanauki.pl/articles/1390501.pdf)

I rederived the one-step and full-block numerators and denominators; the final physical valuation is \(1+v_2(3^tw-1)\) and the total denominator increase of that block is \(t+v_2(3^tw-1)\). The two applications of Yu therefore multiply the coefficient-height bound by at most a constant times \((\log(2+\log H))^2\), independently of the internal run length. The same coefficient representation bounds the intermediate states. Raising that bound to the stated number of steps or blocks, the lower bound \(uH+v\ge H/2\), and the trivial upper and denominator bounds recover the claimed uniform \(L+O(L^{1-\epsilon/2})\) estimate. The Mersenne endpoint \((3^m-1)/2^{v_2(3^m-1)}\) satisfies the theorem's height hypothesis uniformly for large \(m\). The last strict shortcut stopping-time inequality follows from the multiplicative lower bound and remains conditional on such a first descent occurring.
