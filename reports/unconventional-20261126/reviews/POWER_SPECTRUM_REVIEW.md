**Béhani's question.** Pages 14–15 of Béhani's arXiv paper explicitly ask both for the exact radius on \(\omega_0\) and whether it equals \(\sqrt2\). Proposition 4.2 takes the maximum, over \(r\bmod 3^k\), of the sum of normalized slopes of inverse fibers. The ratio in its proof is \((j+1)/(a+1)\) for an input \(j\) with \(T^k j=a\), so this is the increasing coefficient weight in the inverse sum. Large ordinary representatives of each class give all its admissible branches above the discarded monomial degrees. [Béhani, Question 4.1 and Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.03203)

A length-\(k\) inverse word with \(q\) odd edges has normalized slope \(2^k/3^q\), and exactly one admissible residue class modulo \(3^q\). This last fact follows by induction: each inverse odd step adds precisely one congruence on the next freely variable ternary digit because the previous affine numerator has a power of two as its slope. Distinct words do not double-count an ordinary preimage at the same endpoint, since a forward trajectory determines its parity word. Averaging over all \(3^k\) endpoint residues therefore gives exactly \(2^k\sum_q\binom{k}{q}9^{-q}=(20/9)^k\) for the squared norm expression, before maximizing. The spectral radius is at least \(\sqrt{20}/3>\sqrt2\). This answers the proposed equality in the negative and leaves the exact radius undetermined.
