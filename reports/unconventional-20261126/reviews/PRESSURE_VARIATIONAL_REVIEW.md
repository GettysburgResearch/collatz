## An independent derivation of the stationary bound

The geometric parameter is fixed in \(0<p<1\). The transfer on \(C(\mathbb Z_3)\) is positive, bounded and sends the constant function under any iterate to a continuous function. In particular its norm is its value on the constant one in supremum norm. The Jacobian factor 3 cancels the Haar mass of the branch domain, so each iterate preserves the integral and its maximum row \(Z_n\) is at least one. Each chosen maximizing point is fixed while its path of length \(n\) is sampled.

Writing \(P_n(w)=3^n p^{\otimes n}(w)\mathbf1_{\rm legal}(w)/Z_n\), the average total letter is finite for each \(n\). Domination by \(3^np^{\otimes n}\) establishes this before any entropy identity is used. The probability vector has finite entropy by the finite mean at each of the \(n\) coordinates. The exact identity is
\[
  n^{-1}\log Z_n=\log3+n^{-1} H_{P_n}(W)
                    +n^{-1}\mathbb E_{P_n}\sum_j\log p_{E_j}.
\]
Conditioning the \(j\)-th symbol on the actual current state discards some past information because the state is determined by the past symbols and the fixed start. Discarding the independent uniform time index from the edge occupation average can only increase that conditional entropy again. This gives (2), with its indicated inequality direction. No stationary Markov assumption was used for the finite Gibbs paths.

The divergence on the right of (2) is at most \(\log3\). Applying the relative-entropy variational bound with \(t(e\wedge M)\), then increasing \(M\), bounds the first moment uniformly in \(n\) for \(0<t<-\log(1-p)\). Compact state space and Markov's tail inequality ensure tightness of the edge laws. A bounded mean alone need not give uniform integrability of these means. The proof does not take their limits. It uses lower semicontinuity of the *joint* divergence with both probability arguments converging weakly. The product arguments really converge because their second factor \(p\) is fixed. The limiting first moment is finite by lower semicontinuity of this nonnegative cost. Its negative log likelihood is an affine function of that mean and is integrable. Entropy of a nonnegative integer of finite mean is finite by geometric comparison, so the expansion of the limiting divergence in (3) is valid.

The legal graph is clopen in \(\mathbb Z_3\times\mathbb N_{>0}\) with the second coordinate discrete. The endpoint map is continuous on it. For a bounded continuous test function on states the difference between the two marginals of the empirical average is exactly the endpoint difference divided by \(n\). The limit has equal marginals and allocates no mass to a state with no legal branch. Standard Borel disintegration thus produces a transition kernel supported on legal branches almost surely.

To verify the component step, take a two-sided stationary version on labeled edges with the Markov law just constructed. Any invariant event is in the completed past through the current state, excluding the next label. Indeed a sequence of cylinder approximants with errors tending to zero can be shifted into that past and retains exactly the same approximation errors by invariance and stationarity. Conditional next-label probabilities given the past therefore remain \(q_e(X_0)\) after revealing the invariant sigma-field as well. Disintegrating on a countable cylinder generator gives that same transition rule in almost every ergodic component. Component entropy and log likelihood average to the original reward. Almost every component has finite mean even when the constants needed in its later entropy argument depend on the component.

## Conditional information and truncation

The identity \(H(W_n\mid X_0)=n\beta\) concerns the stationary Markov law, where the current state and preceding labels determine the next state. Each unused source digit after \(j\) labels is determined at the required precision by the initial \(n\) digits and the preceding labels. Dropping more information gives the sum in (5). Conditional entropies of a countable symbol decrease to the conditional entropy on the limiting sigma-field because its unconditional entropy is finite. One proof first collapses labels above a cutoff to one label, applies finite-alphabet martingale convergence, and bounds the residual uniformly by the entropy lost by this unconditional collapse.

For (7) the relevant ball probability after choosing the observed label is its probability conditional on the past, multiplied by its mean next-label probability on that ball and divided by the probability of the label given the past. The affine branch transports this ball to the terminal ball bijectively. For the realized label all denominators are positive almost surely. The random negative log conditional label probability converges almost surely and its mean converges to \(\beta\) by the preceding countable-entropy fact. Since it is nonnegative this gives \(L^1\) convergence, without a uniform positive lower bound on the measurable policy. The ergodic averages of the resulting fixed integrable increment converge in \(L^1\) by approximating it by bounded functions on this probability space. This verifies the integrability claims in (8).

Write \(A=\sum_{j<n}E_j\) under this one ergodic component. Its normalized value converges in \(L^1\) to the finite mean. Thus for fixed \(K>\mathbb E E_0\) the contribution \(\mathbb E[A\mathbf1_{A>Kn}]=o(n)\). This is a statement within one stationary process, unlike a possible interchange of means across the maximizing occupation laws. Conditional on \(A=a\), the number of compositions into \(n\) positive letters is at most \(2^a\). Losing the exceptional words therefore costs \(o(n)\) entropy. Recording \(A\) itself costs \(O(\log n)\).

At fixed \(A\), backwards division recovers each \(E_j\) for \(j\ge1\) since all the previous nonempty carries are odd. The remaining letter is determined by the total. In particular the special \(n=1\) case works even though its carry is always one. The carries are distinct for distinct fixed-total words and lie strictly below \(2^A3^n\). Reducing the offset \(V\) modulo \(3^{Kn}\) is injective on those words with \(A\le Kn\) after \(B,A\) are known. This proves the information lower bound (12). In (13) the full-symbol mutual information is finite since \(W_n\) has finite entropy. After \(A\) is recorded, data processing followed by the chain rule subtracts the nonnegative information \(I(X_0;A\mid B)\). No generally false inequality asserting that adding a conditioning variable reduces mutual information is needed.

The local product in (14) conditions each marginal on *its own* lower digits and on \(C\). With exactly this choice the divergence chain rule gives the increment of conditional mutual information of the two full prefixes. The sum of \(m\)-step increments counts each one-step increment at most \(m\) times. Pinsker and finite-alphabet entropy continuity apply after fixing \(m\), even for arbitrarily small convolution gain at that \(m\).

## Adversarial checks

A zero-branching component may have positive stationary label entropy. In that case the current ternary state selects the next label deterministically, while the state itself can have positive entropy. The claimed pressure bound only uses \(\beta=0\) and \(p_e\le p\), so it permits such components. On a positive-branching component the full entropy dimension in (4) gives the reward as \(h_E+\mathbb E\log p_E\). The one-label entropy dominates \(h_E\), and the difference to minus the geometric cross-entropy is a negative relative divergence. This is still true when the marginal law is not geometric. Taking the larger of the two reward bounds handles both sides of \(p=1/3\) and their equality at that bias. The invariant point \(-1\) with fixed label one and the preserved Haar integral provide the claimed lower bounds without any assumption that Haar itself is a stationary inverse policy.

Finite branching recurrent graphs do not supply a hidden positive-entropy atomic counterexample. A recurrent state in such a graph lies on an affine period, hence is rational. The raw denominator \(2^A-3^n\) is odd and prime to 3, and all phases of a nonempty legal inverse cycle are odd at the binary place. The forward odd Syracuse image of every such phase is unique, so two distinct inverse cycles through it cannot coexist. This sanity check is independent of the entropy proof.

For the other entropy steps I rederived the mixture remainder in (9), the cancellation of the \(m\) boundary terms for the moving scale average in (10), and the lower/upper telescoping bounds on \(g_j\) in (15). All changes of order keep \(m\) fixed before \(n\to\infty\). Positive branch entropy guarantees a positive proportion of noise digits at scale one, and the assumed dimension deficit gives low-entropy base blocks on all but a small average fraction of scales. The common lower-prefix carry merely translates the finite cyclic group. No decorrelation of the two goodness events is needed. The product-law local gain is positive by compactness because two noise values distinct modulo 3 have a difference generating \(\mathbb Z/3^m\).

For the operator normalization I checked directly that Béhani uses the shortcut odd branch in the paper body, with the transfer on the ordinary Bergman quotient by the first three degrees. Its abstract describes the associated original map. In the induced resolvent \(t=20/9\) indeed gives \(p=1-2/t=1/10\) and prefactor \(2/(9(t-2))=1\). These are consistent with the proposed radius \(\sqrt{20}/3\).
