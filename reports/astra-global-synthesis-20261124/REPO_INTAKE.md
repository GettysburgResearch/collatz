## What the synthesis changed

The critical-mark and synchronization routes now meet at anchors on one
actual orbit, and the entire family of alternate arms shares one
summable correction bound. This yields an exact bounded-clock normal
form for least unit representatives. Absolute widths then force those
representatives to form a literal orbit on a specified clock sector.

The comparison route initially produced a paired-only language. A later
exact signed-rational search found the core 61 and slope 183/256, which
join singleton and paired paid clusters by changing the intermediate
slopes and odd counts. The final proof uses literal ordinary paths; the
search itself supplies no all-time realization or universal coverage.

The moving-octave and affine-permutation results address variable height
profiles and arbitrary-amplitude cycles. Their hypotheses and finite
Lean interfaces remain separate from the unresolved all-orbit argument.
