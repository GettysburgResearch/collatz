**Quantitative connection to component mass.** Under Mazur’s fixed-target theorem, even $o(X)$ misses along arbitrary unbounded octaves would suffice, with $H,y$ varying. A counterexample gives two colors of positive lower density: reaching one and its complement. Both are exactly invariant under doubling. Each of the first $J$ dyadic subintervals below $X$ injects, preserving color, into $[X,2X)$ by a suitable power of two. The remaining tail contains at most $X2^{-J}+1$ integers. Choosing $J$ large for each fixed positive lower density gives a single $c>0$ with at least $cX$ of either color in every sufficiently large octave. Every gate receives only its own color, hence misses at least $cX$ there, uniformly in both the gate and deadline. Conversely, universal convergence gives finite capture of any chosen octave by gate one. Neither the tower block nor the source-level count of local mergers establishes near-total capture in a macroscopic octave.
