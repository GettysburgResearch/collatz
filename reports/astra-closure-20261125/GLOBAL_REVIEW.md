**Fourier–Stieltjes condition.** A bounded subsequence of the symmetric Fejér polynomials in integral norm is a bounded sequence of finite measures on the compact circle. The separability of its continuous test functions permits a weak-star convergent subsequence. At any fixed frequency the Fejér multiplier tends to one. Conversely, convolution with a nonnegative, integral-one Fejér kernel bounds the full sequence of norms by the total variation of a representing measure. Boolean coefficients imply convolution idempotence by uniqueness. The idempotent theorem applies to bounded complex measures on this compact group, with discrete dual $\mathbb Z$. Its cosets are arithmetic progressions and singletons. Any finite Boolean combination agrees with a union of classes modulo one common positive period outside finitely many points. This proves eventual periodicity on the positive trace. [Green and Sanders, statement of the idempotent theorem](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2008/168-3/p09)

**Ordinary arithmetic.** For positive arguments congruent modulo an eventual period $d$, double them the same number of times to reach the periodic tail, then pull their equality back by doubling invariance. For a global positive period divisible by two, doubling gives the smaller period $d/2$. With $d$ odd, use positive odd representatives to extend $b(3x+1)=b(x)$ across every class. If $3\mid d$, the two affine images of $x$ and $x+d/3$ differ by $d$, so $d/3$ is a period. Reducing gives a period prime to six. On that quotient, the two orders of the affine and doubling maps equate the colors at $6x+1$ and $6x+2$, covering every adjacent pair. The formal theorem quantifies over arbitrary color types, concludes equality only for positive arguments, and assumes the two exact ordinary identities. The true mark satisfies them including at 1 because both 1 and 2 reach 1.

**Norm identity.** The even coefficient at $2k$ in $K_{2N}$ equals the coefficient at $k$ in $K_N$ exactly, by the matching triangular weights and doubling invariance. The symmetric odd part is real and changes sign under half-translation. The even part stays fixed there. Averaging the absolute values gives $\max(|A_N|,|H_N|)$ pointwise for the paired points. Haar measure is preserved by doubling, so subtracting the integral norm of the even part gives precisely the stated overshoot. Its dyadic partial sum telescopes, with no cancellation between scales. The four-way equivalence follows; absolute summability of interpolation-error norms would be a different, stronger sufficient estimate.

**Growth ceiling.** Each difference between neighboring triangular coefficients is bounded by one at a color change and by $1/N$ where the colors agree. Both the true mark and the visit-to-5 mark have matching zero colors at 0 and 1. Summing squares on both halves of the symmetric support gives $O(V(N)+1/N)$. Parseval after multiplication by $1-e(t)$ therefore controls the large-arc integral by $O(\sqrt{(V(N)+1/N)/\eta})$. The short arc contributes $O(\sqrt{N\eta})$. For $V(N)=O(N/\sqrt{\log N})$ and $\eta=(\log N)^{-1/4}$, both are $O(\sqrt N/(\log N)^{1/8})$. Dyadic amplification by at least $\sqrt2$ at **every** sufficiently late doubling would force a positive multiple of $\sqrt N$ once a nonzero frequency enters, contradicting that ceiling. Positivity of the actual overshoot gives no such factor.
