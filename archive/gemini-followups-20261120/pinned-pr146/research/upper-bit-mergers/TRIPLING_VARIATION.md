# Counting changes of component along an ordinary interval

**PROPOSED, unconditional.** Let $f$ be any labeling of positive integers with $f(Tn)=f(n)$ for the ordinary shortcut map. In particular, $f$ may give the merger component itself or indicate membership in one specified component. Let $D_f(X)$ count the odd $n\le X$ with $f(3n)\ne f(n)$. For every fixed $b>1$ there are constants $C_b,C'_b$ such that
$$
 \#\{R\le n\le bR:f(n)\ne f(n+1)\}\le C_b D_f(C'_b R)\qquad(R\ge1). \tag{1}
$$
This bounds the number of changes between consecutive integers even when $R$ and the number of colors vary. It is the discrete estimate used in the [rotation argument](THREE_RAY_VARIATION.md).

## Two comparisons with a neighbor of a multiple

Write $g(y)=f(y-1)$ for $y\ge2$ and $J(y)=f(y+1)$. Each of the comparisons
$$
 f(4h-1)=f(h),\qquad f(16h+1)=f(h) \tag{2}
$$
can fail at only $O_b(D_f(C_b R))$ integers $h\in[R,bR]$. Here and below constants may increase at each use. Doubling always preserves the label. In an equality between labels, removing a factor of three costs at most one test of the odd part of the smaller argument.

For the minus sign we have $g(2u)=g(3u)$ from $T(2u-1)=3u-1$. Also $g(4u)=g(9u)$ by two such exchanges. The odd start $3(8u-1)$ steps to $4(9u-1)$, so $g(4u)=g(8u)$ if $8u-1$ passes the tripling test. Write $h=2^v w$ with $w$ odd. This last comparison strips all $v$ factors of two from $g(4h)$, testing only $8\cdot2^a w-1$ for $0\le a<v$. Next $g(4w)=g(9w)$. We can change $g(3^k w)$ to $g(3^{k+1}w)$ for $k\ge2$ by putting $u=3^{k-2}w$ and using $g(9u)=g(4u)=g(8u)=g(27u)$, at the cost of testing $8u-1$. Some $j\in[2,5]$ gives $Y=3^jw$ congruent to 3 or 5 modulo 16. In these classes $f(Y-1)=f(Y)$ by the finite words in the table below. Strip the $j$ factors of three from $f(Y)$, testing $3^k w$ for $0\le k<j$, to finish the minus comparison.

For the plus sign the two-step identity at $4u+1$ gives $J(4u)=J(3u)$. The paths from $3(16u+1)=48u+3$ and $18u+1$ reach the same component, through $108u+8=4(27u+2)$ and $27u+2$ respectively. Hence $J(9u)=J(18u)$ if $16u+1$ passes, and two ordinary exchanges on each side give $J(16u)=J(32u)$. This last equality strips the $v$ twos from $J(16h)$, testing $16\cdot2^a w+1$ for $0\le a<v$. Now $J(16w)=J(9w)$. For $u=3^{k-2}w$, use
$$
 J(9u)=J(16u)=J(32u)=J(64u)=J(27u).
$$
This increases the exponent $k$ by one at cost of testing $16u+1$ and $32u+1$. Some $j\in[2,33]$ has $Y=3^jw$ congruent to 49 modulo 128 or to 5 modulo 32. Indeed the subgroup generated by 3 modulo 128 has order 32 and consists of the units congruent to 1 or 3 modulo 8. This follows by successive squaring from $v_2(9^{2^a}-1)=a+3$. The two target residues 49 and 5 lie in different cosets. At this $Y$ we have $J(Y)=f(Y)$, then remove the factors of three as before.

| Congruence | Starts at the base residue | Clock | Common endpoint at base | Odd inputs on each arm |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $Y=3\bmod16$ | $2,3$ | 4 | 2 | 2 |
| $Y=5\bmod8$ | $4,5$ | 3 | 2 | 1 |
| $Y=49\bmod128$ | $49,50$ | 7 | 11 | 3 |
| $Y=5\bmod32$ | $5,6$ | 5 | 2 | 2 |

The equal counts and clocks ensure the meetings persist after every shift by the displayed modulus, by the ordinary affine iterate formula. All tripling tests above occur at positive odd inputs $O_b(R)$. A failed test while stripping twos determines $w$ by taking the odd part of the integer recovered from $8\cdot2^a w-1$ or $16\cdot2^a w+1$. Every other possible test belongs to a fixed finite list of affine functions of $w$, each injective. Once $w$ is fixed there are at most $1+\lceil\log_2 b\rceil$ possible $h$ in $[R,bR]$. Counting failures proves the claim for (2).

## A finite chain from one integer to its successor

Put $D=288=2^5 3^2$. Compare the labels at $Dn+p$ for the following offsets, in order.
$$
 0,18,24,32,33,36,48,64,63,72,96,99,108,144,192,198,216,288.
$$
For each edge let $s$ be the absolute difference of its offsets. The table specifies which identity applies after removing $s$, whose prime factors belong to $\{2,3\}$.

| Offsets joined | Comparison after dividing both arguments by $s$ |
|---|---|
| $0,18$ · $32,33$ · $96,99$ · $192,198$ | $16h,16h+1$ |
| $18,24$ · $24,32$ · $33,36$ · $36,48$ · $64,63$ · $63,72$ · $72,96$ · $99,108$ · $108,144$ · $198,216$ · $216,288$ | $4h,4h-1$ in either order |
| $48,64$ · $144,192$ | $3h,3h+1$ with $h$ odd |

In the last row the odd inputs are $6n+1$ and $2n+1$. Their ordinary step puts $3h+1$ in the component of $h$, and tripling identifies $h$ with $3h$ unless $h$ fails. In the first two rows, (2) compares the endpoints, because $f(16h)=f(4h)=f(h)$. In every row $h$ is a positive affine function of $n$ with nonzero slope and integral values. Its range for $n\in[R,bR]$ lies in a fixed-ratio interval of size $O_b(R)$. Thus (2) bounds its contribution.

For completeness, removing any fixed smooth multiplier $s=2^a3^e$ from an integer affine expression $s m(n)$ costs at most $e$ tripling tests at the odd parts of $3^i m(n)$ for $0\le i<e$. Each test argument before removing twos is injective in $n$ and varies over a fixed-ratio interval. One bad odd part has only $O_b(1)$ dyadic lifts in that interval. This controls every use of division by $s$ in the table and the endpoint comparisons $f(Dn)=f(n)$ and $f(D(n+1))=f(n+1)$. A union bound along the chain now proves (1).
