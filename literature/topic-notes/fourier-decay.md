# Fourier decay: stationary self-similar measures versus EQ

## Located results

Li and Sahlsten prove Rajchman and quantitative logarithmic Fourier-decay results for broad classes of real self-similar measures, including irrational logarithmic contraction relations. Solomyak proves power Fourier decay outside a small exceptional parameter set for families of self-similar measures on the real line. [@LiSahlsten2022; @Solomyak2021]

## The repository product

The EQ problem studies finite products attached to coded residue sets modulo `64^K`, with both the modulus and coefficient orbit changing with `K`. Frequencies are restricted to a `K`-dependent survivor range. The object is a triangular array of finite modular measures rather than one fixed stationary real self-similar measure.

## Hypothesis mismatch

No current reduction supplies:

- one fixed real IFS and invariant probability measure whose Fourier transform equals all `S_K`;
- a fixed contraction parameter to which an exceptional-set theorem applies;
- a comparison carrying decay from real frequencies to the required modular frequency window uniformly in `K`.

Consequently, “Li–Sahlsten/Solomyak apply to exactly this system” is too strong. Their renewal, sum-product, and separation mechanisms are plausible methodological templates.

## Strategic reduction target

Construct a stationary skew-product or suspension whose level-`K` marginals are the coded measures, then prove that its Fourier cocycle satisfies a uniform nonconcentration/renewal estimate. Without that step, EQ remains a related but distinct problem.
