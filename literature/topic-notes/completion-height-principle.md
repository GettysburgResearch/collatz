# Completion-height principle

## The recurring mechanism

Four active programs now produce the same logical shape.

1. A finite directive determines one residue class modulo a rapidly growing `p`-power.
2. An infinite directive determines one point in a completion such as `Z_2`.
3. The completion point is an ordinary integer exactly when its least representatives eventually stabilize.
4. Long repetition, low carry energy, or a stationary match produces a rational/algebraic approximant of controlled global height.
5. Excessive `p`-adic agreement forces exact equality by an integer numerator or product-formula contradiction.

Instances:

- `PADIC/T-9401`: periodic approximant versus ordinary survivor code;
- `ADEL/L-9310`: zero-carry run versus a nonzero integer divisible by `81^(...)`;
- `PR3/L-0023`: divided exponentials converging to a `2`-adic logarithm;
- `H/L-9503`: nested ghost cylinders and stabilized representatives;
- `FOUNDRY/T-9601`: a unique completion point selected by causal feedback.

## Abstract target

A useful theorem schema would start with:

```text
x in Qbar,
y_n in Qbar,
|x-y_n|_p <= p^(-L_n),
H(y_n) <= exp(h_n),
```

where `H` is a multiplicative height. If

```text
L_n / h_n -> infinity,
```

then product-formula or Subspace Theorem arguments often force `x=y_n` infinitely often, or force `x` to be transcendental/nonalgebraic.

The repository's hard case is adaptive:

```text
- y_n depends on a growing directive state;
- its height may grow at the same rate as the agreement;
- x may be an unknown ordinary integer rather than a fixed algebraic parameter;
- the directive can steer to avoid exact repetitions.
```

## Literature toolkit

### Elementary product formula

For a nonzero rational numerator `Aq-p`, simultaneous upper bounds on its real magnitude and lower divisibility by a large `p`-power contradict one another. This is the engine already used in `PADIC/T-9401` and `ADEL/L-9310`.

### Ridout / Subspace Theorem

Ridout's theorem handles exceptionally good simultaneous real and `p`-adic approximation to algebraic numbers. Quantitative Subspace Theorem variants can treat families of linear forms and may replace exact repetitions by approximate/stammering ones.

### `p`-adic logarithmic forms

Mahler, Gel'fond, Schinzel, Yu, and later work give lower bounds for nonzero linear forms in `p`-adic logarithms. This may make finite-library power-sum obstructions effective when the native recurrence is reduced to fixed algebraic bases.

### Combinatorics on words

Adamczewski--Bugeaud techniques turn repeated or symmetric prefixes into rational approximants with controlled height. Their direct hypotheses are not automatically satisfied by rational-base Collatz codes, but the proof architecture is relevant.

## Recommended theorem packet

A cross-program packet should prove increasingly strong versions:

1. **Exact repeat:** reproduce `PADIC/T-9401` in a general coprime chart.
2. **Fractional power:** handle prefixes containing `UV^t` with `t>1` and explicit height.
3. **Symmetric pattern:** handle long palindromic or mirrored blocks.
4. **Finite-state directive:** translate bounded transducer memory into approximant height.
5. **Stack directive:** include one unbounded counter and determine the exact height tax.
6. **Adaptive carry:** combine carry energy with approximant height without assuming a repeated word.

## Acceptance boundary

A theorem that only says the completion point is irrational does not exclude an ordinary integer unless the point itself is the proposed initial state. A theorem about a stationary or open-loop directive does not automatically cover the forward adaptive counter-stack.

The decisive target is:

> Every admissible infinite directive whose exact constraints are generated by the proposed finite-control-plus-counter architecture has infinitely many nonzero new residue blocks.

That statement would prove nonstabilization and close the ordinary-integer route for the architecture. Its negation, with an explicit eventually-zero directive, would produce an ordinary candidate initialization.
