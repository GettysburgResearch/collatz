# Rational-base numeration, negative shadows, and renewal graphs

## Repo object

A negative-return edge has an exact quotient equation

```text
N q = M q' + a,
```

with `M=2^L`, `N=3^r`, a signed offset `a`, and a dyadic cylinder restriction inherited from a finite Collatz parity block. A successful finite graph must select admissible edges forever from one ordinary positive quotient.

## Located nearby literature

Akiyama–Frougny–Sakarovitch study canonical rational-base `p/q` representations and powers of rationals modulo one. Hutchinson and Mauldin–Williams supply fixed-point and dimension theory for finite graph-directed contraction systems. Karp supplies finite cycle-mean infrastructure.

## Non-transfers

The repo language is signed, cylinder-selected, and tied to exact finite Collatz blocks. It is not automatically the canonical `p/q` language. A graph-directed attractor may consist entirely of nonordinary completion points. Positive cycle mean says nothing about arithmetic coverage.

## Research interface

A theorem-quality selector should publish:

1. the graph and exact edge cylinders;
2. the affine quotient map on each edge;
3. a deterministic choice rule or a proved nonempty invariant relation;
4. phase potentials/ranking certificate;
5. an ordinary initial quotient;
6. exact replay of the selected Collatz blocks;
7. an explicit finite-versus-adic proof.

The most relevant objective is normalized real-window size and selector persistence, not raw alphabet cardinality.
