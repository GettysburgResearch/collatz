# Collatz critical particles and the spine/many-to-one method

## Native construction

PR #3 constructs an ordered binary particle system in which:

- parent mass is conserved by the two children;
- one distinguished child's rank is the shortcut Collatz image of the parent rank;
- the normalized endpoint law is a Doob transform;
- the marked ordinary rank follows the exact Collatz orbit.

This is not merely analogous to a branching process. Algebraically it is an exact mass-conserving size-biased spine, as formalized in `LIT-KTHM-0032`.

## Literature toolkit

### Many-to-one

Weighted sums over all generation-`L` particles become expectations of one size-biased spine. This can convert a difficult population count into a one-path calculation.

### Additive martingales

If a weight is harmonic for the branching rule, its generation sum is conserved or forms a martingale after normalization.

### Derivative martingales

At a critical parameter, differentiating the additive martingale can produce a nondegenerate limit even when the additive one degenerates. The repository's criticality constants and exact phase pressure make this worth testing.

### Entropy and `L log L`

The Lyons--Pemantle--Peres change of measure identifies when the size-biased law is absolutely continuous or singular relative to the ordinary tree law. A deterministic analogue may quantify how costly the marked ordinary spine is among the unmarked descendants.

### Spine large deviations

The endpoint law can be tilted by growth, phase escape, or connector cost. Comparing these tilts may identify the empirical transition frequencies a successful counter-stack would need.

## Main caution

A positive martingale limit or a typical size-biased path is a measure-level statement. It does not establish that the corresponding infinite connector choices are generated by one finite ordinary state. Every spine theorem must retain the exact marked-rank translation.
