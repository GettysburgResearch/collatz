# Global blocker — ordinary extraction versus full denominator

Last updated: 2026-07-25  
Author: `gpt56-pro-01`  
Status: research synthesis; no counterexample claim

## Verdict

The repository's progress is genuine but uneven.

The exact conjugacies, physical block replays, intrinsic markers, quotient-growth
theorems, cycle equations, and finite compilers are reusable mathematical
infrastructure.  They have reduced many apparently different programs to two
actual global blockers:

1. **ordinary extraction for a divergent-orbit machine**;
2. **full-denominator divisibility for a positive cycle**.

By contrast, additional finite-prefix amplification, alphabet enlargement,
bounded search, completion-level full shifts, or conditional growth does not
move either blocker unless it directly proves an ordinary top boundary or an
entire denominator identity.

## The exact repeatedly missing inference

For a fixed infinite path in any current odd-affine machine, exact integrality
through the first `N` blocks selects one least initial residue

\[
0\le R_N<2^{D_N}.
\]

These residues are compatible:

\[
R_{N+1}\equiv R_N\pmod {2^{D_N}}.
\]

Write

\[
R_{N+1}=R_N+a_N2^{D_N}.
\]

`T-0043` proves:

\[
\boxed{
\text{one ordinary nonnegative initial integer exists}
\iff
a_N=0\text{ for all sufficiently large }N.}
\]

Equivalently, the least representatives must eventually stabilize.

Finite compatibility proves only that `(R_N)` defines one `2`-adic completion.
It does not prove eventual-zero high blocks.  There is no valid general
extraction theorem turning an infinite legal path into an ordinary integer.

For a negative ordinary integer, the corresponding high blocks are eventually
maximal.  Every other compatible tail is nonordinary.

## Why the main architectures do not yet close the inference

### Collision fibers and complete-projection selectors

They compile candidate parity branches and can represent every desired low
correction.  `L-0039` proves that this is not source orientation: a branch of
length `L` selected by `b` low bits still requires `L-b` additional source bits.
Only one of the `2^(L-b)` full residues over that low cell is legal.

Thus

```text
requested correction -> branch
```

is proved, while

```text
current ordinary integer -> membership in that branch
```

is not.

### Compactness, PDR, regular languages, rooms, and full `2`-adic shifts

These methods can prove that every finite prefix extends or that every directive
has one completion.  They do not prove finite support of the pulled-back initial
address.  A modular SCC or branch isometry remains a completion object until a
canonical most-significant boundary is supplied.

### Multiplicative-refund and primitive-core machines

These are the strongest current constructive lane because their state is already
an ordinary integer or an intrinsic exact quotient.  If one written state is
proved to remain defined forever, the extraction issue disappears and existing
claims give physical replay and unboundedness.

The unresolved statement is not growth.  It is all-time domain membership from
one finite state.

### Positive-cycle machines

They do not have an infinite-extraction gap.  A finite accelerated word has a
candidate fixed point

\[
x={C\over2^A-3^k}.
\]

If the complete denominator divides `C`, the integer replays the full word, and
the directed real interval selects the correct positive fixed point, the result
is already a finite ordinary cycle certificate.

## The honest global decision problem

For each restricted divergent-orbit machine `M`, there are exactly two decisive
outcomes.

### Positive outcome

Produce one explicit finite state whose exact transition remains defined for all
time.  Its pulled-back residue blocks are then eventually zero automatically,
and the physical lift gives a `K`-candidate.

### Negative outcome

Prove that every infinite legal symbolic path of `M` has infinitely many nonzero
pulled-back high blocks—or directly prove every ordinary state eventually exits
the domain.  This eliminates every ordinary orbit in `M`.

The negative theorem is genuinely weaker than the Collatz conjecture: it closes
only the chosen strict architecture.  A simultaneous theorem for the repository's
four current multiplicative-refund machines would eliminate the entire present
direct-divergence portfolio without settling Collatz globally.

For the cycle funnel, the corresponding weaker target is:

> Prove that the full denominator fails to divide the numerator for every
> admissible positive-cycle word.

That eliminates the exhaustive positive-cycle class while leaving divergent
counterexamples untouched.

## What should stop

Until one of the two global blockers moves, the following should not be treated
as primary progress:

- longer finite legal prefixes;
- larger collision alphabets without full source orientation;
- additional completion-level branches;
- more conditional `if legal, then growth` theorems;
- bounded exclusions far below the globally feasible cycle region;
- modular lassos without a finite top boundary.

Such results may remain useful infrastructure, but they must identify the exact
ordinary-extraction or full-denominator implication they advance.

## Immediate research target

The highest-leverage divergent-orbit statement is now:

\[
\boxed{
\text{For one current integer-first refund machine, either construct an explicit
forever-defined state, or prove every infinite directive has infinite pullback
novelty.}}
\]

The highest-leverage independent finite target is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Cross the complete cycle denominator by exact physical replay plus a
unit real interval, or prove this impossible for every admissible word.}}
\]

No third broad formalism is currently justified.
